## MANAGEMENT LETTERS

### Key Themes (Chairman's Letter — Suresh J. Patel)

- **Strategic pivot from volume to value**: Entering high-value specialty chemicals (benzene derivatives — MCB, PNCB, ONCB, MNCB, DCB) via greenfield Saykha facility
- **Operational consolidation**: Permanently decommissioned legacy Vatva units (Units I–III, built 1989–93) — not a cost-cutting move but strategic footprint optimization
- **Global supply chain repositioning**: Leveraging India's emergence as preferred chemical sourcing hub (China+1)
- **Zero Liquid Discharge compliance** maintained across major units — positioned as competitive differentiator
- **Measured optimism for FY26** with clear 5-point roadmap (Saykha ramp, domestic deepening, export expansion, governance, people/innovation)

### Stated Opportunities
- Saykha benzene derivatives → pharma, agrochemicals, pigments, polymers markets
- Anti-dumping duty on Chinese TCCA imports (March 2025) → improved domestic pricing power
- Growing demand in water treatment, hygiene, and global specialty chemicals
- Value-added liquid dyes for paper (SE Asia, Europe)

### Stated Risks
- Hyperinflation in Turkey (subsidiary ongoing losses)
- Global chemical industry volatility and trade policy uncertainty
- Stiff competition and slower-than-expected demand for benzene derivatives
- Raw material price fluctuations

### Strategic Priorities (FY26)
1. Ramp up Saykha to 60–70% utilization
2. Deepen domestic leadership across intermediates, dyestuffs, chlor-alkali
3. Expand export revenues with long-term customer programs
4. Sustain governance and environmental compliance standards
5. Invest in people, systems, and innovation

**Insight**: Revenue rebounded strongly but remains below FY23's ₹20,724M peak (from KPI page). PAT recovery from FY24's near-zero is fragile. The FY23 PAT figure of ₹2,849M appears anomalous — likely includes one-offs or restatements.

**Reconciling**: The KPI page shows FY22 revenue at ₹20,724M, FY23 at ₹12,421M, FY24 at ₹14,194M, FY25 at ₹17,449M. This suggests a different basis — possibly before restatement or different consolidation scope. The P&L shows FY25 revenue of ₹17,567M (consolidated).

**Assessment**: The net presentation is aggressive — showing ₹12.5M loss while potentially larger PPE damage is excluded from the reported figure. However, this is within standard practice when final assessment is pending.

## FINAL ACCOUNTING QUALITY SCORE: **AVERAGE** 🟡

### Rationale:
**Strengths:**
- Clean audit opinion, no qualifications
- Conservative accounting policies, no policy manipulation
- Zero promoter pledging, stable ownership
- Strong cash conversion relative to PAT
- Comprehensive RPT disclosures
- Good ESG compliance infrastructure

**Concerns:**
- **Critical**: ROCE/ROE far below cost of capital — economic value destruction
- **Critical**: Debt service coverage ratio below 1.0x — sustainability risk
- **Critical**: ₹544M in stalled CWIP with no impairment
- **Warning**: Credit rating downgrade (A → BBB+)
- **Warning**: No key audit matters flagged despite complexity
- **Warning**: Audit trail gaps in accounting and payroll software
- **Warning**: MSME payment delays with interest accrual
- **Watch**: Turkish subsidiary ongoing losses; goodwill recoverability
- **Watch**: Guarantees surged 147% YoY to ₹373M

**Bottom Line**: Bodal Chemicals demonstrates **technically compliant accounting** with no outright manipulation, but the **financial quality is deteriorating** — heavily leveraged, sub-optimal returns on capital, and trapped in projects that may not generate returns. The company is in a **transition year** with Saykha as the growth catalyst; FY26 execution on Saykha ramp-up and debt reduction will determine whether this becomes a value-creation story or remains a capital trap.
