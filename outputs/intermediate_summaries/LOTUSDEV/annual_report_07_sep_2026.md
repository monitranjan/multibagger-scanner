### Revenue & PAT Growth (3Y Consolidated)

| FY26 | 769 (₹7,689 Mn) | +40% | 243 (₹2,433 Mn) | +6.8% |

**Standalone Revenue anomaly:** FY25 standalone revenue was ₹3,872 Mn, FY26 only ₹1,427 Mn — a **63% decline** because revenue shifted to subsidiaries post-amalgamation/restructuring. Consolidated view is the correct lens.

### EBITDA Margin Trend — Contracting

| Metric | FY26 | FY25 | FY24 |
|---|---|---|---|
| EBITDA Margin (Consol) | 36.5% | 52.6% | 34.2% |
| PAT Margin (Consol) | 31.6% | 41.5% | 26.0% |

- **Verdict:** Margin contraction — explanation (one-off FY25 rate appreciation) is reasonable but should normalise in FY27
- EBITDA absolute: ₹281 Cr (FY26) vs ₹289 Cr (FY25) — **flat despite 40% revenue growth**

### CFO vs PAT — Critical Concern 🟡

| Metric | FY26 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating CFO (Consol) | **₹(325.8) Cr** | ₹(19.5) Cr |
| PAT (Consol) | ₹243.3 Cr | ₹227.9 Cr |

**MAJOR RED FLAG:** CFO is deeply negative while PAT is strongly positive.
- **CFO/PAT ratio: Negative in both years** — profit is NOT converting to cash
- Driven by: Working capital absorption (inventory +₹1,972 Cr; receivables +₹1,246 Cr; unbilled revenue +₹2,269 Cr)
- **Unsatisfied performance obligations: ₹6,100 Cr** (vs ₹839 Cr FY25) — strong forward visibility BUT also represents capital trapped in projects

### Working Capital Movement — Significant Deterioration 🟡

**Standalone:**
- Trade receivables: ₹2,334 Mn (vs ₹1,775 Mn FY25) — +31%
- Inventories: ₹2,109 Mn (vs ₹599 Mn FY25) — **+252%**
- Loans to subsidiaries: ₹8,244 Mn (vs ₹3,941 Mn) — **+109%**
- Other financial assets (unbilled): ₹2,515 Mn (consolidated)

**Consolidated:**
- Trade receivables turnover deteriorated: 3.67 → 0.69 (**-81%**)
- Inventory turnover: 3.75 → 1.05 (**-72%**)

### ROCE/ROE — Artificial Deterioration 🟡

**Size:** Material — RPTs constitute significant portion of balance sheet

- ₹2,416 Mn to Dhyan Projects (largest single exposure)

- ₹981 Mn to Tryksha Real Estate
- ₹930 Mn to Richfeel Real Estate

- **All interest-free except Tryksha (8%)** — significant opportunity cost to holding company

**Borrowings from CMD:** ₹750 Mn from Anand Pandit, **interest-free, unsecured** — long-standing

**Other RPTs:**
- ₹9.6 Mn office rent paid to PC Design (proprietary firm of KMP relative)
- ₹5.47 Mn resource sharing charged to subsidiaries
- ₹3.3 Mn maintenance charges recovered from related entities

**Suspicious flows:** None materially suspicious — all arm's length as per management, but the **scale of inter-corporate fund flows warrants monitoring**, especially as subsidiaries build up inventory without commensurate revenue.

### Auditor Opinion 🟢

- **Standalone: Unqualified** (T.P. Ostwal & Associates LLP, FRN: 124444W/W100150)
- **Consolidated: Unqualified**
- **No fraud reported** under Section 143(12)
- Audit trail (edit log) feature operational throughout year

### Key Audit Matters (KAM) 🟡

**Two KAMs flagged:**

**KAM 1: Revenue Recognition (Ind AS 115, POC method)**
- Significant judgement involved in estimating total contract costs
- Revenue recognized over time based on cost-to-cost method
- Auditor tested sample contracts and demand letters
- **Inherent risk in real-estate POC accounting** — common across industry

**KAM 2: Impairment of Investments & Loans to Subsidiaries**
- Investment in subsidiaries: ₹102 Mn
- Loans to subsidiaries: ₹8,244 Mn
- Requires significant judgement on recoverable amount (selling price, cost to complete)
- Auditor tested using financial condition assessments, sensitivity analysis
- **Risk: If any subsidiary project underperforms, holding company has exposure**

### CARO Report 🟡

- **Holding company: Clean CARO**
- **11 subsidiaries flagged under CARO Clause xvii** (cash losses / going concern) — mostly newly incorporated SPVs with no operations yet:
  - Arahan, Arum, Asvi, Avion, Dhiti, Kunika, Prasati, Rise Root, Sonnet, Srajak, Veera Desai
- **Pattern:** Newly incorporated subsidiaries with start-up losses — **not concerning** but indicates many dormant SPVs being created

### Accounting Policy Changes 🟢

- **Conservative** — percentage-of-completion method, expected credit loss model, no aggressive revenue recognition
- **Amendments adopted:** Ind AS 21 (FX), Ind AS 1 (liability classification), Ind AS 7/107 (supplier finance) — **no material impact**

### Other Forensic Notes

- **Scheme of Amalgamation (FY25):** 4 wholly-owned subsidiaries merged into holding company — Ind AS 103 pooling method used. Capital reserve of ₹12.37 Cr arose
- **RPT transactions disclosed under Section 188:** All in ordinary course, at arm's length — disclosed in AOC-2 not applicable
- **No transactions with struck-off companies**
- **No benami property proceedings**
- **No wilful defaulter declaration**
- **Audit trail preserved per statutory requirements**
- **FY26 GST notice (₹100 Mn):** Pending response — material but disclosed

## FINAL ACCOUNTING QUALITY SCORE

### **AVERAGE** (with notable red flag on cash conversion)

**Rationale:**
- ✅ Clean audit, unqualified opinion, conservative accounting
- ✅ Strong revenue trajectory, profitable, net cash position
- ✅ Low debt, low contingent liabilities, transparent disclosures
- ✅ Reasonable governance, minimal related party concerns beyond scale
- 🔴 **Critical concern:** Negative operating cash flow despite ₹243 Cr PAT — significant working capital absorption signals business is consuming cash as it scales. Real estate developers naturally show this pattern due to project cycle, but the magnitude warrants close monitoring
- 🟡 Margin compression in absolute terms (EBITDA flat YoY despite +40% revenue) suggests scale benefits haven't yet materialized
- 🟡 Large inter-corporate fund flows to subsidiaries (~₹8,244 Mn) — creates dependency on subsidiary project execution

**Bottom Line:** A recently-listed, asset-light Mumbai luxury developer with strong brand positioning and project pipeline, but in capital-intensive growth phase. Quality of reported earnings is decent; the cash conversion issue is structural to the real-estate POC model rather than indicative of aggressive accounting, but investors should track quarterly CFO trends closely.
