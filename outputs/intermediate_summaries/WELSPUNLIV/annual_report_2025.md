## 6. FORENSIC RED FLAGS SUMMARY

### 🔴 High-Priority Flags

1. **Margin compression:** EBITDA margin fell 186 bps despite 8.9% revenue growth. PAT declined 6.2%. Finance costs surged 42%.

2. **Receivable days spike:** +10 days (47→57). Trade receivables grew 31% vs 9% revenue growth. Potential aggressive recognition or collection issues.

3. **Government grants dependency:** ₹620 Cr in receivables (6% of revenue). Key audit matter. If grants are delayed or denied, significant P&L and balance sheet impact.

4. **Corporate guarantees exposure:** ₹1,523 Cr to subsidiaries (₹812 Cr contingent liability = 16.8% of standalone net worth). Any subsidiary distress flows directly to parent.

5. **Promoter stake reduction:** WGMT holding fell from 69.6% to 65.7% — 4.6 percentage point drop via buyback. Combined with ₹278 Cr buyback at ₹220/share, this is a significant capital return at the cost of equity base.

6. **Flooring segment losses:** Flooring reported ₹38.7 Cr PAT loss after tax despite being merged into parent. This is a drag on group profitability.

7. **Audit trail gap:** Privileged access bypass in accounting software + missing backup evidence for 6.5 months. While no tampering was noted, this is a control weakness.

### 🟡 Medium-Priority Flags

8. **Related party concentration:** 80%+ of standalone sales flow through subsidiary WGBL. While disclosed, this concentration means the parent's financial performance is essentially a pass-through.

9. **CWIP explosion:** 8x increase (₹47→₹375 Cr). Major Anjar expansion in execution. Execution risk and potential for future write-offs if projects stall.

10. **Interest coverage deterioration:** Fell from 7.3x to 5.0x. Not yet alarming but trending poorly.

11. **Director resignation reason:** "To avoid potential conflict of interest" — unusual language for an independent director departure.

12. **Dividend policy shift:** Jumped from ₹0.10/share to ₹1.70/share (170% payout) — ₹163 Cr outflow. While positive for shareholders, this was accompanied by a ₹278 Cr buyback, suggesting capital redistribution pressure.

| Governance quality | 🟡 Yellow — strong on paper but promoter stake reduction + director resignation concerns |
| Audit quality | 🟡 Yellow — unqualified but key audit matters are material; control gaps noted |

### Overall Verdict

**Welspun Living is a fundamentally decent business with real scale (₹10,500 Cr revenue, global leader in home textiles) but FY25 reveals meaningful cracks:**

- **The headline story is revenue growth, but the real story is margin erosion.** Management is growing the top line at the cost of profitability — whether through pricing pressure, input cost pass-through failure, or channel investment.
- **Government grants and incentives are a structural dependency** that inflates reported margins and creates balance sheet risk if policy changes.
- **The related party structure is complex** — most revenue flows through subsidiaries, making standalone analysis less meaningful.
- **Promoter behaviour is mixed** — strong ESG and governance commitments, but significant stake reduction via buyback mechanism.
- **The capex cycle is aggressive** (Anjar expansion, Ohio pillow plant, RE projects) — execution over the next 2-3 years will determine whether this investment cycle destroys or creates value.

**For equity analysis, this is a "show me" year ahead — FY26 needs to demonstrate margin recovery, successful capex commissioning, and improved working capital to justify the optimistic management tone.**
