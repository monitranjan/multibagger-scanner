**Conference Call Transcript Analysis: Shilpa Medicare Limited - Q1FY27 Results**  
*(August 5, 2026)*  

---

### **1. Management Commentary & Key Statements**  
**CEO (Keshav Bhutada):**  
- Highlighted company transformation: Net debt-to-EBITDA reduced from 6.7x to 1.3x; ROCE improved from single-digit to double-digit.  
- Stated that investments in biologics, CDMO, novel drug delivery, and NBE are "largely behind us" and "harvesting is ahead."  
- Reiterated operational leverage as plants fill up, expecting incremental revenue and better margins.  
- Noted API division focus on CDMO, peptides, and oncology; peptide manufacturing capacity in India to be commissioned by end of FY27.  
- Mentioned 3 NCE programs in advanced CDMO phases to be commercialized in FY28; on track for 15 new oncology product validations in FY27.  
- Formulation division: Nor-UDCA performing as expected with strong order visibility; global Phase II clinical studies planned for FY27. Three 505(b)(2) products growing quarter-on-quarter; Abraxane, Enzalutamide, Abiraterone formulations on track for FY28 launch.  
- Biologics division: Aflibercept biosimilar clinical studies completed, on track for India launch in FY27; Nivolumab partnership with Orion Corporation secured; India clinical study started, launch expected FY28.  

**CFO (Alpesh Dalal):**  
- Reported record quarterly revenue (INR 469 crores, +43% YoY) and EBITDA (INR 139 crores, +42% YoY) with healthy gross margin of 71%.  
- EBITDA margin at 30%; operating PBT at INR 92 crores vs. INR 50 crores YoY.  
- Reported PAT of INR 101 crores (+115% YoY) due to deferred tax liability reversal; expects tax rate to normalize at ~25% in future quarters.  
- Capex of INR 114 crores in Q1 funded via internal accruals; ROCE improved to 12.5% (18.3% excluding early-stage biologics/NBE).  
- Credit rating upgraded from A+ to AA-; company certified as "Great Place to Work."  

**Segmental Highlights:**  
- **API:** Revenue INR 260 crores (+15% YoY); growth driven by expanded capacities and captive formulation demand.  
- **Formulation:** Revenue INR 198 crores (+100% YoY); base business growth ~112% driven by U.S. complex FDA portfolio.  
- **Biologics:** Revenue INR 52 crores (+42% YoY); driven by licensing/partnerships and CDMO.

### **5. Q&A Dynamics**  
**Analyst Queries vs. Management Answers:**  
- **Nor-UDCA Revenue Mix:** Management evaded product-level details but emphasized strong order trajectory and differentiated mechanism of action vs. semaglutide.  
- **Biologics Scaling:** Acknowledged need for talent building; stated mix of internal team and U.S./EU regulatory consultants.  
- **CDMO Ambitions:** Confirmed CDMO as growth driver; noted integrated capabilities (API, biologics, ADCs) as competitive advantage. Did not disclose specific revenue targets but highlighted 25+ NCE programs.

- **U.S. Tariff Threat:** Stated limited impact due to complex product portfolio; monitoring U.S. policy developments.  
- **R&D Allocation:** Stated each division has its own R&D budget; did not disclose split between formulations and CDMO.  

**Areas of Evasion:**  
- Product-specific revenue contributions (e.g., Nor-UDCA, 505(b)(2) products).  
- Capacity utilization details (referred to IR head).  
- Client-specific confidentiality (e.g., Japanese customers).  
- Specific revenue targets for CDMO/biologics.  

**Areas of High Confidence:**  
- Transformation narrative and operational leverage.  
- CDMO pipeline advancement and monetization.  
- Biosimilar commercialization (Aflibercept, Nivolumab).  
- Integration benefits ("1+1 > 2") across API/formulation/biologics.

### **Summary of Key Takeaways**  
- **Positive Transformation:** Significant deleveraging and ROCE improvement, with portfolio shift toward high-value segments.  
- **Monetization Phase:** Heavy past investments now yielding revenue across API, formulation, and biologics.  
- **Growth Drivers:** CDMO scaling, complex product launches (rotigotine, Abraxane), and biosimilar commercialization.  
- **Risks:** Regulatory challenges, raw material cost inflation, and limited transparency on product-level financials.
