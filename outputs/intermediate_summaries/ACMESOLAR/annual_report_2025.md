## FORENSIC FLAGS

### Related Party Transactions — MAJOR CONCERN 🚩
**This is the most sensitive area in this report.**

- **Volume**: Extremely high. Holding company ACSPL purchases capital goods INR 19,388 mn, sells to subsidiaries, provides O&M, receives/payments for inter-corporate balances. The entire EPC supply chain runs through the promoter group.
- **Loans to related parties**: INR 15,669 mn at year-end (standalone), interest-free, repayable on demand. All 100% to related parties.
- **Corporate guarantees given**: INR 52,574 mn (contingent liability for subsidiary borrowings via ACSPL).
- **Corporate guarantees received**: INR 4,913 mn from ACSPL and VIPL (promoter entities).
- **Proposed future RPTs (AGM approvals sought)**: INR 8,825 mn with ACME Sigma (49% subsidiary); INR 2,270 mn with ACME Alpha (49% subsidiary); INR 2,640 mn with ACSPL.
- **Arm's length claim**: Management states transactions are at arm's length. PwC reports obtained. But the entire value chain is captive within the ACME Group — independent benchmarking is critical.
- **49% subsidiaries (Sigma, Alpha, Renewtech)**: These are controlled by the listed company through nominee directors and call options, but legally 51% is held by ACSPL (promoter). This structure allows the promoter to maintain indirect economic interest in projects being funded by the listed entity's balance sheet.

**Assessment**: The related party web is complex and worth ongoing monitoring. The captive EPC model means most construction cost flows through related parties. PwC arm's length opinions help but aren't definitive. The structure where the listed entity funds 49% of SPVs while the promoter holds 51% creates a potential conflict — the listed entity bears the development/completion risk.

### Contingent Liabilities
| Item | Amount | % of Net Worth |
|------|--------|----------------|
| Income tax disputes | INR 341 mn | 0.8% |
| State tax disputes | INR 88 mn | 0.2% |
| GNA charges dispute | INR 150 mn | 0.3% |
| Financial guarantees (excl. guarantees for subsidiaries) | INR 4,913 mn | **10.9%** ⚠️ |
| MOOWR import duty liability | INR 18,566 mn | **41.2%** 🚩🚩🚩 |

**The MOOWR deferred duty liability of INR 18,566 mn is the single biggest forensic flag.** Six subsidiaries imported solar modules under MOOWR scheme (duty deferment). CBIC issued adverse instruction in Jul 2022; High Court of Delhi quashed it in May 2024, but custom authorities have appealed to Supreme Court. If SC reverses the HC order, the Group could owe INR 18.6 bn in import duties — equivalent to ~41% of net worth. Management believes HC order will be upheld, and no provision is made.

**Excluding MOOWR**: Contingent liabilities ~12% of net worth — yellow flag.

## FINAL ASSESSMENT

**Accounting Quality Score: AVERAGE (leaning Good)**

The financial statements are technically clean — unqualified audit, good disclosure quality, healthy cash conversion, and transparent management. The EBITDA margin and cash flow profile are genuinely impressive for an Indian solar IPP.

However, three issues prevent a "Good" rating:

1. **MOOWR contingent liability** is the elephant in the room. INR 18.6 bn could crystallize if the Supreme Court reverses the Delhi HC order. This is a tail risk but a real one — management must provide more scenario analysis.

2. **The 49/51 subsidiary structure** for under-construction SPVs creates a governance grey zone. The listed entity funds, guarantees, and operates these projects, but ACSPL holds the controlling stake. The call option to acquire the remaining 51% post-lock-in helps, but until then, there's a misalignment between economic ownership and control.

3. **Related party transaction density** is unusually high for a newly listed company. The captive EPC model may be efficient, but investors need to trust the arm's length pricing.

**For a first-time listed company in a capital-intensive sector, this is a solid but complex report. The business quality is high; the governance complexity requires ongoing diligence.**
