# BHEL Q4FY22 Earnings Conference Call — Transcript Analysis

**Date:** 21 May 2022
**Company:** Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL)
**Management Present:**
- Dr. Nalin Shinghal — Chairman and Managing Director (CMD)
- Mr. Subodh Gupta — Director, Finance
- Ms. Renuka Gera — Director, Industrial Systems and Products (Additional Charge: Director, Engineering, R&D)

## 1. MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY — KEY STATEMENTS

### CMD Dr. Nalin Shinghal (Opening Remarks)

**Energy Transition & Strategic Positioning:**
- The Power Sector is in the midst of a "major energy transition." India has made specific commitments under COP 26.
- Infrastructure development, PLI schemes, and the AatmaNirbhar Bharat initiative will drive continuous growth in power demand.
- The country is looking toward a "sustainable energy mix comprising of renewables coupled with hydro, nuclear, as well as clean coal."
- Coal is expected to remain the "primary energy driver" given India's huge coal reserves and lack of oil/gas reserves, with emphasis shifting toward "Clean Coal."
- A "visible pipeline of over 7,700 MW of coal-based power plants" is being witnessed.

**Two-Pronged Strategy:**
1. Indigenous development of clean coal technologies — "Coal to Chemicals" (especially coal gasification), comprehensive emission control solutions, lower-rating supercritical plants.
2. Growth in non-coal business — nuclear, hydro, railways, defence, aerospace, and others.

**Nuclear & Hydro Leadership:**
- BHEL has maintained market leadership in nuclear and hydro sectors.
- Received the "largest ever order for EPC of Turbine Island package for 6 units of 700 MWe Pressurized Heavy Water Reactors from NPCIL" under fleet mode procurement.
- Also received orders for Reactor Header Assemblies and Steam Generators in fleet mode.
- BHEL "continues to remain the sole Indian company to supply nuclear turbine generators."

**Diversification & Strategic Partnerships:**
- Making efforts to enter strategic partnerships with Global OEMs in rail transportation, defence & aerospace, and new energy sources.
- Secured an order for "16 sets of 3-phase IGBT based propulsion systems for Vande Bharat Express Trains from ICF Chennai" — marking entry into domestic semi-high-speed mobility segment.

**Order Book (FY21-22):**
- Total orders booked: **Rs. 23,693 Crores**
- Total order book as of 31 March 2022: **Rs. 1,02,542 Crores**
  - Power sector: Rs. 85,664 Crores
  - Industry sector: Rs. 11,836 Crores
  - Export projects: Rs. 5,042 Crores
- Power segment orders: Rs. 17,931 Crores (nuclear orders, FGD packages, etc.)
- Industry segment orders: Rs. 5,660 Crores (upgraded SRGM main gun on Indian warships — BHEL is sole supplier, strategic equipment for Ministry of Defence, propulsion systems for Vande Bharat trains)

**Project Execution Milestones:**
- Successful commissioning of 800 kV, 6,000 MW UHVDC link between Western Region Grid and Southern Region Grid.
- Commissioned and synchronized **4,119 MW** of power plants (utility, solar, captive segments) plus **1,460 MW** of Steam Generators.
- Commissioned first FGD plant at NTPC Dadri.
- Commissioned country's largest Air Cooled Condenser at North Karanpura — done "in the absence of OEM support due to the geo-political situation, thus demonstrating the engineering prowess of TEAM BHEL."
- Final stages of commissioning 100 MW floating solar power plant at NTPC Ramagundam — "by far the largest floating solar power plant in the country."
- Two-thirds of total floating solar capacity in India is installed and commissioned by BHEL.

**Technology Development:**
- Indigenously designed, installed, and demonstrated 0.25 TPD Coal to Methanol Technology Demonstration Plant using high ash Indian Coal — "first of its kind technology demonstration."
- Working on commercializing this technology to contribute to the National Coal Gasification Mission.

**Financial Performance (FY21-22):**
- Achieved breakeven in FY21-22 and strengthened balance sheet, despite 2nd COVID wave impacting Q1.
- **Total Revenue from Operations:** Rs. 21,211 Crores (23% higher than Rs. 17,308 Crores in FY20-21)
- **Profit Before Tax:** Rs. 437 Crores (vs. loss of Rs. 3,612 Crores in FY20-21)
- **EBITDA:** More than Rs. 1,100 Crores — despite "significant pressure on margins due to sharp spike in metal prices and increase in other material and fuel costs."
- **Cost Reduction:** Manufacturing, Administrative, Sales & Distribution expenses contained at **7% of Turnover** (vs. 9% in previous year).
- **Profit After Tax:** Rs. 410 Crores (vs. loss of Rs. 2,717 Crores in FY20-21) — "significantly aided by efforts in taxation area which resulted in refunds of Rs. 667 Crores."

**Q4 FY21-22 Financials:**
- Total Revenue from Operations: Rs. 7,600 Crores (up 13% vs. Rs. 6,752 Crores)
- Profit Before Tax: Rs. 1,098 Crores (vs. loss of Rs. 1,385 Crores)
- Profit After Tax: Rs. 909 Crores (vs. loss of Rs. 1,033 Crores)
- EBITDA: Rs. 1,280 Crores
- Manufacturing/Admin/Sales & Distribution expenses restricted to **5.5%** (vs. 7% in corresponding period previous year)

**Working Capital & Cash Flow:**
- Working capital of Rs. 586 Crores released despite revenues rising by 24%.
- Net positive cash generation of Rs. 660 Crores from operations (vs. Rs. 560 Crores in prior year).
- Cash and bank balances (net of borrowings): Rs. 2,409 Crores (vs. Rs. 1,868 Crores at end of FY20-21).
- Total receivables: Rs. 33,169 Crores — "only marginally higher" than March 2021 levels of Rs. 31,292 Crores despite 24% revenue rise.
- Trade receivables at Rs. 6,229 Crores — "the lowest in the last 10 years."

  - Provisions: Rs. (842) Crores

**Full Year Other Expenses Breakup:**
- Total other expenses: Rs. 162 Crores
  - Manufacturing/admin/selling & distribution: Rs. 1,355 Crores (down from Rs. 1,480 Crores prior year)
  - Provisions: Rs. (1,526) Crores
  - Power and fuel cost: Rs. 415 Crores
  - ERV gain: Rs. 82 Crores
- No merit-based provisions required this year (vs. Rs. 1,827 Crores merit-based provisions taken last year).
- Manufacturing expenses of Rs. 1,355 Crores described as "the lowest in last many years" — level came from 9% to 7% of top line.

**Tax Refund Details:**
- PBT of Rs. 437 Crores and PAT of Rs. 410 Crores — gap of only Rs. 27 Crores.
- Current tax: minus Rs. 77 Crores (includes impact of refund).
- Refund of Rs. 126 Crores of tax refund — income under that head.
- Two components: (1) interest component taken to other income; (2) tax part taken to taxes (previously expensed, now booked to income).
- Refunds relate to disputed tax cases prior to 2010-11 won at tribunal level.

**Material Cost Impact:**
- Material cost at **72% of revenue** — "all time high" (historically in the range of 60%).
- Price variation clauses (PVC) are largely index-based; index may not rise as much as steel, copper, or cement prices.
- PVC has a cap/limit, so pass-through is restricted.
- Engineering optimization savings of Rs. 600-700 Crores achieved.
- Time extension requests submitted to customers due to COVID — once approved, some PVC benefit will accrue.

**Order Book & Receivables Breakup:**
- Order receipt FY21-22: Rs. 23,693 Crores
  - Power: Rs. 17,931 Crores
  - Industry: Rs. 5,660 Crores
  - International: Rs. 102 Crores
- Outstanding order book: Rs. 1,02,542 Crores
  - Power: Rs. 85,664 Crores
  - Industry: Rs. 11,836 Crores
  - International: Rs. 5,042 Crores
- Receivables breakup (Rs. 33,168 Crores including contract assets):
  - Central PSU: 36%
  - State utility: 41%
  - Private customer: 14%
  - Export: 8%

**Trade Receivables vs. Contract Assets (FY22 vs. FY21):**
- FY22: Trade receivables Rs. 6,229 Crores; Contract assets Rs. 26,940 Crores
- FY21: Trade receivables Rs. 7,213 Crores; Contract assets Rs. 24,079 Crores
- Increase in contract assets partly due to unfavorable payment terms in NTPC projects — additional Rs. 3,600 Crores reflected as contract assets (indirectly a funding for projects from BHEL).

## 4. FUTURE OUTLOOK & GUIDANCE

### Margins
- **EBITDA:** Rs. 1,100+ Crores for FY21-22; Rs. 1,280 Crores in Q4 alone.
- **Material cost pressure:** 72% of revenue (all-time high, vs. historical ~60%). Major concern area.
- **Cost reduction:** Manufacturing/admin/selling & distribution expenses at 7% of turnover (vs. 9% prior year). Q4 at 5.5%.
- **PVC (Price Variation Clause):** Index-based with caps; limited pass-through. Time extension requests to customers may unlock some PVC benefit.
- **Engineering optimization:** Rs. 600-700 Crores saved.
- **Provisions:** No merit-based provisions required this year (vs. Rs. 1,827 Crores last year). Better withdrawals improved provision numbers.
- **Tax:** Refunds of Rs. 667 Crores aided PAT. Disputed tax cases prior to 2010-11 won at tribunal.

### Volume Growth / Order Pipeline
- **7,700 MW pipeline** of coal-based power plants:
  - ~4,000 MW in advanced bidding (Talabira 3×800 MW, Bhandora/Adani 2×800 MW)
  - ~3,000-4,000 MW expected within one year (Talcher 2×660 MW, Singrauli 2×800 MW, Lara Phase-2 2×800 MW)
- **Nuclear:** Fleet mode ordering received (6 units of 700 MWe TGs worth Rs. 10,800 Crores). "Not immediately" more orders, but "nuclear will continue to play a very strong role" in future.
- **Defence:** Bullish — orders at Rs. 1,500 Crores (highest ever), expected to go up further. Geopolitical situation adds urgency to indigenize.
- **Railways:** Bullish — first Vande Bharat order secured, technology tie-ups in pipeline.
- **Coal Gasification:** Government announced four plants in budget. Moving with leading power, coal, and lignite producers. Expected to speed up.
- **FGD:** State and private sector ordering not yet happened; deadlines exist. Visibility not yet there but expected.
- **Hydrogen:** Dedicated business group; 5 kW fuel cell developed; discussions for electrolyzer technology tie-ups.
- **Order book:** Rs. 1,02,542 Crores as of 31 March 2022.

### CAPEX
- **FY23 CAPEX:** Rs. 231 Crores (planned).
- **Long-term CAPEX areas:**
  - ISRO tankages: Rs. 200+ Crores
  - Upgraded SRGMs: Rs. 140 odd Crores
  - Coal gasification: "substantial CAPEX required"
- **Note:** CMD mentioned earlier that if certain orders from DRDO come, CAPEX would increase (referenced by analyst, but no specific DRDO CAPEX figure given in this transcript).

### M&A Integration
- **No specific M&A integration discussed** in this transcript. No mention of Fluence Pharma, BTM, or Reginald. (These appear to be from a different company's template and are not present in this BHEL transcript.)

### Asset Monetization
- Government has set up a separate body to handle asset monetization.
- BHEL has identified certain properties.
- Process is in a "different sort of phase" — procedures need to fall in place.
- CMD expects "clarity to emerge quite soon" — no specific timeline given.

### Areas Where Management Was Evasive or Less Confident

1. **Module Manufacturing (PLI):** CMD stated "the call is not to get into this" without providing any reasoning or future consideration. Brief and non-committal.

2. **Asset Monetization:** CMD acknowledged that government has set up a separate body and procedures need to fall in place. No specific timeline, no specific assets named. Used vague language: "clarity to emerge quite soon."

3. **FGD Ordering Visibility:** CMD stated "The visibility is not yet there but we are expecting visibility to happen." ED Marketing noted potential dampener from government considering two-year extension. Management was honest about uncertainty but could not provide concrete ordering timeline.

4. **Nuclear Future Orders:** CMD said "Not immediately, but yes in the future, I think nuclear will continue to play a very strong role." No specific future order numbers or timelines provided.

5. **Material Cost / PVC Pass-Through:** Management was candid about the challenge (72% material cost, all-time high) but acknowledged that PVC caps limit pass-through. No specific mitigation timeline beyond "working on it."

6. **DRDO CAPEX:** Referenced by analyst but not elaborated upon by management in this transcript.

## SUMMARY

BHEL's Q4FY22 earnings call reflects a company in recovery mode — having achieved breakeven after significant losses, with a strong order book of over Rs. 1 lakh crore, and a clear strategic pivot toward diversification (defence, railways, nuclear, hydrogen, coal gasification). Management is confident on execution and cost discipline but candid about material cost pressures (72% of revenue, all-time high) and limited PVC pass-through. The 7,700 MW thermal pipeline provides near-term visibility, while defence and railways represent growth vectors. FGD ordering and asset monetization timelines remain uncertain. No M&A integration (Fluence Pharma, BTM/Reginald) or Project Neev/direct distribution topics were discussed in this BHEL transcript.
