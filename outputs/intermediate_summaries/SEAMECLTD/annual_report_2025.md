# SEAMEC LIMITED — ANNUAL REPORT FY 2024-25 FORENSIC ANALYSIS

---

## MANAGEMENT LETTERS

**Key Themes:**
- Chairman acknowledges **decline in turnover and profitability** vs prior year
- Blames **drydocking of assets in non-working season** → fewer deployment days → additional cost with no revenue
- Cites **geopolitical unpredictability** (Iran/Russia sanctions, crude oil volatility) as headwinds
- Positions company as "pioneer" in DSV sector with fleet diversification push (OSVs, accommodation barges)
- Conserve resources → **no dividend recommended**

**Stated Opportunities, Risks, Priorities:**
- Opportunities: India's Maritime Vision 2030, Sagarmala, increasing offshore exploration acreage
- Risks: Age restrictions on older tonnage, geopolitics, renewable energy shift, skilled manpower shortage
- Strategic Priority: Acquiring SEAMEC ANANT and NPP NUSANTARA from HAL/subsidiary to consolidate fleet under SEAMEC fold

**Walk-the-Talk Check:**
- Revenue declined 10.6% (consolidated) — **partially explained** by Swordfish drydocking/deferral of Saudi charter
- PAT declined 27% — partially offset by tax change (tonnage tax → current tax expense of ₹18.9 Cr vs nil prior year)
- Fleet acquisition of SEAMEC Diamond (completed) ✓; SEAMEC ANANT/NPP NUSANTARA acquisition **still underway** — partial miss
- No dividend — consistent with conservation stance ✓
- ⚠️ Overseas segment reported **₹38.4 Cr loss** (vs ₹22 Cr loss prior year) — widening overseas losses not adequately addressed by management

**🟢🟡🟢🟡 — Mixed delivery; revenue decline largely explainable but overseas losses widening unaddressed**

## BUSINESS & STRATEGY

**Core Segments & Revenue Mix:**
- **Offshore Support Services (DSVs/OSV/Barge)** — 99% of standalone revenue (₹61,733L)
- **Bulk Carrier (via subsidiary Seamec International FZE)** — negligible in consolidated, one carrier sold during year
- Domestic: ₹55,874L (90.5%) | Overseas: ₹5,859L (9.5%)
- Top 4 domestic customers = 91.6% of revenue — **extreme customer concentration**

**New Segments/Products/Geographies:**
- UK subsidiary (Seamec UK Investments) acquired London property (Fountain House Combined) for ₹281 Cr — nominally "global office"
- Tunnelling JV (Seamec Nirman Infra) **wound down** after ₹6.4 Cr loss
- Joint Venture "SEARETE INDIA IFSC PRIVATE LIMITED" in GIFT City (50:50 with Arete Shipping) — ship owning/leasing
- No new operational segments added

**Capex:**
- Standalone PPE additions: ₹54.8 Cr (mainly fleet drydocking, Seamec Diamond acquisition)
- Consolidated capex: ₹55.2 Cr — **well within plan**
- Capital commitments surged to **₹806 Cr** (from ₹98 Cr prior year) — this is a major forward commitment, likely the SEAMEC ANANT/NPP NUSANTARA acquisitions
- Large capex commitments vs current net worth of ₹987 Cr — **leverage risk building**

**🟢🟡🟡🟡 — Customer concentration extreme; new ventures mostly failed/winding down; capital commitments have tripled**

## FINANCIAL QUALITY CHECK

**Revenue & PAT Growth (3Y Trend):**

| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (Consol) | ₹72,929L | ₹72,929L → ₹66,557L (SL) | ₹65,182L |
| PAT (Consol) | ₹12,065L | ₹12,065L | ₹8,791L |

- Revenue: flat FY23→FY24, down 10.6% FY24→FY25
- PAT: down 27% YoY — driven by (i) lower revenue, (ii) tax reversal (was negative last year), (iii) prior year exceptional gain of ₹13 Cr (vessel sale)
- **Underlying PAT ex tax reversal: ~₹8.8 Cr vs ₹12 Cr — ~27% real decline**

**EBITDA Margin Trend:**
- Standalone EBITDA margin: 40% (FY25) vs 41% (FY24) — **stable, slight contraction**
- Consolidated EBITDA margin: 35.6% vs 36.8% — mild compression

**CFO vs PAT:**
- Standalone CFO: ₹425 Cr vs PAT ₹115.5 Cr — **CFO massively exceeds PAT** due to ₹230 Cr working capital release (receivable collection)
- Consolidated CFO: ₹298.5 Cr vs PAT ₹87.9 Cr — same dynamic
- **Profit converting to cash strongly** — driven by receivable reduction

**Working Capital Movement:**
- Trade receivables fell ₹63 Cr (standalone) — **significant cash release** from collections
- Inventory stable
- Payables fell ₹43 Cr — cash outflow
- **Overall net working capital improvement — positive signal**

**ROCE/ROE Trend:**
- ROE: 12% (FY25) vs 24% (FY24) — **halved** due to PAT decline
- ROCE: 13% vs 20% — **declining**
- Both metrics deteriorating — driven by profitability decline and growing capital base

**Debt Position:**
- Total borrowings (consolidated): ₹211.8 Cr (NC: ₹120.6 Cr + Curr: ₹91.2 Cr)
- Net debt: ~₹208 Cr (after deducting cash+deposits of ₹140 Cr)
- D/E ratio: 0.21 (consolidated) — down from 0.34 — **improving**
- Interest coverage: 10.9x (standalone) — **comfortable**
- However, **₹806 Cr capital commitments** will dramatically worsen leverage if executed

**🟡🟡🟡🟡🟡🟡🟡🟡 — Strong cash generation and declining leverage, but profitability and returns trending down; capital commitments pose future risk**

## FINAL ACCOUNTING QUALITY SCORE: **AVERAGE (YELLOW)**

**Summary:** SEAMEC has clean audited books and conservative accounting policies. The core domestic DSV business generates strong cash flows with manageable leverage. However, **three material concerns** emerge: (1) massive ₹806 Cr capital commitments with no disclosed funding strategy, (2) widening overseas segment losses with no clear exit strategy, and (3) other expenses running at 7.2% of revenue driven by recurring management fees to a related entity. The company is at an inflection point — if fleet acquisitions proceed without equity raise, balance sheet leverage will deteriorate materially. The Swiber receivable loss (₹113.5 Cr) is fully provisioned but represents a permanent economic loss. For investors, the key question is whether the fleet expansion will generate returns sufficient to justify the capital deployment, given that existing overseas investments have been value-destructive.
