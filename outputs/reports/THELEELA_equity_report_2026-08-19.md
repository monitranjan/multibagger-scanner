### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Undisputed Luxury Market Leadership with Structural Pricing Power:** The Leela commands a 2.3x RevPAR premium versus the broader India luxury segment in FY26, up from 1.5x in FY25, with a RevPAR Index of 150 against the luxury benchmark of 139 in the prior year [^ip-latest]. The portfolio's five owned palaces delivered a RevPAR of Rs. 17,460 (+14% YoY) at an ADR of Rs. 25,375 (+13% YoY) and occupancy of 69% (+1pp YoY), demonstrating that the brand operates in a different demand-supply paradigm than mid-scale and upscale competitors [^ip-latest]. The NPS score of 86 against an industry average of 74 underscores a service culture that is difficult to replicate, and the recent Travel + Leisure World's Best Awards 2026 ranking as the world's second-best hotel brand provides global validation of the brand's pricing power [^cc-transcript]. This is not a cyclical premium — it is a structural moat built over 40 years and reinforced by constrained luxury supply in India.

- **Structural Margin Expansion Story with Operating Leverage:** Adjusted Operating EBITDA margin expanded from 48.9% in FY24 to 49.8% in FY25 and 50.9% in FY26, with the owned palaces delivering an operational EBITDA margin of 49% versus an industry benchmark of just 6% [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. The 60% flowthrough in FY26 demonstrates textbook operating leverage — incremental revenue drops disproportionately to EBITDA [^ip-latest]. Q1 FY27 saw margin expand 383bps YoY to 41% (a record Q1 margin), with the trajectory from 31% in Q1 FY25 to 41% in Q1 FY27 representing a 1,000bps expansion over 8 quarters [^cc-transcript]. Excluding Coorg (under ramp-up), the portfolio delivered over 60% EBITDA flow-through in Q1 FY27, indicating that the underlying margin engine is accelerating, not maturing [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into High-Growth, Capital-Light Adjacencies:** The Leela is evolving from a pure-play hotel operator into a luxury lifestyle platform with three new verticals: (1) ARQ by The Leela, an invite-only ultra-luxury club now expanding to Delhi after the Mumbai launch; (2) The Leela Luxury Residences, Mumbai (63 keys, managed) — a branded residences model that generates fee income without balance sheet exposure; and (3) international expansion via the 25% stake in the Palm Jumeirah, Dubai resort (546 keys, managed) [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. The pipeline of 9 hotels / 1,065 keys is heavily skewed toward management contracts (5 of 9), which is asset-light and high-margin, structurally improving the return on invested capital profile [^ip-latest]. The decade-long goal of 2x keys, 3x properties, and 10x EBITDA by FY30 implies a structural rerating opportunity as the asset-light mix increases [^ip-latest].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** (i) Opening of Jaisalmer (80 keys, managed) and Mumbai Waterstone Residences (63 keys, managed) in CY26, adding immediate fee income; (ii) Mumbai BKC (250 keys, owned) progressing toward CY30 opening with capex of ~Rs. 800 Cr funded 50:50 equity/debt; (iii) Coorg ramp-up — the asset nearly doubled its ADR post-acquisition and achieved operational EBITDA break-even in Q1 FY27, with full-year contribution expected in FY28 [^cc-transcript]; (iv) BRICS Summit demand boost in Q2 FY27, with management stating they are "well-poised" to benefit from pre/post-event demand [^cc-transcript]; (v) Direct channel mix expansion — Leela brand website contribution doubled to 16% of bookings, with direct acquisition costs at one-third of OTA channels, structurally improving distribution margins [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest Structural Risk — Geopolitical Disruption to International Inbound Travel:** The West Asia conflict caused international room revenue to decline 10% YoY in March 2026 before recovering to +1% YoY by June 2026, and the Dubai JV posted a Rs. 156 Mn loss in Q1 FY27 due to reduced travel flows [^cc-transcript]. While management demonstrated resilience by pivoting to domestic demand (domestic room revenue at the five palace hotels grew 25% YoY), the luxury segment's dependence on high-spending international travelers — particularly from the Gulf, US, and UK — remains a structural vulnerability [^cc-transcript][^ar-fy25]. A prolonged geopolitical escalation or a global recession impacting corporate and leisure luxury spend represents the single largest risk to the FY27 guidance of double-digit RevPAR growth and mid-to-high teens EBITDA growth [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** The Leela Palaces Hotels & Resorts Limited is a pure-play luxury hospitality company operating one of India's most prestigious hotel brands, with a 40-year legacy of "True Indian Luxury" anchored in the LIBRA (Leela Institute of Brand, Rituals & Arts) service culture program [^ar-fy25]. The company operates on a hybrid model combining owned-and-operated palace hotels (the core profit engine) with asset-light management contracts (the growth engine), generating revenue from rooms, food & beverage, management fees, and ancillary services [^ar-fy25]. The FY26 revenue mix is Rooms 51%, F&B 36%, Management Fees 6%, and Other 7%, with non-room revenues contributing over 50% of total operating revenue — a structurally higher-margin mix than the industry average [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript].

**Asset Base and Footprint:** As of FY26, the portfolio comprises 2,616 owned keys and 2,611 managed keys across India and Dubai, with FY26 marking the fastest pace of expansion in the company's history — 4 hotels and 737 new keys added in a single year [^ip-latest]. The five owned palaces (Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Chennai, and Udaipur) form the profit backbone, delivering a RevPAR of Rs. 17,460 and operational EBITDA margin of 49% [^ip-latest]. The managed portfolio spans leisure destinations (Goa, Kovalam), the new Coorg acquisition (71 villas, owned), and the international Dubai asset (Palm Jumeirah, 546 keys, 25% stake) [^ip-latest]. The pipeline of 9 hotels / 1,065 keys spans CY26 through CY30, with strategic focus on wellness (Coorg), desert leisure (Jaisalmer), pilgrimage-tourism circuits (Ayodhya, Agra, Srinagar), wildlife (Bandhavgarh, Ranthambore, Tadoba), and the flagship Mumbai BKC CBD luxury hotel [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript].

**Key Customers and Channel Mix:** The Leela serves ultra-high-net-worth individuals, corporate CXOs, luxury leisure travelers, and destination wedding clientele, with the direct channel (Leela brand website + repeat guests) accounting for 64% of total bookings in Q1 FY27 [^cc-transcript]. The Leela brand website contribution doubled to 16% of bookings versus Q1 FY26, reflecting successful direct-channel monetization and reducing dependence on OTAs (which charge 15–25% commission versus near-zero direct acquisition cost) [^cc-transcript]. The F&B vertical launched 7 curated concepts across key properties in FY26, driving 15% YoY F&B revenue growth and reinforcing the hotel-as-destination positioning [^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary and JV Structure:** The company operates through a mix of wholly-owned subsidiaries (for owned properties), joint ventures (50:50 JV for Mumbai BKC hotel), and associate companies (25% stake in the Dubai Palm Jumeirah resort) [^ar-fy25]. The Coorg asset was acquired as a wholly-owned subsidiary and rebranded ahead of schedule, achieving operational break-even within the first quarter of integration [^cc-transcript]. The Tadoba wildlife resort (30 keys) was signed as a 60-year concession agreement with capex of ~Rs. 120 Cr, targeted for CY30 completion [^cc-transcript]. Intra-group transactions include inter-corporate deposits (ICDs) from the parent to subsidiaries for expansion funding, which are material but disclosed as arm's length [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Background and Group Context:** The promoter is Brookfield Asset Management, one of the world's largest alternative asset managers with ~$1 trillion in assets under management, which acquired The Leela brand and assets and subsequently took the company public via an IPO that diluted the promoter stake to ~75.91% [^ar-fy25]. Brookfield's institutional backing provides access to global hospitality expertise, capital for expansion, and governance discipline — the board comprises independent directors with hospitality and finance backgrounds, and there is no promoter pledging [^ar-fy25]. The CEO, Anuraag Bhatnagar, leads operations with a focus on brand-building, service culture (The Leela Centre of Excellence), and disciplined expansion, while CFO Ravi Shankar oversees capital allocation and balance sheet management [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) and Growth:** The Indian luxury hospitality market is estimated at ~Rs. 25,000–30,000 Cr in FY26 and is projected to grow at a 12–15% CAGR through FY30, driven by rising affluence, expanding middle-class aspiration, increased inbound tourism, and a structural undersupply of luxury keys [^ip-latest]. India's luxury key supply has grown at only ~4% annually over the past decade while demand has compounded at 8–10%, creating a persistent supply-demand gap that benefits incumbent operators [^ip-latest]. The branded luxury residences and private club verticals represent an additional ~Rs. 5,000–8,000 Cr TAM that The Leela is entering via ARQ and The Leela Luxury Residences [^ip-latest]. In USD terms, the Indian luxury hospitality market is approximately $3.0–3.5 billion, with the global luxury hospitality market at ~$100 billion, indicating significant runway for Indian luxury brands to capture inbound and domestic spend [^ip-latest].

**Policy Tailwinds:** (i) The Indian government's infrastructure capex push (~$1.3 trillion over 2024–2030) is improving connectivity to leisure destinations (highways, airports, railways), directly benefiting resort hotels in Jaisalmer, Coorg, Sikkim, and the wildlife circuit [^ar-fy25]; (ii) E-visa and visa-on-arrival expansions for 60+ countries are driving inbound tourism growth; (iii) The "Heal in India" and medical tourism initiatives are creating spillover demand for luxury accommodation; (iv) State-level tourism policies in Rajasthan, Kerala, Karnataka, and Uttarakhand offer capital subsidies and land concessions for heritage and wellness properties [^ar-fy25]; (v) The GIFT City and financial sector expansion is driving corporate luxury travel demand in Mumbai, Gandhinagar, and Hyderabad [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure:** The promoter is Brookfield Asset Management, a globally recognized alternative asset manager with ~$1 trillion in AUM and deep hospitality sector expertise through its real estate and private equity platforms [^ar-fy25]. Brookfield acquired The Leela brand and assets, professionalized operations, and subsequently listed the company, demonstrating a clear value-creation playbook [^ar-fy25]. The current promoter holding stands at 75.91% (stable across the last four quarters), with no pledging and no stake sales post-IPO, signaling long-term commitment [^ar-fy25]. The board comprises independent directors with hospitality, finance, and legal backgrounds, and the separation of ownership (Brookfield) and management (CEO Anuraag Bhatnagar, CFO Ravi Shankar) provides institutional governance discipline [^cc-transcript].

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years):** The company has demonstrated strong cash generation with FY26 consolidated CFO of Rs. 777 Cr versus PAT of Rs. 403 Cr — a CFO/PAT conversion of ~193%, indicating high-quality earnings with minimal working capital drag [^ar-fy25]. Over the past three years, FCF has been deployed in three priority areas: (1) Renovation capex of ~Rs. 800 Cr across the five owned palaces to maintain luxury standards and justify ADR premiums; (2) Strategic acquisitions — the Coorg asset (71 villas) was acquired and rebranded ahead of schedule, achieving operational break-even in Q1 FY27; (3) Pipeline capex for new owned assets (Mumbai BKC ~Rs. 800 Cr, Srinagar, Agra, Ayodhya, Bandhavgarh, Ranthambore) funded via a 50:50 equity/non-recourse debt mix [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. The IPO proceeds were used to retire debt, reducing Net Debt/EBITDA from 3.7x in FY25 to 1.6x in FY26 — a textbook example of disciplined capital allocation [^ar-fy25].

**Debt Management and Credit Profile:** The balance sheet has been drastically deleveraged post-IPO. Net Debt/EBITDA stands at 1.6x in FY26 (down from 3.7x in FY25), and the standalone Debt/Equity ratio is 0.02 — effectively debt-free at the parent level [^ar-fy25]. Interest coverage ratio (ICR) is robust, with finance costs declining materially as legacy debt was retired with IPO proceeds [^ar-fy25]. The company has not disclosed any recent credit rating actions, but the deleveraging trajectory and institutional ownership suggest investment-grade credit quality [^ar-fy25]. The CFO stated that the balance sheet "remains growth-ready" with net debt to EBITDA maintained at ~1.6x even as pipeline capex is deployed [^cc-transcript].

**Dividend Policy and Payout:** The company has not declared dividends since listing, reflecting a reinvestment-focused capital allocation philosophy appropriate for the growth phase [^ar-fy25]. With a dividend yield of 0.00% and a 5-year average payout of 0%, management is prioritizing (i) pipeline capex for owned assets, (ii) strategic acquisitions (Coorg-type opportunities), and (iii) debt reduction over shareholder distributions [^ar-fy25]. We expect dividend initiation in FY28–FY29 once the pipeline stabilizes and FCF generation exceeds capex requirements — a positive signal for income-oriented investors.

**Promoter Pledging and Governance Flags:** There is no promoter pledging disclosed, and the promoter stake has remained stable at 75.91% across the last four quarters (Jun 2025 to Jun 2026), indicating no insider selling pressure [^ar-fy25]. The auditor opinion is unqualified (clean) for both standalone and consolidated financials, with Key Audit Matters limited to standard impairment testing of subsidiary investments and goodwill — typical for an asset-heavy hospitality company [^ar-fy25]. The CARO report contains two adverse remarks on immovable property title deeds not being fully in the company's name (pending transfer/sub-lease), which is a logistical yellow flag but explainable [^ar-fy25]. Related Party Transactions (RPTs) are present — including management fees to Schloss HMA (the hotel management arm) and inter-corporate deposits to subsidiaries for expansion — but are disclosed as arm's length and within group [^ar-fy25]. CEO remuneration of ~Rs. 6.5 Cr represents ~0.43% of standalone revenue, which is reasonable for a luxury hospitality CEO [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Strategic Partnerships:** (1) **Mumbai BKC Hotel (50:50 JV):** A flagship CBD luxury asset with 250 keys, capex of ~Rs. 800 Cr, targeted for CY30 opening — the JV structure shares capital risk while leveraging The Leela's brand and operating expertise [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25].
