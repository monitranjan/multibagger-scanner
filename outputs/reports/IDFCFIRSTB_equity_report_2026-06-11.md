### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS (5 bullet points)

*   **Market Leadership / Moat:** IDFC First Bank has successfully built a strong retail liability franchise, with CASA ratio steadily improving to 49.6% as of Q4 FY24, significantly higher than its legacy infrastructure financing days. Its digital-first strategy, including proprietary mobile banking and wealth management platforms, provides a scalable and cost-efficient customer acquisition channel, differentiating it from traditional banks.
*   **Structural Margin Story:** The ongoing shift in loan book mix towards higher-yielding retail and SME segments (currently ~89% of total funded assets) from lower-yielding infrastructure loans, combined with improving cost of funds due to better CASA accretion, is expected to drive sustainable Net Interest Margin (NIM) expansion. We project NIMs to stabilize around 6.3-6.5% over FY25-FY27E, leading to robust NII growth.
*   **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** The bank is strategically expanding into new, high-growth retail lending categories such as consumer durable loans, credit cards, and two-wheeler financing, leveraging its strong digital capabilities. Furthermore, its focus on semi-urban and rural markets, with a growing network of branches, provides access to underserved segments with strong credit demand, diversifying revenue streams beyond traditional urban retail.
*   **Near-term Catalysts (6–12 months):** Key catalysts include the successful completion of the IDFC Ltd reverse merger, which is expected to simplify the corporate structure, improve governance perception, and potentially lead to a re-rating. Continued robust deposit growth, especially in CASA, coupled with controlled credit costs and consistent asset quality improvement (GNPA/NNPA expected below 2.0%/0.7% by FY25E), will further bolster investor confidence.
*   **Biggest Structural Risk:** The primary structural risk remains the potential for higher-than-expected credit costs from its relatively unseasoned retail loan book, particularly in unsecured segments, given economic volatility or rising interest rates. While the bank has demonstrated improving asset quality metrics, a sharp deterioration in macroeconomic conditions could disproportionately impact its retail portfolio.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

*   **Core Business Model:** IDFC First Bank operates as a universal bank, offering a comprehensive suite of banking products and services to retail, corporate, and rural customers. Its core focus has transitioned from infrastructure financing to building a robust retail liability franchise and expanding its retail and SME lending portfolio.
*   **Revenue Split by Division (% of Revenue - Q4 FY24):**
    *   Retail & Commercial Banking: ~89% (includes retail loans, SME loans, wealth management, and fee income from these segments)
    *   Corporate & Wholesale Banking: ~11% (legacy infrastructure and corporate loans, trade finance)
*   **Key OEM/End Customers per Division:**
    *   **Retail & Commercial Banking:** Millions of individual retail customers for savings, current accounts, personal loans, home loans, vehicle loans, and credit cards. Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) across various sectors for working capital and term loans.
    *   **Corporate & Wholesale Banking:** Large corporates, project finance entities (though significantly reduced), and institutional clients for treasury and trade finance services.
*   **Subsidiary Structure:** The bank operates primarily as a standalone entity. IDFC Asset Management Company (AMC) was divested in 2022. The upcoming reverse merger with its parent company, IDFC Ltd, will consolidate the entities.
*   **Manufacturing Footprint (States, Plant Count):** As a bank, it does not have manufacturing plants. Its "footprint" is defined by its branch network and ATM presence. As of Q4 FY24, IDFC First Bank had 898 bank branches and 1,029 ATMs across India, with a growing presence in semi-urban and rural areas.
*   **Promoter Background and Group Context:** IDFC First Bank was formed by the merger of IDFC Bank (a demerged entity of IDFC Ltd, which was an infrastructure financing company) and Capital First Ltd (a leading NBFC focused on consumer and SME lending) in December 2018. The merger brought together IDFC Bank's strong liability franchise aspirations with Capital First's established retail lending expertise. Mr. V. Vaidyanathan, who previously led Capital First, is the MD & CEO, instrumental in the bank's retail transformation.

### SECTION 8 — VALUATION

**BULL:** Optimistic revenue + margin assumptions → EPS → P/E multiple → target
IDFCFIRSTB's strong deposit growth (CAGR 25%+) and improving asset quality (GNPA 1.53% in Q4FY24) suggest continued momentum. We project a faster loan book expansion of 20% YoY for FY27E, with NIMs improving to 6.8% driven by lower cost of funds and higher-yielding retail assets. This translates to an FY27E EPS of Rs. 7.50. Applying a higher P/E multiple of 18x (reflecting sustained growth and improved profitability vs. peers), our Bull Case target is **Rs. 135**.

**BASE:** Base case → EPS → P/E multiple → 12M target (anchors cover page target)
Based on a sustainable loan book CAGR of 17% for FY27E and NIMs stabilizing around 6.5%, IDFCFIRSTB is projected to deliver an FY27E EPS of Rs. 6.33. We assign a P/E multiple of 15x, in line with its improving RoA/RoE profile and discount to larger private banks. This yields our 12M target of **Rs. 95**.

**BEAR:** Stress case → EPS → P/E multiple → downside
A challenging macroeconomic environment leading to slower credit growth (12% YoY for FY27E) and higher credit costs (GNPA rising to 2.0%) could compress NIMs to 6.0%. This results in an FY27E EPS of Rs. 4.80. Applying a conservative P/E multiple of 12x (reflecting increased risk and slower growth), our Bear Case target is **Rs. 58**.

**Method 1: P/E-based target**
We value IDFCFIRSTB at 15x FY27E EPS of Rs. 6.33, arriving at a target price of Rs. 95. This multiple is justified by the bank's improving ROCE (expected to reach 14-15% by FY27E from ~10% currently), strong retail focus, and consistent liability franchise build-out, positioning it for re-rating towards larger private bank peers (trading at 18-25x P/E).

**Method 2: EV/EBITDA-based target**
Not directly applicable for banks. Instead, we use P/B multiple.
We value IDFCFIRSTB at 2.0x FY27E Book Value per Share (BVPS) of Rs. 47.50, arriving at a target price of Rs. 95. This is slightly below the sector median for mid-sized private banks (2.2-2.5x P/B) but reflects its still-developing profitability and scale.

**Blended target and upside %**
Blended Target: Rs. 95 (weighted average of 100% P/E-based, aligning with P/B).
Upside %: (95 - 72.95) / 72.95 = **30.2%**.

**FCF yield on current market cap**
Banks are typically valued on P/E or P/B. FCF is not a primary metric for valuation due to regulatory capital requirements and the nature of lending.

**Re-rating potential narrative**
IDFCFIRSTB's re-rating potential hinges on achieving sustained RoA of >1.2% and RoE of >15%. Consistent delivery on these profitability metrics, coupled with stable asset quality and continued retail deposit growth, would trigger a multiple expansion towards 2.0-2.5x P/B and 18-20x P/E, aligning it closer to established private banking peers.
