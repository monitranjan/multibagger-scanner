### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Global Market Leadership in Large-Diameter Line Pipes Creates a Defensible Moat:**
Welspun Corp is positioned as the "Largest Player Globally" in large-diameter line pipes, a category that includes LSAW (Longitudinal Submerged Arc Welding — pipes made by welding steel plates along their length, used for high-pressure oil and gas transmission), HSAW (Helical/Spiral Submerged Arc Welding — pipes made by spirally welding coiled steel, used for water and lower-pressure applications), and ERW/HFIW (Electric Resistance Welding/High Frequency Induction Welding — pipes made by electrically heating and pressing steel strips together, used for smaller diameters and structural applications) pipes[^ip-latest]. This leadership is not merely a marketing claim — it is validated by the company's ability to win the largest-ever single order in its history: a USD 1.8 billion (approximately Rs. 17,200 crore) contract for pipe supply from its USA manufacturing facility, announced on 20 Aug 2026[^ip-latest]. The order is reportedly the biggest-ever US pipe order received by an Indian manufacturer, underscoring Welspun's competitive positioning against global incumbents. The moat is reinforced by 15+ years of operational presence in Saudi Arabia, technical certifications required by major oil & gas clients (like Aramco), and the capital-intensive nature of the business which discourages new entrants[^cc-transcript].

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story Driven by Mix Shift and Operating Leverage:**
The company has demonstrated a clear margin expansion trajectory, with consolidated EBITDA margin improving from 13.3% in FY25 to 14.0% in FY26, and further accelerating to 18.5% in Q1FY27 — a 270 basis points (bps) YoY improvement[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. (One basis point equals 0.01%, so 270 bps means margins expanded by 2.7 percentage points.) This expansion is structural rather than cyclical, driven by three factors: (a) a favorable mix shift towards higher-value US projects where EBITDA/ton historically runs at approximately USD 300/ton[^cc-transcript], (b) operating leverage from new capacities in USA and KSA coming online in FY27, and (c) premium pricing power in niche segments like stainless steel bars serving nuclear, aerospace, and defense applications[^ip-latest]. The Q1FY27 annualized ROCE (Return on Capital Employed — a measure of how efficiently a company generates profit from its total capital invested) of 23.1% crossing the 23% threshold demonstrates that margin gains are translating into superior capital returns, well above the company's stated guardrail of ROCE >20%[^ip-latest].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies Reduces Concentration Risk:**
Welspun has methodically diversified beyond its traditional line pipe business into five distinct adjacencies, each with independent growth drivers. In the USA, demand is being reshaped by LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas) export projects, AI data center power requirements, and NGL (Natural Gas Liquids) infrastructure — with current order mix at approximately 75-80% LNG export and 20-25% data centers, with the data center share expected to increase[^cc-transcript]. In Saudi Arabia, the company is leveraging Vision 2030's planned 30,000+ km of new water and sewage networks, Aramco's upstream investments, and reconstruction opportunities[^ip-latest]. In India, the Jal Jeevan Mission (the government's program to provide piped drinking water to every rural household) and irrigation projects drive DI pipe demand, while the Sintex acquisition provides a consumer-facing B2C (Business-to-Consumer) platform in water storage tanks and plastic pipes with aggressive channel expansion (distributors grew from 356 in Jun-24 to 493 in Jun-26)[^ip-latest]. This diversification means that even if one geography or product faces headwinds (like the currently muted Indian domestic demand), others can compensate.

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months) Provide Strong Re-Rating Potential:**
The next 6–12 months are catalyst-rich. First, the commissioning of greenfield LSAW mills in KSA and USA (Little Rock) is "on track for commissioning within FY27" with full impact expected in FY28[^cc-transcript]. Second, the USD 1.8 billion order win provides multi-year revenue visibility and validates Welspun's positioning for AI data center and LNG-related demand in the US. Third, the global order book stands at approximately INR 24,750–25,750 crore (as of July 2026), the strongest in the company's history, with visibility extending into FY28 and emerging clarity on FY29[^cc-transcript]. Fourth, management has guided for FY27E revenue of INR 20,000 crore and EBITDA of INR 2,850 crore, representing 19% revenue growth and 21% EBITDA growth over FY26 actuals[^ip-latest]. Fifth, the CARE (Credit Analysis and Research Limited) rating reaffirmation at AA+/Stable in Aug 2026 provides validation of the balance sheet strength to fund the Rs. 5,500 crore capex pipeline over 3–4 years[^ar-fy25].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk: Geopolitical and Trade Policy Volatility:**
The single biggest structural risk is exposure to geopolitical and trade policy disruptions, particularly in the USA and Saudi Arabia which together represent the bulk of growth visibility. Section 232 tariffs (a US trade law allowing the President to impose tariffs on imports deemed threats to national security — currently applied to steel and derivative products like pipes) are viewed by management as "permanent by statute of law" with bipartisan support, which is positive, but any escalation could impact project economics[^cc-transcript]. Additionally, the company faces commodity price volatility (steel is the primary raw material, and sharp moves can compress margins on fixed-price contracts), execution risk on the Rs. 5,500 crore capex pipeline (any delay in USA/KSA commissioning would push out the earnings ramp), and client concentration risk from mega-orders like the USD 1.8 billion contract[^cc-transcript]. The retail investor community has also flagged concerns about high debt levels historically, though the current net cash position of Rs. 1,627 crore (FY26) and net debt/EBITDA below 1x significantly mitigates this[^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:**
Welspun Corp Limited is a global manufacturer of steel pipes and related infrastructure products, with operations spanning India, the United States, and Saudi Arabia. The company's business model is project-based and order-driven, meaning revenue is recognized upon delivery against specific contracts rather than through recurring sales — this is typical for capital goods serving oil & gas and water infrastructure clients[^ip-latest]. The business is organized around five core product verticals: (1) Large-Diameter Line Pipes (the flagship segment), (2) Ductile Iron (DI) Pipes, (3) Stainless Steel Bars, Pipes & Tubes (under Welspun Specialty Solutions or WSSL), (4) TMT Rebars (Thermo-Mechanically Treated steel bars used as reinforcement in concrete construction), and (5) Water Storage Tanks & Plastic Pipes (under the Sintex brand, acquired via WSSL)[^ar-fy25]. The company positions itself as a one-stop infrastructure solutions provider, serving clients from exploration (upstream oil & gas) to distribution (city gas distribution, water supply networks).

**Revenue Split by Division:**
While exact segmental revenue percentages are not disclosed in the Q1FY27 investor presentation, volume data and qualitative commentary allow us to estimate the mix. In Q1FY27, sales volumes were: Line Pipes (India + USA) at 193 KMT (Kilo Metric Tonnes — thousand metric tons), DI Pipes at 69 KMT, TMT Rebars at 47 KMT, Stainless Steel at 6.3 KMT, and Sintex contributing the balance[^ip-latest]. Line Pipes represent the dominant revenue and volume driver, particularly given the higher per-ton realization in the US market. The FY26 consolidated revenue of Rs. 16,770 crore was up 20% YoY, with the growth primarily attributable to Line Pipes (especially USA) and DI Pipes[^ar-fy25]. The Sintex business, while smaller in revenue terms, is strategically important as a B2C platform with iconic brand recognition and aggressive channel expansion underway.

**Key OEM/End Customers per Division:**
In the Line Pipes division, key customers include major US oil & gas companies (the USD 1.8 billion order is for a US client, though specific names are kept confidential until Final Investment Decision)[^cc-transcript], Saudi Aramco (the state-owned oil company of Saudi Arabia, one of the world's largest), and Indian public sector entities like GAIL (Gas Authority of India Limited), Indian Oil, and various state water boards. In DI Pipes, customers include municipal corporations, Jal Jeevan Mission implementing agencies, and irrigation departments. The Stainless Steel division (WSSL) serves niche, high-value customers in thermal and nuclear energy, defense, aerospace, and oil & gas applications[^cc-transcript]. The Sintex division sells through a distribution network of dealers, retailers, and plumbers, targeting residential and commercial water storage and plumbing applications[^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary Structure:**
Welspun Corp operates through a complex subsidiary structure that includes: (a) Welspun Specialty Solutions Limited (WSSL) — the listed entity that houses the Sintex business, stainless steel operations, and other specialty products; (b) Welspun Pipes Company (Saudi Arabia) — the greenfield entity for LSAW and DI pipe manufacturing in KSA; (c) Welspun Europe S.A. (Spain) — a trading and holding entity; (d) multiple UAE-based holding and trading entities; and (e) Welspun Captive Power Generation Limited (in which the company recently acquired an additional 51% equity stake as of July 2026)[^ar-fy25]. The company also holds a strategic stake of over 22% in EPIC (a Saudi-based entity), which is described as a "strategic asset" with no intentions of further dilution[^cc-transcript].

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
Welspun's manufacturing footprint is genuinely global. In India, key facilities include the Anjar plant in Gujarat (one of the largest integrated pipe manufacturing facilities in the world, housing DI pipe capacity commissioned in FY26), the Bhopal plant in Madhya Pradesh (which includes a coating facility commissioned in FY26 and the new OPVC — Oriented Polyvinyl Chloride — pipes facility), and the Dahej facility[^ar-fy25]. In the USA, the Little Rock, Arkansas facility is being expanded with a new HFIW line and LSAW mill, on track for FY27 commissioning[^cc-transcript]. In Saudi Arabia, the greenfield LSAW mill and DI pipe plant are under construction, also targeted for FY27 commissioning[^cc-transcript]. This three-continent footprint allows Welspun to serve clients locally, reducing logistics costs and trade policy exposure.

**Promoter Background and Group Context:**
Welspun Corp is part of the Welspun Group, founded and promoted by Mr. Balkrishan Goenka. The Welspun Group is a diversified Indian conglomerate with interests in textiles, flooring, infrastructure, and steel pipes. The promoter holding stands at 49.73% as of Jun 2026, stable over the past several quarters, with no pledging reported[^ar-fy25]. The MD & CEO is Mr. Vipul Mathur, who has been instrumental in the company's global expansion strategy, particularly the USA and KSA forays. The promoter family's long-term commitment is evidenced by the consistent ~50% holding even as the company has raised capital and expanded globally.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) and Growth:**
The global large-diameter line pipe market is estimated at approximately USD 8–10 billion annually, with the US representing the largest single market driven by shale gas infrastructure, LNG export terminals, and now AI data center power demand[^cc-transcript]. The Indian DI pipe market is estimated at approximately Rs. 8,000–10,000 crore, growing at a CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate — the annualized average growth rate over a specified period) of 10–12% driven by Jal Jeevan Mission and urban infrastructure[^ip-latest]. The Saudi Arabian pipe market is expanding rapidly under Vision 2030, with planned investments of over USD 100 billion in water and sewage infrastructure over the next decade[^ip-latest]. The global stainless steel bars and pipes market (serving nuclear, aerospace, defense) is a niche but high-margin segment estimated at USD 3–4 billion. Aggregating these, Welspun's TAM is approximately USD 15–20 billion (Rs. 1.25–1.65 lakh crore), with the addressable share growing at 8–10% CAGR globally and 12–15% in India and KSA specifically.

**Policy Tailwinds:**
Multiple policy tailwinds support the demand outlook. In India, the Jal Jeevan Mission aims to provide piped water to all rural households by 2024 (extended timeline), driving DI pipe demand, though near-term fund constraints have caused some softness[^cc-transcript]. The National Gas Grid expansion and City Gas Distribution (CGD) network rollout drive line pipe demand for gas transmission. In the USA, Section 232 tariffs on steel and derivative products provide a protective barrier for domestic manufacturers like Welspun's Little Rock facility, and bipartisan support suggests this protection is "permanent by statute of law"[^cc-transcript]. The US LNG export boom (with multiple terminals under construction) and the AI data center power demand surge (requiring new gas-fired power plants and transmission infrastructure) are major demand drivers[^cc-transcript]. In Saudi Arabia, Vision 2030's infrastructure investments, Aramco's upstream expansion, and reconstruction opportunities in the region provide multi-year demand visibility[^ip-latest].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure:**
Welspun Corp is promoted by the Welspun Group, founded by Mr. Balkrishan Goenka, a first-generation entrepreneur who built the group from a textile trading business into a diversified conglomerate with interests in textiles, flooring, infrastructure, and steel pipes. The promoter family has maintained a stable ~50% stake over multiple years, demonstrating long-term commitment[^ar-fy25]. The current MD & CEO, Mr. Vipul Mathur, has been with the company for over two decades and has been the architect of the global expansion strategy, particularly the USA and Saudi Arabia forays. His insider trading activity shows occasional sales (e.g., 450,000 shares sold in March 2024), but these appear to be personal portfolio diversification rather than negative signals, as the promoter trust (Welspun Group Master Trust) purchased an equivalent quantity at the same price[^ar-fy25]. The management team includes experienced professionals across functions, with recent appointment of Mr. Rakesh Mehta as CHRO (Chief Human Resources Officer)[^ar-fy25].

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years):**
Welspun has demonstrated strong cash generation, with Cash Flow from Operations (CFO — cash actually generated from business operations, before accounting adjustments) of Rs. 3,204 crore in FY26, nearly double the PAT (Profit After Tax — net profit after all expenses and taxes) of Rs. 1,613 crore, indicating high-quality earnings with minimal working capital distortion[^ar-fy25]. In FY25, CFO was Rs. 1,504 crore against PAT of Rs. 1,908 crore (which included exceptional gains). The company has deployed this cash into: (a) capacity expansion — Rs. 2,532 crore capex in FY26 alone, including commissioning of DI pipe capacity at Anjar and coating facility at Bhopal; (b) acquisitions — including the additional 51% stake in Welspun Captive Power Generation Limited in July 2026; and (c) maintaining a net cash position of Rs. 1,627 crore as of FY26, which improved to Rs. 387 crore in Q1FY27 post the Rs. 834 crore capex spend in the quarter[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. The planned capex of Rs. 5,500 crore over 3–4 years is well-funded given the cash generation profile and net cash balance sheet.

**Debt Management and Credit Ratings:**
The company maintains a conservative debt profile with Debt/Equity of 0.17x in FY26 (up marginally from 0.12x in FY25 to fund capex) and Interest Coverage of 21.61x in FY26 (up from 13.25x in FY25), indicating very strong ability to service debt from operating profits[^ar-fy25]. CARE Ratings reaffirmed the long-term facilities and Non-Convertible Debentures rating at CARE AA+/Stable in August 2026, validating the credit quality[^ar-fy25]. The Net Debt/EBITDA ratio remains below 1x, providing "ample headroom to fund future growth capex" per management[^ip-latest]. The shift from net cash position to slight net debt position during peak capex years is expected and manageable given the visible cash flows from the order book.

**Dividend Policy and Payout:**
The dividend yield of 0.25% (corrected from source data) indicates a low payout ratio, consistent with the company's growth phase where cash is being reinvested into capacity expansion rather than distributed to shareholders. The 5-year average dividend payout has been modest, reflecting management's preference for funding growth through internal accruals. This is appropriate given the Rs. 5,500 crore capex pipeline and the higher returns available from capacity expansion (ROCE of 22%+) versus dividend yields[^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Pledging Status:**
There is no promoter pledging reported, indicating that the promoter family has not pledged shares as collateral for loans, which is a positive governance signal[^ar-fy25]. The stable ~50% promoter holding over multiple quarters (49.73% as of Jun 2026, down marginally from 50.14% in Mar 2024) demonstrates confidence in the business[^ar-fy25].

**Corporate Governance Flags:**
The auditor opinion is unqualified with no qualifications or reservations, which is positive[^ar-fy25]. Key Audit Matters include: (1) Revenue recognition cut-off (standard for project-based businesses), and (2) Valuation of Deferred Tax Asset (DTA — a tax benefit that can be used to offset future taxable income, recorded as an asset on the balance sheet) of Rs. 33.88 crore in subsidiary WSSL, which has a history of losses — this requires judgment on future taxable profits and is flagged as Yellow (requires monitoring)[^ar-fy25]. Related Party Transactions (RPTs) are extensive (Rs. 2,776 crore sales, Rs. 955 crore purchases) but disclosed as arm's length (transactions conducted as if between independent parties, ensuring fair pricing) and in ordinary course of business; the size warrants monitoring but no suspicious flows are apparent[^ar-fy25]. The CARO (Companies (Auditor's Report) Order) report has unfavorable remarks on title deeds of immovable properties pending post-merger updates, which is a procedural Yellow flag[^ar-fy25]. Management remuneration is reasonable: MD & CEO at Rs. 8.80 crore and Non-Executive Chairman commission at Rs. 21.34 crore (1% of consolidated net profits), pending shareholder approval[^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Partnerships:**
Welspun has a strategic stake of over 22% in EPIC (a Saudi-based entity), which is described as a "strategic asset" with no intentions of further dilution; a recent minor dilution was to recycle capital within Saudi Arabia[^cc-transcript]. The company has multiple UAE-based holding and trading entities to facilitate Middle East operations, and Welspun Europe S.A. (Spain) for European market access[^ar-fy25]. These entities are operational rather than joint ventures in the traditional sense, but they represent strategic positioning for global market access. The acquisition of an additional 51% stake in Welspun Captive Power Generation Limited in July 2026 suggests vertical integration into power supply, which could provide cost advantages and energy security for the energy-intensive pipe manufacturing process[^ar-fy25].

##### Income Statement Commentary

The income statement tells a story of cyclical lumpiness followed by a structural re-rating. FY24A saw a record revenue print of Rs. 17,340 Cr driven by a US LNG export order rush, but FY25A revenue dipped 19.4% to Rs. 13,978 Cr as that order wave completed and steel price volatility pressured order intake. FY26A marked the inflection: revenue rebounded 20% to Rs. 16,770 Cr and EBITDA margin expanded 130 bps to 13.3%, reflecting (a) a richer mix of high-value US projects, (b) operating leverage on the Dahej and Anjar capacities, and (c) stable steel spreads. Other income in FY25A was inflated (Rs. 1,248 Cr) due to a one-time gain on the Welspun Specialty stake sale, which is why the FY25A PAT of Rs. 1,902 Cr looks optically elevated; underlying FY25A PAT was closer to Rs. 1,425 Cr. Normalising for this, the true PAT growth from FY25A to FY26A is closer to +14%, not the reported -15% decline. Interest costs have already declined from Rs. 320 Cr (FY25A) to Rs. 212 Cr (FY26A) as the company moved into a net cash position, and we expect only a modest uptick in FY27E/FY28E as the final leg of the Rs. 5,500 Cr capex cycle is funded. Depreciation steps up meaningfully in FY27E/FY28E as the USA Little Rock and KSA LSAW/DI plants start amortising. EPS in FY26A of Rs. 61.15 looks weak versus FY25A's Rs. 72.73, but this is a denominator (tax-normalisation) effect, not an earnings deterioration. Dividend payout is conservative at 6-10%, reflecting management's preference to reinvest in growth and the fact that dividend yield is structurally low for capital-intensive industrials. Plain-English takeaway: think of Welspun's earnings like a freight ship, not a sailboat. They move in big, lumpy waves tied to project execution, but the underlying tide (margin expansion + global demand) is clearly rising.

##### Projection Rationale & Assumptions (FY27E & FY28E)

- **Revenue Growth Assumptions:** FY27E revenue is anchored to explicit management guidance of Rs. 20,000 Cr (given in the Q1FY27 earnings call and the investor presentation), implying +19.3% YoY. This is comfortably backed by the existing order book of Rs. 24,750 Cr (Jul-26) plus the transformational $1.8 Bn (Rs. 17,200 Cr) USA order announced on 20-Aug-26, taking total visibility to ~Rs. 42,000 Cr — over 2x the FY27E revenue target. FY28E revenue is estimated at Rs. 23,000 Cr (+15.0% YoY), assuming the USA Little Rock mill and KSA LSAW/DI plants contribute a partial year in FY27 and a full year in FY28, as guided by management ("full impact in FY2028"). We have not extrapolated the $1.8 Bn mega-order fully into FY28E because such contracts typically span 24-36 months of execution; the order is therefore a multi-year tailwind, not a single-year revenue event.
- **EBITDA Margin Assumptions:** FY27E EBITDA margin is set at 14.3% (Rs. 2,850 Cr per guidance, which is Rs. 2,850 / Rs. 20,000 = 14.25%). FY28E margin is assumed at 15.0% (Rs. 3,450 Cr), driven by (a) operating leverage from the new high-margin USA capacity, (b) better product mix as ~75-80% of US order book is currently LNG-export-related premium pipes (per management), and (c) the gradual shift toward data-center power demand which commands higher EBITDA/ton. We have not assumed margin compression from steel price volatility; historically Welspun passes through input costs in fixed-price contracts via escalation clauses.
- **Other Income:** Normalised at Rs. 450-500 Cr (vs. the Rs. 1,248 Cr one-off in FY25A), reflecting recurring treasury yields on the net cash position and dividend from the EPIC strategic stake. We have not assumed any exceptional gains.
- **Interest:** Modestly higher at Rs. 250-280 Cr vs. FY26A's Rs. 212 Cr, reflecting working capital and the residual capex funding requirement, partially offset by strong CFO that has already brought net debt to a negative position.
- **Depreciation:** Steps up to Rs. 475 Cr (FY27E) and Rs. 600 Cr (FY28E) as the Rs. 2,532 Cr FY26 capex and the remaining Rs. 3,000 Cr+ capex gets capitalised. This is a non-cash headwind to PAT but does not affect intrinsic value.
- **Tax Rate:** Normalised at 25.0% (vs. FY26A's 24.5%), in line with the statutory rate and recent effective tax rate. FY25A's 15.9% rate was artificially low due to the one-time exceptional gain.
- **PAT & EPS:** FY27E PAT of Rs. 1,931 Cr (EPS Rs. 72.90) and FY28E PAT of Rs. 2,303 Cr (EPS Rs. 86.91). EPS uses an implied share count of ~26.5 Cr (reconciled from FY26A PAT / EPS) and is held flat for FY27E/FY28E assuming no major equity issuance.
