### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Chalet Hotels has constructed a formidable economic moat in the premium hospitality and mixed-use real estate segment, particularly within the Mumbai Metropolitan Region (MMR) where acute land scarcity creates natural barriers to entry for new competitors [^ip-latest]. By franchising/operating under global giants like Marriott and Accor, and domestic leader IHCL (Taj), they plug into massive global loyalty databases—this is analogous to having a permanent, low-cost customer pipeline that unknown local hotels cannot replicate [^cc-transcript]. Their owned resort brand "Athiva" is gaining rapid traction with a 4.9+ customer rating, demonstrating emerging pricing power in the leisure segment [^cc-transcript].

- **Structural margin story:** The company’s EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization—a clean measure of core operating cash profit before accounting noise) margin expanded by 231 basis points (bps—where 100 bps equals exactly 1%, so this is a 2.31 percentage point improvement) YoY to 46.7% in Q1 FY27 for the ex-residential base [^ip-latest]. Hotels are inherently high fixed-cost businesses: whether a room is occupied or not, the building stands and taxes are paid. Therefore, when occupancy rises or rates increase, the extra revenue flows almost directly to the bottom line. The Commercial Real Estate (CRE—office space leasing) division runs at an astonishing 85.0% margin because once a tower is built, the incremental cost to maintain it is tiny versus the rent collected [^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Chalet is strategically transitioning from a pure-play hotelier into a mixed-asset real estate compounder. The CRE run-rate hit Rs 290 mn/month in Jun’26, and they are adding CIGNUS Powai Tower II (0.9 msf—meaning million square feet of office space) by Q4 FY27 [^cc-transcript]. On the hospitality side, they are diversifying geographically away from a Mumbai-centric base into Hyderabad (Ritz Carlton, 330 keys), Goa (Athiva Varca, 205 keys), and Delhi (Taj, 380 keys) [^ip-latest]. This geographic and asset-class spread reduces the risk of a single-city demand downturn hurting the entire portfolio.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The recent rebranding of the Vashi property from Four Points to ATHIVA Pulse Navi Mumbai Autograph Collection (announced Aug 2026) should allow immediate room rate (ADR) premiums as the new flag attracts higher-spending transient guests [^cc-transcript]. The launch of Taj Delhi Int’l Airport (~380 keys) in Q4 FY27 and the completion of CIGNUS II will act as step-change earnings accelerators [^ip-latest]. Additionally, the Powai complex porch completion by end Q2 FY27 should remove construction noise, aiding MMR occupancy recovery toward management’s targeted 70%+ range [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The most significant structural risk is geopolitical volatility (e.g., West Asia conflicts) which suppresses Foreign Tourist Arrivals (FTA), as evidenced by Hyderabad’s flattish Q1 performance due to its FTA dependence [^cc-transcript]. Furthermore, the planned INR 30bn CAPEX over FY27-FY29, while stated to be internally funded, carries execution risk; any delay in local approvals (such as the Udaipur army cantonment clearances) could push timelines and temporarily strain liquidity buffers of ~INR 4bn [^ip-latest].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Chalet Hotels operates a three-pronged business model: Hospitality (hotels/resorts), Commercial Real Estate (CRE—leasing office towers to corporates), and Residential (selling apartments) [^ip-latest]. In Q1 FY27, total consolidated revenue was Rs 5,213 mn. To explain in simple terms: Hospitality means money from hotel room nights and restaurant/food sales; CRE means monthly rent from companies using their office buildings; Residential is one-off flat sales recognized upon handover [^ip-latest].

The revenue split shows Hospitality dominating at 80.3% (Rs 4,185 mn), CRE at 16.6% (Rs 865 mn), and Residential at merely 1.4% (Rs 73 mn, representing just 1 unit handed over versus 95 units last year) [^ip-latest]. Within Hospitality, Room revenue contributed 42.6% and F&B (Food & Beverage) 30.5% [^ip-latest]. Key customer segments for hotels include "Transient" (individual travelers booking directly or via agents) at 69%, "Groups" (weddings/corporate conferences) at 22%, and "Contract" (long-stay corporate bulk deals) at 9% [^ip-latest]. Distribution channels were 53% direct (property walk-ins/voice), showing low reliance on Online Travel Agents (OTAs) which typically charge 15-20% commissions [^cc-transcript].

Subsidiary structure includes the acquisition of 100% of SHPL (Seasons Hotels) for Rs 1,710 mn in May’26 as a pure asset acquisition to expand the Udaipur footprint [^ip-latest]. The manufacturing/property footprint spans Maharashtra (Mumbai, Khandala, Pune), Goa, Karnataka (Bengaluru), Telangana (Hyderabad), Uttarakhand (Rishikesh), and Rajasthan (Udaipur via acquisition), diversifying monsoon and seasonality risks [^cc-transcript]. The promoter group is the K. Raheja Corp family, a legacy Mumbai real estate developer, providing deep domain expertise in construction and land assembly [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian branded hospitality market across key cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru etc.) has a premium supply base of ~100,000 keys, with a Total Addressable Market (TAM) estimated at Rs 2,50,000 Cr (approx USD 30bn) growing at a CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate—the smooth mean annual growth rate over a period, stripping out volatility) of 10-12% [^vp-thread]. Premium supply CAGR in key markets is only 5-6% FY25-FY28, meaning demand is structurally outstripping new supply, a classic pricing power environment [^ip-latest]. Policy tailwinds include government tourism infra capex, visa reforms, and legacy G20 events supporting domestic leisure [^vp-thread].

Competitive moat analysis:
- **Switching costs:** Moderate. Guests can easily book a rival hotel tomorrow, but Marriott/Accor loyalty points create stickiness for frequent travelers [^ip-latest].
- **Scale:** Strong. Portfolio of >5,000 keys with 500+ added yearly creates massive purchasing power for linen, food, and utilities [^cc-transcript].
- **Pricing power:** Strong. ADR (Average Daily Rate—the average price per room sold) grew 8.5% YoY to Rs 13,247 even with a slight occupancy dip, proving they can raise prices without losing all customers [^ip-latest].
