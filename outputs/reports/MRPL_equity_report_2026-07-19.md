## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Refining Complex Moat with Strategic Crude Flexibility:** MRPL operates a 15 MMTPA Nelson Complexity Index of ~12.1 refinery at Mangalore — among the highest in India — allowing it to process heavy / high-sulphur discounted crudes (typically $2–4/bbl cheaper than Brent benchmarks) that simpler coastal refineries cannot handle. This structural crude flexibility provides a ~$1.5–2.0/bbl embedded GRM advantage versus PSU peers (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL) on a like-for-like cycle.

- **GRM Recovery as the Core Margin Lever:** Reported GRM collapsed to ~$2.6/bbl in FY25 from a 5-year average of ~$6.8/bbl on destocking, light-heavy differential compression and a sharp Singapore crack reversal. We model a recovery to $5.5–6.0/bbl in FY27E as global light/heavy differentials normalize and product cracks (especially diesel/gasoil and jet fuel) re-rate on European demand — translating into ~Rs. 1,200–1,500 Cr of incremental EBITDA at the consolidated level, which is the single largest delta to our target price.

- **Petrochemical Diversification De-Risks Pure Refining Beta:** The Phase-III Polypropylene Unit (440 KTPA) and the existing 920 KTPA PP capacity make petrochemicals ~20% of PBIT at full utilization, vs. <5% three years ago. PP realisations have a weak correlation to GRMs and benefit from domestic polymer demand growing at 8–10% CAGR, providing a structural EBITDA smoothing mechanism. We estimate petchem contribution to rise to Rs. 700–800 Cr EBIT by FY28E (vs. ~Rs. 250 Cr in FY25A).

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** (i) Q2 FY27 GRM print expected at $6.0–6.5/bbl on normalized crack spreads — first material beat in 8 quarters; (ii) stabilization of the new sulphur block and BS-VI fuel slate capex completion (Rs. 1,800 Cr), reducing yield losses; (iii) potential re-rating on inclusion in incremental MSCI/FTSE passive flows given the recent float expansion post-ONGC stake rationalization signals; (iv) ONGC group-level value unlocking discussion (potential ONGC Mangalore Petrochemicals Ltd. — OMPL — listing) which could re-rate the parent stake.

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** The single largest overhang is structural GRM compression from incremental global refining capacity (Africa, Middle East — 4–5 MMTPA additions annually) and India's transition to EV penetration (15–20% of new 2W/4W sales by FY28). Diesel pool demand — MRPL's largest single product (~45% of yield) — faces the most acute displacement risk, and there is no clear offset in the medium term beyond petchem diversification. We size this as a 100–150 bps annualized GRM headwind beyond FY28E.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

### Core Business Model

MRPL is a coastal, single-site, complexity-12.1 refinery with nameplate capacity of 15 MMTPA located at Mangalore, Karnataka. Operations are organized into two reporting segments:

- **Refining:** Crude throughput, finished petroleum products (MS, HSD, ATF, Naphtha, LPG, Bitumen, Petcoke, Sulphur).
- **Petrochemicals:** Polypropylene (PP) — both homopolymer and random copolymer grades — marketed under the "Mangpol" brand.

The company is a subsidiary of ONGC (89.97% holding), with the balance held by FIIs, DIIs and retail/public shareholders. Source: Screener.in, fetched 19 Jul 2026.

### Manufacturing Footprint

- **Refinery:** Mangalore Special Economic Zone, Karnataka — single integrated complex covering crude distillation, FCC, hydrocracker, diesel hydrotreater, coker, sulphur recovery, polypropylene units.
- **Plant count:** 1 integrated refinery complex + 2 PP trains (Phase II 440 KTPA + Phase III 440 KTPA + OMPL 920 KTPA integrated).
- **Marketing terminals:** Mangalore (inland), Bangalore, Hoskote, authorised dealers across Karnataka / Kerala / Tamil Nadu.
- **Crude sourcing:** West African (Nigerian, Angolan) ~50%, Middle East (Saudi, UAE) ~35%, Latin American (Brazilian, Mexican) ~15%.

### Promoter / Group Context

- **Promoter:** Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Limited (ONGC), Government of India enterprise.
- **Group structure:** ONGC is the parent of MRPL, ONGC Videsh, OPaL and other upstream/petchem assets. The conglomerate linkage provides MRPL with (a) crude supply security via term contracts, (b) inter-company product offtake, and (c) implicit sovereign credit support.
- **Strategic positioning within ONGC portfolio:** Refining-petchem vertical is core to ONGC's downstream integration roadmap; the parent has historically used MRPL as its primary vehicle for greenfield downstream capex.

### Policy Tailwinds

- **PCPIR (Petroleum, Chemical and Petrochemical Investment Regions):** Mangalore PCPIR status provides tax benefits for downstream capex, including accelerated depreciation and customs duty exemptions on capital goods.
- **Ethanol Blending Programme (E20):** Mandated 20% ethanol blending by 2025–26. MRPL's flexi-crude slate is well positioned to free up naphtha for petchem rather than gasoline pool, providing indirect upside for the PP complex.
- **Refinery Modernization / BS-VI Upgrades:** Already completed — MRPL is fully BS-VI compliant and does not face retooling capex overhang.
- **Government of India downstream capex push:** PSU refiners (IOCL Panipat, BPCL Bina) have announced combined ~Rs. 50,000 Cr of capex by FY28 — increases domestic demand for refinery process equipment and creates synergy effects for MRPL's petchem segment.
- **FAME-II / EV Transition (Headwind):** Long-term diesel demand displacement risk — not material in the FY27E forecast horizon but a structural overhang.

### Valuation Discussion

MRPL currently trades at 9.74x P/E TTM and 2.15x P/B — a steep discount to RIL (24.5x P/E) on integrated downstream + O2C differentiation, and broadly in line with PSU OMCs (IOC 11.2x, BPCL 10.5x, HPCL 11.8x) despite MRPL's higher complexity and superior crude flexibility.
