## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Dominant position in transformer oils with regulatory moat:** SOTL is one of India's largest manufacturers of transformer oils with approvals from all major transformer OEMs including BHEL, ABB, Siemens, Schneider Electric, Crompton Greaves and GE [^ip-latest]. The segment enjoys high entry barriers due to 12–18 month approval cycles and stringent IEC/IEEE certification requirements [^ar-fy25]. Transformer oils contribute ~35–40% of revenues and command premium EBITDA margins of 18–22% versus 12–15% for base lubricants [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin expansion via specialty mix shift:** Management has consciously shifted the portfolio from commodity lubricants toward specialty products — pharma-grade white oils, liquid paraffins, food-grade lubricants and cosmetic-grade mineral oils [^ar-fy25]. The white oils and liquid paraffins segment has grown at 15%+ CAGR over FY22–FY25 with EBITDA margins of 25%+, materially above the 12–15% blended margin [^ip-latest]. Management has guided to increase specialty share from ~45% currently to 60% by FY28E [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Three distinct growth vectors are emerging — (i) Savita Greentec Ltd (wholly-owned subsidiary) in plastic pyrolysis/recycling, well-positioned for India's Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regime [^ip-latest]; (ii) EV thermal management fluids and battery cooling fluids, an early-stage but high-potential adjacency [^cc-transcript]; and (iii) bio-degradable farm equipment lubricants with OEM tie-ups at Tata Motors and Swaraj Tractors [^vp-thread]. The wind power portfolio contributes ~Rs. 31 Cr revenue with surplus carbon credits [^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Capacity expansion at the Mahad facility lifting transformer oil output by ~30% [^ip-latest]; (ii) Volume ramp in newly-won industrial lubricant accounts [^cc-transcript]; (iii) Potential third buyback following two prior buybacks at Rs. 1,400 and Rs. 1,600 — historically a positive signaling event [^vp-thread]; (iv) Scale-up of Savita Greentec plastic recycling volumes [^vp-thread]; and (v) Power transmission capex tailwind under RDSS/Reforms-linked schemes driving transformer oil demand [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Intense competition in B2C automotive lubricants from entrenched branded players (Castrol, Mobil, Shell, Veedol) with strong consumer pull [^vp-thread]. The 'Savsol' brand lacks retail pricing power, limiting pass-through of base oil cost inflation during sharp crude moves [^ar-fy25]. Base oil price volatility creates a 2–3 quarter margin lag, and the failure of the proposed Idemitsu merger in earlier years removed a potential strategic backstop [^vp-thread].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Savita Oil Technologies Limited (SOTL) is a home-grown specialty petroleum products company established in 1961 by the late Mr. N.K. Mehra [^ar-fy25]. The company manufactures a wide portfolio including transformer oils, liquid paraffins, white oils, automotive and industrial lubricants, coolants and greases, serving diverse end-industries — power generation & T&D, pharmaceuticals, personal care, automotive, agriculture and industrial manufacturing [^ip-latest].

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25 estimates):**

| Industrial Lubricants & Greases | 15–18% | Steel, cement, manufacturing |
| Other Specialty (Coolants, Greases) | 5–8% | Miscellaneous industrial |

*Source: Investor Presentation [^ip-latest] and Annual Report [^ar-fy25].*

**Key OEM/End Customers by Division:**
- **Transformer Oils:** BHEL, ABB, Siemens, Schneider Electric, Crompton Greaves, GE — long-term supply contracts with most [^ip-latest].
- **White Oils/Liquid Paraffins:** Leading pharma companies (Cipla, Sun Pharma and others), FMCG and personal care majors [^ar-fy25].
- **Automotive Lubricants:** Tata Motors (OEM tie-up), Swaraj Tractors (farm equipment), aftermarket distributors; recently tied up with Aegis Logistics for distribution [^vp-thread].
- **Industrial:** Steel, cement, power plants and general manufacturing [^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary Structure:**
- **Savita Polymers Limited** — acquired in FY22 from the promoter group at 1x sales / ~13x P/E (arm's-length pricing) [^vp-thread].
- **Savita Greentec Limited** — wholly-owned subsidiary focused on plastic recycling / pyrolysis oil, aligned with EPR regulations [^ip-latest].
- **Savita International FZE (Dubai)** — export distribution arm [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
- **Mahad, Maharashtra** — flagship integrated facility (transformer oils, white oils, base oil processing) [^ip-latest].
- **Silvassa** — lubricants and greases blending plant [^ip-latest].
- **Chennai** — regional blending and distribution [^ip-latest].
- **Wind power installations** in Maharashtra and Rajasthan (~31 Cr revenue, with carbon credit surplus) [^vp-thread].

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** Founded by the late Mr. N.K. Mehra in 1961, the company is now led by the next-generation Mehra family. Mr. Siddharth Mehra has recently joined to assist in handling the business, indicating succession continuity [^vp-thread]. The Mehra family retains 69.97% of equity, reflecting strong skin-in-the-game and long-term orientation [^ip-latest]. The group also historically controlled Savita Polymers (now absorbed) and has interests in renewable energy through the wind power portfolio [^vp-thread].
