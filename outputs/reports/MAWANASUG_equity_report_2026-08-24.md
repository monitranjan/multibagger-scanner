### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat**: Mawana Sugars Limited (the "Company") is a mid-sized integrated sugar producer with crushing capacity concentrated in Western Uttar Pradesh (UP), a region that accounts for the largest cane acreage in India [^ar-fy25]. A "moat" in simple terms is a durable competitive advantage that protects a company’s profits from rivals, much like a water-filled ditch protected a medieval castle. Mawana’s moat is primarily locational: its mills are situated close to cane farms, reducing transport cost and improving cane recovery (net sugar extracted per tonne of cane). In FY26, net sugar recovery improved to 10.20% versus 9.71% in the prior year, a gain of roughly 49 basis points (a "basis point" or "bps" is one-hundredth of a percentage point, so 49 bps = 0.49%) [^ar-fy25]. However, the switching cost for farmers to sell cane to another mill is low, and pricing power over cane is absent because cane prices (Fair and Remunerative Price, or FRP) are set by the government. Thus the moat is Moderate on scale/efficiency but Weak on pricing power [^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin story**: The Company’s standalone operating profit margin contracted from 6.88% to 4.91% in FY26, a compression of about 197 bps, driven by lower cane crush (25.66 lac tonnes vs 28.70 lac tonnes) and absence of a one-off exceptional gain that boosted prior-year base [^ar-fy25]. The structural offset is the distillery segment: ethanol output rose to 33,798 KL from 29,657 KL, and mandatory E20 ethanol blending (20% ethanol in petrol) from April 2026 provides a higher-margin outlet for molasses-based alcohol [^ar-fy25]. We estimate that a 100 bps improvement in distillery revenue mix could lift consolidated EBITDA margin by ~30-40 bps over FY27E-FY28E as fixed mill overheads get spread over more value-added volume.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies**: Beyond bulk sugar, the Company acquired the consumer brand ‘MAWANA’ in FY25 and made Mawana Foods Private Limited (MFPL) a 100% subsidiary to retail sugar and soap, creating a branded FMCG adjacency [^ar-fy25]. The proposed amalgamation of MFPL into the parent (NCLT pending) will consolidate branded sales, which carry higher realisations than commodity sugar [^ip-latest]. This diversification reduces dependence on cyclical sugar cycles; branded segment contribution is still small (<2% of revenue) but grows at double-digit rates vs flat bulk sugar volume [^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months)**: (i) NCLT approval of MFPL merger, expected by Q3 FY27E, simplifying structure and unlocking cross-selling [^ar-fy25]; (ii) Full allocation utilisation of 38,000 KL ethanol quota from OMCs (only 89% utilised in FY26) as E20 mandate lifts off-take [^ar-fy25]; (iii) Energy-efficiency capex of Rs 4.10 cr commissioned in FY26 should lower power cost per tonne of cane crushed by an estimated 15-20 bps in FY27E [^ar-fy25]; (iv) Stable credit rating (BBB+ Stable / A2) reaffirmed in Aug-2026 reduces refinancing risk and may tighten bond yields by 25-30 bps [^ar-fy25]. These collectively support a re-rating from current P/E of 21.4x toward peer median of ~18-20x on higher quality earnings.

- **Biggest structural risk**: The single largest threat is contingent liability from cane-interest claims: Rs 479.86 cr of contingent liabilities (out of total Rs 496.56 cr) sits against net worth of Rs 524.45 cr, meaning if invoked, it could wipe out ~91% of equity [^ar-fy25]. Although management deems realization "remote", the Top Borer pest damaging CO238 cane variety and urbanisation of UP reducing cultivable land threaten long-term cane availability, potentially forcing mill utilisation below 70% and amplifying fixed-cost leverage [^ar-fy25]. This is a Weak-rated structural risk that requires monitoring of monsoon and pest control policy.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Mawana Sugars operates an integrated model: it crushes sugarcane to produce sugar, converts molasses (a by-product) into industrial alcohol (ethanol) at its distillery, and cogenerates power from bagasse (fibrous cane residue) for captive use and sale to the grid. In plain language, the Company runs a "field-to-fork-plus-fuel" cycle where one crop yields three income streams. Revenue split for FY26 standalone external sales is shown below (all figures in Rs Cr) [^ar-fy25]:

The Company’s manufacturing footprint is entirely domestic, with sugar mills and a distillery in Uttar Pradesh (principally Mawana and Mansurpur complexes) [^ar-fy25]. Subsidiary structure includes MFPL (100% held since Dec-24) which handles branded retail sugar and soap; the proposed merger will absorb MFPL [^ar-fy25]. Promoter Krishna Shriram holds 63.49% of equity, part of the Siel Group lineage with decades of agri-business experience [^ar-fy25]. The group context is a family-promoted enterprise with conservative payout but related-party transactions (e.g., purchase of corporate office from Usha Intl for Rs 28 cr, advance Rs 7 cr) that warrant governance vigilance [^ar-fy25].
