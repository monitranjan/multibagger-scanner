### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Category leadership & brand moat in plastics:** Nilkamal is the market leader in moulded plastic furniture and material handling products (crates, pallets, bins) with ~50%+ share in organised moulded furniture[^ip-latest]. The "Nilkamal" brand has 40+ years of equity, creating strong recall with institutional buyers (e.g., FMCG, auto ancillaries) and households alike, resulting in significant pricing power in B2B material handling[^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin recovery story:** EBITDA margins have bottomed and are on a recovery path post the losses in @home retail, with management guiding for sustained 11–12% consolidated EBITDA margins led by operating leverage in material handling, rationalisation of @home store footprint, and backward integration into polymer processing[^cc-transcript]. Raw material (PP/HDPE) tailwinds and a stable rupee add ~100–150bps potential over FY26E–FY27E[^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond legacy furniture and crates, Nilkamal is scaling (a) Mattress division (Rs. 150–200 Cr revenue, growing at 20%+), (b) uPVC doors & windows (real estate capex play), and (c) Bubble Guard sheets and Returnable Transit Packaging (RTP) — addressing the organised e-commerce/auto logistics packaging opportunity worth Rs. 8,000–10,000 Cr[^ar-fy25]. @home has turned profitable and is targeting 8–10% store-level margins[^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Festive season demand boost for furniture and mattresses in H2FY27, (ii) Pre-buying ahead of potential polymer duty changes (BIS norms), (iii) Margin tailwind from declining PP/HDPE prices (down ~8–10% YoY), (iv) Capacity expansion at new Sangli and Halol plants coming online in Q3FY27, (v) Likely re-rating as FII/DII holdings inch higher from current 8.75%/5.84%[^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Subdued real estate and discretionary consumption slowdown directly impacts furniture and @home retail (~25–30% of revenue); a prolonged slowdown could compress margins by 150–200bps and keep ROCE trapped at sub-5% levels, limiting re-rating despite cheap valuations[^ar-fy25][^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business:** Nilkamal operates a portfolio-led plastic products platform spanning moulded furniture, material handling (crates/pallots/bins), mattresses, @home retail (home furniture & interiors), uPVC doors & windows, and Bubble Guard protective packaging[^ar-fy25].

- **Revenue mix (FY26E):**
  - Material Handling (crates, pallets, bins): ~52% — caters to FMCG, auto, e-comm, pharma.
  - Moulded Furniture: ~22% — institutional + retail.
  - Mattresses: ~6% — growing 20%+ YoY.
  - @home (Retail): ~10% — premium home interiors and furniture.
  - Others (Bubble Guard, uPVC, mattresses): ~10%[^ip-latest].

- **Key OEM/Customers:** Maruti, Tata Motors, M&M, Hyundai (auto crates/pallets); Hindustan Unilever, ITC, Nestlé, Amul (FMCG crates); Amazon, Flipkart, Delhivery (e-commerce RTP); leading real estate developers for uPVC[^ar-fy25].

- **Subsidiary structure:** Listed parent Nilkamal Ltd houses the manufacturing businesses; @home is a wholly-owned subsidiary (retail); Nilkamal Crates & Containers, Niko Industries, and Sri Raghavendra Industries are key step-downs[^ar-fy25].

- **Manufacturing footprint:** 12+ plants across Maharashtra (Mumbai, Sangli, Halol), Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Karnataka, and Uttarakhand — combined installed capacity of ~1.5 lakh MTPA[^ip-latest].

- **Promoter & group context:** Founded 1981 by Vamanrai Parekh; currently led by **Manish V. Parekh (Managing Director)** and **Nikhil V. Parekh (Joint MD)**, second-generation promoters with deep polymer industry expertise. The Parekh family holds 70.00% — a stable, long-term oriented ownership structure[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **TAM:** Indian plastic products industry is ~Rs. 3.5–4 lakh Cr (USD 42–48 bn), growing at 10–12% CAGR; material handling & RTP sub-segment is Rs. 18,000–20,000 Cr; organised moulded furniture Rs. 6,000 Cr; mattresses Rs. 25,000 Cr (organised only ~Rs. 5,000 Cr)[^ip-latest].

- **Policy tailwinds:** (a) PLI scheme for plastic processing & auto components supports capex, (b) PM Awas Yojana–Urban drives housing/demand for furniture and uPVC, (c) BIS quality standards for plastic crates favour organised players like Nilkamal vs. unorganised, (d) Make-in-India pushing RTP localisation for e-commerce supply chains[^ar-fy25].

- **Competitive moat analysis:**
  - **Brand & distribution (Strong):** Pan-India dealer network of 1,200+ plus institutional direct sales[^ip-latest].
  - **Scale & cost leadership (Strong):** Largest polymer offtaker in furniture category, backward integration into moulds[^ar-fy25].
  - **Switching costs (Moderate):** Material handling crates have moderate lock-in via design customisation but not contractual exclusivity[^vp-thread].
  - **Pricing power (Moderate):** Can pass on 60–70% of RM cost moves but lags in retail furniture vs. unorganised[^cc-transcript].
  - **Innovation/R&D (Moderate):** Stepping up in RTP and Bubble Guard but lags specialty players[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree & tenure:** Parekh family (Vamanrai → Manish & Nikhil) has led Nilkamal since 1981 — 45 years of continuous family stewardship. Both MD and JMD are polymer engineers from IIT/UDCT backgrounds with 20+ years in the business[^ar-fy25].

- **FCF deployment track record (3 years):** FCF has been volatile: FY23: Rs. (45) Cr (capex drag); FY24: Rs. 110 Cr; FY25: Rs. 175 Cr — improving with @home break-even and reduced capex intensity. Management has earmarked Rs. 150–180 Cr capex for FY27 (new plants + uPVC line)[^ip-latest].

- **Debt management:** Net debt/EBITDA at 1.4x (FY25), interest coverage of 4.2x — comfortable. CRISIL rating: **CRISIL A+/Stable** reaffirmed March 2026[^ar-fy25]. Working capital cycle stable at ~90 days (CCM days)[^vp-thread].

- **Dividend policy:** Consistent payout — dividend yield 1.11% (FY25), payout ratio ~24% (5-yr avg ~22%). Dividend per share rose from Rs. 12 (FY22) to Rs. 19 (FY25), signalling cash flow confidence[^ar-fy25].

- **Promoter pledging:** **Zero pledging** as of June 2026 — clean shareholding structure[^ip-latest].

- **Corporate governance flags:** No auditor changes in last 10 years (M/s B. K. Khare & Co.); no qualified audit opinions in the last 5 years; related-party transactions are immaterial (<0.5% of revenue)[^ar-fy25].

- **JVs / Partnerships:** Strategic technical collaboration with **Solfac GmbH (Germany)** for bubble guard technology; distribution tie-ups with global polymer players for raw material security[^ip-latest]. No material M&A in pipeline — focus remains organic.
