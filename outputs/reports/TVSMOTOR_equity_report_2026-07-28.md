## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership in premium motorcycles and 3Ws, with a credible EV franchise:** TVS Apache is India's #1 premium sports motorcycle with **>50% share in the 150–200cc performance segment**; the Ronin 225 (modern retro) and the BMW Motorrad co-developed Apache RTR 310 extend the premium moat into the 300cc+ segment.[^ip-latest] TVS King dominates the 3W passenger-carrier market and is the preferred platform for last-mile mobility in Tier 1/2 cities, while iQube has crossed **20,000 units/month run-rate** and is now the #2 retail EV scooter brand in India after Ola.[^cc-transcript]

- **Structural 100–150bps margin expansion story over FY25–FY27E:** Three drivers — (a) premium mix rising from 28% in FY24 to ~32% by FY27E with ASP growth of 6–7% CAGR (ahead of input cost inflation), (b) operating leverage from volume scaling at the newly commissioned Mysore 2W plant (~1 Cr unit capacity), and (c) the iQube EV business reaching **EBITDA break-even by FY27E** (currently ~Rs. 150 Cr EBIT loss annualised).[^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies reducing concentration risk:** Exports now contribute **~22% of revenue** across 60+ countries (Africa, LatAm, SE Asia, Europe via Norton); the recent **Norton (UK) and EGO Movement (Swiss e-bikes) acquisitions** open the developed-market premium segment; the 3W export business to Africa/LatAm is a 25%+ growth pocket; TVS Credit (captive finance, Rs. 17,000 Cr AUM) provides through-cycle earnings stability (~12% of consolidated PAT).[^ip-latest]

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (1) iQube launch in 10 new cities and a new product variant (expected higher-power iQube X) in Q3 FY27, (2) Norton Commando 961 relaunch in Europe — a halo brand monetisation event, (3) EGO Movement consolidation from Q2 FY27 with a possible EU e-cargo bike launch, (4) PLI accrual receipts of Rs. 200–300 Cr over FY26–FY28, (5) Q1 FY27 results preview indicates 18% YoY revenue growth and ~120bps margin expansion.[^cc-transcript][^ip-latest]

- **Biggest structural risk:** Competitive intensity in the EV 2W space — **Ola Electric, Bajaj Chetak, Hero Vida, Ather, Honda, and Suzuki** are all scaling fast with comparable specs at lower price points, and Ola's aggressive pricing (entry variants at Rs. 80k) is a structural ASP threat.[^vp-thread] Secondary risks: FAME-III redesign potentially shifting from demand-incentive to manufacturing-incentive (could compress EV margins 200–300bps), metro ICE-ban proposals by 2027–28, and lithium-cell / steel / aluminium cost volatility.[^ar-fy25]

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

TVS Motor Company Limited (NSE: TVSMOTOR, BSE: 532343) is the flagship two-wheeler and three-wheeler manufacturing arm of the Sundaram-Chettiar family-promoted TVS Group, founded in 1911 in Madurai and headquartered in Chennai.[^ar-fy25] It is the **3rd largest 2W manufacturer in India by volume** (~16–17% domestic market share) and a top-3 player in the 3W passenger carrier segment.

- **Core business model:** Design, manufacture, and sale of two-wheelers (motorcycles, scooters, mopeds, EVs) and three-wheelers through an India-wide dealer network of **3,000+ outlets**, with exports to 60+ countries.[^ip-latest] Ancillary revenue from spares (~10% of revenue), TVS Credit captive financing, and Sundaram Auto Components (aluminium die-casting).

- **Revenue split by division (FY25A, approximate):**
  - Motorcycles: ~55% of revenue (~Rs. 21,000 Cr) — Apache, Ronin, Star City+, Radeon, Sport, Platina
  - Scooters: ~22% (~Rs. 8,400 Cr) — Jupiter, Ntorq, iQube (EV)
  - Mopeds: ~5% (~Rs. 1,900 Cr) — XL100
  - Three-wheelers: ~5% (~Rs. 1,900 Cr) — King, King Duramax, King EV
  - Spares, services & others: ~13% (~Rs. 5,000 Cr) — parts, accessories, tooling income[^ar-fy25]

- **Key end customers per division:** Predominantly B2C retail across India, with institutional sales to fleet operators (3W) and export distributors in Africa, LatAm, SE Asia, and Europe (via Norton/EGO).[^cc-transcript] **No single customer concentration >5%** — a structural de-risking vs. CV peers and a resilience positive through cycles.

- **Subsidiary structure:**
  - **TVS Credit Services (100%)** — captive financing arm, ~Rs. 17,000 Cr AUM
  - **Sundaram Auto Components (100%)** — aluminium die-casting and machined components
  - **TVS Motor (Singapore) Pte Ltd** — holding company for Norton and other overseas assets
  - **Norton Motorcycles (UK, 100% acquired Apr 2020)** — premium superbike brand
  - **EGO Movement (Switzerland, ~74% acquired Apr 2025)** — e-bike subscription platform
  - **PT TVS Motor Company Indonesia** — 2W assembly for SE Asia exports
  - **TVS ASL (Netherlands)** — strategic sourcing office[^ip-latest]

- **Manufacturing footprint:**
  - **Hosur, Tamil Nadu** — flagship 2W + 3W plant, ~3.5 Cr units/yr capacity
  - **Mysore, Karnataka** — 2W expansion plant, ~1 Cr units/yr (commissioned FY24)
  - **Nalagarh, Himachal Pradesh** — 2W plant, ~50 lakh units/yr (tax-efficient location, excise benefits)
  - **Karambaim, Goa** — 3W plant (under relocation / expansion to Hosur Phase 2)
  - **Total installed capacity:** ~5 Cr two-wheelers + 1.5 lakh three-wheelers annually[^ip-latest]

- **Promoter background and group context:** The TVS Group was founded by T.V. Sundaram Iyengar in 1911 as a bus service in Madurai and expanded into auto distribution, finance, and manufacturing.[^ar-fy25] The 2nd generation (T.S. Srinivasan, T.V. Sundaram Iyengar Jr.) built the auto and finance businesses. The 3rd generation — **Venu Srinivasan (Chairman, ~40 years at TVS Motor helm)** and **Sudarshan Venu (Managing Director since 2018, MIT + Wharton educated)** — lead the group today. The broader TVS Group includes Sundaram Finance, Sundaram Fasteners, Brakes India, Lucas TVS, and India Motor Parts — a vertically integrated auto ecosystem that provides cost and supply-chain advantages to TVS Motor.[^ar-fy25]

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **TAM in Rs. Cr and USD:**
  - India 2W industry: ~2.0 Cr units in FY25, **~Rs. 1,45,000 Cr (~USD 17 Bn)**
  - Of which ICE 2W: ~1.9 Cr units, ~Rs. 1,32,000 Cr (~USD 15.5 Bn)
  - EV 2W (emerging): ~10 lakh units, ~Rs. 8,000 Cr (~USD 1.0 Bn) — growing at 40%+ CAGR
  - 3W (incl. e3W): ~8 lakh units, ~Rs. 12,000 Cr (~USD 1.4 Bn)
  - Global 2W addressable for TVS exports: ~USD 80–100 Bn across Africa, LatAm, SE Asia, Europe[^ip-latest]

- **Industry CAGR (FY24–FY30E):** ICE 2W at 6–8% CAGR (driven by premiumization, replacement demand, exports), **EV 2W at 40–50% CAGR** (penetration from <1% to ~15% by FY30), 3W at 8–10% CAGR.[^ip-latest] Total 2W + 3W industry CAGR: ~9–10% in INR terms.

- **Relevant policy tailwinds:**
  - **PLI scheme for auto sector** (Rs. 25,938 Cr outlay) — TVS qualifies under both Component Champion and Vehicle Champion categories; Rs. 200–300 Cr of accrual receipts expected over FY26–FY28.[^cc-transcript]
  - **FAME-II** (Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of Electric Vehicles, ~Rs. 10,000 Cr outlay) — TVS iQube benefits from per-vehicle subsidy of Rs. 10,000/unit. FAME-III under consultation and may shift to manufacturing-linked incentive.
  - **State EV subsidies** — Karnataka and Tamil Nadu offer road tax exemption, registration waiver, and per-vehicle subsidy (Rs. 5,000–10,000/unit).
  - **AIS 156 amendment for battery safety** (Apr 2023) — TVS compliant, a competitive moat vs. unorganised EV players.
  - **BS-VII norms** likely from FY28 — drives R&D investment but is a level-playing-field event.
  - **PM E-Drive scheme** (~Rs. 2,000 Cr for EV charging infrastructure) — supports charging ecosystem scaling.[^ip-latest]

- **Competitive moat analysis (Strong / Moderate / Weak with reasoning):**
  - **Brand strength in performance bikes:** **Strong.** TVS Apache is a 20+ year franchise with deep aftermarket, racing DNA, and BMW Motorrad co-developed technology. Halo effect drives consideration across the portfolio; Ntorq 125 is the #1 125cc scooter by sales.[^ip-latest]
  - **Scale economies:** **Strong.** 5 Cr+ units/yr capacity makes TVS the lowest-cost-per-unit producer in India on a fixed-cost allocation basis, comparable to Hero and Bajaj.
  - **Distribution depth:** **Strong.** 3,000+ dealer outlets across India with rural penetration via the TVS Credit captive finance arm — a key differentiator vs. EV-pure-plays (Ola, Ather).
  - **R&D capability:** **Moderate-to-Strong.** R&D spend ~3% of revenue (~Rs. 1,100 Cr in FY25), 200+ patents filed, in-house EV powertrain, and AI/ML capability via the Singapore R&D centre.[^ar-fy25]
  - **Pricing power in mass-market 2W:** **Moderate.** Mass-market commuter bikes (Platina, Star City) face price elasticity; price hikes of 1–2% typically meet resistance. Premium segment has stronger pricing power (3–5% hikes absorbed).
  - **EV technology & ecosystem:** **Moderate.** iQube is a credible product but lags Ola/Ather in software/connected features; charging network needs scale-up. Norton and EGO provide technology optionality.
  - **Switching costs for end customers:** **Weak–Moderate.** 2W is largely a discretionary purchase; brand loyalty matters but switching is common — TVS counters with finance tie-ups, exchange programmes, and service quality.

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree and tenure:** The TVS Group's auto business is led by **Venu Srinivasan (Chairman, ~40 years at the helm, son of T.S. Srinivasan)** and **Sudarshan Venu (Managing Director since 2018, 4th generation, MIT and Wharton educated).**[^ar-fy25] Venu Srinivasan is widely respected in Indian industry for the transformation of TVS from a domestic commodity-player to a premium global brand — the 2005 BMW Motorrad partnership, 2020 Norton acquisition, and the aggressive 2022 EV pivot are key milestones. Sudarshan Venu drives the technology, EV, and international agenda and is viewed as a credible long-term successor.

- **FCF deployment track record (3 years):**
  - **FY23 FCF:** ~Rs. 1,400 Cr | Deployed: Capex Rs. 1,200 Cr (Hosur expansion, EV tooling) + Dividend Rs. 200 Cr

  - **Consistent re-investment bias:** ~80% of FCF deployed into growth capex, only 15–20% returned to shareholders via dividends. **No buybacks in the past 5 years — a flag given the strong cash generation profile and peer practices** (Hero and Bajaj have both been active on buybacks).

- **Debt management:**
  - Total debt (FY25 est.): ~Rs. 3,500 Cr; cash & equivalents ~Rs. 2,500 Cr → **Net debt ~Rs. 1,000 Cr**
  - **Net debt/EBITDA: ~0.25x** — very comfortable leverage
  - Interest coverage: >15x
  - **Credit ratings (all top-of-the-class, triple-A across agencies):**
    - **CRISIL AAA/Stable** (re-affirmed Nov 2024)

  - FY25 dividend: Rs. 8/share (~0.31% yield, Rs. 368 Cr payout, ~17% payout ratio)
  - **5-year average payout: 15–18% of PAT** — on the lower end of Indian auto peers (Hero Moto ~50%, Bajaj ~65%) reflecting re-investment philosophy

- **Promoter pledging status:** **Nil.** The Sundaram family holding of 52.21% is fully unencumbered — no shares pledged with any lender. This is a clean capital structure relative to several mid-cap Indian autos and a key governance positive.[^cc-transcript]

Rationale: access to UK engineering talent (Donington Hall facility), brand for developed markets, halo brand for global premium positioning. **Norton Commando 961 relaunch in 2026 is the key monetisation event** — pre-orders strong in UK and EU.
  - **EGO Movement (Switzerland, ~74% stake Apr 2025):** E-bike subscription platform in EU, ~5,000 active subscribers. Rationale: entry into EU micromobility (~USD 5 Bn addressable), B2B fleet model (Swiss Post, DPD are existing customers), early-stage but high-ROIC optionality. Q2 FY27 first full-quarter consolidation.
  - **TVS Credit (100% subsidiary):** Captive finance arm, ~Rs. 17,000 Cr AUM, ~2,800 branches. Rationale: financing 40%+ of TVS retail sales, drives rural penetration, contributes ~8% of consolidated revenue and ~12% of consolidated PAT.
  - **Strategic technology advisory with Future Shape (Tony Fadell):** Tech/product strategy advisory, not a financial investment — provides access to Silicon Valley product/UX expertise for iQube and connected-vehicle roadmap.[^ip-latest]
