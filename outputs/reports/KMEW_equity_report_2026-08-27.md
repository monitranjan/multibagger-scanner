### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

We initiate coverage on **Knowledge Marine & Engineering Works Ltd (KMEW)** with a **BUY** rating and a **12-month target price of Rs. 3,800** (28% upside from CMP of Rs. 2,969). The thesis rests on five pillars:

**1. Niche market leadership in a high-barrier-to-entry marine services franchise.**
KMEW is one of India's leading integrated dredging and marine services operators, with a fleet of 45+ vessels running at 100% utilization and ownership of "River Pearl 47" — India's largest and deepest self-propelled backhoe dredger (1,000 BHP, 4m–30m dredging depth, bucket capacity 1.75 m³–12 m³) [^ip-latest]. To put this in plain English, a "dredger" is essentially a giant floating vacuum cleaner/excavator that scoops sand, silt, and rock from the seabed of harbors, rivers, and shipping channels so that big cargo ships can navigate. KMEW's fleet includes some of the most sophisticated dredging equipment in the country, and new entrants find it nearly impossible to compete without spending Rs. 100–150 Cr on a single cutter-suction dredger and waiting 12–18 months for regulatory mobilization approvals [^cc-transcript]. The company has built an FY26 unexecuted order book of **Rs. 1,644.89 Cr** (up from Rs. 1,395.43 Cr the prior period), secured a fresh contract intake of **Rs. 1,075 Cr** during FY26, and counts marquee public-sector clients like **Jawaharlal Nehru Port Authority (JNPA), V.O. Chidambaranar Port Authority (VOCPA), Visakhapatnam Port Authority (VPA), Deendayal Port Authority (DPA), Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI), and Dredging Corporation of India (DCI)** [^ip-latest]. The India dredging market is projected to reach **USD 982 million by 2029 at an 8.4% CAGR** — more than double the 3.5% global CAGR — driven by Sagarmala, Maritime Vision 2030, and ongoing port-led infrastructure capex [^ip-latest]. KMEW is positioned as a "preferred partner" for technically intensive dredging and continues to win disproportionately larger and more complex projects vs. smaller fleet operators [^ip-latest].

**2. Structural margin story — Tonnage Tax + asset sweat + high-IPP contract structures.**
The single biggest structural margin driver is the company's adoption of the **Tonnage Tax regime from FY26**, which has effectively eliminated corporate income tax (a >90% reduction) on income derived from qualifying shipping activities (dredging, chartering, shipbuilding) [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript]. To explain this simply: Tonnage Tax is a special tax framework for shipping companies where instead of paying corporate tax of ~25% on profits, the government levies a tiny daily fee per "GT" (Gross Tonnage — a measure of vessel size). For asset-heavy marine operators like KMEW, this is a transformational tailwind because every additional rupee of operating profit drops almost fully to the bottom line. Combined with 100% fleet utilization, this drives **Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of ~64%** (on a project-mix tailwind) and a **normalized guided EBITDA margin of 35–40%** [^cc-transcript] — versus typical EPC / construction companies at 12–18% margins. Management has stated that even at 35–40% EBITDA, the Q1 64% is "not the sustained guidance" but reflects the "intersection of specialized assets, technical capability, and disciplined project execution" [^cc-transcript]. Importantly, Tonnage Tax savings are being **reinvested into fleet expansion and technology adoption** rather than flowing out as dividends, which compounds the long-term moat [^ip-latest].

**3. Diversification into three high-growth adjacencies.**
KMEW has matured from a single-vertical dredging contractor into a **three-pillar integrated marine services platform**: (i) **Dredging services** (72% of FY26 revenue, with planned entry into the large Trailing Suction Hopper Dredger (TSHD) segment) — think of TSHDs as the largest "vacuum trucks" of the sea, capable of sucking thousands of cubic meters of seabed material per cycle; (ii) **Chartering and hire of port ancillary crafts** (7% of FY26 revenue but the fastest cash-compounding segment, with 15-year-duration Green Tug charters totalling Rs. 692.80 Cr from VOCPA and VPA secured in FY26) — these long-tenure contracts behave like infrastructure "toll-road annuities" with predictable, inflation-indexed cash flows [^ip-latest]; and (iii) **Shipbuilding** (21% of FY26 revenue, growing rapidly) via the upcoming **Saphale shipyard** (20 acres, near the greenfield Vadhavan Port) and the existing **Knowledge Shipyard** subsidiary [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript]. Shipbuilding pre-subsidy margins are guided at 15–20%, but post-subsidy (under the Shipbuilding Subsidy Scheme) margins are expected to expand to "easily beyond 35%" — bringing the blended EBITDA margin into a structurally higher tier from FY28E onward [^cc-transcript]. Management also plans a future **onshore marine infrastructure** line ("Water to Land") for shore-side vessel repair and jetty construction, which diversifies the asset base away from cyclical vessel deployment [^ar-fy25].

**4. Near-term catalysts over the next 6–12 months.**
The next 6–12 months are densely packed with identifiable catalysts that can drive multiple expansion: (a) **Q1 FY27 strong print reset expectations** — revenue grew 138% YoY to Rs. 115.41 Cr and PAT grew 466% YoY to Rs. 62.75 Cr, prompting management to upgrade FY27 revenue growth guidance from 30–40% to "northwards of 60% YoY" [^cc-transcript]; (b) **Saphale Shipyard Phase 1 commissioning by end-FY27**, which unlocks incremental shipbuilding revenue from Q4 FY27 onwards and is a significant milestone tracked by investor forums [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread]; (c) **Bid pipeline of Rs. 3,500+ Cr** awaiting conversion, which keeps the order book trajectory visible [^cc-transcript]; (d) **Continued fleet capex execution** — Rs. 1,000 Cr over the next 1.5 years including Rs. 200–250 Cr for green tugs/vessels, Rs. 450 Cr for dredging assets, and the balance for shipyard/shipbuilding infrastructure [^cc-transcript]; (e) **Long-term revenue target of Rs. 1,000 Cr by FY29** — a 4-year CAGR of ~58% from FY26 base, providing management with skin in the game for execution [^cc-transcript]; (f) **Equity build-up** — Rs. 250 Cr preferential issue and Rs. 100 Cr block deal validated KMEW's strategy with marquee institutional sponsorship (360 ONE, FLC Invesco, Bank of India Mutual Fund, SBI Funds) [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread]; (g) **Stock split** (Rs. 5 → Rs. 1 face value, effective Aug 2026) — although not value-creating by itself, this broadens retail participation and improves intraday liquidity [^ar-fy25].

**5. Biggest structural risk — execution intensity and governance overhang.**
The biggest risk to our BUY thesis is **execution risk at scale**: the company is attempting to 4x revenue to Rs. 1,000 Cr by FY29 while deploying Rs. 1,000 Cr of capex over 18 months — a level of operational aggression that leaves little margin for slippage. The second pillar is the **contingent liability pile of Rs. 1,121.6 Cr against a net worth of Rs. 2,198.9 Cr (FY25)** — equivalent to **51% of net worth**, primarily comprising bank guarantees and corporate guarantees extended to subsidiaries [^ar-fy25]. While these are operational (performance bonds for dredging contracts) rather than debt-funded, they represent contingent cash calls if invoked. The third risk is **promoter stake reduction** from 67.09% (Sep 2023) to 49.99% (Aug 2026), including a 2.10% bulk-deal sale in June 2026 (Kanak Kewalramani sold 345,620 shares; Dinesh Kewalramani sold 167,200 shares) [^vp-thread]. The good news is that **FII holdings rose from 0.74% (Sep 2025) to 12.64% (Aug 2026) and DII from 0.15% to 3.90%**, signalling institutional endorsement of the strategy despite the promoter trim [^vp-thread]. The fourth risk is **deteriorating working capital** — receivable days extended from ~86 (FY24) to ~101 (FY25), even as revenue grew, suggesting some softness in client collections (particularly government PSUs) that needs to be monitored [^ar-fy25]. The fifth risk is **competitive intensity** — although management asserts "very little to no competition" in technically complex dredging today, the longer the GTTP and Saphale shipyard story plays out, the more likely that well-capitalized peers (Adani, JSW Infra, foreign players) will enter the niche [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model — the "asset-right" marine services franchise.**
KMEW runs an integrated marine services business organized around three verticals, all anchored on ownership and deployment of specialized assets. To a beginner investor, the cleanest mental model is: KMEW **owns expensive, hard-to-build marine equipment** (dredgers, tugs, work boats, accommodation boats), then **rents these out to government and PSU clients** on multi-year contracts. This is a "capital-intensive but high-barrier" model — like a fleet of commercial aircraft — where the moat comes from the physical asset, the operational expertise, and the regulatory certifications required to operate these vessels safely in Indian waters [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].

**Vertical 1 — Dredging Services (72% of FY26 revenue, Rs. 184.31 Cr segment contract value).**
Dredging involves removing accumulated silt, sand, and sometimes hard rock from the seabed of harbors, shipping channels, river stretches, and inland waterways. There are two main sub-types: (a) **Capital dredging** — initial deepening of harbors or creation of new berths/approach channels, where the depth may need to increase from 12m to 18m to accommodate new-generation container ships (think of these as "supertankers" of the global shipping fleet); and (b) **Maintenance dredging** — recurring removal of silt that naturally accumulates over 12–18 month cycles (every harbor in the world needs this). KMEW operates a diversified fleet including **Cutter Suction Dredgers (CSDs)** — vessels that use a giant rotating blade at the seabed to break up material before suctioning it through a pipe; **Backhoe Dredgers** — essentially floating excavators with a long arm and bucket; and **Trailing Suction Hopper Dredgers (TSHDs)** — the "vacuum trucks" of the sea that drag a suction head along the seabed while moving, collecting thousands of cubic meters of slurry into an onboard hold before sailing to a disposal site. Key customers include major port trusts (JNPA, VPA, VOCPA, DPA) and **Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI)** for river dredging on National Waterway-1 (the Ganga) [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].

**Vertical 2 — Chartering & Hire of Port Ancillary Crafts (7% of FY26 revenue, Rs. 18.71 Cr segment contract value, but the highest-growth segment in terms of contracted value).**
This vertical supplies **specialized boats** that ports need for safe operations: **Pilot Boats** that ferry harbor pilots to incoming cargo ships; **Mooring Boats** that help berth large vessels; **Speed Patrol Boats** for port security; **Service Boats** for general logistics; and **Tugs** (60-ton Bollard Pull — "Bollard Pull" is a measure of a tug's pulling power, i.e. how hard it can pull a large ship) including **Conventional Tugs**, **Hybrid Tugs**, and **Green Tugs compliant with the Green Tug Transition Program (GTTP)** [^ip-latest]. To put this in plain English: imagine every major port is like a busy airport that needs lots of small vehicles (follow-me cars, baggage tractors, pushback tugs) for the giant aircraft to land safely. KMEW is increasingly the supplier of the "green/electric" versions of these small service vehicles. The segment's growth was turbocharged by the **two Green Tug charter wins in FY26 — Rs. 367.09 Cr from VOCPA and Rs. 325.70 Cr from VPA — both 15-year contracts**, providing long-duration visible recurring revenue [^ip-latest].

**Vertical 3 — Shipbuilding (21% of FY26 revenue, Rs. 53.26 Cr segment contract value, but the segment with the most operational expansion potential).**
This vertical designs and constructs small and specialized vessels in-house, including **dredgers, work boats, accommodation boats, and green tugs** [^ip-latest]. The newest strategic bet is the **Saphale shipyard** — a 20-acre greenfield facility near the upcoming **Vadhavan Port** (one of India's largest greenfield mega-ports), with Phase 1 scheduled for commissioning by end-FY27 [^cc-transcript]. Existing in-house capacity includes the recently acquired **Knowledge Shipyard** subsidiary, focused on commercial shipbuilding and repairing [^ip-latest]. The **Order Book composition as of Mar 2026** shows Rs. 233.06 Cr in Shipbuilding Contracts with Rs. 183.56 Cr unexecuted, indicating the segment is in active execution phase [^ip-latest].

**Geographic mix (FY25 consolidated).**
India 58.9%, Bahrain 22.8% (largely sand mining), Myanmar 18.3% [^ar-fy25]. The international contribution reflects legacy contracts; FY27 management focus is clearly India-led (75%+ of new wins are Indian), with selective overseas exposure only where payment terms are comparable to domestic PSUs [^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary structure.**
KMEW has recently acquired (FY25) **Kamal Marine** for shipbuilding capability and **Knowledge Shipyard** for in-house commercial shipbuilding/repair [^ar-fy25]. The consolidated structure also includes operating SPVs for specific long-term contracts (e.g., dedicated subsidiaries for the VOCPA and VPA green tug 15-year charters), enabling clean ring-fencing of contract-level economics and ring-fenced capex.

**Manufacturing footprint / physical infrastructure.**
- **Fleet:** 45+ vessels (expanded from 16 in FY24 to 40 by FY25-end; +12.5% incremental to reach 45+ by FY26) with 100% utilization, making KMEW one of the largest private Indian-fleet operators [^ar-fy25] [^ip-latest].
- **Saphale Shipyard:** 20 acres (15 newly acquired + 5 existing) — Phase 1 commissioning end-FY27; located near Vadhavan Port to capture the greenfield mega-port's shipbuilding opportunity [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].
- **Knowledge Shipyard:** Existing acquired facility for commercial shipbuilding and repair [^ip-latest].
- **Gross block of PPE** increased **74% YoY to Rs. 23,686 Lakhs** in FY25, with **Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP) of Rs. 5,015 Lakhs** — vessels under construction; the company's capex profile is clearly accelerating, not decelerating [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter & group context.**
KMEW is led by the **Kewalramani family** — a three-generation maritime business family. The promoter group is held through **Kanak Sujay Kewalramani (MD & CFO, ~35.7% pre-dilution), Pinkesh Kewalramani (~12.8%), and Saurabh Mukesh Daswani (Whole-time Director, ~12.8%)** [^ar-fy25]. CEO **Sujay Mohanlal Kewalramani** is the operational head and primary spokesperson on concalls [^cc-transcript]. The "**Knowledge**" group identity extends across the company's marine services, shipbuilding, and offshore contracting ventures, with KMEW serving as the listed flagship. Tenure: the promoter family has been operating in marine services for over a decade, with FY25 marking "a decade of transformation" per the Annual Report cover letter [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**India's dredging market — TAM, growth, and policy tailwinds.**
India's dredging market is a **USD 982 million opportunity by 2029**, expanding at an **8.4% CAGR** — more than 2x the **global CAGR of 3.5%** [^ip-latest]. The fast growth is driven by four policy-and-demand tailwinds that simultaneously increase the addressable work-pool for KMEW:

1. **Sagarmala Programme (USD 80+ Bn committed):** Modernization of existing ports, creation of new berths, deepening of approach channels — every capex project on this list requires a dredging contract. KMEW has bagged Rock Dredging at JNPA (Rs. 49.49 Cr) and Maintenance Dredging at multiple ports in FY26 alone [^ip-latest].
2. **Maritime Vision 2030:** Aspirational targets of 3 Bn+ MT port capacity, 10,000+ MT per ship average, and India becoming a Top-5 ship-repair hub. This drives both dredging demand and ancillary fleet demand [^ar-fy25].
3. **National Logistics Policy + Inland Waterways Expansion:** IWAI's development of National Waterway-1 (Ganga), NW-2 (Brahmaputra), and NW-3/4/5 has created a parallel "river dredging" market. KMEW holds active IWAI contracts worth Rs. 127.12 Cr (CSDs & Boats) and Rs. 68.38 Cr (Work & Accommodation Boats) [^ip-latest].
4. **Green Tug Transition Program (GTTP, 2024–2040):** A 5-phase program to convert **60% of the operational tug fleet at all major ports to Green Tugs** (LNG/electric/hybrid). This is essentially a multi-decade ship-replacement program. KMEW has secured 2 of the early green tug contracts (VOCPA + VPA totalling Rs. 692.80 Cr) and management has indicated this is just the first wave [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].

**Projected 2029 service mix (within India's USD 982M dredging market):** Capital Dredging 54%, Maintenance Dredging 25%, Others (sand mining, environmental dredging) 21% [^ip-latest]. KMEW is positioned across all three sub-segments but has the strongest franchise in **capital and rock dredging** — the highest-margin, technically most-demanding sub-categories.

**Secondary addresses: Green Shipping, Inland Waterways, Offshore Mining.**
Beyond core dredging, KMEW is exposed to the **Shipbuilding Subsidy Scheme** (post-subsidy margins >35% guided), the **"Harit Nauka" mission** for green marine assets (subsidized hybrid ferries), and the **Samudra Manthan** deep-sea mining programme (still in "latent stage," expected to require meaningful KMEW deployment in 2–3 years) [^cc-transcript]. The cumulative "potential addressable market" across these adjacencies is materially larger than the dredging-only TAM.

**Competitive moat analysis.**
To a beginner investor, "competitive moat" simply means "what stops competitors from taking KMEW's business." KMEW's moat has four components:

**Debt management and credit profile.**

Total debt post-raise is expected to be manageable well below 1.0x D/E — well within prudent leverage thresholds for asset-heavy infrastructure services companies.

**Dividend policy — zero payout, growth reinvestment.**
KMEW has paid **zero dividends** across the past 5 years (confirmed by Div Yield of 0.00%). Management's clear articulated philosophy: Tonnage Tax savings and operating cash flow are redirected into **fleet expansion, Saphale shipyard build-out, and green tug capex** rather than shareholder distribution [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript]. To a beginner investor: this means **you don't get a "yield" from owning KMEW** — the return is entirely **capital appreciation** from growth in earnings and book value. This is appropriate at the current growth stage but may become a sentiment risk if the Rs. 1,000 Cr FY29 revenue target is achieved and free cash remains unutilized.

The income statement tells us three things: (1) **how much money came in the door**, (2) **how much profit remained after paying for fuel, crew, and loan interest**, and (3) **how much the bottom-line per share grew**.

Revenue growth is the headline story. After a brief dip in FY24 (when one major project ended and the next hadn't started), revenue is now compounding at 23% (FY25), 27% (FY26), and we project 60% in FY27. The Q1 FY27 number (Rs. 115 Cr in just three months) is a clear proof point — the company's new dredgers are working around the clock on premium projects like the JNPA capital rock dredging job.

The **margin expansion** is the second key story. EBITDA margin went from 30% (FY24) to 38% (FY26), and we project 46% in FY27. That sounds like a huge jump, but it's because of two specific reasons: (a) Q1 FY27 had an unusually favourable project mix (high-value capital dredging contracts using the company's best dredger), and (b) the **Tonnage Tax regime** (a special tax for shipping companies) now means almost no corporate tax — which directly inflates post-tax profits without changing pre-tax profits. The **Tonnage Tax** is a government incentive for owning ships in India; instead of paying 25% tax on profits, the company pays a tiny tonnage-based fee. Think of it as the government's way of subsidising domestic shipbuilding.

**PAT (Profit After Tax) growth of 97% in FY27E** is the third story — and it's where "operating leverage" kicks in. **Operating leverage** is a fancy term meaning: when revenue goes up, certain costs (like office rent, salaried staff) stay roughly the same, so each extra rupee of revenue drops more to the bottom line. Q1 FY27 already showed this: revenue grew 138%, but PAT grew 466% — meaning every extra Rs. 100 of revenue delivered ~Rs. 40 of additional profit, far more than the usual Rs. 12-15.

The **EPS growth** from Rs. 32 (FY26) to Rs. 64 (FY27E) and Rs. 85 (FY28E) is what shareholders ultimately care about — this is the per-share slice of the company's profit pie.

The **0% dividend payout** is deliberate: the company is reinvesting every rupee of profit into buying more vessels and building the Saphale shipyard. The Rs. 1,000 Cr capex programme is the bridge from today's ~Rs. 256 Cr revenue to the targeted Rs. 1,000 Cr by FY29.

The most important number here is **Free Cash Flow (FCF) = CFO + CFI** — this is the cash left over after the company has paid for all its new investments. In FY24, FY25, and FY26, FCF was **negative** (Rs. -2 Cr, -34 Cr, -69 Cr respectively) because the company was busy buying dredgers and tugs. **CFO/EBITDA ratio** of 60% in FY26 and 70% in our projections is a strong indicator of **earnings quality** — it means 60-70 paise of every Rs. 100 of reported profit actually converted to cash in the bank. Anything above 70% is excellent for a capital-intensive business.

**ROCE (Return on Capital Employed)** is a critical ratio for asset-heavy businesses. It measures how much profit the company generates per Rs. 100 of capital tied up in the business. The historical 55% (FY24) and 23-25% (FY25-26) reflect the deployment cycle — FY24 had a small asset base; FY25-26 saw a surge in CWIP (vessels under construction) that hadn't yet started earning. **ROE (Return on Equity)** is similar but only considers the shareholders' money (not borrowed funds). At 24-25% in FY27E-28E, this is comfortably above the company's cost of equity (~14% for an Indian small-cap), meaning the company is creating genuine value for shareholders.

The company is paid slowly (high DSO), holds almost no inventory (5 days — these are services, not goods), and pays its suppliers in about 40 days. The CCC of 75 days in FY27E and 55 days in FY28E is healthy for the dredging industry, where government counterparties are the norm.
