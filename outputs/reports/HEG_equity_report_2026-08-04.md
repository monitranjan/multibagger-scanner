### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership & moat**: HEG operates the world's largest single-location graphite electrode (GE) plant at Mandideep, Madhya Pradesh, with capacity being expanded from ~85,000 MT to ~100,000 MT — a scale unmatched by any global peer[^ip-latest]. The technology stack for UHP-grade electrode manufacturing is highly specialised, with the last major technology shift occurring nearly 25 years ago, creating a natural entry barrier[^vp-thread]. HEG is one of only 6–7 qualified global suppliers for UHP-grade electrodes used in EAF steelmaking[^vp-thread].
- **Structural margin story**: Needle coke, which constitutes ~40–50% of GE production cost, is in severe global supply constraint because of the lithium-ion battery industry absorbing incremental supply, pushing prices up ~6x in recent cycles[^vp-thread]. Vertically integrated producers with secured needle coke offtake enjoy an estimated 800–1,200 bps EBITDA margin advantage over non-integrated peers, a structural wedge that we believe persists for at least 2–3 years[^vp-thread]. Captive power at Mandideep adds another 200–300 bps cushion during energy price spikes[^ip-latest].
- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies**: HEG is actively seeding specialty graphite production targeting EV battery anodes, semiconductor applications, and fuel cell components — markets growing at 15–18% CAGR through FY28E[^ip-latest]. We model specialty graphite contributing 4–5% of revenue in FY26E rising to 12–15% by FY28E, materially de-risking the cyclical core[^ip-latest]. Adjacent R&D is also exploring captive needle coke sourcing through tie-ups, which could close the cost gap further[^ip-latest].
- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months)**: (i) China transitioning from net exporter to net importer of graphite electrodes due to environmental curbs on needle coke calcination removes ~120,000 MT from global supply[^vp-thread]; (ii) Global EAF capacity additions — 57 new Chinese EAF plants (60–70 MT) plus India, MENA, and US greenfield projects — imply incremental GE demand of ~150,000 MT over 24 months[^vp-thread]; (iii) commissioning of the Mandideep 100,000 MT expansion expected to flow through volumes in H2FY27E[^ip-latest]; (iv) potential announcement of specialty graphite capacity (Phase 2) by Q3FY27E[^ip-latest].
- **Biggest structural risk**: GE pricing remains highly correlated to global EAF steel spreads and Chinese export behaviour[^vp-thread]. A 10% correction in realisations from current ~$7,000–8,000/MT levels toward a trough of ~$4,500/MT could compress FY27E EPS by 40–50%, taking HEG back to a Rs. 350–400 share price level[^vp-thread]. Any reversal in Chinese environmental enforcement or restart of idle Chinese capacity remains the single largest swing factor for our thesis[^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model**: HEG Limited (formerly Hindustan Electro Graphite) is a single-product graphite electrode manufacturer producing UHP, HP, and RP grade electrodes used primarily in Electric Arc Furnace (EAF) steelmaking[^ip-latest]. The company operates a fully integrated model from calcination to electrode machining, with captive power for energy security[^ip-latest]. Revenue is concentrated in electrodes with specialty graphite and by-product sales forming the residual[^ar-fy25].
- **Revenue split by division (FY25)**:
  - Graphite Electrodes: ~93–95% of revenue
  - Specialty Graphite (HEG Graphite): ~3–5% of revenue
  - Power / Others (captive utilisation sales): ~1–2% of revenue[^ip-latest]
- **Key OEM / end customers by division**:
  - Graphite Electrodes: Domestic EAF steelmakers (JSW Steel, SAIL-EAF route, Tata Steel BOF-EAF conversions); exports to Middle East (Emirates Steel, Hadeed), Europe (ArcelorMittal EAF), Americas (Nucor, Steel Dynamics), and Southeast Asia[^ip-latest].
  - Specialty Graphite: Battery cell manufacturers, semiconductor OEMs, fuel cell component fabricators (early stage)[^ip-latest].
- **Subsidiary structure**:
  - HEG Graphite — specialty graphite operating division[^ar-fy25]
  - Bhilwara Energy Ltd (BEL) — group-level power subsidiary (not consolidated for HEG standalone electrode ops)[^ar-fy25]
  - Various step-down overseas marketing/SPV entities for export distribution[^ar-fy25]
- **Manufacturing footprint**:
  - Mandideep, Madhya Pradesh — single mega-plant, world's largest single-location GE facility, capacity being expanded from 85,000 MT to 100,000 MT[^ip-latest]
  - Captive power plant at Mandideep (~50–60 MW) — ensures energy cost insulation[^ip-latest]
  - R&D centre co-located at Mandideep for needle coke and specialty graphite work[^ip-latest]
- **Promoter background & group context**: HEG is part of the LNJ Bhilwara Group promoted by the late Lakshmi Niwas Jhunjhunwala. The group has a 5-decade operating track record spanning power (Bhilwara Energy), textiles (RSWM), mining, and electrodes[^ar-fy25]. The promoter family continues to control the company with 59.33% holding, with second-generation involvement through Ravi Jhunjhunwala and family members[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **TAM & growth**: Global graphite electrode market is estimated at ~$8–10 Bn (~Rs. 65,000–82,000 Cr) as of FY25, expected to grow at 5–7% CAGR through FY28E driven by EAF capacity additions globally[^ip-latest]. Indian GE market is ~Rs. 4,000–5,000 Cr, with India targeting 40–45% EAF route share by FY30 vs ~25% currently under the National Steel Policy — translating to incremental domestic GE demand of 80,000–100,000 MT over 5 years[^ip-latest]. USD-denominated realisations of UHP electrodes currently trade at $7,000–8,000/MT vs a long-term mid-cycle band of $4,500–6,000/MT.
- **Policy tailwinds**:
  - National Steel Policy 2017 — 300 MT capacity target by FY30, with EAF route incentivised[^ip-latest]
  - PLI Scheme for Specialty Steel — supports domestic value chain including EAF steelmakers[^ip-latest]
  - Global carbon border adjustment mechanisms (CBAM, EU) favour EAF over blast furnace route, structurally lifting long-term GE demand[^vp-thread]
  - FAME/RE targets indirectly support EAF-based steel for EV and renewable infra supply chains[^ip-latest]
- **Competitive moat analysis**:
  - **Switching costs — STRONG**: EAF steelmakers qualify graphite electrodes over 6–12 months with extensive OEM approval cycles; once qualified, supply continuity is prioritised and switching is rare[^vp-thread].
  - **Scale economics — STRONG**: HEG's 100,000 MT single-site capacity is the global benchmark; new entrants would need 4–5 years and ~Rs. 2,000 Cr capex to reach economic scale, with limited access to needle coke[^vp-thread].
  - **Pricing power — MODERATE**: GE trade as a global commodity but UHP grades command a 5–8% brand premium for established suppliers; pricing tracks needle coke spreads with a lag[^vp-thread].
  - **Raw material access — STRONG for incumbents**: Needle coke supply is concentrated and constrained; HEG's long-term offtake relationships with Phillips 66, Petrocokes Japan, and Indian Oil provide structural moat vs new entrants[^ip-latest].
