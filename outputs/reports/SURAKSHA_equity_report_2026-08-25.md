### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Suraksha Diagnostic is the dominant organised player in Eastern India’s diagnostic services market, deriving 95.5% of FY26 revenue from West Bengal and operating 55+ centres (hubs and spokes) with 160+ polyclinic chambers and 1,000+ empanelled doctors as of 30 Jun 2026 [^ip-latest]. A “moat” in simple terms is a durable competitive advantage that protects a company’s profits from rivals—like a castle surrounded by a protective ditch. Here the moat is built on high switching costs: patients and local physicians develop trust in the brand’s NABL/CAP‑accredited labs and integrated consultation model, making them reluctant to move to a new lab [^ip-latest]. Management explicitly confirmed it is “the largest organized player in East” when asked by analysts [^cc-transcript]. This regional scale creates a self‑reinforcing network effect: more doctors join, more patients come, and fixed costs get spread over larger volumes.

- **Structural margin story:** The company’s EBITDA margin (a measure of operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation—think of it as the core profitability of the lab network before financing costs) expanded from 31.8% in FY26 to 36.0% in Q1 FY27, a jump of ~420 basis points (bps; 1 bps = 0.01%, so 420 bps = 4.2 percentage points) YoY [^ip-latest]. Mature centres (older than 2 years) now deliver 40.9% EBITDA margin, while younger centres turned profitable at 6.5% versus –5.5% earlier [^cc-transcript]. The structural lever is operating leverage: as footfall rises, the heavy fixed costs of MRI machines and lab equipment are shared across more tests, lifting margins without price hikes [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, shifting biochemistry reagents from Siemens to Roche negotiated a better deal, lowering cost of goods sold (COGS—the direct cost of performing tests) [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Suraksha has launched Suraksha Genomics (Jul 2025), a dedicated vertical for advanced genetic/molecular diagnostics with prenatal, oncology and pharmacogenomics panels [^ip-latest]. Genomics revenue was ₹13.7 Mn in Q1 FY27, up 136% YoY, with five consecutive quarters of ~24% sequential growth [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. It also introduced a blood test for Alzheimer’s and an AI project with CSIR for early detection from brain MRI [^cc-transcript]. The acquisition of Fetomat Wellness (63% stake) adds foetal medicine capabilities [^ar-fy25]. These adjacencies address under‑penetrated East India genomics market and lift average revenue per patient (currently ₹2,321) via esoteric, higher‑value tests [^ip-latest].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Network expansion: 4 hubs & 7 spokes planned for remainder of FY27 (total 100 centres by FY28), including first Jharkhand hub at Dhanbad and Tripura entry [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. (ii) Capex of ₹70–80 Cr in FY27 to commission 3T MRI hubs (Mukundapur, Berhampur) driving radiology mix [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. (iii) CGHS rate increase from mid‑Oct ’25 gives ~1.5% top‑line benefit on corporate/PSU contribution of ~15% [^cc-transcript]. (iv) Genexus equipment (only one in Eastern India) cuts oncology genomic turnaround from 14‑17 days to 48 hours, boosting B2B volumes [^cc-transcript]. (v) Wellness contribution ~4‑5% and B2B ramp from 6.9% to higher share provides incremental scale [^cc-transcript][^ar-fy25].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Extreme geographic concentration—95.5% revenue from West Bengal exposes the model to state‑specific shocks: doctors’ strikes, regulatory changes, or geopolitical spillover from Bangladesh [^ar-fy25][^ip-latest]. Intense competition from national chains (Dr Lal, Metropolis) and digital platforms could erode pricing power in B2C [^ar-fy25]. If expansion outside WB lags or new centres take longer than 8‑9 months to break even at hub level, the premium valuation (P/E 46x) could compress [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Suraksha Diagnostic operates an integrated diagnostics model—meaning it offers a one‑stop shop for pathology (blood/urine/tissue tests), radiology (X‑ray, MRI, CT scans) and medical consultation (doctors’ chambers) under a single brand “Suraksha” [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. For a beginner: pathology is the lab work that analyses samples; radiology uses imaging machines to see inside the body; consultation is when a physician interprets results. The core revenue engine is a “hub‑and‑spoke” network: large hubs house expensive machines (e.g., 3T MRI) and comprehensive labs, while smaller spokes collect samples and do basic tests, sending complex work to hubs [^cc-transcript].

Revenue split by division (% of FY26 revenue): Pathology 50%, Radiology 44%, Other (doctor consultancy + pharmacy) 6% [^ip-latest]. Channel mix is 91% B2C (direct patients walk in or referred by empanelled doctors) and 9% B2B/institutional (CGHS, corporate health checks, hospital partnerships) [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. Key end customers per division: Pathology & Radiology serve individual patients (B2C) and institutions like CGHS (Central Government Health Scheme) and PSUs (~15% of revenue) [^cc-transcript]; Genomics serves both B2C (80%) and B2B (20%) referring oncologists and prenatal clinics [^cc-transcript].

Subsidiary structure: Standalone entity plus subsidiary Fetomat Wellness Pvt Ltd (63% stake post‑Mar 2025) for foetal medicine/genomics [^ar-fy25]; Suraksha Genomics is a vertical, not separate legal entity. Manufacturing/operational footprint: 55+ centres across West Bengal (Greater Kolkata 67% of revenue, Rest of Bengal 29%), Assam 3%, Bihar 1%, plus new Jharkhand, Tripura, Meghalaya presence [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. Plants are not relevant; instead “centres” are the assets. Promoter background: Dr Somnath Chatterjee (Chairman & JMD) and Ritu Mittal (JMD & CEO) lead the Nirmal‑Minda‑style group? Actually it’s the Chatterjee/Mittal promoter group with decades in Eastern healthcare [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian diagnostics industry (FY24E) is fragmented: standalone labs 45–49% of total, hospital‑based 13–17%, and chains (multiregional 36–40% of chain segment, regional 60–65%) [^ip-latest]. East India holds 61–63% of population but lowest diagnostics market share, with CAGR 10.5–12.5% FY24–FY28 [^ip-latest]. Translating to rough TAM: if total Indian diagnostics ~₹80,000 Cr (≈US$9.6 bn at ₹83/$), East India likely ₹12,000–15,000 Cr (US$1.4–1.8 bn) and organised chain share within East is under‑penetrated [^ip-latest]. Policy tailwinds: CGHS rate revision (Oct ’25) improves realisation for government beneficiaries [^cc-transcript]; Ayushman Bharat and rising preventive health awareness post‑COVID structurally lift volume [^ar-fy25].

Competitive moat analysis (rated Strong/Moderate/Weak):
- Switching costs: Strong. Patients and doctors in WB trust Suraksha’s accredited reports; changing lab risks diagnostic discontinuity [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript].
- Scale: Strong in East (largest organised network, 55+ centres) but Weak nationally versus Dr Lal/Metropolis [^cc-transcript].
- Pricing power: Moderate. B2C full‑price realisation with no margin challenge, but B2B/corporate negotiates discounts; overall ability to raise prices is limited by competition [^cc-transcript].
