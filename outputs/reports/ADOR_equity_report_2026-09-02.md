### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**(1) Market Leadership & Moat — A 75-Year-Old Domestic Champion with 17–18% Market Share.** Ador Welding is the only listed Indian-origin Tier-1 welding platform with a 75-year operating history, 5 manufacturing plants, 850+ employees, and a distribution footprint spanning 15+ countries[^ip-latest]. The company holds an estimated 17–18% market share in the domestic organized welding market, making it the #2 player after ESAB India[^cc-transcript]. The moat is anchored in three structural strengths: (a) deep OEM relationships built over decades with customers in heavy engineering, shipbuilding, defence, railways, oil & gas, cement, and steel; (b) a 1,200+ distributor network covering 31,235 Indian pincodes; and (c) a 1.2 mn sq. ft. manufacturing footprint with 90,000 MT/year of consumables capacity and 30,000 units/year of equipment capacity[^ip-latest]. The brand "Ador" is virtually synonymous with welding in India, creating significant brand-recognition pricing power[^vp-thread].

**(2) Structural Margin Story — EBITDA to Expand 100–200 bps Over Coming Periods.** Ador's gross margin already expanded 240 bps to 37.8% in FY26 (from 35.4% in FY25), and EBITDA margin rose 250 bps to 11.6% (from 9.1%)[^ip-latest]. Management has guided to a further 100–200 bps EBITDA expansion from (a) operating leverage as capacity utilization moves from the current 70% toward 85–90%; (b) product mix shift toward high-margin consumables (where OPM is approaching 20%) versus lower-margin equipment and legacy M&R lines; and (c) operational efficiencies from the ongoing ₹80 Cr modernization program[^cc-transcript]. The Q1 FY27 print is a clear proof point: EBITDA margin of 11.6% (+220 bps YoY) and PAT of ₹28 Cr (versus a loss of ₹3.9 Cr in Q1 FY26)[^ip-latest]. We model FY27E/FY28E EBITDA margin of 13.0%/14.2%.

**(3) Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — Automation, Robotics, Cobots, Laser Welding.** The welding industry is undergoing a generational shift toward Industry 4.0, robotic welding, cobots, and laser-based cutting, driven by rising labour costs and the need for higher productivity. Ador has built a credible automation portfolio — Champ Multi 400/500R robotic systems, Champ Pulse 400/500iR, Axbot 101 (India's standard advanced cobot), and the Ador Xlogic Welding Cell — and is now seeing its "best-ever order book for welding automation"[^cc-transcript]. The strategic collaboration with Miller (ITW Group) for submerged arc welding, and the High Alloy/Nickel Base consumables portfolio (now including NPCIL-approved products for nuclear applications) position Ador in critical-welding applications[^ip-latest]. Wind manufacturing consumables and shipbuilding (where ~2% of steel consumption is welding consumables) are additional tailwinds[^cc-transcript].

**(4) Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months).** (i) Resolution of the BIS contingent liability matter (₹36.43 Cr) — magistrate court hearing scheduled for June-July 2026 with management hopeful of a "logical" outcome; closure would remove a multi-year overhang[^cc-transcript]. (ii) Shipbuilding capex cycle — global shipping order book is at multi-decade highs, and Ador is well-positioned with ~75–80% approvals in place; the company could meaningfully participate in India's shipbuilding renaissance. (iii) Uran project closure — now 96–97% complete, with no major exposures remaining, freeing up management bandwidth and capital. (iv) Export recovery — US traction improving after tariff headwinds, and European interest building post the FTA[^cc-transcript]. (v) Continued insider buying by promoter Ms. Deep Ashda Lalvani — 8+ acquisitions totaling ~8,500 shares between Sep 2025 and Mar 2026, a clear skin-in-the-game signal. (vi) Continued DII accumulation — DII holding has risen from 4.0% in Sep 2023 to 10.5% in Jun 2026 (with peaks of 12.6% in Mar 2026).

**(5) Biggest Structural Risk — Organized-vs-Unorganized Competitive Pressure & Cyclicality.** The Indian welding market remains ~50% unorganized, with local players using lower-grade raw materials, direct sales models, and cost efficiencies to compete on price[^vp-thread]. This caps organized-player pricing power, particularly in commoditized stick electrodes. Additionally, Ador's end-market exposure is heavily cyclical — heavy engineering, oil & gas, cement, steel, and infrastructure capex are all macro-sensitive. The welding business tracks IIP/GDP growth (6–8% baseline), and management itself acknowledges that 18–20% YoY growth to reach ₹1,500–1,600 Cr is "dependent on macroeconomic support"[^cc-transcript]. ### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model — A Vertically Integrated Welding Platform.** Ador Welding Limited (NSE: ADOR) is India's largest pure-play welding platform, founded in 1951 (75-year operating history as of FY27). The company operates a fully integrated business model that spans the entire welding value chain: (a) **welding consumables** (the "razors" — high-margin consumables that are used up during welding and need to be repeatedly purchased), including stick electrodes (SMAW), filler metals (MIG/TIG), submerged arc welding (SAW) products, flux-cored arc welding (FCAW) wires, and high-alloy/nickel-base consumables for critical applications; (b) **welding & cutting equipment** (the "razor handles" — capital equipment sold once and used for many years), including arc welders (200–1,200 Amp), diesel-engine welding generators, motor-generator welders, and the recently launched RHINO-E (India's first 200A battery-operated welding machine); (c) **industrial maintenance, repair, and overhaul (M&R)** — a legacy division providing low-heat-input welding alloys, cobalt/nickel-based alloys, reclamation services, and wear-resistant products to cement, mining, oil & gas, and steel customers; and (d) **welding & cutting automation** — the strategic growth vertical, encompassing robotic systems (Champ Multi 400/500R, Champ Pulse 400/500iR), cobots (Axbot 101), laser welding solutions, CNC cutting systems, and customised welding cells (Ador Xlogic Welding Cell)[^ip-latest]. The 80–85% distribution-led model (rather than direct sales) means the company relies on a 1,200+ distributor network for India, with a 15-person international sales team based in the JAFZA free zone in Dubai serving the Middle East, North America, South America, Europe, Africa, and Australia[^cc-transcript].

**Revenue Split by Division — Limited Public Disclosure, but Welding is Dominant.** As per the FY26 annual report, the welding segment accounted for 93.4% of standalone revenue, while Flares & Process Equipment (the legacy M&R-adjacent business) was 5.0%[^ar-fy25]. Management has now restructured the Flares & Process Equipment division (effective end-March/early-April 2026) and integrated it into the welding vertical as a smaller product line (expected ~₹20–30 Cr in revenue), eliminating it as a separate reporting division[^cc-transcript]. Within welding, consumables are the highest-margin and most strategically important sub-segment, with per-kg realizations trending upward and OPM approaching 20% (versus sub-10% OPM in non-consumables for a decade)[^vp-thread]. Unfortunately, Ador does not publicly disclose granular segment-wise revenue splits, which is a transparency gap we flag in Section 5. Total consolidated revenue was ₹1,140 Cr in FY26, growing 1.5% YoY from ₹1,122.7 Cr in FY25, while Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹309 Cr grew 22.9% YoY — a strong opening to the new fiscal year[^ip-latest].

**Key OEM / End Customers Per Division.** Ador's customer base is highly diversified across heavy engineering, shipbuilding, defence, railways, oil & gas, cement, steel, power, fertilizer, automotive, and mining. The company has "deepened direct relationships with key customers" and maintains a strong key-customer account team with a CRM process implemented in recent months[^cc-transcript]. While the company does not disclose specific customer names in its public filings, the end-markets served include (a) **structural fabrication** (the primary growth driver, including pre-engineered buildings, infrastructure, and industrial construction); (b) **shipbuilding** (a key strategic focus, with ~75–80% approvals in place); (c) **defence** (a growing customer segment, where Ador's NPCIL-approved products and high-alloy consumables are critical); (d) **railways** (a stable, multi-year demand driver); (e) **automotive** (currently picking up); and (f) **renewables/wind** (newer vertical, in approvals phase)[^cc-transcript]. The Hypertherm partnership gives Ador master-distributor rights for air plasma cutting systems in India, while the ITW/Miller partnership covers submerged arc welding solutions for power, structural, and shipbuilding segments[^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary Structure.** Ador has a relatively simple corporate structure, with the parent company (Ador Welding Limited) housing the main welding business and one material subsidiary — **3D Future Tech** (3D printing / additive manufacturing) — which continues to post losses[^ar-fy25]. The Ador Fontech amalgamation was completed post NCLT approval in 2023, simplifying the group structure[^vp-thread]. The Uran project (a legacy loss-making project) is now ~96–97% complete and is no longer expected to create further losses, with the largest hit taken in Q1 FY26[^cc-transcript]. Kuwait project receivables (₹14 Cr) were recovered in FY26 (pending since 2020–2021)[^cc-transcript].

**Manufacturing Footprint.** Ador operates **5 manufacturing plants** spread across India, with a combined **1.2 mn sq. ft. of manufacturing area**[^ip-latest]. The consumables plants have a capacity of **90,000 MT/year** and the equipment plants have a capacity of **30,000 units/year**[^ip-latest]. The R&D centre is recognised by the Government of India, ISO 17025 accredited, employs 38 researchers, and has conducted 1,000+ case studies — making it one of the most credible welding R&D facilities in India[^ip-latest]. The Silvassa plant has received the CII–Sohrabji Godrej Green Business Centre's GreenCo Silver rating for sustainability[^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background and Group Context.** The Ador Group (Ador Family) has been in the welding business for 75 years, making it one of the oldest industrial houses in India focused on a single industry vertical. The current MD, **Mr. Aditya Malkani**, is the third-generation promoter and has been leading the strategic transformation toward automation, robotics, and high-end consumables. The company has 35,275 shareholders as of Jun 2026 (down from a peak of 41,468 in Dec 2024), suggesting consolidation toward higher-quality institutional holders[^ip-latest]. Recent senior management additions include a **COO, Head of International Operations, and Head of Strategy** — a deliberate professionalisation move, although the high churn at the senior level (CFO resignation in July 2026, new Head-HR appointment in August 2026) is a mild governance flag we discuss in Section 5.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) and Growth.** The Indian welding market is estimated at **₹8,000–10,000 Cr (~USD 1.0–1.2 Bn)** as of FY26, with welding consumables accounting for ~50% of the market, equipment ~35%, and automation/services ~15% (analyst estimates). Globally, the welding market is estimated at **USD 25–30 Bn** (₹2,00,000–2,50,000 Cr), with India accounting for ~3–4% of global demand — a meaningful under-penetration given India's 18% share of global steel production. The Indian welding market is expected to grow at an **8–10% CAGR over FY25–FY30E**, driven by (a) **rising steel consumption** (India targets 300 MTPA by 2030 vs. ~150 MTPA currently); (b) **infrastructure capex** of ₹11+ lakh Cr under the National Infrastructure Pipeline; (c) **shipbuilding renaissance** (India targeting 5% of global shipbuilding market by 2030 vs. <1% currently); (d) **defence indigenisation**; and (e) **renewables / wind manufacturing**. The welding consumables sub-segment is structurally more attractive as it is a recurring revenue model (consumables are "razors" used repeatedly), whereas equipment is a one-time capex purchase.

**Policy Tailwinds — A Robust Multi-Year Demand Backdrop.** (1) **PLI Scheme for Specialty Steel** (₹6,322 Cr outlay) — incentivises domestic manufacturing of high-grade steel, driving higher welding consumable intensity. (2) **Defence Production & Export Policy 2020** — ₹1.5 lakh Cr domestic defence procurement target by 2025, where welding is mission-critical. (3) **FAME-II / EV Manufacturing** — drives demand for specialised welding in EV battery pack and motor manufacturing. (4) **Green Hydrogen Mission** (₹19,744 Cr outlay) — H2 cutting and specialised welding solutions are new demand pools. (5) **National Infrastructure Pipeline** — ₹111 lakh Cr over 5 years drives structural fabrication, bridges, and industrial construction. (6) **BIS quality standards** — gradually forcing unorganized players out of the market, structurally benefiting organized players like Ador[^vp-thread].
