### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership in denim & woven fabrics:** Arvind is India's largest integrated denim manufacturer with an estimated ~25–30% domestic market share, supplying to global majors including Levi's, Wrangler, H&M, Zara, Marks & Spencer and Tommy Hilfiger [^ip-latest]. Scale-driven cost advantage, captive spinning capacity and a 90-year brand heritage (since 1931) constitute a defensible moat in a fragmented industry [^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin expansion underway:** EBITDA margin trajectory has improved from sub-8% in FY22 to ~12% in FY25, driven by (i) shift from grey fabric to finished/branded textiles, (ii) value-added technical textiles scaling up, and (iii) operating leverage from higher capacity utilisation at the Santej mega-plant [^ip-latest]. We model further 150–200 bps expansion over FY26E–FY28E as the Advanced Materials division scales [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** The Advanced Materials & Technical Textiles segment (currently ~12–15% of revenue) targets 15–20% revenue CAGR, riding on PLI scheme incentives, geotextile demand from infra capex, and defence textiles [^ip-latest]. This is the single largest re-rating lever versus pure-play commodity textile peers.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (a) Continued net debt reduction (targeted sub-Rs. 1,200 Cr by FY27 vs. Rs. 1,800 Cr in FY25), (b) Q1 FY27 results showing seasonal strength, (c) potential value unlocking from residual holdings in listed subsidiaries (Arvind SmartSpaces, Arvind Fashions), and (d) commissioning of expanded technical textile capacity [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Cotton price volatility and demand cyclicality in the global apparel value chain remain the principal risks — Arvind's EBITDA margin has historically swung 300–400 bps with cotton cycle [^vp-thread]. Additionally, the dividend yield data point flagged in the header warrants reconciliation, as it implies an unsustainable payout inconsistent with the company's reinvestment-led capital allocation history.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** Arvind Limited operates as a vertically-integrated textile manufacturer spanning the cotton value chain — from spinning, weaving, knitting, processing to garmenting. The company has progressively pivoted from a commodity grey-fabric supplier to a branded, value-added textile player with growing exposure to technical textiles [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue split by division (FY25 estimates):**

| Division | Est. % of Revenue | Key Products |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Denim | ~38–42% | Denim fabric for global apparel brands |
| Woven & Knits | ~25–28% | Shirting, suiting, casualwear fabrics |
| Advanced Materials / Technical Textiles | ~12–15% | Geotextiles, defence textiles, composites |
| Garmenting & Branded Retail (incl. subsidiaries) | ~12–15% | Finished garments, value-added apparel |
| Others (incl. realty, residual) | ~3–5% | Wind power, residual businesses |

**Key OEM/end customers per division:** Denim — Levi's, Wrangler, H&M, Zara, Marks & Spencer, Tommy Hilfiger [^ip-latest]. Woven — PVH, Arrow, US Polo, Indian Terrain. Technical Textiles — government bodies (defence, roads/infra), industrial customers [^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary structure:** Arvind Fashions Ltd (listed — operates Unlimited, Arrow, US Polo, Tommy Hilfiger, Calvin Klein retail), Arvind SmartSpaces Ltd (listed — real estate, demerged from parent), and various technical textile JVs [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing footprint:** Gujarat (Santej — flagship integrated mega-plant near Ahmedabad; recently shifted registered office to Vibrant Mega Industrial Park, Daskroi, Ahmedabad per 18-Jul-2026 announcement), Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, and Maharashtra — total 15+ manufacturing facilities [^ip-latest].

**Promoter background:** The Lalbhai family — Sanjay Lalbhai (Chairman, ~40 years at the helm) represents the third generation of the Lalbhai group, which traces back to the Sarabhai-Lalbhai industrial house of Ahmedabad. The group has interests spanning textiles, chemicals, and real estate [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter pedigree & tenure:** Sanjay Lalbhai (Chairman & MD) has led Arvind for ~40 years and is widely respected in Indian industry circles — past president of CII, FICCI [^ar-fy25]. The promoter family (Lalbhai) brings multi-generational textile expertise dating back to 1931. However, the ValuePickr community has historically flagged governance concerns around related-party transactions and the complexity of the group structure (parent + listed subsidiaries Arvind Fashions, Arvind SmartSpaces) [^vp-thread].

**FCF deployment track record (FY23–FY25):** Cumulative operating cash flow ~Rs. 2,400 Cr over FY23–FY25 has been deployed primarily toward (i) net debt reduction of ~Rs. 1,200 Cr, (ii) capex of ~Rs. 800 Cr (technical textiles expansion, Santej modernisation), and (iii) modest dividend payouts [^ar-fy25]. The demerger of the real estate business into Arvind SmartSpaces (listed separately) was a significant capital allocation move to unlock value and sharpen the parent's textile focus [^vp-thread].

**Debt management:** Net debt has declined from ~Rs. 3,000 Cr (FY22) to ~Rs. 1,800 Cr (FY25); debt/EBITDA improved from ~4.5x to ~2.5x. Interest coverage ratio stands at ~3.5x. Credit ratings: CRISIL A+/Stable (reaffirmed Mar-2026), India Ratings IND A+/Stable [^ar-fy25].

**Dividend policy:** Payout ratio has averaged 15–20% over the past 5 years, reflecting a clear reinvestment-led philosophy. The flagged 70% yield in the header is inconsistent with this historical pattern and likely reflects a data anomaly requiring reconciliation [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter pledging:** Nil/Negligible promoter share pledging as of latest filings — a positive governance signal [^ar-fy25].

**Corporate governance flags:** No auditor changes in the past 5 years (BSR & Co. LLP, since FY21). No qualified audit opinions in recent years. Related-party transactions exist (inter-cos with Arvind Fashions, Arvind SmartSpaces) but are disclosed and at arm's length per the annual report [^ar-fy25].

**JVs & strategic partnerships:** Technical textiles JV with global players for defence and industrial applications; partnerships with brand customers for co-developed fabric innovations [^cc-transcript]. The strategic rationale is to access technical know-how and de-risk capex in emerging textile adjacencies.
