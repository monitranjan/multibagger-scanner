### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

* **Unassailable Technological Moat in High-Precision 3D Rotating Parts**: Azad Engineering is one of the few global manufacturers capable of producing highly complex, "life-limiting" 3D rotating components (such as blisks, impellers, and critical airfoils) for gas/steam turbines and aerospace propulsion systems. These components operate under extreme thermal and mechanical stress, requiring zero-defect manufacturing. The qualification process for these parts takes 3 to 5 years, creating an exceptionally high entry barrier and securing long-term supply agreements (LTSAs) with top-tier global OEMs like General Electric (GE), Honeywell, and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries.
* **Structural Margin Expansion Driven by Product Mix and Scale**: We project Azad's EBITDA margins to expand by ~200 bps from 32.6% in FY24 to 34.6% by FY27E. This margin expansion is structurally supported by: (1) a rising share of high-complexity Aerospace & Defence components, which command gross margins exceeding 45%, and (2) strong operating leverage as the newly commissioned Tuniki Bollaram facility scales up production, diluting fixed overheads.
* **Strategic Diversification into High-Growth Aviation and Defense Adjacencies**: While the company’s legacy lay in energy turbine components, Azad has successfully diversified into high-growth military aviation and defense propulsion systems. The company has secured critical component manufacturing mandates for global defense programs, including missile subsystems and military helicopter engines. This structural pivot expands Azad’s addressable market by over 3x and reduces its historical reliance on the cyclical power-generation sector.
* **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months)**: Key operational catalysts include: (1) the commercialization and rapid volume ramp-up of Phase-1 of the new 50-acre Tuniki Bollaram manufacturing facility in Telangana, (2) the execution of recently signed multi-year export contracts valued at over Rs. 2,000 Cr with global aerospace majors, and (3) potential new customer acquisitions among European defense and aerospace OEMs currently seeking China-plus-one alternative suppliers.
* **Key Structural Risk — High Customer Concentration**: Despite its expanding order book, Azad faces significant customer concentration risk, with its top 5 customers historically contributing over 65% of total revenues. Any delay in technology platform lifecycles, global supply chain disruptions at customer assembly plants, or contract renegotiations by major OEMs like GE or Honeywell could materially impact manufacturing capacity utilization and projected cash flows.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

* **Core Business Model**: Azad Engineering Ltd is a niche manufacturer of high-precision, safety-critical components for the Aerospace & Defence, Energy, and Oil & Gas industries. The company specializes in the engineering of complex 3D rotating components, including turbine blades, compressor blades, fan blades, blisks, impellers, and specialized shafts. Its business model is built on securing long-term supply agreements (LTSAs) with global OEMs, acting as a sole-source or preferred dual-source partner for critical engine and turbine programs.
* **Revenue Split by Division (FY24/FY25 Representative)**:
  * *Energy (Gas & Steam Turbines)*: ~50% of revenue.
  * *Aerospace & Defence (Propulsion, Missile, and Structural parts)*: ~35% of revenue.
  * *Oil & Gas and Industrial Adjacencies*: ~15% of revenue.
* **Key OEM and End Customers**:
  * *Aerospace & Defence*: General Electric (GE Aviation), Honeywell International, Boeing, Rolls-Royce, and Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL).
  * *Energy*: Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI), Siemens Energy, MAN Energy Solutions, and General Electric (Power Division).
  * *Oil & Gas*: Baker Hughes and other global oilfield service providers.
* **Subsidiary Structure**: Azad Engineering operates with a highly clean, streamlined corporate structure. The core manufacturing operations, intellectual property, and customer contracts are housed within the parent entity, Azad Engineering Ltd, with minimal operational leakage through subsidiaries.
* **Manufacturing Footprint**: The company operates four state-of-the-art manufacturing facilities located in Hyderabad, Telangana. To support its massive order backlog, Azad is currently executing a large-scale capacity expansion at a new 50-acre site in Tuniki Bollaram, Telangana, which will house advanced multi-axis CNC machining centers, automated robotic lines, and specialized vacuum heat treatment furnaces.
* **Promoter Background & Group Context**: The company was founded and is led by Mr. Rakesh Chopdar, a first-generation entrepreneur with over two decades of experience in precision engineering and metal-cutting technologies. Under his leadership, the company transitioned from a localized precision machining shop into a globally qualified Tier-1 partner for Fortune 500 industrial conglomerates. The promoter group maintains a highly focused and professionalized management style, with zero cross-holdings or unrelated group business diversions.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

* **Total Addressable Market (TAM) & Growth Drivers**: The global addressable market for high-precision rotating components across Aerospace, Defence, and Power Generation is estimated at ~Rs. 1,25,000 Cr ($15 Billion USD). This market is projected to grow at a CAGR of 9.5% over the 2024–2030 period. Key structural growth drivers include: (1) a massive global commercial aircraft order backlog (exceeding 13,000 aircraft across Airbus and Boeing), (2) global defense modernization programs, and (3) the rising demand for high-efficiency gas turbines for grid stability alongside renewable energy integration.
* **Policy Tailwinds**: The company is a prime beneficiary of several domestic and global policy initiatives:
  * *Make in India & Defence Offsets*: Mandates local sourcing of defense components, driving foreign OEMs to partner with qualified domestic players like Azad.
  * *PLI Schemes*: Government incentives for advanced manufacturing and drone/aerospace component localization.
  * *China-Plus-One Strategy*: Global aerospace and industrial OEMs are actively diversifying their supply chains away from China, shifting high-value manufacturing orders to qualified Indian precision engineers.
* **Competitive Moat Analysis**:
  * *Switching Costs (Strong)*: Once an OEM qualifies Azad's components for an aeroengine or turbine platform, switching to an alternative supplier requires a rigorous 3-to-5-year re-certification process, costing millions of dollars. This creates highly sticky, multi-decade revenue streams.
  * *Scale and Capital Intensity (Strong)*: The high upfront capital expenditure required for advanced multi-axis CNC machines, specialized metallurgical labs, and testing facilities prevents smaller competitors from entering the high-precision 3D rotating segment.
  * *Pricing Power (Moderate to Strong)*: Because Azad manufactures sole-source or dual-source critical components, its contracts typically include raw material (superalloy) pass-through clauses, protecting its operating margins from inflationary pressures.
