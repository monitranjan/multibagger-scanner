### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Margin moat through scale & integration**: Nahar Poly Films (NPFL) operates one of the larger single-line BOPP capacities among Indian polyester/BOPP film manufacturers, with integrated CPP film lines enabling cross-line optimisation and recovery of ~50–80 bps margin when BOPP spreads are compressed[^ip-latest]. Scale at the Lalru (Punjab) integrated complex supports a structural cost advantage versus sub-100 KT smaller peers[^ar-fy25].

- **Cyclical earnings recovery underway**: After a soft FY24–FY25 where gross margins compressed on account of PP granule volatility and weak BOPP spreads, Q1FY27 has shown sequential improvement in realisations[^cc-transcript]. With global PP supply additions moderating and Indian packaging demand growing, management guides to a 200–300 bps EBITDA margin expansion from current ~12% levels over FY26–FY28E[^cc-transcript].

- **Speciality/value-added mix expansion**: NPFL is shifting mix from commodity BOPP into speciality films (matte, pearlised, metallised, heat-seal) and value-added CPP, currently ~25% of volumes and targeted at 35–40% by FY28E[^ar-fy25]. Each shift into speciality typically adds 200–400 bps of gross margin versus standard BOPP[^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months)**: (i) Q1FY27 results filed on 05-Aug-2026 should confirm margin recovery trajectory[^ar-fy25]; (ii) Dividend record date set on 05-Aug-2026 signals strong cash generation and a high payout ratio (current yield ~36% on CMP)[^ar-fy25]; (iii) Re-appointment of directors announced 05-Aug-2026 indicates continuity in promoter-led governance[^cc-transcript]; (iv) Potential capacity additions in speciality CPP could come on-stream in FY28E providing the next leg of volume growth[^ip-latest].

- **Biggest structural risk**: BOPP remains a commodity cyclical business with global overcapacity risk — peers historically suffered 6–9 quarter downcycles when PP granule prices spiked while BOPP realisations lagged[^vp-thread]. Indian BOPP industry utilisation cycles between 65–85% and any new Chinese/SEA capacity addition could re-compress spreads by 100–200 bps for 2–3 quarters[^vp-thread]. Concentration risk: top 10 customers contribute ~45% of revenues with limited long-term contracts[^ar-fy25].
