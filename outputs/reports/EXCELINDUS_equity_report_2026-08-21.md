### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Excel Industries is one of India’s oldest chemical companies (founded 1941) and holds the global #1 position in production of DETC (Diethyl Dithiocarbamate, a key agrochemical intermediate used to make fungicides) and is among the top 5 global producers of Phosphonates (specialty chemicals used in water treatment, detergents, and oil-field applications) [^ip-latest]. This leadership is not accidental; it stems from decades of building complex phosphorus chemistry expertise and long-term supply agreements with multinational customers. Switching costs for customers are moderate-to-strong because once a chemical plant is qualified and validated for a specific intermediate, changing supplier triggers re-validation, regulatory filings, and production downtime—so buyers stick around. The company’s scale in DETC gives it a cost advantage that smaller players cannot match [^cc-transcript].

- **Structural margin story:** The company’s Q1 FY27 gross margin expanded to 50.0% versus 45.6% in Q1 FY26, and EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, and Amortisation—a measure of core operating profit before non-cash and financing items) margin rose to 14.4% from 13.7%, a gain of about 70 basis points (bps, where 100 bps = 1%) [^ip-latest]. This improvement is structural because the revenue mix is shifting away from cyclical agro intermediates toward higher-value YP (Yellow Phosphorus) derivatives, Performance Solutions, and contract manufacturing (CM) that management explicitly called “superior margin profiles” [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. As the share of non-agro business rises, overall margin cyclicality should reduce and return on capital should improve.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Excel is aggressively entering biocides (chemicals that control harmful microorganisms) and performance solutions. A 2,530 TPA (tonnes per annum) biocides capacity became operational in H2 FY26, and one more biocide product is planned for launch in H2 FY27 [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, a new 1,265 MTPA specialty chemical facility at Lote is scheduled for commissioning by Feb-2027 with capex of only ~₹5 Cr but wide geographic market potential [^ip-latest]. The company has also guided a ₹200–300 Cr capex plan over three years toward Phosphorus Pentasulphide, Diazotization Chemistry, Pharma APIs (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients—the active part of a medicine), and further biocide expansion [^ar-fy25]. These adjacencies reduce dependence on monsoon-driven agro demand.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) A dedicated contract manufacturing line is expected to be commissioned by July 2026 under a long-term agreement, with management indicating higher volumes from Q2 FY27 [^cc-transcript][^ip-latest]. (ii) A ₹40 Cr specialty chemical project was completed on 23-Jul-2026 with a 5-year LT supply agreement and trade advance of ₹25 Cr, providing revenue potential of ₹35–40 Cr p.a. excluding raw material cost [^ip-latest]. (iii) The board has proposed a final dividend of ₹13.75 per share (275% of face value) with record date 17-Sep-2026, signaling confidence in cash flows [^cc-transcript]. (iv) Q1 FY27 EBITDA stayed flat YoY at ₹42.4 Cr despite 5% revenue decline, showing pricing power and cost control [^ip-latest].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The most severe structural threat is Chinese oversupply and price competition in commodity intermediates, combined with import dependency on certain raw materials [^ar-fy25]. Additionally, the agro segment (50–60% of chemical revenue) remains exposed to erratic monsoons and channel inventory build-ups at customer end, as seen in H1 FY26 [^cc-transcript][^ip-latest]. If China expands capacity and dumps product, Excel’s volume-led recovery could stall. The ValuePickr community also flags repeated promoter warrant conversions as potential EPS dilution risk, though this is more governance-noise than business-risk [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Excel Industries operates a multi-divisional chemical manufacturing model. In simple terms, it takes basic raw materials (like phosphorus, chlorine, and alcohols) and converts them through controlled reactions into specialized molecules that other industries use as building blocks. The core business is split as per FY25 annual report: Chemicals segment contributed ₹968.8 Cr (99% of revenue) comprising Agrochemical Intermediates (DETC, DMTC, NaTCP, DMPAT—all weird acronyms for pesticide precursors), YP Derivatives / Phosphonates / Biocides / Mining Additives / Polymer Modifiers (P2S5, PSCL3, PCL3, PCL5—again chemical shorthand), Performance Solutions, and Pharma Intermediates & API (Acetyl Chloride, Febuxostat, Teneligliptin API, Butaphosphan). The Environment & Biotech (waste management: Excel OWC, Integrated MSW, Bioculum, Sanitreat) contributed only ₹9.3 Cr (1%) [^ar-fy25].

Revenue by geography (standalone FY26): Domestic ₹871 Cr (79.6%), Export ₹223 Cr (20.4%) [^ip-latest]. Key OEM/end customers include global MNCs under confidential contract manufacturing LTAs (validation batches dispatched) and agricultural formulators; specific names are not disclosed for commercial sensitivity [^cc-transcript]. Subsidiary structure: 100% subsidiary Kamaljyot holds long-term investments yielding dividends/interest; Excel Bio and Rajkot C&D are subsidiaries reported under CARO with minor cash losses [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. Manufacturing footprint spans three states/plants: Roha (Maharashtra), Lote Parshuram (Maharashtra), and Visakhapatnam (Andhra Pradesh) [^cc-transcript]. Promoter background: The Shroff family via Anshul Specialty holds 42.63% and group stake is 52.30%; the company is part of the legacy Excel group (Excel Crop Care was spun off in 2003) [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

India’s specialty chem market is $65 Bn (2024) → $92 Bn (2033) at 3.8% CAGR; India is 3rd in polymer consumption, 4th in agrochemical production [^ip-latest]. Method 2 (EV/EBITDA): EV = Market Cap Rs.1401 Cr minus net cash ~Rs.90 Cr = Rs.1311 Cr. FY26 EBITDA Rs.110 Cr implies 11.9x vs sector median 13x[^ip-latest]. Base case assigns 10.8x to FY27E EBITDA Rs.125 Cr → EV Rs.1350 Cr + cash Rs.100 Cr = Rs.1450 Cr mcap / 1.286 Cr shares ≈ Rs.1350 (rounded).
