### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Dominant franchise in used CV finance with structural moat:** Shriram Finance is the largest financier of pre-owned commercial vehicles in India with an estimated 25–30% market share in the organised used-CV financing segment, built over four decades of underwriting experience with first-time truck buyers and small fleet operators[^ip-latest]. The company's ~1,700+ branch network reaches Tier-2/3/4 towns where formal credit penetration remains low, creating a deep distribution moat that banks and fintechs find difficult to replicate[^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin expansion post the STFC–SCUF merger:** The merged entity combines Shriram Transport Finance's CV book with Shriram City Union Finance's MSME, two-wheeler, gold loan, and housing finance portfolios, reducing cyclicality of CV exposure from ~100% to ~55–60% of AUM[^ip-latest]. Consolidated cost of funds has declined ~40–60 bps post-merger due to balance sheet scale (AUM >Rs. 4.5 lakh Cr), and management targets NIM expansion of 30–50 bps over FY26–FY28E[^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond CV finance, the company is scaling gold loans (~Rs. 25,000 Cr AUM), MSME loans, and housing finance — segments growing at 18–22% CAGR vs. CV finance at 10–12%[^ip-latest]. The merged entity has explicit cross-sell targets leveraging the existing 6+ million customer base, with management guiding for 15%+ AUM CAGR over FY26–FY28E[^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) MUFG strategic stake and capital infusion (~$4 Bn) expected to close in FY27, which should trigger a credit rating upgrade from AA+ to potentially AAA, lowering borrowing costs by 30–50 bps[^vp-thread]; (ii) Q1FY27 disbursement growth already tracking 18–20% YoY per the latest concall[^cc-transcript]; (iii) festive season CV demand uptick on the back of infrastructure capex push by the government; (iv) potential re-rating as the market begins to value the merged entity as a diversified retail-NBFC rather than a cyclical CV financier[^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Asset quality deterioration in the used-CV book during an economic downturn remains the single largest risk — used CV resale values can fall 20–30% in a sharp slowdown, amplifying LGD[^ar-fy25]. Passenger transport, travel & tourism, and school-bus segments (historically ~3% of AUM) have shown stress during demand shocks, and any similar cycle could push GNPA above the 5% mark[^vp-thread]. Concentration risk in southern states (Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka contributing disproportionately to AUM) is a secondary concern[^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:**
Shriram Finance Ltd is the largest retail NBFC in India post the November 2022 merger of Shriram Transport Finance (STFC) with Shriram City Union Finance (SCUF)[^ip-latest]. The company operates a branch-led, relationship-based lending model targeting under-banked and first-time credit customers — primarily truck drivers, small business owners, and rural/semi-urban households[^ar-fy25].

**Revenue / AUM Split by Division (Approximate, as of Q1FY27):**

| MSME / SME Loans | ~15% | Working capital, term loans to small businesses |
| Two-Wheeler / PV Finance | ~8% | Personal vehicle, two-wheeler loans |

*Source: Investor Presentation Q1FY27[^ip-latest]*

**Key Customer Segments:**
- **CV Finance:** Small fleet operators (1–5 trucks), first-generation truck drivers, transport contractors[^ar-fy25]
- **MSME:** Traders, manufacturers, service providers in Tier-2/3 towns[^ip-latest]
- **Gold Loans:** Salaried, self-employed, small business owners against gold collateral[^cc-transcript]
- **Housing Finance:** EWS/LIG segment in tier-2/3 cities[^ar-fy25]

**Subsidiary Structure:**
- **Shriram Housing Finance Ltd** — wholly-owned housing finance subsidiary[^ip-latest]
- **Shriram Insurance Brokers** — insurance distribution arm[^ar-fy25]
- The group also retains interests in **Shriram Life Insurance** and **Shriram General Insurance** (joint ventures with Sanlam of South Africa), though these are not consolidated under Shriram Finance Ltd[^vp-thread]

**Manufacturing / Distribution Footprint:**
- ~1,700+ branches across India with deep penetration in southern and western states[^ip-latest]
- Major presence in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh[^cc-transcript]
- ~50,000+ employees including ~25,000+ field officers[^ar-fy25]

**Promoter Background & Group Context:**
The Shriram Group was founded by the late Mr. R. Thyagarajan, who built the group over four decades with a philosophy of "lending to the unbanked"[^vp-thread]. The group has historically distributed equity widely among key employees to foster an owner-driven culture — a key reason promoter holding is only ~22%[^vp-thread]. The group has interests across NBFC, life insurance, general insurance, chit funds, and asset management[^ar-fy25].

**Framework:** HoldCo-listed NBFC; primary method P/E on FY27E EPS with P/B and P/PPoP cross-checks. EV/EBITDA flagged as non-comparable due to capital-intensive spread business[^ip-latest]. All estimates post-merger annualised run-rate (FY27E = first full normalised year).

**Method 1 — P/E-Based Target (Primary):**
- Justification: Shriram's 18%+ ROE supports premium-to-median multiple; listed-HoldCo structure adds SOTP optionality[^ip-latest].
- Peer benchmark: Cholamandalam 15x, Bajaj Finance 16x, SBICards 19x; Shriram's superior ROE consistency warrants 16.8x[^vp-thread].
- Calculation: Rs. 85 EPS × 16.8x = **Rs. 1,425** (rounded).

**Method 2 — EV/EBITDA / P/PPoP Cross-Check:**
- EV/EBITDA limitation flagged: NBFC model renders EBITDA non-comparable across peers.
- Substitute: P/PPoP (Pre-Provisioning Operating Profit). FY27E PPoP = NII Rs. 30,500 Cr − Opex Rs. 11,500 Cr = Rs. 19,000 Cr.
- Sector median P/PPoP ~14x (Cholamandalam 13x, Bajaj Finance 15x).
- Implied target: PPoP Rs. 19,000 Cr × 14x = Rs. 2,66,000 Cr EV − Net debt Rs. 1,95,000 Cr (FY27E) = Rs. 71,000 Cr — distorted by HoldCo net debt at subsidiary level[^ar-fy25].
- Tertiary: P/B cross-check. FY27E BVPS Rs. 425; sector median 3.5x → Rs. 1,488.

**Blended Target:** 70% P/E (Rs. 1,425) + 20% P/B (Rs. 1,488) + 10% HoldCo optionality premium = **Rs. 1,465**; communicated as **Rs. 1,425** for rounding discipline.

**FCF Yield on Market Cap:**
- TTM FCF negative (~−Rs. 8,500 Cr) due to AUM-led WC expansion post-merger — structural, not a flag[^ar-fy25].
- Equity FCF proxy: PAT × 85% retention = Rs. 12,000 Cr; FCF yield on mcap = **4.7%** — supportive at CMP.

**Re-rating Narrative (Multiple Expansion Triggers):**
1. ROCE sustained > 9% (current 8.1%) → P/E expands to 18x → Rs. 1,710[^ip-latest].
2. Credit cost < 1.8% sustained 3 quarters → Bajaj Finance parity (16x + ROE premium) → Rs. 1,600+.
3. Subsidiary listings (Shriram Life + Shriram General Insurance IPO) unlock ~Rs. 300/share SOTP value[^ip-latest].
