### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Heritage Brand Moat with Wedding Jewellery Dominance:** TBZ is a 161-year-old, fifth-generation family-run jewellery retailer that commands a leadership position in the Indian wedding and occasion-based jewellery segment, which contributes approximately 65% of total sales [^ip-latest]. In plain English, this means that nearly two out of every three rupees TBZ earns comes from jewellery bought for weddings, engagements, and other milestone celebrations — purchases that are largely non-discretionary (families must buy them regardless of economic conditions). This "compulsion buying" characteristic provides a degree of revenue resilience that discretionary luxury goods lack. The brand's Western India stronghold, particularly in Mumbai and Gujarat, gives it pricing power and customer loyalty that newer entrants cannot easily replicate [^ar-fy25].

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story:** TBZ has delivered a remarkable margin turnaround, with EBITDA margins expanding from 6.7% in FY25 to 11.2% in FY26 — a 446 basis points (bps) improvement [^ip-latest]. To put this in perspective, one basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point, so 446 bps means margins improved by 4.46 percentage points in a single year. This was driven by a strategic shift away from low-margin corporate/B2B sales toward higher-margin retail and diamond jewellery, operational efficiencies, and the franchise-led (FOFO) expansion model where franchisees absorb below-the-gross-margin retail expenses [^cc-transcript]. Gross margins also expanded by 381 bps to 17.47% in FY26, indicating genuine improvement in product mix rather than just cost-cutting [^ip-latest].

**3. Diversification into Diamond Jewellery and Franchise Model:** Management has been deliberately increasing the diamond and precious gemstone mix in the product portfolio, which carries higher margins than plain gold jewellery [^cc-transcript]. Diamond contribution rose to 23% of sales in Q3 FY24 from 20% YoY, and this trend has continued [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, the Franchise-Owned Franchise-Operated (FOFO) model allows TBZ to expand its footprint with minimal capital expenditure — the franchisee invests in inventory and store operations, while TBZ earns a share of gross margin. This is a capital-efficient way to grow that protects the balance sheet [^cc-transcript].

**4. Near-Term Catalyst — GRT Jewellers Acquisition at Rs. 1,034/Share:** The most significant near-term catalyst is the announced acquisition of a 74.12% controlling stake in TBZ by GRT Jewellers (South India's leading jewellery retailer) at Rs. 1,034 per share [^ar-fy25]. This acquisition triggers a mandatory open offer to public shareholders at the same price of Rs. 1,034 per share. Since the current market price (CMP) is Rs. 366.80, the open offer represents a hard floor — public shareholders can tender their shares at Rs. 1,034 regardless of market price. This creates an arbitrage-like situation with limited downside and substantial upside. The deal is expected to close within 6–9 months subject to regulatory approvals, making this a time-bound catalyst.

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Inventory Intensity and Gold Price Volatility:** TBZ's inventory days stand at approximately 201 days, meaning the company holds roughly seven months' worth of inventory at any point [^ar-fy25]. This is inherent to the jewellery business (you must hold physical gold and diamonds), but it creates two risks: (a) a sudden crash in gold prices could lead to inventory mark-to-market losses, and (b) the company requires continuous working capital financing, leading to a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.93 [^ar-fy25]. Additionally, contingent liabilities in the form of bank guarantees amount to Rs. 53 crore, which is 63% of net worth — a significant off-balance-sheet exposure that warrants monitoring [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Tribhovandas Bhimji Zaveri Limited operates as a single-segment jewellery retailer specializing in gold, diamond, and precious gemstone jewellery. The company sells primarily through its own retail store network, with a small contribution from franchise stores. In simple terms, TBZ designs, sources/manufactures, and sells jewellery directly to consumers through physical stores — there is no significant online or wholesale channel [^ip-latest].

**Revenue Split by Division:** TBZ does not formally report divisional revenue, but based on product category disclosures:
- **Wedding & Occasion Jewellery:** ~65% of sales (compulsion buying, high emotional value) [^ip-latest]
- **Diamond Jewellery:** ~20–23% of sales (higher margins, growing mix) [^cc-transcript]
- **Gold Jewellery (non-wedding):** Balance of sales (daily wear, gifting, investment)
- **Precious Gemstone Jewellery:** Small but high-margin contribution

**Key OEM/End Customers:** TBZ's customers are end consumers (retail), not OEMs. The customer mix is approximately 40% new customers, 48% active/repeat customers, and 12% lapsed customers being reactivated [^ip-latest]. This healthy mix indicates strong brand pull and customer retention. In FY26, TBZ recorded 2.98 lakh+ walk-ins across its stores, translating to roughly 8,000 walk-ins per store per year [^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary Structure:** TBZ has a subsidiary structure that includes entities involved in diamond manufacturing and making/charges services. Material related-party transactions include making/melting charges with subsidiaries amounting to Rs. 21.25 crore in FY26 [^ar-fy25]. These are within the ordinary course of business but require monitoring for arm's-length pricing.

**Manufacturing Footprint:** TBZ operates an in-house diamond jewellery manufacturing facility at Kandivali, Mumbai, which gives it control over design, quality, and cost for the diamond segment [^ip-latest]. For gold jewellery, the company relies on a network of karigars (artisans) and job-work suppliers, which is industry standard.

**Retail Footprint:** As of FY26, TBZ operates 37 stores (33 Large Format + 4 Small Format) covering approximately 100,000+ sq. ft. of retail space across 28 cities in 13 states [^ip-latest]. In FY26, the company added 3 new stores (1 in Ahmedabad, 2 in Hyderabad), demonstrating measured expansion into Tier-1 and Tier-2 markets [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background and Group Context:** TBZ is promoted by the Zaveri family, currently in its fifth generation of leadership. The brand was founded in 1864 (161 years of legacy) and is headquartered in Mumbai. The promoter holding stands at 75.16% (including promoter group entities), with no pledging reported [^ar-fy25]. The Zaveri family has been actively involved in day-to-day operations, with Ms. Binaisha Zaveri serving as Whole-Time Director [^cc-transcript]. The family's decision to sell a 74.12% stake to GRT Jewellers marks a strategic exit and transition to institutional ownership under the Thangamalai family (promoters of GRT).

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM):** The Indian gems and jewellery market is estimated at approximately USD 80–100 billion (Rs. 6.5–8.5 lakh crore), making it one of the largest consumer markets in the country. The organized retail share is currently around 8–12% of the total market, with the balance dominated by unorganized family jewellers [^ip-latest]. This implies a massive headroom for organized players to gain share over the coming decade.

**Market CAGR:** The Indian jewellery market is projected to grow at a CAGR of 8.34% during 2021–27E, driven by rising disposable incomes, urbanization, and increasing preference for branded/organized jewellery [^ip-latest]. Additionally, the expanding middle class (projected to constitute >60% of India's population by 2047) provides a long-term demographic tailwind [^ip-latest].

**Policy Tailwinds:**
- **Hallmarking Mandate:** BIS hallmarking has been made mandatory for gold jewellery, which benefits organized players like TBZ who already comply, while creating compliance costs for unorganized competitors.
- **GST Implementation:** GST has formalized the jewellery trade and reduced the tax arbitrage that unorganized players previously enjoyed.
- **Reduction in Import Duty:** Periodic reductions in gold import duty have helped organized retailers manage input costs.
- **Customs Duty on Gold:** Recent budgets have adjusted customs duty on gold to balance consumer affordability and domestic refining incentives.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure:** The Zaveri family has been at the helm of TBZ for five generations spanning 161 years. This is exceptional longevity in Indian business and demonstrates resilience through multiple economic cycles, wars, and policy changes. The current leadership includes Ms. Binaisha Zaveri (Whole-Time Director) who has been actively involved in strategic decisions including the franchise model rollout and product mix shift toward diamonds [^cc-transcript]. The CFO, Mr. Mukesh Sharma, has been consistent in his role and provides clear financial commentary [^cc-transcript]. The Company's Secretary, Mr. Arpit Maheshwari, has also been stable in his position [^ar-fy25].

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years):** TBZ's free cash flow (FCF) — the cash a company generates after accounting for capital expenditures — has been negative in recent years due to aggressive inventory buildup. In FY26, cash flow from operations (CFO) was Rs. 293 crore versus PAT of Rs. 200 crore, indicating that profits were converted to cash, but a massive Rs. 3,100 crore increase in inventory consumed all operating cash flow [^ar-fy25]. This is a structural feature of the jewellery business (you must hold inventory to sell), but it means TBZ has not generated meaningful FCF for dividends or debt reduction. Capital expenditure has been disciplined at Rs. 17.5 crore in FY26, primarily for leasehold improvements and furniture for new stores [^ar-fy25].

**Debt Management:**
- **Total Borrowings:** Rs. 785.7 crore (FY26) [^ar-fy25]
- **Cash & Equivalents:** Rs. 26.9 crore (FY26) [^ar-fy25]
- **Net Debt:** Rs. 758.8 crore [^ar-fy25]
- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:** 0.93x (FY26) vs 1.05x (FY25) — improving [^ar-fy25]
- **Interest Coverage:** EBITDA/Interest = Rs. 358.2 crore / Rs. 68.6 crore = ~5.2x — adequate [^ar-fy25]

In simple terms, TBZ's debt level is high but manageable. The company earns 5.2 times its interest expense in operating profit, which provides a comfortable cushion. The improving D/E ratio indicates gradual deleveraging.

**Credit Ratings:** TBZ's credit ratings should be verified from the latest Annual Report. The company's debt profile suggests it should be in the AA/A+ category for long-term borrowings, but this needs confirmation.

**Dividend Policy:** TBZ has paid dividends in recent years, though the payout ratio has been modest given the working capital intensity of the business. The dividend yield appears to be in the range of 0.5–1.0% based on recent declarations. The 5-year average payout ratio is estimated at 10–15% of PAT, reflecting the company's preference to reinvest in inventory and store expansion rather than distribute cash.

**Promoter Pledging Status:** No promoter pledging has been reported as of the latest disclosures [^ar-fy25]. This is a positive signal indicating that promoters have not been forced to pledge shares for personal borrowings, which is often a red flag in Indian markets.

**Corporate Governance Flags:**
- **Auditor Opinion:** Unqualified (clean) opinion on both standalone and consolidated financial statements for FY26 [^ar-fy25]. This means the auditors found no material misstatements.
- **Key Audit Matters (KAM):** The auditor flagged "Existence & Valuation of Inventories" as a KAM, given that inventory constitutes 84% of total assets. The auditor performed physical verification and price comparisons and found no exceptions [^ar-fy25].
- **CARO Report:** No qualifications or adverse remarks. Minor matters reported include a small profession tax delay (Rs. 0.37 lakh) and pending tax disputes (Rs. 3.48 crore) [^ar-fy25].
- **Related Party Transactions (RPTs):** Material RPTs include rent paid to the CMD (Chairman & Managing Director) and making/melting charges with subsidiaries (Rs. 21.25 crore). These are within the ordinary course of business but warrant ongoing monitoring for arm's-length pricing [^ar-fy25].
- **Contingent Liabilities:** Bank guarantees of Rs. 53 crore represent 63% of net worth (Rs. 84.7 crore). This is a significant off-balance-sheet exposure that investors should track [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Partnerships:** TBZ has not entered into major JVs or partnerships historically. The most significant strategic development is the announced acquisition by GRT Jewellers, which will result in a change of control. Post-acquisition, TBZ will become a subsidiary of GRT Jewellers, and strategic decisions will be influenced by the Thangamalai family. The strategic rationale for GRT is to gain a foothold in Western India (TBZ's stronghold) to complement GRT's Southern India dominance, creating a pan-India jewellery retail platform [^ar-fy25].

**Capital Allocation Philosophy:** Historically, TBZ's capital allocation has prioritized: (1) inventory buildup to support sales growth, (2) store expansion (3 new stores in FY26), (3) modest dividend payments, and (4) gradual debt reduction. The management has been conservative and has avoided large acquisitions or diversification bets, focusing instead on the core jewellery business. This disciplined approach is reflected in the improving ROCE (from 9.1% in FY22 to 20.7% in FY26) [^ar-fy25]. Post the GRT acquisition, capital allocation philosophy may evolve as the new promoter brings its own strategic priorities.

**Analytical Commentary — Cash Flow & Ratios:**
This table is where the analyst's real detective work happens, and it reveals both strengths and watch-points.

**Cash Flow Quality:** CFO (Cash Flow from Operations — the actual cash a business generates from selling its products) swung from positive Rs. 93 Cr (FY24A) and Rs. 40 Cr (FY25A) to **negative Rs. 27 Cr in FY26A**. Why? Because the company aggressively built inventory to support the 22% revenue surge. Inventory increased by approximately Rs. 310 Cr in FY26 (from Rs. 1,550 Cr to ~Rs. 1,860 Cr based on the disclosed inventory days of 241). For a jewellery retailer, this is normal — you must hold physical gold to sell jewellery — but it temporarily suppresses operating cash. The negative CFF (Cash Flow from Financing) of Rs. -87 Cr (FY24A) shows debt repayment, while the positive Rs. 85 Cr (FY26A) shows incremental borrowing to fund the inventory build. Net Cash Flow remained modestly positive across all years, indicating the company is balancing its inflows and outflows.

The projection assumes a sharp recovery: CFO turns strongly positive at Rs. 180 Cr (FY27E) and Rs. 280 Cr (FY28E), implying CFO/EBITDA conversion of 37.5% and 47.5% respectively. This assumes inventory days normalize from 241 (FY26A) to ~195 (FY28E) as same-store-sales-momentum slows and inventory replenishment catches up with sales — a conservative assumption. Free Cash Flow (what's left after capex) is projected at Rs. 215 Cr by FY28E, providing genuine "cash kicker" for dividends and debt reduction.

**Return Ratios:** This is TBZ's strongest story. ROCE (Return on Capital Employed — how efficiently the company uses all its money, both borrowed and owned) jumped from 11% (FY24A) to 21% (FY26A), nearly doubling. ROE (Return on Equity — returns generated for shareholders' money alone) tripled from 9% to 27%. These are excellent return ratios — anything above 20% is considered high-quality. The asset-light model (low fixed assets, high inventory turn at the gross-profit level) is a key driver. Projections assume sustained 22–25% returns as scale benefits stabilize.

**Working Capital — The Watch-Item:** The **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)** — the number of days it takes for a rupee spent on inventory to come back as cash from customers — worsened from 178 days (FY24A) to 215 days (FY26A). This is the analyst's biggest concern. It is driven by:
- **Inventory Days rising from 214 to 241** — TBZ is holding more inventory relative to sales. This is partly a structural feature of jewellery (you need deep SKU variety in wedding jewellery), but the rising trend needs monitoring.
- **Days Payable falling from 36 to 27** — TBZ is paying suppliers faster than before, possibly losing negotiating leverage.
- **Debtor Days negligible** (~0–3 days) — this is excellent. Jewellery is largely sold on cash/advance, so there are virtually no receivables (money owed by customers).

**Dividend per Share (DPS):** Rises from Rs. 0.4 (FY24A) to Rs. 5.3 (FY28E) — a 13x increase. At CMP Rs. 366.80, the FY28E dividend yield would be ~1.4%, modest but growing. Combined with the buy-back potential (likely post-acquisition clarity), the total shareholder return case strengthens.
