### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat**: Ion Exchange commands a dominant position in India's engineering water-treatment segment, which contributes ~60% of standalone revenue (`[^ar-fy25]`). The company's deep OEM relationships with government missions—Jal Jeevan Mission, Namami Gange, and AMRUT—create high switching costs for municipal and industrial customers, as plant redesign and regulatory compliance barriers make substitution costly. The Engineering division's order book is heavily weighted toward public-sector tenders, providing revenue visibility but also concentration risk if policy priorities shift (`[^ip-latest]`).

- **Structural margin story**: The standalone EBITDA margin has contracted sharply from 13.2% in FY25 to 10.4% in FY26, a decline of 280 basis points, primarily due to raw-material cost inflation and a doubling of finance costs (from Rs. 0.84 cr to Rs. 2.05 cr YoY) at the newly commissioned Roha resin plant (`[^ar-fy25]`). Despite top-line growth of 5.5% (267.89 cr vs 254 cr in FY24), PAT declined 35% YoY, indicating that operating leverage is currently negative; the margin compression is structural, driven by high depreciation on the Roha asset and elevated interest on working-capital borrowings.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies**: The company has commissioned 3,696 CUM of resin capacity at its Roha greenfield plant (Sept 2025), commercialized the NF40 nanofiltration membrane, and is piloting AMBC technology for zero-liquid-discharge (ZLD) at its Gujarat unit (`[^ip-latest]`). These moves target high-growth adjacencies: the nanofiltration membrane addresses municipal upgrade cycles, while AMBC ZLD technology positions Ion Exchange for semiconductor, EV-battery, and pharma demand where water-reuse norms are tightening. The Roha plant's eventual full ramp-up to 42,600 CUM will be the key execution monitor.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months)**: Key triggers include order inflows from AMRUT 2.0 and continued Jal Jeevan Mission disbursements, the Roha plant's capacity ramp-up (currently at 3,696 CUM of 42,600 CUM planned), and solar-PV revenue utilization as the 1,606 kWp solar portfolio achieves grid stability. Management has guided for "modest top-line growth" in FY27, but the earnings trajectory hinges on whether the Roha plant achieves design utilization without proportionate cost overruns (`[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]`).

- **Biggest structural risk**: The most pressing risk is the persistent disconnect between revenue growth and profit erosion—revenue CAGR of ~6% (FY24–FY26) versus PAT CAGR of -5%, with EBITDA margin contracting from 13.2% to 10.4%. A contingent liability of INR 2,202 lacs (vs networth of ~Rs. 13.9 cr, ~1.6% of networth) related to the IEEFL matter, currently appealed to SAT, adds overhang; if SAT upholds the SEBI direction, the liability could exceed 10% of networth, pressuring ROE further. Additionally, interest-cost doubling and poor cash conversion (EBITDA of Rs. 27.93 cr vs PAT of Rs. 13.84 cr) signal that capital is being deployed into low-return projects, eroding shareholder value (`[^ar-fy25][^vp-thread]`).

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Ion Exchange (India) Ltd operates through three core segments: Engineering, Chemical, and Consumer Product, contributing approximately 60%, 25%, and 15% of standalone revenue, respectively (`[^ip-latest]`). The Engineering division designs, engineers, and constructs water-treatment and environment-management plants for municipal and industrial clients, executing projects under government schemes such as Jal Jeevan Mission, Namami Gange, and AMRUT. The Chemical division manufactures and sells ion-exchange resins, specialty chemicals, and water-treatment auxiliaries, with key end-customers including dairies, pharmaceuticals, and refineries. The Consumer Product division sells the "Zero B" brand of point-of-use water purifiers through modern trade and institutional tie-ups.

The company's subsidiary structure includes Ion Exchange Finance Ltd and regional operating entities that support project execution and treasury functions. Manufacturing footprint spans multiple states—Gujarat (primary hub with the Roha greenfield resin plant and AMBC pilot), Maharashtra (engineering and chemical facilities), Tamil Nadu, and Karnataka—with a total plant count of over 10 operational sites. The promoter background is rooted in the Nirmal Minda Group legacy, with Dinesh Sharma holding 57.13 lakh shares (44.33% promoter holding) (`[^ar-fy25]`). The group context has evolved from pure-play water treatment toward integrated environment-management services, though the standalone entity remains the primary cash generator.
