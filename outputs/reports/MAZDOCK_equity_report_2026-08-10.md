## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Sole-Builder Moat in Strategic Warship Category**: MAZDOCK is the only Indian shipyard to have built destroyers and conventional submarines for the Indian Navy, a near-monopoly position in the most strategically critical warship segment[^ip-latest]. This is reinforced by 90+ years of operational history and 795+ vessels delivered since 1960, including 25 warships and 3 submarines[^ar-fy25].

- **Structural Margin Story via Cost-Plus Pricing**: The cost-plus pricing model with the Ministry of Defence (MoD) provides revenue visibility and margin stability, with EBITDA margins consistently in the 18–22% band[^cc-transcript]. The current order book of Rs. 35,000+ Cr provides 4–5 years of revenue visibility, de-risking near-term earnings[^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies**: MAZDOCK is expanding into (a) indigenous midget submarine manufacturing under the MSSM program, (b) export markets (recently secured first export order for a naval vessel), and (c) ship repair/refit which carries higher margins (25–30%) and shorter working capital cycles[^cc-transcript]. The P-75I submarine pipeline alone is estimated at Rs. 43,000 Cr[^ip-latest].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months)**: (a) P-75I submarine RFP finalization and award, (b) Project 17A follow-on destroyer order, (c) Q1FY27 results announced 30 July 2026 showing order inflows, (d) potential interim dividend at 93rd AGM on 27 August 2026, (e) export order pipeline expansion to ASEAN and African navies[^cc-transcript][^vp-thread].

- **Biggest Structural Risk**: Single-customer concentration with the Indian Navy (~95% of revenue) creates execution and budget allocation risk[^vp-thread]. Any delay in MoD approvals, budget cuts, or shift in procurement policy could materially impact the order pipeline. Additionally, PSU governance, slow decision-making, and limited pricing power under cost-plus contracts remain structural headwinds[^vp-thread].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core Business Model**: MAZDOCK is primarily engaged in building and repairing ships, submarines, and various types of vessels and related engineering products for domestic and international clients, with a dominant focus on the defence sector[^ar-fy25]. The company operates on a cost-plus pricing model with the Indian Navy, providing revenue visibility but limiting margin upside[^cc-transcript].

- **Revenue Split by Division**:
  - New Construction (Warships & Submarines): ~85% of revenue[^ip-latest]
  - Ship Repair & Refit: ~15% of revenue[^ip-latest]
  - Exports: <2% of revenue (nascent)[^cc-transcript]

- **Key OEM/End Customers per Division**:
  - Warships: Indian Navy (primary), Indian Coast Guard[^ar-fy25]
  - Submarines: Indian Navy (sole client for Scorpene-class, future P-75I)[^ip-latest]
  - Ship Repair: Indian Navy, Indian Coast Guard, commercial vessels[^cc-transcript]
  - Exports: Friendly foreign navies (recently secured first export order)[^cc-transcript]

- **Subsidiary Structure**: MAZDOCK operates as a standalone entity with no material subsidiaries; the company is a Miniratna Category-I PSU under the Ministry of Defence[^ar-fy25].

- **Manufacturing Footprint**: Single integrated dockyard in Mumbai (Mazagon), strategically located at the headquarters of the Western Naval Command of the Indian Navy[^ip-latest]. The dockyard includes multiple construction bays, dry docks, and fitting-out berths. The company is evaluating capacity expansion at the Mumbai site and potential greenfield expansion[^vp-thread].

- **Promoter Background & Group Context**: Promoted by the Government of India (81.22% holding), MAZDOCK was incorporated as a private company in 1934 and taken over by the Government in 1960[^ar-fy25]. The company was listed on the stock exchanges in 2018 via a disinvestment IPO. It is part of the broader Indian defence PSU ecosystem under the Ministry of Defence[^ar-fy25].
