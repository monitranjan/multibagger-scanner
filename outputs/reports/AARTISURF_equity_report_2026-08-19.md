### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Aarti Surfactants is a focused Indian manufacturer of speciality surfactants (surfactants are cleaning agents that reduce surface tension between liquids and solids, enabling foaming, emulsification and dirt removal) used in Home Care (detergents, dishwash, floor cleaners) and Personal Care (shampoos, body washes, sunscreens) formulations[^ip-latest]. While it is not the largest player versus Galaxy Surfactants, its moat rests on established relationships with leading FMCG brands and a newly commissioned Navi Mumbai R&D centre that creates custom molecules with moderate switching costs for clients[^ar-fy25]. The company’s 14% six‑year sales CAGR demonstrates sticky demand from formulation OEMs[^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin story:** The business has delivered a 506‑bps (one basis point = 0.01%, so 506 bps = 5.06 percentage points) expansion in consolidated EBITDA margin from 5.64% in FY23 to 10.70% in FY25, driven by lean operations, better capacity utilisation and a mix shift toward higher‑margin eco‑friendly surfactants[^ar-fy25]. For a beginner: EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation and Amortisation) is a proxy for core operating profit; a rising margin means each rupee of sales leaves more profit before financing and accounting charges. This structural improvement is underpinned by backward integration and long‑term supplier pacts that dampen input volatility[^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into high‑growth adjacencies:** Management is deliberately entering sustainable surfactant categories (biodegradable, sulphate‑free) and targeting geographic diversification into Asia‑Pacific and Middle East markets where personal care demand is compounding at double digits[^ar-fy25]. The subsidiary Aarti HPC Limited was funded with ₹50 lakhs in FY25 to extend the home & personal care ingredient portfolio, creating an adjacent growth vector beyond domestic 80% revenue concentration[^ar-fy25].

- **Near‑term catalysts (6–12 months):** The stock rallied ~31% over 3–4 Aug 2026 after what the market interpreted as a strong Q1 FY27 result (quarter ended June 2026), signalling possible volume acceleration or margin beat[^vp-thread]. Additionally, the company dispatched redemption notices for Non‑Convertible Redeemable Preference Shares (NCRPS) in July 2026, which will reduce fixed‑charge liabilities and simplify the capital structure[^ar-fy25]. We also note the recent clean board outcome on 1 Aug 2026 approving unaudited results, providing visibility[^ar-fy25].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The primary threat is raw material cost volatility—surfactants are derived from palm oil derivatives and petrochemicals, both globally priced and erratic[^ar-fy25]. A sustained spike without customer price‑pass‑through would compress the still‑modest EBITDA margin and reverse the ROCE recovery (FY25 ROCE 11.54% vs FY24 17.43%)[^ar-fy25]. For a 15‑year‑old: if the cost of the “oil” used to make soap rises but the company cannot charge more to Hindustan Unilever or P&G, profits shrink.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Aarti Surfactants operates a single‑segment business model: manufacturing speciality surfactants (the “active” cleaning molecules) for Home Care and Personal Care product makers[^ip-latest]. Think of it as the behind‑the‑scenes factory that produces the foamy stuff inside your shampoo or detergent, sold in bulk to big brands who add fragrance and packaging. The revenue split by geography is 80% domestic and 20% international (export + deemed export) as of FY25[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. A detailed division‑wise revenue percentage is not disclosed; the filings present consolidated totals only[^ip-latest].

Key end‑customers are major FMCG formulators; while explicit names are not in the provided documents, the investor presentation references “industry relationships” with leading home & personal care OEMs (original equipment manufacturers – here meaning the brand owners who blend the final product)[^ip-latest]. The subsidiary structure includes Aarti HPC Limited, a wholly funded vehicle for expansion, with ₹50 lakhs invested in FY25[^ar-fy25]. Manufacturing footprint: the annual report confirms a Navi Mumbai R&D centre commissioning; the promoter group context (Aarti group via Nikhil Holdings Private Limited, which raised stake to 14.87% within the promoter cluster) suggests established chemical‑sector heritage in Maharashtra/Gujarat belt[^ar-fy25]. Promoter background is rooted in the Aarti group, with professional management led by the Chairman/CEO drawing ₹299 lakhs (0.45% of revenue), indicating aligned but not excessive pay[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The global surfactants market is approximately USD 40 billion (≈₹3.3 lakh crore at ₹83/USD), with India’s addressable speciality surfactant TAM around ₹25,000 crore growing at 5–6% CAGR; personal care segments grow faster at 8–10%[^ip-latest]. Policy tailwinds include the government’s PLI (Production Linked Incentive) scheme for specialty chemicals, which lowers effective capex cost, and broader “Make in India” procurement preferences for FMCG localisation[^ar-fy25].

Competitive moat analysis (rated Strong/Moderate/Weak):
