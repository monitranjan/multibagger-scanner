### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**(1) MARKET LEADERSHIP & EXPORT-FORTRESS MOAT IN STAINLESS-STEEL WASHERS:**
Ratnaveer Precision Engineering is the **#1 exporter of stainless-steel washers from India**, a position it has held consistently through 15 consecutive years of EEPC (Engineering Exports Promotion Council) Star Performer awards from 2003 to 2025 [^ip-latest]. Think of a washer as a small metal disc with a hole that sits under a bolt head to distribute load and prevent loosening — Ratnaveer makes **1.8 billion+ of these washers annually** [^ip-latest]. The washers segment alone contributed **52.38% of FY26 revenue** (Rs. 565 Cr of Rs. 1,078 Cr total), making it the largest single contributor [^ip-latest]. The company serves **219 active clients across 31 countries**, supported by **89 global distribution partners** [^ip-latest]. This is not just scale — it is "scale + global customer stickiness + 25 years of operational history" (the company was founded in 2002-2003) that creates a meaningful moat [^ar-fy25]. Why does this matter? Because precision washers are a high-volume, low-differentiation product where only the lowest-cost, highest-quality integrated producer wins export contracts from global fastener distributors in Europe (Germany, UK, France, Italy) and North America (USA, Canada) [^ip-latest]. Ratnaveer is that producer.

**(2) STRUCTURAL MARGIN STORY — 25% CAGR ACROSS REVENUE, EBITDA & PAT WITH OPERATING LEVERAGE:**
Over the FY24-FY26 period, the company has delivered exceptional financial compounding: Revenue grew from **Rs. 602.40 Cr (FY24) → Rs. 895.53 Cr (FY25) → Rs. 1,078.41 Cr (FY26)**, a 33.98% CAGR; EBITDA expanded from **Rs. 57 Cr → Rs. 94.2 Cr → Rs. 121.88 Cr**, a 46.23% CAGR; and PAT jumped from **Rs. 31.05 Cr → Rs. 46.53 Cr → Rs. 64.30 Cr**, a 43.92% CAGR [^ip-latest]. EBITDA margins improved from **10.2% in FY25 to 11.4% in FY26**, and Q4 FY26 alone delivered a sharp **+535 bps YoY margin expansion (8.97% → 14.32%)** [^ip-latest]. The drivers are: (a) **backward and forward vertical integration** — the company runs its own melting/rolling mill for stainless steel coils and sheets, eliminating intermediate margin leakage [^cc-transcript]; (b) **80-90% green power operations** reducing energy costs [^ip-latest]; (c) **operating leverage** — production volume scaled from 36,129 MT (FY25) to **46,668 MT (FY26)**, a +29.17% YoY increase, spreading fixed costs [^ip-latest]. Management has consistently guided for ~25% CAGR across all three P&L lines, which is being delivered [^cc-transcript]. For a beginner: this means every additional Rs. 100 of revenue is dropping more money to the bottom line than before — that is what "margin expansion" means in plain English.

**(3) DIVERSIFICATION INTO A STEP-CHANGE ADJACENCY — COPPER CLAD LAMINATE (CCL) FOR INDIA'S PCB INDUSTRY:**
The single biggest transformation in Ratnaveer's history is the planned entry into **FR-4 grade Copper Clad Laminate (CCL)**, the foundational substrate material used in **Printed Circuit Boards (PCBs)** which go into every smartphone, laptop, EV, 5G base station, and defense electronic system [^ip-latest]. In simple terms: CCL is the "skeleton" on which all electronic circuits are built — without it, no PCB; without PCB, no electronics. India's PCB market is projected at **Rs. 1,76,790 Cr by 2032 (16.4% CAGR)**, and the Indian CCL market is estimated at **Rs. 29,880 Cr by 2031** [^ip-latest]. Critically, **>90% of India's CCL demand is currently imported** — predominantly from China, Taiwan, and Korea [^ip-latest]. Ratnaveer aims to be **India's first integrated CCL manufacturer** with **5 production lines planned**, of which Line 1 is targeted for commissioning in **November 2026** with machinery already inspected in China [^ip-latest]. The economics are exceptional: CCL division is projected at **~20% EBITDA margin and ~13% PAT margin**, vs. existing stainless steel business at 9-10.5% EBITDA — a **doubling of profitability per Rs. of revenue** [^cc-transcript]. This single adjacency could potentially **redefine Ratnaveer's multiple** from a commodity SS manufacturer (industry P/E ~21.6x) to a specialty electronics-materials play.

**(4) NEAR-TERM CATALYSTS (NEXT 6-12 MONTHS):**
Three material catalysts are converging in the next 6-12 months that should drive re-rating: (a) **November 2026 — CCL Line 1 commissioning**, the single largest near-term event, where commercial production would validate the import-substitution thesis and could trigger institutional investor interest [^cc-transcript]; (b) **September 2-9, 2026 — Rs. 330 Cr Rights Issue at Rs. 264/share**, which has already been Board-approved and represents a discount of ~10.5% to CMP, signalling management's confidence in equity raising at current levels [^vp-thread]; (c) **Closure of European acquisition within 10-12 months**, targeting a precision components business to acquire customers and is a pure inorganic growth lever for the existing SS business [^cc-transcript]. Additional supporting catalysts include: **effective tax rate guidance of 13-15%** (boosting PAT vs. historical effective rate), **80-85% utilization target on CCL by 2028** (when first line scales) [^cc-transcript], and the recent **appointment of Mrs. Seema Sanghavi as Whole-Time Director** (signalling next-generation promoter family formalization into the board) [^ip-latest].

**(5) BIGGEST STRUCTURAL RISK — CCL EXECUTION & CHINESE TECHNOLOGY DEPENDENCE:**
The single largest risk to the thesis is **execution of the CCL project**, which represents ~60% of the incremental Rs. 2,500 Cr revenue target [^cc-transcript]. Specifically: (a) **Technology partner concentration risk** — the Chinese technology partner's name has been deliberately withheld, and management confirmed "no royalty" payments but the entire production formula, machine specifications, and epoxy formulation are sourced from a single Chinese vendor [^cc-transcript]. Any disruption in that relationship (export controls, geopolitical events, IP disputes) could derail Line 1 commissioning; (b) **Pre-order visibility is soft** — management candidly admitted they have only "soft confirmations" and not binding purchase orders from PCB customers for CCL output [^cc-transcript]; (c) **Capacity ramp risk** — management targets 80-85% utilization by 2028, but this is highly dependent on customer qualification cycles in the conservative PCB industry which typically take 6-12 months [^cc-transcript]; (d) **Capital structure stress** — Debt/Equity has expanded from 0.50x (FY25) to fund the capex, and contingent liabilities stand at **Rs. 163.33 Cr (44% of Net Worth)** as per FY25 annual report, which is a red flag warranting monitoring [^ar-fy25]. Investors should size positions recognizing that CCL is a J-curve bet — meaningful cash flows will only start accruing from FY28.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**CORE BUSINESS MODEL:**
Ratnaveer Precision Engineering Limited is a **vertically integrated stainless steel products manufacturer** headquartered in **Vadodara, Gujarat**, with 25 years of operational history since incorporation in 2002-2003 [^ar-fy25]. The company operates a **"mine-to-mill-to-market"** model — it procures stainless steel scrap, processes it through its own melting and rolling facilities, and converts it into finished precision components [^cc-transcript]. This backward integration into the **melting/rolling division** for stainless steel coils, sheets, and flat products is what differentiates Ratnaveer from trading houses or job-work converters who simply buy and resell SS products [^cc-transcript]. Forward integration extends to **finishing, distribution, and direct export to 31 countries** [^ip-latest]. In simple terms for a 15-year-old: imagine a flour mill that also owns the wheat farm, the bakery, AND the delivery trucks — that is Ratnaveer in the stainless-steel world. This end-to-end control allows the company to capture margin at every step of the value chain [^cc-transcript].

Washer production volumes exceed **1.5-1.8 billion units annually** across **3,300+ SKU variants in 12 specialized grades** [^ip-latest]. The Fasteners division has **3,500+ total SKUs** (metric and inch standards) [^ip-latest].

**PROJECTION RATIONALE & ASSUMPTIONS:**

The FY27E and FY28E forecasts are anchored to the management's articulated guidance from the FY26 Q4 earnings call and the Q4 FY26 investor presentation. Revenue for the existing stainless-steel business is projected to grow at 25% YoY in both FY27E and FY28E — in line with management's stated CAGR aspiration of "25% over the next two years" and the FY27E target of Rs. 1,350 Cr for legacy operations. An additional ~Rs. 10 Cr is layered in for FY27E from the Copper Clad Laminate (CCL) Line 1, which is scheduled to commission in November 2026, contributing roughly four to five months of partial-year revenue. For FY28E, full-year Line 1 (Rs. 22 Cr) plus the ramp-up of Line 2 (~Rs. 40 Cr) add incremental Rs. 60-65 Cr, taking the consolidated top line to Rs. 1,700 Cr. EBITDA margin is assumed to expand by ~50 bps annually (10.5% → 11.0% → 12.0%) — driven by (i) operating leverage on the legacy SS business as capacity utilization improves post the recent capex, (ii) blending of the higher-margin CCL division (guided 20% standalone EBITDA), and (iii) backward integration benefits. PAT is grown at 30% in FY27E and 46% in FY28E, reflecting operating-leverage kick-in plus depreciation stepping up post CCL commissioning. The effective tax rate is normalized at 18% (vs volatile 15-23% historically) — at the higher end of management's 13-15% guidance band to account for MAT reversals and ind-AS adjustments. Share count for FY27E onwards reflects the Rs. 330 Cr rights issue (issue price Rs. 264) adding 1.25 Cr new equity shares to the FY26 ending base of 7.95 Cr, taking fully-diluted shares to ~9.20 Cr. The dividend payout is held at 0% across the projection period as the company is in a heavy capex phase (CCL + European acquisition) and is reinvesting all cash flows back into growth.

**Commentary on Table 1:**

The top line has nearly doubled in just two years — from Rs. 602 Cr in FY24A to Rs. 1,069 Cr in FY26A — a 33% CAGR. The deceleration from 48% YoY in FY25A to 20% YoY in FY26A reflects base-effect normalisation after the post-IPO growth surge, but the absolute incremental revenue (Rs. 177 Cr added in FY26 alone) is the largest in the company's history. EBITDA margin has expanded from 9.5% to 10.5% over the same period, indicating that the company is not just chasing volume but extracting incremental operating leverage as fixed costs get spread over a larger base. The forecast margin expansion to 12% by FY28E is the most critical assumption to monitor — it depends on (a) successful CCL commissioning without cost overruns, (b) the legacy SS business sustaining 10-11% margins despite raw-material (nickel) volatility, and (c) absence of further one-off start-up costs. The sharp jump in depreciation (Rs. 25 Cr FY26A → Rs. 45 Cr FY28E) and interest (Rs. 20 Cr → Rs. 32 Cr) is the inevitable consequence of funding the ~Rs. 400 Cr CCL capex — this will compress near-term PBT margins but is necessary for the long-term margin uplift. Other income has nearly tripled in FY26A (Rs. 10 Cr) due to treasury gains on the large cash pile post fundraise — this tailwind is expected to persist in FY27E (Rs. 14 Cr) but normalise as cash is deployed into CCL capex. EPS growth of 12% (FY26A) → 30% (FY28E) is understated relative to PAT growth because of the equity dilution from the rights issue — this is the "cost" of funding the CCL project without straining the balance sheet.

**Overall Earnings Quality Rating: MEDIUM**

The headline P&L shows strong top-line growth, margin expansion, and improving cash conversion — all of which are positive. However, the MEDIUM rating reflects a cluster of structural red flags that prevent a HIGH classification:

- **Receivables surge (RED)**: The 166% jump in trade receivables against only 20% revenue growth in FY26 is the single most concerning data point. This could indicate either (a) channel-stuffing / aggressive credit policy to push volume, or (b) one-off large orders with extended payment terms. Investors should specifically check the Q1 FY27 receivables trend — if the absolute number stays at Rs. 175 Cr or rises further despite higher revenue, it is a major warning.

- **Contingent Liabilities (RED)**: At 44% of net worth, contingent liabilities (typically disputed tax demands, pending litigation) are uncomfortably high. While the auditor's report is clean (no provisioning required), the size warrants ongoing monitoring of any adverse outcomes.

- **RPT Concentration (RED)**: The Rs. 226 Cr of outstanding promoter loans is a governance red flag. Although the company states these are at arm's length and approved by the Audit Committee, the magnitude relative to revenue and net worth suggests the promoter group is using the listed entity as a partial credit source. This should normalise over time as the company deleverages internally.

- **Tax Rate Volatility (AMBER)**: The swing from 23% (FY25) to 15% (FY26) and management's 13-15% forward guidance suggests a one-off MAT credit or tax holiday benefit in FY26 that may not recur. Our 18% normalized assumption for FY27E/FY28E is conservative, but the actual rate could be lower, providing PAT upside.

- **Auditor Tenure (AMBER)**: The recent reappointment of the statutory auditor for a second 5-year term is compliant with Companies Act 2013 rotation norms, but the lack of auditor change during a period of significant business transformation (CCL entry, European acquisition) is a mild governance concern.

**Watch-Points for the Next Two Quarters:**
1. Trade receivables absolute level and days metric in Q1 FY27 — must not sustain above 60 days.
2. Status of contingent liabilities disclosure in Q2 FY27 — any material movement in disputed tax/regulatory matters.
3. Promoter loan balance trajectory — should reduce as company deleverages.
4. Effective tax rate disclosure in Q1 FY27 — should be in 15-18% range as guided.
5. Auditor continuity / change at the next rotation window (FY30) — for governance reset.

### SECTION 8 — VALUATION

**Valuation Philosophy:** Ratnaveer is a stainless-steel precision engineering franchise transitioning into a higher-margin electronics substrate (CCL) business. Commodity cyclicality in the legacy SS business distorts P/E signals quarter-to-quarter, so we anchor valuation on **EV/EBITDA** (normalizes capex/D&A differences across peers) and **EV/tonne** (sector-standard capacity-based metric for metals processors), with P/E used as a cross-check[^ip-latest]. All forward estimates are on a **post-rights-issue diluted share base of ~8.05 Cr** (6.80 Cr existing + 1.25 Cr from the ₹330 Cr rights issue at ₹264/share)[^cc-transcript].
