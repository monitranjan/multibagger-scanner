### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Aditya Infotech, operating under the flagship CP PLUS brand, has achieved a dominant 43.3% volume share in the Indian video surveillance market as of FY26, effectively doubling its share from 20.2% in FY25 [^ar-fy25]. This explosive gain in market share was catalyzed by the mandatory STQC (Standardization Testing and Quality Certification) norms implemented in April 2025, which forced non-compliant Chinese brands to exit the market, leaving CP PLUS as the primary beneficiary due to its localized manufacturing and certified product stack [^cc-transcript]. The moat here is not just regulatory arbitrage but deep channel penetration with 800+ distributors and 1,800+ system integrators that create a high barrier to entry for new domestic challengers [^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin story:** The company is undergoing a profound margin expansion structural story. In Q1 FY27, gross margin expanded by 810 basis points (bps) year-on-year to 30.8%, while EBITDA margin jumped 605 bps to 14.8% [^ip-latest]. To explain in simple terms, a "basis point" is one-hundredth of a percentage point, so 810 bps means an 8.1% absolute improvement in profitability at the gross level before operating costs [^cc-transcript]. This is driven by a richer product mix—IP (Internet Protocol) cameras now constitute 79% of the CP PLUS portfolio versus older analog tech—and the ability to pass on 10-20% price hikes gradually without losing volume [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, finance costs dropped 59% due to IPO-driven debt repayment, boosting PAT margins to 10.1% [^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond core surveillance, the management is deliberately diversifying into adjacent high-growth verticals. They are launching Home IoT products (video door phones, smart locks, door bells) and the premium "CP PLUS PRO" line with global shutter and explosion-proof cameras developed in their Taiwan R&D center [^ip-latest]. Furthermore, the Corelink Cable Technology JV with Orient Cables is on track to contribute "not less than 5%" of overall accessories business, creating a captive cable supply chain and improving BOM (Bill of Materials—the list of parts needed to build a device) localization [^cc-transcript]. Exploratory R&D in machine vision cameras, drone gimbal modules, and industrial AMRs (Autonomous Mobile Robots—self-navigating factory bots) widens the total addressable market beyond traditional security [^ip-latest].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The most concrete near-term catalyst is the capacity expansion at the Kadapa greenfield facility (housing and enclosure plant) becoming operational by Q3 FY27, which improves backward integration and reduces import dependency [^cc-transcript]. The Rajasthan Cable JV facility (~1 lakh sqft) will commence commercial production by end of FY27, adding a new revenue line with higher stickiness [^cc-transcript]. On the product front, CP PLUS Pro and Home IoT launches targeted for Q3/Q4 FY27 will likely re-rate the consumer discretionary multiple as investors recognize recurring IoT economics [^ip-latest]. Export opportunities from the enterprise portfolio are expected to materialize in 18-24 months, but initial LOIs could surface sooner as a sentiment boost [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The primary structural risk remains global semiconductor and memory supply chain tightness, as 35% of the bill-of-materials is still imported Taiwanese semiconductors [^cc-transcript]. While management hedges 85-90% of forex receivables weekly, a severe rupee depreciation could squeeze margins if price hikes lag [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, the competitive landscape has 40+ domestic players attempting to fill the void left by Chinese brands, which could trigger localized pricing wars in the entry-level segment and compress the premium multiple the stock currently enjoys [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** Aditya Infotech Limited (founded 1995) is fundamentally a "make-and-market" electronics manufacturer focused on electronic security and surveillance [^ar-fy25]. Think of them as the "Maruti" of Indian CCTV—they design, manufacture, and sell cameras, recorders (NVR/DVR), and AI software under the CP PLUS brand [^ar-fy25]. Their revenue model is B2B2C: they sell to distributors and system integrators who then deploy at homes, offices, and government projects, meaning they do not carry heavy direct consumer credit risk [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue split by division:** The company reports consolidated financials without granular segment splits, but channel checks and concalls reveal the CP PLUS brand contributes ~87% of total Q1 FY27 revenue, with the remaining from subsidiary/contract manufacturing and other brands like EYRA and NEXIVUE for tier-2/3 penetration [^cc-transcript]. Within CP PLUS, IP products (network cameras that stream video over internet) are 79% of the mix, while analog is fading fast as STQC favors digital compliance [^cc-transcript]. Software/AI SaaS (like CP PLUS.AI analytics) is in early commercialization but currently a tiny fraction of ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue—subscription income) [^ip-latest].

**Key OEM/end customers:** End customers span government (smart city projects), enterprise (banks, factories), and SMB/retail [^ar-fy25]. They partner with Qualcomm and L&T Semiconductor to co-develop SoCs (System-on-Chip—the brain of the camera that processes video) which deepens their tech moat [^ar-fy25].

**Subsidiary structure:** The group has 3 wholly-owned subsidiaries: AIL Dixon Technologies Private Limited (joint manufacturing vehicle with Dixon), Shenzhen CP Plus International Ltd (procurement support in China for non-restricted parts), and Aditya Infotech Taiwan Co. Ltd (R&D hub for high-end chips and lenses) [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing footprint:** Primary plant is in Kadapa, Andhra Pradesh with capacity of 2.5 million units/month, utilizing 85-90% of capacity [^cc-transcript]. A second cluster is planned in Greater Noida, and the Kadapa enclosure plant expands in Q3 [^ip-latest]. They have 50 branch offices and 54 RMA (Return Merchandise Authorization—repair centers) across 500+ cities ensuring service depth [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter background:** The Khemka family (Hari Shanker Khemka as Chairman, Aditya Khemka as MD, Ananmay Khemka as WTD) has run the business for three decades, transitioning from trading to domestic manufacturing [^ar-fy25]. Group context is purely surveillance/electronics, with no unrelated diversifications that could distract capital allocation [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**TAM and growth:** The Indian video surveillance market is estimated at Rs. 15,000 Cr (approx USD 1.8bn) and growing at a 15-20% CAGR, accelerated by security concerns and smart city pushes [^ar-fy25]. With CP PLUS at 43% share, there is still headroom as the market formalizes and unorganized players exit [^cc-transcript].

**Policy tailwinds:** The April 2025 STQC mandate is the single biggest tailwind—it acts as a non-tariff barrier against cheap Chinese imports that lacked local testing [^ar-fy25]. PLI (Production Linked Incentive—government cash rewards for local manufacturing) for electronics indirectly benefits their Kadapa expansion [^ip-latest].

**Moat analysis:**
- *Switching costs:* Moderate. Once a system integrator trains staff on CP PLUS software, switching is cumbersome, but large enterprises can mandate changes if pricing diverges too far [^cc-transcript].
- *Scale:* Strong. At 2.5M units/month and 43% share, they achieve economies of scale in plastic/metallic housing (30M annual capacity) that smaller rivals cannot match, lowering per-unit fixed cost [^ar-fy25].
- *Pricing power:* Strong. They have executed 10-20% price hikes seamlessly due to brand trust and lack of compliant alternatives, protecting margin in inflation [^cc-transcript].
