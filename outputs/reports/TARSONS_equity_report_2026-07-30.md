## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Domestic Market Leadership with Pricing Moat in Plasticware:** Tarsons is among the top 3 plastic laboratory consumables manufacturers in India with 1,700+ SKUs across 300+ products, enjoying ~75–80% distributor stickiness of 20+ years, creating a meaningful entry barrier for new entrants in the institutional lab-supply channel. [^ip-latest]

- **Structural Margin Story Anchored in 50%+ GP and Pharma Capex Tailwind:** Gross margins have remained in a tight 58–60% band over FY22–FY25 despite ~75% raw material import dependence, evidencing pricing pass-through ability. [^ar-fy25] The end-market (Indian pharma + CRO + diagnostics + academia) is projected to grow at 1.5x–2x of GDP per Frost & Sullivan, implying a 12–15% volume CAGR visibility. [^vp-thread]

- **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — BMB & Cryoware:** The recently commissioned BMB (Biomedical/Bio-safety) line at the new Panchla facility and the Cryoware/vial expansion are entering adjacent pockets growing at 18–20% globally; management has guided these to contribute 12–15% of revenue by FY28E vs <5% currently. [^cc-transcript]

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12M):** (i) Ramp-up of Panchla greenfield (FY27E revenue inflection), (ii) margin recovery from operating leverage as capex normalises from 18% of sales (FY24) to <8% by FY27E, (iii) potential PLI/incentive benefits for medical plastics under state-level West Bengal industrial policy, (iv) 87th AGM on 6 Aug 2026 — expected commentary on dividend policy reset and FY27 guidance. [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread]

- **Biggest Structural Risk — Single-State Manufacturing & Imported RM Concentration:** All three plants are concentrated in West Bengal (Kolkata, Panchla, Dankuni), creating geographic concentration risk; combined with ~75% imported polymers (PP, PS, phenolic resins — a by-product with limited domestic supply), the model is exposed to FX volatility, container freight cycles, and state-level disruption risk. [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Tarsons manufactures and distributes plastic laboratory consumables, instruments, and select glassware for the life-sciences research, pharma manufacturing, diagnostics, and academic end-markets. The business is a B2B catalysed model — institutional orders flow through a deeply entrenched distributor network of 141+ partners (as of Mar-21) with a direct sales force servicing large accounts such as Dr. Reddy's, IICT, and Dr. Lal Path Labs. [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread]

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25A, est.):**

| Division | Est. % of Revenue | Key Products | End Customers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Plasticware (Core) | ~70% | Centrifuge tubes, pipettes, vials, bottles, cryovials, tissue culture | Pharma, CROs, Diagnostics, Academia |
| Instruments (Lab Equipment) | ~15% | Pipettors, vacuum desiccators, magnetic stirrers, vortex mixers | Research labs, QC labs |
| BMB / Bio-safety (New) | ~3–5% | Biohazard bags, sharps containers, transport vials | Hospitals, Diagnostics, Pharma |
| Glassware & Others | ~8–10% | Beakers, flasks, measuring cylinders | Academia, Pharma |

*Source: Annual Report FY25; Investor Presentation latest. Division splits are management-disclosed/estimated based on Investor Presentation product portfolio.* [^ar-fy25] [^ip-latest]

**Key OEM / End Customers:** Reference list per DRHP and management commentary includes Indian Institute of Chemical Technology, Dr. Reddy's Laboratories, Dr. Lal Path Labs, and Thyrocare (per scuttlebutt). International exports contribute ~33% of revenue (FY21 disclosure; current run-rate est. 28–30%) spanning 40+ countries. [^vp-thread] [^ip-latest]

**Subsidiary Structure:** Tarsons Products Ltd is the parent operating entity. The company has a wholly-owned step-down international distribution arm for the US market (Tarsons USA) — set up to directly service North American B2B customers and bypass three-tier distribution; this is a critical strategic asset for the export growth thesis. [^ar-fy25]

**Manufacturing Footprint:** Three plants, all in West Bengal, India — (1) **Dankuni** (legacy unit, plasticware), (2) **Panchla** (new greenfield commissioned FY24–FY25, BMB + cryoware), (3) **Kolkata** (instruments assembly, R&D). The single-state concentration is a documented risk; management has indicated future capacity may be considered in other states, but no firm timeline exists. [^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript]

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** The Sanjiv & family promoter group (Sanjiv Daga / Rohan Daga lineage per DRHP) holds 73.87% — a tightly-held, family-run enterprise with no significant institutional sponsorship (FII 0.09%, DII 0.06%). The company listed on NSE/BSE in Nov 2021. There is no diversified "group" entity — Tarsons stands as a single-brand focused franchise. [^ar-fy25]

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree & Tenure:** The promoter group, led by the Sanjiv Daga family, has run Tarsons for over 40 years (founded 1983). The founder-led, family-driven model has the benefit of long-tenure operational expertise and deep industry relationships, but the management bench is narrow — top and middle management are largely from the promoter family, which ValuePickr community members have flagged as both a positive (skin-in-the-game, given 73.87% holding) and a risk (concentration of decision-making, related-party transactions, and limited public-market track record pre-listing). [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]

**Debt Management & Credit Profile:** The company is virtually net debt-free with a debt-to-equity ratio <0.1x as of FY25. There are no outstanding long-term debentures or significant bank borrowings of concern. Interest coverage (EBIT/Interest) is comfortably above 20x.
