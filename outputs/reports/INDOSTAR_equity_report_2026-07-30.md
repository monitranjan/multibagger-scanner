## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Brookfield-anchored liability franchise, but under-monetised asset side:** IndoStar is now 81.36% owned by Brookfield (Everstone fully exited via the 2024 change-in-control transaction), giving the entity one of the lowest-cost liability stacks among mid-sized NBFCs — but the asset book is still in mid-cycle restructuring[^ip-latest]. Spread compression in FY25 (-24.18 EPS print) is more reflective of transition opex and legacy corporate book runoff than franchise deterioration, in our view[^ar-fy25].

- **Retail mix has structurally inflected — Vehicle Finance is the swing factor:** As of Q1FY26, Retail/Vehicle/Home Finance now constitute ~55% of AUM vs. ~26% in FY18[^ip-latest]. The Vehicle Finance vertical — led by ex-Shriram management — is the highest-yielding segment (yield ~16-17%) and the cleanest growth lever over FY26-28E[^cc-transcript]. We model VF AUM compounding at ~30% CAGR through FY28E[^ar-fy25].

- **Margin recovery is a function of corporate book runoff, not new stress:** Gross Stage-3 assets have stabilised at ~3.2% of book (down from peak ~5.8% in FY22)[^ip-latest]. With ~Rs. 1,400 Cr of legacy corporate exposure scheduled to amortise/run-off over FY26-27, the credit-cost-to-AUM ratio normalises from ~3.1% in FY25 to ~1.4% by FY28E — driving ~250-300 bps ROA expansion[^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months):** (i) Q2FY27 disbursement print post-monsoon agri/CV uptick, (ii) potential co-lending/JV announcement in Vehicle Finance leveraging Brookfield's global LP relationships, (iii) potential inclusion in MSCI India Domestic index once float-adjusted FII limit rises above current 0.71%[^vp-thread], (iv) reduction in corporate book provisioning as Stage-2 to Stage-3 migration appears largely complete[^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Concentration in CV/used-CV financing (~38% of VF book) creates direct exposure to freight-cycle volatility and the slow pace of scrappage-policy implementation — historically the single largest tail risk for this segment[^vp-thread]. A delayed scrappage policy or a freight-rate downturn would push credit costs back above 2.5% and derail our FY28E ROA trajectory.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

IndoStar Capital Finance is a Mumbai-headquartered, RBI-registered NBFC operating across four verticals — Corporate Lending (legacy), SME Lending, Vehicle Finance, and Housing Finance (through wholly-owned subsidiary IndoStar Home Finance Pvt. Ltd.)[^ar-fy25]. Incorporated in 2009, the company listed on the bourses in May 2018 and has since undergone a complete ownership transition — from Everstone Capital promoter to Brookfield (now 81.36%) following the 2024 open offer at Rs. 290/share[^vp-thread].

**Revenue / AUM split (Q1FY26):**

**Key OEM / corporate relationships:** Major corporate borrowers historically include names from the infra, manufacturing and renewables space (anonymised in disclosures post Brookfield acquisition for confidentiality). Vehicle Finance originates primarily through proprietary dealer network (~85%) and direct sales (~15%), with no single dealer concentration above 2% of VF book[^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary structure:**
- IndoStar Home Finance Pvt. Ltd. — 100% subsidiary, NHB-registered HFC
- IndoStar Asset Management Pvt. Ltd. — 100% (currently non-material)

**Manufacturing footprint:** Not applicable (financial services entity); however, the company operates ~95 branches across 12 states with Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Rajasthan constituting ~62% of the branch network[^ar-fy25].

**Promoter / group context:** Brookfield Asset Management (NYSE: BAM) is the ultimate parent post the 2024 change-in-control. The Brookfield India platform also controls financial services vehicles in real estate credit and infra debt, providing IndoStar with potential cross-sell and co-lending optionality[^cc-transcript].
