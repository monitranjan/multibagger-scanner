## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Asset-light CFS franchise with high ROCE moat:** Sical operates one of the largest private Container Freight Station (CFS) networks in India, with a notified capacity of ~7.5 lakh TEUs spread across Chennai, Krishnapatnam, Vizag and Mangalore[^ip-latest]. The CFS business is asset-heavy on real estate but generates high incremental ROCE on volume ramp-up; the company's 22.86% ROCE and 37.44% ROE reflect the post-restructuring capital efficiency, materially above pre-FY22 levels[^ar-fy25]. CFS realizations have firmed 8–10% YoY in FY26 on EXIM volume recovery, supporting operating leverage[^cc-transcript].

- **Structural margin expansion from mix shift:** Management is consciously de-risking from low-margin surface transport to higher-margin mining-dedicated rail logistics and integrated EXIM contracts[^ip-latest]. Mining logistics EBIT margins run 200–300 bps above legacy CFS, and incremental contract wins in the coal and iron-ore segments are visible on the order book[^cc-transcript]. Consolidated EBITDA margins have expanded from ~9% in FY23 to ~14% in FY25 and management guides to a glide path of 16–17% by FY27E[^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into mining/rail adjacencies is the next leg:** Sical is one of the few listed Indian players with simultaneous exposure to (i) port-linked CFS, (ii) mining overburden removal, and (iii) dedicated rail freight through a JV with a public sector miner[^ip-latest]. This triple-engine model reduces dependence on volatile EXIM cycles and ties revenue to long-tenor (3–7 year) mining contracts, providing revenue visibility[^vp-thread]. Mining and rail together now contribute ~40% of revenue vs. ~15% three years ago[^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (a) Award of incremental mining contracts in the eastern coal belt — order book expansion is the single largest swing factor for FY27E EPS[^cc-transcript]; (b) Krishnapatnam Port volume ramp as APEX port operator stabilises; (c) Re-rating trigger from any re-initiation of FII/DII coverage given current 0% institutional holding leaves a clean float[^vp-thread]; (d) Reduction in pledged shares / debt which is a structural overhang at peer benchmarks.

- **Biggest structural risk — corporate governance and key-man:** Within a span of seven days in July 2026, both the CFO (Mr. K. Rajavel) and the Company Secretary & Compliance Officer (Ms. Vaishali Jain) resigned, announced to exchanges on 9 July 2026 and 3 July 2026 respectively[^ip-latest]. The simultaneous exit of two KMPs at a 81.26% promoter-owned, thinly-traded microcap warrants a discount in our valuation framework and is the single largest reason we do not ascribe a peer-average multiple to Sical[^vp-thread]. Promoter concentration of 81.26% with zero institutional scrutiny (FII 0.00% / DII 0.00%) is a structural overhang that will only clear with cleaner board disclosures over the next 2–3 quarters.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Sical Logistics Limited is an integrated, multi-modal logistics platform offering end-to-end supply chain solutions spanning port-side container handling, surface transport, rail logistics, mining services and warehousing[^ip-latest]. The company operates across the EXIM and domestic logistics value chain with a primary footprint on India's east and south coasts.

**Revenue split by division (FY25, indicative):**

| Division | % of Revenue | Key End-Customers / Clients |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Container Freight Stations (CFS) | ~35% | EXIM shippers, container lines, CHA community |
| Mining & Overburden Removal | ~30% | Coal India subsidiaries, Singareni, private miners |
| Rail Logistics (incl. JV) | ~10% | NTPC, NMDC, cement companies, container lines |
| Road Transport / Surface | ~20% | Automotive, FMCG, large industrials |
| Warehousing & Others | ~5% | E-commerce, 3PL customers |

*Source: Company disclosures, FY25 Annual Report[^ar-fy25]; FY26 Investor Presentation[^ip-latest].*

**Manufacturing / infrastructure footprint:**

- 4 CFS terminals (Chennai, Krishnapatnam, Vizag, Mangalore) with combined notified capacity of ~7.5 lakh TEUs[^ip-latest]
- ~500+ owned/leased trailers and tractor fleet
- Dedicated rail rakes under long-term contracts
- Mining project sites across Odisha, Jharkhand, Telangana and Chhattisgarh[^cc-transcript]

**Subsidiary / JV structure:**

- Wholly-owned subsidiaries handling mining services, road transport and warehousing[^ar-fy25]
- JV with a public-sector entity for dedicated rail freight — strategic rationale is to secure guaranteed rakes and back-haul EXIM volumes[^ip-latest]
- No material step-down subsidiaries or offshore holding entities

**Promoter background and group context:**

- Promoter holding: 81.26% (held through a closely-held group entity post the 2020 Coffee Day Group exit and subsequent debt resolution)[^ar-fy25]
- The current promoter group took control post the corporate insolvency resolution process; since then the company has executed a balance-sheet cleanup, retiring a significant portion of legacy debt through a one-time settlement with lenders[^cc-transcript]
- Group context: Sical is now a focused, standalone logistics platform without the earlier conglomerate-style cross-holdings that had plagued the capital structure pre-FY22[^vp-thread]

**Valuation discount/premium vs. peers:**

Sical trades at 10.9x P/E vs. the peer median of ~26x, a ~58% discount[^ip-latest]. The discount is only partially explained by: (i) lower scale (1/50th of CONCOR's market cap), (ii) historical balance-sheet stress still remembered by the market, and (iii) zero institutional holding meaning limited discovery. We argue the discount is overdone given (a) ROCE of 22.9% is the highest in the peer set, (b) ROE of 37.4% is industry-leading, and (c) the company has been FCF-positive for three consecutive years post-restructuring[^cc-transcript]. We apply a target P/E of ~20x (vs. peer median 26x) — a 25% governance-driven discount on the multiple, while still implying 84% multiple expansion from current levels. On FY27E EPS of Rs. 6.50, this yields our Rs. 130 target.
