### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat**: Steel Strips Wheels commands dominant domestic market share across vehicle segments, with FY23-27 domestic market share for Steel Wheels standing at OTR 39%, Passenger Vehicle 43%, MHCV 53%, 2-3 Wheeler 39%, and Tractor 35% [^ip-latest]; key OEM customer concentration further cements moat, as Mahindra accounts for 56% of Steel Wheels business, Sonalika 48%, Escorts 60%, JCB 62%, and New Holland 31% [^ip-latest]. In the Alloy Wheels PV segment, Maruti Suzuki contributes 36%, Hyundai 25%, Mahindra 56%, Honda an exceptional 96%, MG Motors 100%, and Kia 30% of business [^ip-latest]. The management’s assertion of being the “lowest cost producer ex-China” at 80–90% true [^cc-transcript] combined with technical superiority in wheel design creates a high barrier to entry for new competitors.

- **Structural margin story**: The Q1 FY27 EBITDA per wheel expanded 40 bps year-on-year to INR314 (from INR262 in Q1 FY26), driven by favourable product mix, cost optimization initiatives, and rising share of premium products [^ip-latest]; historical EBITDA margins have compressed from 12.7% in FY22 to 9.9% in FY26, reflecting raw material volatility, yet the management’s focus on premium alloy and knuckle segments—identified as high-margin accretive [^ip-latest, Page 16, 22]—positions the company for margin re-rating as capacity utilization exceeds 95% across all units [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies**: Aluminium Knuckles, contributing Rs. 23.0 crores (2%) of Q1 FY27 revenue, are scaling from 5.0 lakh units capacity in FY26 to 11.0 lakh units in FY27E, with the Bhuj facility driving this ramp [^ip-latest, Page 13, 16]; the CFO confirmed aluminium metal business projected at INR110-130 crores this year, almost doubling from last year [^cc-transcript], while Alloy Wheels export revenue, though declining to Rs. 127 crores in FY27 from Rs. 160 crores in FY26 [^ip-latest, Page 14], is benefiting from tariff normalization across 31+ export destinations [^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months)**: Q1 FY27 revenue surged 27.2% YoY to Rs. 1,510 crores [^ip-latest, Page 1], with two new Bhuj facilities (aluminium wheels 1.2M units/year, knuckles 1.1M units/year) targeting trial production in Q4 FY27 [^cc-transcript]; the order book for knuckles is “almost completely sold out” [^cc-transcript], and capacity utilization across all plants remains above 95%, supporting operating leverage [^ip-latest, Page 1]; export tariff stabilization at a level playing field (10% vs previous higher barriers vs Vietnam/Thailand) per CFO commentary [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk**: The primary structural risk is raw material price volatility, specifically aluminium cost increases exceeding INR150 crores pressure, passed to customers with a timing lag [^cc-transcript]; interest burden remains elevated at 8–9% on ~INR826 crores of FY26 borrowings, with factoring limits of INR400-500 crores explaining P&L interest cost variance [^cc-transcript, ^ar-fy25]; furthermore, the tractor segment—SSWL’s highest value-added business—is monsoon-dependent, and any rural demand slowdown could compress volumes and margins [^ar-fy25, ^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Steel Strips Wheels Limited (SSWL) operates as a standalone entity within the auto ancillaries wheels space, designing and manufacturing steel wheels, alloy wheels, and aluminium steering knuckles for OEM and aftermarket channels [^ip-latest, Page 1]. The Q1 FY27 revenue mix underscores the segment hierarchy: Alloy Wheels contributed Rs. 532.9 crores (35%) of total Rs. 1,510 crores, Steel Wheels Rs. 953.8 crores (63%), and Aluminium Knuckles Rs. 23.0 crores (2%) [^ip-latest, Page 17]; historically, the alloy wheel share has declined from 64% in Q1 FY26 to 35% in Q1 FY27 as steel wheels regained traction, while knuckle contribution rose from 1% to 2% [^ip-latest, Page 17]. Key OEM partnerships are highly concentrated: Steel Wheels business derives 56% from Mahindra, 48% from Sonalika, 60% from Escorts, 62% from JCB, and 31% from New Holland across FY23-27 [^ip-latest, Page 12]; Alloy Wheels PV segment is led by Maruti Suzuki at 36%, Hyundai at 25%, Mahindra at 56%, Honda at an extraordinary 96% model coverage, MG Motors at 100%, and Kia at 30% [^ip-latest, Page 12]. The company’s manufacturing footprint spans multiple states: Mehsana (Gujarat) hosts the primary alloy wheel and steel wheel production lines, with a recent Brownfield expansion for agri-wheel augmentation; Chennai plant services commercial vehicle and truck wheel production; Bhuj (Gujarat) hosts the new aluminium wheel (1.2 Mn units/year) and aluminium knuckle (1.1 Mn units/year) facilities commissioned in FY26, with Saraikela (Jharkhand) housing Hot Rolling Mills for backward steel wheel integration commissioned in FY2026 [^ip-latest, Page 9, 10]. Promoter background: The Garg family, led by Managing Director Dheeraj Garg who holds 29.52% (46.40 million shares), controls a promoter group stake of 77.46%, with zero pledged shares in FY26 [^ar-fy25, ^vp-thread]; the group’s tenure spans decades in automotive components, and the company transitioned from a pure steel wheel player to a diversified wheel portfolio through strategic acquisitions such as AMW Autocomponent Limited at Bhuj in 2024, enabling an additional 1 million aluminium wheel and 0.6 million knuckle capacity [^ip-latest, Page 10].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian auto components industry is estimated at over Rs. 4.5 lakh crores, with the wheel segment constituting a significant sub-segment; SSWL’s FY26 standalone revenue of Rs. 5,182.8 crores positions it among the top three wheel manufacturers in the country [^ip-latest, Page 1]. Over the next five years, the Steel Wheel Market is projected to grow at a CAGR of ~4% p.a., while the Alloy Wheel Market is expected to expand at a much faster CAGR of ~12% p.a., driven by premiumization, EV adoption, and weight-reduction requirements [^ip-latest, Page 16]. Policy tailwinds are substantial: the PLI scheme for auto components incentivizes domestic manufacturing scale, FAME-II subsidies accelerate aluminium knuckle and lightweight wheel adoption in EVs, and the government’s 500 GW renewable energy target by 2030 underpins infrastructure capex that benefits MHCV and tractor wheel demand [^cc-transcript, ^ip-latest]. Competitive moat analysis: Switching costs are Strong for OEM-qualified wheels once type-approved, as re-qualification costs and long-term contracts create barriers, though aftermarket switching is Moderate [^cc-transcript]; Scale is rated Strong, given SSWL’s ~5,200 crore revenue base and top-3 domestic position, enabling cost advantages in raw material procurement [^ip-latest]; Pricing Power is rated Moderate, as the company has demonstrated the ability to pass on input cost increases (e.g., aluminium >INR150 crore pressure) with a quarterly lag, but raw material volatility limits sustained margin expansion [^cc-transcript].
