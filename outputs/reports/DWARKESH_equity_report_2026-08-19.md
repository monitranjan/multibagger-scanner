### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Dwarikesh Sugar operates as a mid-sized integrated player with a crushing capacity of 21,500 TCD (Tons of Cane per Day – think of this as how many tonnes of raw sugarcane the factory can "chew" and process every single day) spread across three plants in Uttar Pradesh, a prime cane-growing belt [^ar-fy25]. Its moat is moderate, built on long-standing relationships with local farmers and an integrated distillery footprint of 337,500 LPD (Liters Per Day) for ethanol production, which smaller standalone mills lack [^ip-latest]. This regional density allows for lower logistics costs in procuring cane compared to distant competitors.

- **Structural margin story:** The company is systematically shifting its revenue mix toward ethanol (Industrial Alcohol), which historically delivers fatter profits than plain sugar. To explain in simple terms: EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) is the raw profit from core operations before paying bank interest or government taxes. In Q1 FY26, the distillery segment posted an EBITDA margin of 15.5% versus sugar’s deeply negative -7.9% in Q1 FY27 [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. Every 100 basis points (bps – a "basis point" is just 0.01%, so 100 bps equals 1%) of margin recovery in ethanol directly lifts consolidated bottom-line resilience [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond making table sugar, Dwarikesh runs 94 MW of cogeneration (this means using one fuel – sugarcane waste called bagasse – to simultaneously make electricity while processing sugar, like a two-for-one deal) and is pivoting to an "Integrated Biorefinery" model [^ip-latest]. It leverages B-heavy molasses (the sticky dark syrup left after sugar is extracted, which can still be fermented into fuel) and sugarcane juice for ethanol under the government’s E20 program (a rule saying 20% of petrol must be ethanol) [^vp-thread]. This strategy de-risks the company from the volatile sugar price cycle.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** We expect firm domestic sugar prices in the Rs. 4,200–4,300 per quintal range due to a tighter national supply-demand balance [^ip-latest]. A pending ethanol procurement price revision by the government is expected "any time now" per management, which would directly boost distillery realizations [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, "Project Neev" – the replacement of red rot (a plant disease that rots sugarcane and slashes sugar output) affected Co 0238 variety with disease-resistant types by Sugar Season 2027 – should restore crushing volumes [^cc-transcript][^ar-fy25].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The foremost risk is agricultural and policy dependency. The business lives or dies by the monsoon and crop health; red rot previously crushed volumes to 243 lakh quintals versus a 268 lakh target [^ar-fy25]. Compounding this, the government sets the SAP (State Advised Price – the fixed rate mills must pay farmers for cane) and the ethanol price; if SAP rises but sugar/ethanol prices stagnate, margins get squeezed, as noted by investor community tracking cost-price mismatches [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** Dwarikesh Sugar Industries operates a classic integrated agro-processing model. It buys sugarcane from farmers, crushes it to extract sugar, diverts the residual molasses to distilleries for ethanol (a clean-burning alcohol fuel), and burns the fibrous bagasse in boilers to generate power sold to the grid. This triple-output model maximizes the value extracted from a single crop [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue split by division (% of revenue):** Based on Q1 FY27 segment reporting, the mix is heavily skewed to sugar, though distillery is rising in strategic importance [^ip-latest]:

| Segment | Q1 FY27 Revenue (Rs. M) | % of Total Segment Rev | Key Dynamics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sugar | 3,205.7 | 89.5% | Volume up 13.3% YoY but realizations soft |
| Distillery | 482.6 | 13.5% | Ethanol sales down 63.9% due to no crushing |
| Cogeneration | 0.5 | Negligible | Power export collapsed 93% YoY |

**Key OEM/end customers per division:** Sugar is sold to domestic bulk traders and wholesalers; ethanol is supplied to government-backed Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs) like IOCL/BPCL under allocated quotas; power is fed to the Uttar Pradesh state electricity grid [^ar-fy25]. There are no export sales in FY26, though management swaps export quotas with coastal mills for a ~Rs. 4,000/MT premium [^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary structure & manufacturing footprint:** The company runs integrated units at Dwarikesh Nagar (162.5 KLPD distillery) and Dwarikesh Dham (175 KLPD distillery), with three sugar plants all located in Uttar Pradesh [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. It is a single listed entity without complex sub-holdings, simplifying the structure for investors.

**Promoter background:** The firm is part of the Dwarikesh Group, led by Managing Director Vijay Banka, who has steered the company through multiple agricultural cycles with a cautious, debt-averse philosophy [^cc-transcript][^ar-fy25]. The promoter group holds 42.44% of shares, ensuring aligned interests [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**TAM and growth:** The Indian sugar industry represents a total addressable market (TAM – the full potential revenue opportunity) of roughly Rs. 1.5 lakh crore (approx $18 billion), while the ethanol blending push adds a $5 billion adjacent opportunity [^vp-thread]. Sugar volume CAGR is muted at 3–4%, but ethanol output is growing double-digits as the E20 mandate forces OMCs to buy more [^ip-latest].

**Policy tailwinds:** The Ethanol Blending Program (E20), state subsidies for distillery capex, and partial decontrol of sugar (removing levy quotas) are structural positives [^vp-thread]. However, unlike PLI schemes for electronics, sugar gets indirect support via fixed ethanol offtake prices.

**Competitive moat analysis:**
- *Switching costs:* Moderate. Farmers are tied to local mills by cane area reservation laws, but they can switch if prices differ [^ar-fy25].
- *Scale:* Moderate. At 21,500 TCD, Dwarikesh is mid-pack; leaders like Balrampur are 2x larger [^ip-latest].
- *Pricing power:* Weak. Sugar MSP and ethanol prices are diktats from New Delhi, not market-set [^vp-thread].
