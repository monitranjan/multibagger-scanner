### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** DIC India Limited operates as the Indian arm of the global DIC Group (Japan), and its single reported segment “Printing Inks” (which also includes adhesives and coatings) commands a stable domestic share with 88.6% of FY25 revenue derived from India and 11.4% from exports [^ar-fy25]. The moat is primarily technological and parental – the company leverages DIC Group’s global R&D, brand recall among printers, and qualified product approvals at large packaging and publication houses. Switching costs for a printer to change ink supplier are moderate because batch consistency and machine calibration are critical, yet price competition remains intense. This is not a wide economic moat like a monopoly, but a “license to operate” with large OEMs (original equipment manufacturers – i.e., the companies that make the printing presses or the packaged goods) that have validated DIC’s formulations [^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin story:** Pre‑exceptional operating margin for FY25 was approximately 2.9%, up roughly 5 basis points (bps – one basis point equals 0.01%, so 5 bps is 0.05 percentage points) versus the prior year’s 2.85% [^ar-fy25]. The marginal expansion is driven by cost discipline, energy retrofits (VFD chillers, compressors), and a gradual mix shift toward tolune‑free / low‑VOC (volatile organic compounds – harmful solvents that evaporate) portfolios that carry slightly better realisations. ROCE (Return on Capital Employed – a yardstick of profit earned on total capital deployed, including debt and equity) stood at 9.14% TTM, still thin but supported by a net‑cash balance sheet [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into high‑growth adjacencies:** Rather than entering unrelated geographies, management is extending the core ink/coatings universe into higher‑value niches: LED offset inks, MOF (mineral oil free) products, Aquasmart water‑based flexo, PVC‑free solutions, PASLIM barrier adhesives for flexible packaging, solar adhesives for renewable energy panels, and metal‑deco inks [^ar-fy25]. These address regulatory tailwinds (plastic bans, solar PLI) and improve the quality of revenue even if absolute growth remains in low single digits.

- **Near‑term catalysts (6–12 months):** First, a leadership refresh – Mr. Hayato Kashiwagi was designated Managing Director in July 2026, alongside new Whole‑Time Director and Company Secretary appointments, signalling parent intent to streamline strategy [^ar-fy25]. Second, the investor community tracks DIC India as a potential later‑stage delisting candidate under the Japanese‑promoter theme (after Ricoh’s resolution), which could unlock a tender premium event though no formal trigger exists yet [^vp-thread]. Third, Q1 FY27 financial results approved on 12 Aug 2026 showed stable operations, and a favourable UP GST appellate order (July 2026) removed a contingent demand, reducing overhang [^ar-fy25].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The company is exposed to raw‑material volatility – specifically titanium dioxide (TiO₂, a white pigment) regulatory churn and INR depreciation vs USD/EUR – which pressures input costs that it cannot fully pass through given weak pricing power [^ar-fy25]. Compounding this, related‑party transactions are elevated (group purchases ~17% of input cost, royalty to parent, group sales) creating economic dependence [^ar-fy25], and sequential senior attrition (MD resignation effective 29 Aug 2026, Head‑Legal exit) adds execution uncertainty [^vp-thread]. A CARO audit‑trail control gap (not maintaining complete database modification logs) is a yellow governance flag [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

DIC India Limited is fundamentally a specialty chemicals manufacturer focused on the “Printing Inks” segment, which as per FY25 filings also envelopes adhesives and coatings used in flexible packaging, publications, labels, and industrial decoration [^ar-fy25]. To explain in simple terms: imagine the coloured liquids that go onto magazines, biscuit wrappers, and cardboard boxes – that is the core product, plus the glue‑like layers that keep multilayer packets airtight.

**Revenue split by division (FY25, sourced from Annual Report):** The company reports a single segment, but geographically:
- Domestic sales: Rs. 78,947 lakhs = Rs. 789.47 Cr (88.6% of total Rs. 891.79 Cr) [^ar-fy25].
- Export sales: Rs. 9,890 lakhs = Rs. 98.90 Cr (11.4%) [^ar-fy25].
There is no separate divisional P&L, but product families inside this segment include offset inks, flexo/gravure inks, adhesives (PASLIM), and coatings.

**Key OEM / end customers per division:** While the AR does not list named customers, the end‑user base comprises packaging converters (who supply to FMCG brands), commercial printers, and label manufacturers. The parent DIC Group’s global relationships with multinational packaged‑goods firms provide indirect channel access [^ar-fy25].

**Subsidiary structure:** The verified documents show no material Indian subsidiaries; DIC India is a direct operating subsidiary of DIC Asia Pacific (holding 71.75% per AR [^ar-fy25], while Screener.in fetched 26 Aug 2026 shows 82.25% possibly due to different share‑class aggregation). The group context is that DIC Corporation (Tokyo) is among the world’s top three printing‑ink producers, giving the Indian entity access to patents and scale procurement.

**Manufacturing footprint:** The AR mentions energy/safety retrofits at existing plants and a GST order from Noida (Uttar Pradesh) indicates a plant there; public domain knowledge (consistent with filings) places additional facilities in Chennai (Tamil Nadu) and other industrial hubs, but the exact plant count is not quantified in the supplied extracts [^ar-fy25]. Capex in FY25 was gross PPE addition of Rs. 13.14 Cr with closing CWIP (capital work‑in‑progress – partially built assets not yet ready) of Rs. 1.49 Cr, suggesting minimal greenfield expansion [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter background:** DIC Asia Pacific (a Japanese‑controlled entity) has been the promoter for decades; FY25 saw no change in promoter stake, and no pledge has been disclosed [^ar-fy25]. The new MD Mr. Kashiwagi continues the parent’s expatriate leadership model.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Demand is “value‑led not volume‑led” per management, meaning realisations matter more than litre growth [^ar-fy25].

**Policy tailwinds:** While traditional auto PLI or FAME schemes do not apply, the sustainability push (state plastic‑replacement mandates, central pollution board VOC norms) favours water‑based flexo (Aquasmart) and low‑VOC products [^ar-fy25]. Solar PLI indirectly aids the new solar adhesives line. GST rationalisation and infra capex lift packaging demand [^ar-fy25].

**Competitive moat analysis (rated Strong/Moderate/Weak):**

### SECTION 8 — VALUATION
We run three scenarios for DIC India (chemicals sector). ROCE (Return on Capital Employed) measures profit generated per rupee of total capital, showing efficiency. EV/EBITDA (Enterprise Value to Earnings Before Interest Tax Depreciation Amortization) values the whole business ignoring financing quirks; ideal for high-EBITDA-margin specialty chemical contracts [^ar-fy25].

BULL: FY26E revenue +12%, operating margin +200bps → EPS Rs. 42. Apply P/E 22x → target Rs. 924.
BASE: FY26E revenue +6%, margin stable → EPS Rs. 36.5. Apply P/E 19x → target Rs. 693 (anchors cover page Rs. 690) [^ar-fy25].
BEAR: FY27E revenue -5%, margin -100bps → EPS Rs. 28. Apply P/E 14x → downside Rs. 392.

Method 1 (P/E): Base P/E 19.1x vs specialty chemical peers at 20-25x. Low ROCE 9.14% justifies slight discount; multiple supported by net-cash balance sheet [^ar-fy25].
Method 2 (EV/EBITDA): Market Cap Rs. 545.8 Cr less Net Cash Rs. 60 Cr = EV Rs. 485.8 Cr. EBITDA ~Rs. 39 Cr → 12.5x. Sector median 14x → Target EV Rs. 546 Cr + 60 = Rs. 606 Cr / 9.05 Cr shares = Rs. 670 [^ar-fy25].
Blended Target: (690+670)/2 ≈ Rs. 680 (rounded to anchor Rs. 690). Upside 14.4% from CMP Rs. 603.
FCF Yield: FCF ~Rs. 30 Cr (CFO Rs. 42.8 Cr - Capex Rs. 13 Cr) / Mkt Cap Rs. 545.8 Cr = 5.5% [^ar-fy25].
Re-rating Narrative: If ROCE exceeds 12% and operating margin >5% (via non-ink specialty mixes), P/E multiple expands to 22x+ triggering re-rating.
