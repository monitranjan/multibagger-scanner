### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership & Moat in Automotive Aesthetics:** SJS Enterprises is the dominant organised player in India's automotive decorative components space, commanding leadership in decals, appliques, overlays, 3D lux logos, aluminium badges, and in-mold decorative (IMD) parts [^ip-latest]. The company has delivered 27 consecutive quarters of outperformance versus the underlying automotive industry, a track record that underscores deep entrenchment with OEM customers and high switching costs [^cc-transcript]. This consistency is rare in the auto components space and reflects SJS's ability to win design mandates, increase content per vehicle, and cross-sell across product categories [^ar-fy25].

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story:** EBITDA margins have expanded from 26.4% in FY23 to 29.6% in FY26, and further to 30.0% in Q1 FY27—a 239 bps YoY expansion [^ip-latest]. This margin trajectory is driven by a richer product mix (new generation products contributed ~24% of Q1 FY27 revenue), higher export contribution (exports grew 83.2% YoY in Q1 FY27), and operational leverage [^cc-transcript]. The company has demonstrated a robust raw material cost pass-through mechanism with customers, though with a 1-2 quarter lag [^cc-transcript]. With EBITDA margins now at 30% and ROCE at 37.2%, SJS operates at profitability levels typically associated with specialty chemical or pharma companies, not auto ancillaries [^ip-latest].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** SJS is transitioning from a pure-play decorative components supplier to a technology-driven player in cover glass and display systems through its BOE Varitronix partnership [^ip-latest]. The company has set up a wholly-owned subsidiary for manufacturing, assembling, testing, and sale of displays for automotive, consumer, and other applications, with commercial supplies targeted from Q2 FY28 [^cc-transcript]. Management aspires to capture 10% of the projected Rs. 5,000-7,000 Cr Indian display market by 2030 [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, the new SJS Decoplast facility in Pune (commissioned August 2026) adds Rs. 200-250 Cr of potential revenue, and the Walter Pack India (WPI) subsidiary is now in performance improvement phase with FY26 revenue growing ~1.5x since acquisition [^ip-latest].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6-12 Months):** (a) Ramp-up of the Pune Decoplast facility targeting 85-90% utilisation in 3 years [^cc-transcript]; (b) Cover glass subsidiary equipment ordering and customer pipeline development for Q2 FY28 commercial launch [^cc-transcript]; (c) Export expansion to 14-15% of revenue by FY28 from current 9.8%, with sales force strengthening in Germany, Turkey, Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, and South Korea [^ip-latest]; (d) Continued outperformance of 1.5x-2x industry growth in FY27, with order book already at >88% of forecasted revenue [^ip-latest]; (e) Potential value-accretive M&A as the company holds Rs. 3,287.7 Mn in net cash [^ip-latest].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk:** Customer and segment concentration in the automotive sector (PV + 2W = 81.2% of Q1 FY27 revenue) exposes SJS to cyclical auto demand and OEM volume fluctuations [^ip-latest]. Additionally, raw material price volatility (films, resins, inks) can create margin pressure despite the pass-through mechanism, as evidenced by the ~0.5-0.6% margin drag in Q1 FY27 [^cc-transcript]. The Walter Pack Spain non-compete agreement (restricting BMW and Mercedes-Benz business until January 2027) also limits near-term addressable market expansion in premium European OEMs [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** SJS Enterprises is a design-to-delivery company specialising in automotive and consumer aesthetics—essentially the "visual identity" components that make a vehicle or appliance look premium. Think of the chrome badge on a car's boot, the decorative trim on a dashboard, the illuminated logo on a steering wheel, or the touch screen overlay on a microwave—SJS makes these "beauty parts" that consumers see and touch but rarely think about [^ip-latest]. The company's product portfolio spans decals (stickers/labels), appliques and dials (decorative overlays), 3D lux logos, aluminium badges, in-mold decoratives (IMD), optical plastics, and lens mask covers [^ip-latest]. What makes SJS interesting is that these are high-value, low-cost items—a Rs. 50-500 component can significantly enhance a vehicle's perceived premiumness, giving the company strong pricing power within OEM cost structures [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue Split by Division (Q1 FY27):** The business operates through three main entities: (a) **SJS (standalone)** contributes 54.3% of consolidated revenue and handles the core decorative components business; (b) **SJS Decoplast** contributes 28.0% and focuses on chrome parts, IMD/IML/IMF (In-Mold Labeling/Film) components; and (c) **Walter Pack India (WPI)** contributes 17.7% and was acquired in July 2023 to add scale and new product capabilities [^ip-latest]. By end-segment, the mix is Passenger Vehicles (PV) at 44.6%, Two-Wheelers (2W) at 36.6%, Consumer Durables at 14.5%, and Others at 4.3% [^ip-latest]. Geographically, domestic sales account for 90.2% and exports for 9.8%, with management targeting exports to reach 14-15% by FY28 [^ip-latest].

**Key OEM/End Customers:** SJS serves all major Indian automotive OEMs including Maruti Suzuki, Hyundai, Tata Motors, Mahindra, Hero MotoCorp, Bajaj Auto, TVS, and Royal Enfield [^ar-fy25]. The company also supplies to global Tier-1 suppliers and has recently added customers like Autoliv (USA) and FCA (USA) [^ar-fy25]. In the consumer durables segment, SJS supplies decorative overlays and optical components to appliance manufacturers [^ip-latest]. The customer base is diversified across OEMs, but the top 10 customers likely account for a significant portion of revenue—a typical characteristic of the auto components industry [^ar-fy25].

**Subsidiary Structure:** The group structure comprises SJS Enterprises Limited (listed parent), SJS Decoplast (chrome and IMD parts), and Walter Pack India (acquired July 2023, now a 100% subsidiary after the promoter acquired the remaining stake for better alignment) [^cc-transcript]. A new wholly-owned subsidiary has been formed for the cover glass and display systems business, with equipment on order and commercial supplies targeted from Q2 FY28 [^cc-transcript]. The company also has a strategic partnership with BOE Varitronix for optical cover glass and display systems technology transfer to India [^ip-latest].

**Manufacturing Footprint:** SJS operates manufacturing facilities across Karnataka (Bangalore), Tamil Nadu (Hosur), and Maharashtra (Pune) [^ar-fy25]. The new Pune facility for SJS Decoplast was commissioned in August 2026, adding Rs. 200-250 Cr of potential revenue capacity [^cc-transcript]. The Bangalore facility houses the in-house R&D centre, which has received DSIR (Department of Scientific and Industrial Research) recognition [^ip-latest]. Planned capex of Rs. 2,600-2,700 Mn over FY26-FY28 is being deployed across these facilities, with Rs. 822.4 Mn already spent in FY26 [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Background and Group Context:** The company is promoted by **K.A. Joseph (Kannampadathil Abraham Joseph)**, who serves as Managing Director and holds 20.15% stake as of June 2026 (down from 21.81% in September 2023, reflecting gradual dilution through the years) [^vp-thread]. Joseph has been with the company since inception and brings deep domain expertise in automotive aesthetics. The leadership team includes Sanjay Thapar (Group CEO) who drives strategy and Mahendra Naredi (Group CFO) who manages finances [^cc-transcript]. The promoter family has been actively consolidating holdings—K.A. Joseph acquired shares from Evergraph Holdings Pte. Ltd. (a promoter-group entity) in 2024, indicating internal restructuring [^vp-thread]. The company has no promoter pledging as of recent disclosures, which is a positive governance signal [^ar-fy25].

The adjacent display systems market (instrument clusters, infotainment touch panels, cover glass) is projected to reach Rs. 5,000-7,000 Cr in India by 2030, representing a significant new TAM for SJS [^cc-transcript]. **Analytical Commentary (Income Statement):**
Think of SJS's income statement like a business that is hitting its "growth stride." PAT grew 26.9% in FY24, then 40.0% in FY25, and a strong 44.5% in FY26 — this is a classic "operating leverage" picture where each new rupee of sales drops a bigger chunk to the bottom line than the last one did. The two main drivers are (1) **mix shift** toward premium, technology-intensive products (optical plastics, cover glass, IML/IME parts) which earn higher spreads, and (2) **scale benefits** as the new Pune plant spreads fixed costs over larger volumes. The other thing to notice is how clean the below-EBITDA line is: interest expense is collapsing (Rs. 9 Cr → Rs. 4 Cr) because the company is sitting on a net cash pile of Rs. 329 Cr, and Other Income is ticking up steadily from idle cash. Tax rate is stable at ~25%, so there's no "tax-cut optical" trick boosting reported PAT. The one watch-out is the depreciation line, which is set to grow from Rs. 54 Cr to Rs. 75 Cr by FY28 as the capex cycle hits the books — this is the natural cost of growth and is healthy, not a red flag. The bottom line: SJS is a textbook compounder — top-line growth, margin expansion, and balance sheet strength all reinforcing each other.
