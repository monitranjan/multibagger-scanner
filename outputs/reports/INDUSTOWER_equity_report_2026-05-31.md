### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

*   **Market Leadership & Moat:** Indus Towers commands a dominant market share of approximately 55-60% in India's passive telecom infrastructure space, operating over 215,000 towers. This scale provides a significant cost advantage and network effect, making it challenging for new entrants to replicate. The company's deep penetration across urban and rural areas, coupled with long-term Master Service Agreements (MSAs) with all major telecom operators (Bharti Airtel, Vodafone Idea, Reliance Jio), creates a strong competitive moat with high switching costs for tenants.
*   **Structural Margin Story:** The ongoing 5G rollout and network expansion by telecom operators are driving higher tenancies per tower and increased loading of existing sites. This 'colocation' strategy, where new tenants or technologies are added to existing infrastructure, is highly margin accretive for Indus Towers given the largely fixed cost nature of tower operations. We expect EBITDA margins to expand by 100-150 bps over FY27E-FY28E as tenancy ratios improve from current levels.
*   **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** Indus Towers is strategically exploring and investing in new revenue streams beyond traditional tower leasing. This includes small cells, in-building solutions (IBS), and fiber-to-the-tower (FTTT) infrastructure, which are critical for enhancing network density and capacity, especially for 5G. These adjacencies represent a significant growth opportunity, potentially contributing 5-7% of incremental revenue by FY28E.
*   **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** The primary near-term catalyst is the continued deleveraging of Vodafone Idea (VIL) and its subsequent capital expenditure for 5G rollout. As VIL stabilizes and invests, it will lead to improved collections for Indus Towers and potentially new tenancy additions. Additionally, the government's focus on digital infrastructure and the potential for new policies supporting tower infrastructure sharing could provide further tailwinds. Resolution of long-standing receivables from VIL is also a key positive catalyst.
*   **Biggest Structural Risk:** The most significant structural risk remains the financial health and competitive intensity within the Indian telecom operator landscape, particularly the long-term viability and payment discipline of Vodafone Idea. Any further deterioration in VIL's financial condition or a significant shift in operator strategies (e.g., extensive network sharing or consolidation reducing tenancy demand) could negatively impact Indus Towers' revenue and receivables.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Indus Towers Ltd. is India's largest telecom tower company, providing passive infrastructure services to all major Indian wireless telecommunication service providers. Its core business model involves owning, operating, and managing wireless communication towers and related infrastructure, which are then leased out to telecom operators on a shared basis. This sharing model helps operators reduce their capital expenditure and operating costs.

*   **Revenue Split by Division (% of Revenue):**
    *   Tower & Related Infrastructure Leasing: ~98-99% (Core business)
    *   Other Services (Energy, O&M, etc.): ~1-2% (Ancillary services to tenants)
    *(Note: Indus Towers primarily operates as a single segment business reporting, with revenue predominantly from passive infrastructure leasing.)*
*   **Key OEM/End Customers per Division:**
    *   **Tower Leasing:** Bharti Airtel, Vodafone Idea, Reliance Jio Infocomm. These three are the primary tenants, with Bharti Airtel and Vodafone Idea being significant shareholders.
*   **Subsidiary Structure:** Indus Towers was formed by the merger of Bharti Infratel and Indus Towers in November 2020. It operates largely as a single entity, with no significant operational subsidiaries currently driving separate business lines.
*   **Manufacturing Footprint:** As a tower infrastructure provider, Indus Towers does not have a "manufacturing footprint" in the traditional sense. Its operational footprint is spread across all 22 telecom circles in India, with a presence in all states. The company manages over 215,000 towers across the country.
*   **Promoter Background and Group Context:** Indus Towers was initially a joint venture between Bharti Airtel (approx. 42% stake at inception), Vodafone Group (approx. 42%), and Idea Cellular (approx. 16%). Following the merger with Bharti Infratel, Bharti Airtel became the largest shareholder. The company benefits from the strong lineage and operational expertise of the Bharti Group in the telecom sector, ensuring sound strategic direction and industry insights.
