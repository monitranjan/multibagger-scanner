### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Chalet Hotels has built a concentrated prime-real-estate moat in the Mumbai Metropolitan Region (MMR) and is expanding into high-barrier leisure markets. To explain in simple terms: "RevPAR" (Revenue Per Available Room) is the average money a hotel earns from each of its rooms every day — like measuring how much a lemonade stand makes per stall per day. Chalet's combined portfolio RevPAR rose to ₹8,582 in Q1 FY27, with resorts at ₹9,314, showing pricing power in scarce leisure locations [^ip-latest]. The company's ownership of large integrated plots such as the Powai complex (where it is adding office towers alongside hotels) creates a "scale moat" because such land is nearly impossible for new competitors to replicate in Mumbai [^cc-transcript]. Its branded-key presence in Mumbai (16.5k market base) and leadership in premium resorts (Athiva brand) gives it a durable edge [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin story:** The core margin driver is "operating leverage" — once a hotel is built, most costs are fixed, so small increases in room prices or occupancy fall straight to profit. In Q1 FY27, consolidated ex-residential EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, Amortization — a proxy for operating profit before non-cash and financing items) grew 15.2% YoY to ₹2,400 mn, and margin expanded 231 basis points (bps — one bps = 0.01%, so 231 bps = 2.31%) to 46.7% [^cc-transcript]. Hospitality margin rose 92 bps to 42.6%, while Commercial Real Estate (CRE) margin hit 85.0% (up 193 bps) because office rents are almost pure profit after construction [^ip-latest]. This structural shift toward higher-margin CRE and resort mix is a multi-year earnings amplifier.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Chalet is not just a hotelier; it is becoming a mixed-asset developer. It acquired 100% of SHPL (Seasons Hotels) for ₹1,710 mn in May 2026 as an asset purchase, adding the Udaipur property (144 keys) to be rebranded Athiva [^ip-latest]. The pipeline includes ~1,655 rooms: under-construction Taj Delhi Airport (~380 keys, Q4 FY27), Athiva Varca Goa (205 keys, FY28), Ritz Carlton Hyderabad (330 keys, FY29) [^ip-latest]. CRE pipeline adds CIGNUS Powai Tower II (0.9 msf) [^ip-latest]. This diversifies from pure city-hotel cycles into leisure and commercial rentals, which have lower volatility.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Opening of Taj Delhi Int’l Airport minimum 70 rooms in Q4 FY27 should add high-ADO (Average Daily Occupancy) luxury supply [^cc-transcript]; (ii) CIGNUS Powai Tower II completion by Q4 FY27 will step-change CRE rentals to ₹300–320 mn/month from ₹290 mn currently [^cc-transcript]; (iii) Rebranding of Vashi property to "ATHIVA Pulse Navi Mumbai Autograph Collection" announced Aug 2025 improves brand premium [^ar-fy25]; (iv) MMR occupancy recovery toward 70%+ as Powai construction noise ends in H2 FY27 [^cc-transcript]; (v) Resort ramp at Athiva Khandala (ADR >₹15,000) validates new brand [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The most material threat is external demand shock — specifically West Asia geopolitical conflict reducing Foreign Tourist Arrivals (FTAs), which already pressured Hyderabad and Bangalore in Q1 [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, premium supply CAGR in key markets is 5–6%, and if demand lags, RevPAR could compress [^ip-latest]. A secondary risk is community/investor disengagement: ValuePickr threads show zero active theses or red-flag tracking, implying thin retail support that can amplify volatility [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Chalet Hotels Limited operates as an owner-operator of premium hotels and commercial real estate. Think of it like a landlord who rents rooms nightly (hotels) and desks monthly (offices). Its business model is "asset-heavy": it buys land, builds properties, and partners with global brands (Marriott, Accor, Taj) who manage operations for a fee while Chalet owns the real estate and captures most profit [^ip-latest].

Revenue split for Q1 FY27 (total ₹5,213 mn) is: Hospitality 80.3% (₹4,185 mn), CRE 16.6% (₹865 mn), Residential 1.4% (₹73 mn from one unit handed over) [^ip-latest]. Within Hospitality, room revenue is 42.6%, F&B 30.5%, others 7.3% [^ip-latest]. Customer segments: Transient (walk-in/individual) 69%, Groups (weddings/conferences) 22%, Contract (corporate bulk) 9% [^ip-latest]. Distribution: Property direct/voice 53%, Global Distribution System 23%, Brand.com 14%, OTA (online travel agents like MakeMyTrip) 10% [^ip-latest].

Key end-customers are multinational corporates, leisure tourists, and airline crews (JW Marriott Sahar near airport). Subsidiary structure includes special purpose vehicles (SPVs — separate companies for each asset to isolate risk) and the acquired SHPL [^ip-latest]. Manufacturing footprint is irrelevant; instead, property footprint spans Maharashtra (Mumbai Powai, Vashi, Pune, Khandala), Goa, Hyderabad, Delhi, Bengaluru, Udaipur — total >5,000 keys targeted [^cc-transcript]. Promoter group is K. Raheja Corp (Raheja family); Mr. Ravi Raheja retires by rotation but seeks re-appointment at AGM Sep 2026 [^ar-fy25]. The group context is deep real-estate pedigree, giving Chalet preferential access to land in Mumbai.

EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation) is a measure of a company’s core operating profit from its hotels and real estate, before accounting for how it is financed or taxed. A higher EBITDA margin means the company keeps more profit from each rupee of sales. For Chalet, the actuals show a jump in revenue in FY26A (61% growth) largely due to one-time residential unit sales (Koramangala) and strong post-pandemic travel recovery. The margin held at 43% because fixed hotel operating costs spread over more guests (operating leverage). In simple terms, once a hotel is built, adding more guests costs little extra, so profit rises faster than sales. The tax rate in FY25A was unusually high (67%) due to deferred tax adjustments, while FY24A had a tax credit. PAT (Profit After Tax) is the final bottom-line profit belonging to shareholders. EPS (Earnings Per Share) is that profit divided by the number of shares (~21.9 crore, derived from equity capital of Rs 219 Cr and face value Rs 10). Dividend payout is the portion of profit given as dividend; FY26A paid Re 1 per share, a low 3.4% payout, meaning the company retains most profit to fund expansion.
