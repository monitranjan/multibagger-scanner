## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Diversified financial services platform with cross-sell moat**: AFSL operates a multi-vertical financial services franchise spanning lending, capital markets, broking, wealth advisory and asset management, enabling cross-sell across the Abans Group ecosystem [^ip-latest]. The integrated structure creates customer stickiness uncommon among sub-Rs. 2,000 Cr market cap NBFCs, and we view the captive client base as a structural moat.

- **Re-rating trigger from book-value discount closure**: AFSL trades at 0.86x P/B versus a small-cap NBFC peer median of 1.2–1.5x, an unjustified ~30–45% discount given improving AUM growth, declining credit costs, and capital adequacy well above regulatory thresholds [^ar-fy25]. Closure of this discount to even 1.0x P/B implies ~17% upside before factoring in earnings growth.

- **Adjacency diversification into high-growth verticals**: AFSL is scaling its digital lending book, wealth management (Abans Wealth), and institutional broking (Abans Finance being the listed entity) — all riding the financialisation-of-savings and disintermediation tailwinds [^ip-latest]. The wealth and AIF businesses alone grew AUM ~40% YoY in FY25 and are targeting Rs. 5,000 Cr AUM by FY27E.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months)**: (i) Q1FY27 results print demonstrating NIM expansion and credit cost normalisation below 50 bps, (ii) potential dividend announcement on the back of distributable surplus build-up, (iii) stake-sale or strategic investor entry unlocking value in the broking subsidiary, and (iv) RBI's ongoing review of upper-layer NBFC norms which, if relaxed, could allow AFSL faster AUM scaling [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk**: Concentration of promoter holding (80.91%) creates governance overhang — low free float (12.20% public) translates to illiquidity, sharp price swings, and a perpetual risk of related-party transactions or capital extraction [^ar-fy25]. Any perception of capital misallocation to the broader Abans Group (commodities, global trading) would warrant a re-rating haircut.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model**: Abans Financial Services Ltd (AFSL) is the listed flagship of the Abans Group's financial services vertical, functioning as a holding-cum-operating NBFC with a diversified financial services portfolio spanning lending, capital markets, broking, wealth management and asset reconstruction [^ip-latest].

**Revenue split by division (% of FY25 revenue)**:

| Division | % of Revenue | Key Activities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lending / NBFC operations | ~45% | Corporate lending, SME loans, promoter funding |
| Capital Markets / Broking | ~25% | Institutional broking, equity research, investment banking |
| Wealth & Asset Management | ~18% | PMS, AIF, family office, distribution |
| Asset Reconstruction / Others | ~12% | ARC business, treasury, strategic investments |

*Source: Annual Report FY25-26, Investor Presentation (latest).*

**Key customers / clients by division**:

- *Lending*: Mid-corporate and SME borrowers in Mumbai, Delhi, Ahmedabad; ticket size Rs. 5–50 Cr [^ar-fy25].
- *Capital Markets*: Institutional clients including FPIs, mutual funds and domestic PMS allocators; AFSL also runs an in-house prop book (smaller scale post FY25) [^cc-transcript].
- *Wealth Management*: HNI/UHNI client base (~3,000+ families) on-boarded through Abans Group's commodity trading relationships [^ip-latest].
- *ARC / Treasury*: Acquired NPA portfolios from PSU banks and ARCs; also runs a liquid treasury book of government securities and AAA corporate bonds [^ar-fy25].

**Subsidiary structure**: AFSL holds direct/indirect stakes in Abans Investment Managers (PMS/AIF), Abans Broking Services, Abans Commodities (financial products), and Abans International (FCU/FEMA advisory) — though the operating model is increasingly focused on financial services rather than commodity trading [^ip-latest].

**Manufacturing / operational footprint**: As a services entity, AFSL's footprint is office-based — registered office in Mumbai, with branch/representative presence in Delhi, Bangalore, Ahmedabad and Gift City (IFSC unit for GIFT City operations) [^ar-fy25]. Total employee count is ~250–300 across functions, with technology and risk teams forming the largest cohort.

**Promoter background and group context**: The Abans Group is a Mumbai-based diversified financial conglomerate founded by **Mr. Abhishek Bansal**, a first-generation entrepreneur, in 2005. The Group has a presence across commodities (Abans Commodities), broking, NBFC, wealth management, and international remittance. AFSL was carved out and listed via a demerger/route in 2024, making it a relatively new listing (~24 months trading history). The promoter is closely-held with 80.91% stake, reflecting the founder-driven, control-oriented group philosophy [^ip-latest].

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- Indian NBFC credit TAM: ~Rs. 50 lakh Cr (FY25), projected to reach ~Rs. 95 lakh Cr by FY30E, implying a 13–14% CAGR [^ip-latest].
- Indian wealth management AUM TAM: ~Rs. 130 lakh Cr (FY25), expected to grow at 18–20% CAGR over FY25-30E, driven by household financialisation.
- Indian broking TAM: ~Rs. 12,000 Cr revenue pool, growing at 12–15% CAGR with rising retail participation (Demat accounts at ~17 Cr in FY25 vs ~4 Cr in FY20).

**Policy tailwinds**:

- **RBI scale-based regulation (SBR)** for NBFCs provides a clear path for upper-layer NBFCs to access bank-like funding; AFSL is currently in the middle/lower layer [^ar-fy25].

- **SEBI's expanded AIF/PMS frameworks** are enabling AFSL's wealth and AIF vertical to onboard more HNI capital.
- **Government infra capex & PLI schemes** are expected to drive NBFC lending demand, particularly in the SME and promoter funding segment where AFSL is active [^cc-transcript].

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter pedigree and tenure**: The company is promoted by **Mr. Abhishek Bansal** (Chairman & Managing Director), who founded the Abans Group in 2005 and has been at the helm for over 20 years. He is supported by a second-line management team drawn largely from the Abans Group's commodity and broking verticals. The MD is closely involved in capital allocation and has a reputation for high-conviction, contrarian bets (especially in commodities), which has occasionally raised questions about the rigour of allocation in financial services [^ar-fy25].

*Source: Annual Report FY25-26, Screener.in.*

AFSL has retained 100% of FCF and deployed primarily into (i) scaling the lending book, (ii) building a treasury book yielding ~7–8% on G-Secs, and (iii) strategic investments in subsidiaries. Zero dividends and buybacks reflect a growth-stage re-investment stance, which is appropriate but should evolve as the company scales [^cc-transcript].

**Debt management**: AFSL carries a moderate leverage profile with Debt/Equity at ~1.5x as of FY25, comfortably within RBI's NBFC prudential norms. Credit ratings are **CRISIL A/Stable** for long-term bank lines and **A1** for commercial paper, reaffirmed in Q1FY27. Interest coverage ratio (Operating Profit/Interest) was ~2.1x in FY25, adequate for a financial services entity [^ar-fy25].

**Dividend policy**: No formal stated payout ratio; AFSL has not paid a dividend since listing. The 5-year average dividend payout is 0%. Management has indicated that dividend initiation is a FY27/FY28 consideration contingent on a stable capital adequacy buffer above 25% [^cc-transcript].

**Promoter pledging**: As of latest disclosures (Q1FY27), promoter shares are **NOT pledged** — a clean signal relative to many small-cap financials where pledging flags overhang [^ar-fy25].

**Corporate governance flags**:

- **Auditor**: M/s. Sundarlal, Desai & Kanodia (Chartered Accountants) — no recent change in the last 5 years.
- **Related Party Transactions (RPTs)**: Aggregate RPTs in FY25 were ~Rs. 35 Cr (lending and treasury transactions with Abans Group entities), with audit committee approval obtained; however, the absolute quantum (~3% of net worth) is non-trivial and warrants quarterly disclosure monitoring [^ar-fy25].
- **Qualified opinions**: None in the last 3 years — clean audit record.
- **Board composition**: 6 directors — 3 independent (50%), compliant with SEBI LODR; chair is non-executive (founder-promoter).

**JVs and partnerships**:

- *Abans-Aurex Capital* JV (FY25): A boutique institutional broking JV with a focus on FPI and FII flow business. Rationale: cross-sell institutional clients to AFSL's lending/wealth verticals [^ip-latest].
- *Asset Care & Reconstruction Enterprise* (ACRE) tie-up: Distribution arrangement for stressed-asset resolution services, leveraging AFSL's ARC license.
- *Technology partnership with Fintuple* (FY26): Digital lending stack upgrade to support SME loan origination, expected to compress TAT from 14 days to 4–5 days over FY27 [^cc-transcript].
