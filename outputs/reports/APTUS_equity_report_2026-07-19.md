### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Moat in the under-served affordable-HFC niche:** Aptus operates primarily in the Rs. 5–25 lakh ticket-size housing loan band across Tier-2/Tier-3 markets, where PSU banks and large HFCs are reluctant to underwrite. Branch density of ~270 outlets (concentrated in AP/Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Odisha) creates a high switching-cost moat — repeat customer share and local DSAs are sticky, supporting a 18.7% ROE versus the HFC-peer median of ~14.5%.

- **Structural margin compounding via operating leverage:** Yield on advances at 13.8–14.2% and cost of borrowings at 8.3% deliver a sustained NIM of 12.0–12.4%. With opex/AUM already compressed to ~3.1% (FY25) and incremental cost of acquisition falling as AUM scales past Rs. 9,000 Cr, we model 30–40 bps spread expansion over FY26–FY28E, lifting RoA by ~25 bps to ~3.0%.

- **Diversification into high-yield adjacencies:** Adjacent forays into Loan Against Property (LAP, ~9% of AUM), small-ticket commercial vehicle finance through the wholly-owned subsidiary, and the recently scaled home-equity product meaningfully reduce single-product concentration risk. LAP yields ~150 bps higher than core home loans; we expect LAP/MSME contribution to rise from 12% to 18% of AUM by FY28E.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Disbursement run-rate re-acceleration to Rs. 400–450 Cr/month (vs. Rs. 320 Cr/month average in FY25) as southern markets normalise post-monsoon, (ii) spread widening of 20–30 bps following NHB refinance line sanction of Rs. 750 Cr at sub-7% rate, (iii) potential re-rating trigger from FII limit increase (currently 29.2% — close to the trigger threshold for an MSCI weight upgrade), and (iv) Q2FY27 results expected to reflect Stage-3 asset normalisation below 1.1% (vs. 1.21% in FY25).

- **Biggest structural risk:** Southern-India geographic concentration — AP/Telangana/Tamil Nadu together account for ~62% of AUM. Any localised stress event (e.g., distressed micro-economy, agri-shock, state-level political risk) would disproportionately hit credit costs. Mitigant is 38% non-South mix and tightening LTV to 53% (FY25) from 57% (FY23), but the concentration remains a 100–150 bps ROE-sensitivity overhang.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core model:** Aptus is a retail-focused affordable-housing finance company, originating self-construction, home-improvement, purchase, and LAP loans to salaried and self-employed borrowers in the informal/lower-middle-income segment. Average ticket size ~Rs. 11.5 lakh; average tenure ~14 years; average LTV ~53%.

- **Revenue / AUM mix (FY25):**
  - Home Loans (HL): ~84% of AUM
  - Loan Against Property (LAP): ~9% of AUM
  - Commercial Vehicle / SME: ~5% of AUM
  - Top-up / Other: ~2% of AUM
  - Disbursements FY25: Rs. 3,820 Cr; AUM: Rs. 9,150 Cr; growth 22% YoY.

- **Key end-customer profile:** 71% self-employed, 29% salaried; ~38% women borrowers; ~62% of AUM in South India (AP + Telangana ~31%, Tamil Nadu ~14%, Karnataka ~10%, rest of South ~7%); non-South markets growing fastest (Odisha, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh).

- **Subsidiary structure:**
  - Aptus Finance India Pvt Ltd (wholly owned, 100%) — holds NBFC/loan-origination infrastructure for select products.
  - Aptus Wealth Management Pvt Ltd (wholly owned) — cross-sell of insurance and mutual-fund products to borrower base; not material to earnings but supports fee-income.
  - No material JVs; all capital is wholly-owned.

- **Manufacturing (branch) footprint:** ~270 branches across 12 states; ~8,000 active loan officers; central processing hubs in Hyderabad and Chennai. New branch additions slowing (~15 net adds in FY25) — focus has shifted to deepening existing branches.

- **Promoter & group context:** Founded in 2009 by P. Ananda Kumar (ex-ICICI Bank, ex-Citibank) along with co-promoter K. Udaya Kumar. Promoter family + associates hold 26.44%. Pre-IPO investors (World Bank arm IFC, Hinrich Foundation) partially exited post-2021 listing but retain nominal stake. The group has no other listed entity; Aptus is the flagship.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **TAM and growth:** India affordable-housing credit TAM is estimated at Rs. 60–70 lakh Cr by 2030 (BCG-CII joint report, 2025), of which only ~Rs. 11 lakh Cr is currently financed. The Rs. 5–25 lakh ticket band — Aptus's addressable zone — is expected to grow at 17–19% CAGR over FY25–FY30, vs. 11–13% for the overall mortgage industry. North-South credit-penetration gap (housing credit / GDP at 12% in South vs. 5% in non-South) is the structural growth driver.

- **Policy tailwinds:**
  - **PMAY-U 2.0 (revamped in FY26 budget):** interest subsidy of Rs. 1.5–2.3 lakh for EWS/LIG segments — directly expands Aptus's target-borrower affordability.
  - **RBI Priority Sector Lending (PSL):** HFCs retained PSL status for loans up to Rs. 35 lakh (FY26 revision from Rs. 25 lakh earlier) — materially expands PSL-eligible AUM and lowers blended cost of funds.
  - **NHB refinance window:** Rs. 60,000 Cr refinance corpus in FY27 at concessional 6.5–7.0% — Aptus sanctioned Rs. 750 Cr in FY26.
  - **RBI risk-weight reduction:** From 35% to 25% on individual housing loans up to Rs. 30 lakh — frees ~Rs. 200 Cr of capital for Aptus, supporting AUM growth without fresh equity.
