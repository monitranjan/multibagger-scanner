### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Retail-focused NBFC with structural moat in secured lending:** IIFL Finance has consciously pivoted its loan book toward secured retail assets — home loans, loan against property (LAP), and gold loans now constitute ~75% of AUM, materially de-risking the portfolio from the unsecured personal loan exposure that has tripped up peers like Bajaj Finance and Cholamandalam in recent quarters [^ip-latest]. The retail mortgage book has compounded at ~35% CAGR over FY21–FY25, taking the mortgage AUM to over Rs. 28,000 Cr [^ar-fy25].

- **Margin expansion story driven by product mix and cost of funds:** Blended cost of funds has declined ~80 bps over the past four quarters as the company reduced its reliance on short-term commercial paper and NHB refinance lines, replacing them with longer-tenor NCDs and ECB borrowings [^cc-transcript]. Combined with a higher share of high-yield LAP and business loans (NIMs of 9–10%), we expect NIMs to expand from 5.4% in FY25 to ~6.2% by FY27E, driving ~150 bps of RoA expansion [^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies — digital lending and MSME:** IIFL has launched a digital lending platform ("IIFL Loans") targeting salaried and self-employed segments with ticket sizes of Rs. 1–10 lakh, leveraging its 2,000+ branch footprint for origination and the in-house credit bureau for underwriting [^cc-transcript]. The MSME vertical, currently ~Rs. 4,500 Cr, is targeted to scale to Rs. 12,000 Cr by FY28E, riding the formalisation tailwind from GST and UPI adoption [^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1FY27 disbursement growth of ~28% YoY signals a strong festive-season pipeline [^cc-transcript]; (ii) potential re-rating trigger from the proposed listing of IIFL Securities and IIFL Wealth as separate entities, which could unlock ~15–20% sum-of-parts value [^vp-thread]; (iii) RBI rate cut cycle (we expect 50 bps of cuts by Dec 2026) will directly benefit spread-sensitive NBFCs [^ip-latest].

- **Biggest structural risk — asset quality in the LAP and commercial real estate book:** LAP GNPAs stood at 3.8% as of Q4FY26, up from 2.9% a year ago, reflecting stress in the SME and real estate developer segments [^ar-fy25]. Any further deterioration here — particularly if commercial real estate prices correct 10–15% — could trigger credit costs of 1.5–2.0% and compress RoA by ~50 bps [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** IIFL Finance Ltd is a retail-focused, asset-light NBFC that lends primarily against physical collateral (property, gold) and to MSMEs/salaried individuals. Post the 2018 demerger from IIFL Holdings, the company operates as a pure-play lending entity with three reporting verticals: (1) Home Loans, (2) Loan Against Property (LAP) & Business Loans, and (3) Gold Loans [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue Split by Division (% of FY25 AUM):**
- Home Loans & Mortgage: ~52% (Rs. 28,400 Cr)
- LAP & Business Loans: ~28% (Rs. 15,300 Cr)
- Gold Loans: ~15% (Rs. 8,200 Cr)
- Digital/MSME & Others: ~5% (Rs. 2,700 Cr)

**Key Customer Segments per Division:**
- Home Loans: Salaried class in Tier 1–2 cities, average ticket size Rs. 35–45 lakh, average LTV 65%
- LAP: Self-employed professionals, SME business owners, ticket size Rs. 25–75 lakh

- Digital/MSME: Salaried millennials and first-time credit users [^cc-transcript]

**Subsidiary Structure:** IIFL Finance is the listed parent; key subsidiaries include IIFL Home Finance (100%, housing finance arm), IIFL Samasta Finance (100%, microfinance), and CSL Finance (strategic stake in gold loan JV) [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing/Branch Footprint:** 2,847 branches across 22 states and 3 union territories; major concentration in Maharashtra (18%), Tamil Nadu (12%), Gujarat (10%), Karnataka (9%), and UP (8%) [^ip-latest]. Gold loan branches: 1,650+; LAP branches: 850+; Home loan branches: 340+.

**Promoter Background:** Founded by Nirmal Jain (Chairman) in 1995 as part of the India Infoline (IIFL) group. The promoter group — comprising Nirmal Jain, R.
