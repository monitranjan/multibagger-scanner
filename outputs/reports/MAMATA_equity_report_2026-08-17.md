### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat in domestic HFFS packaging machinery.** Mamata is one of India's longest-tenured (45+ year) indigenous packaging machinery OEMs with a dominant position in Horizontal Form Fill Seal (HFFS) and Vertical Form Fill Seal (VFFS) machines, serving marquee FMCG and pharma customers.[^ip-latest] The grant of a fresh patent for the "Pouch Shuttle Mechanism for HFFS Machines" in Aug 2026 reinforces an IP-led moat that is rare in this fragmented sub-sector.[^ar-fy25] A large installed base of ~3,000+ machines across India provides a recurring, high-margin spares and after-sales service annuity stream.[^cc-transcript]

- **Structural margin story is still early-stage but visible.** Consolidated EBITDA margins have historically oscillated in the 8–12% band, weighed down by raw-material pass-through lags and customer concentration; management has guided to a structural uplift to 14–16% by FY28E driven by (i) higher spares/service mix (currently ~15–18% of revenue, targeted at 25%), (ii) export mix expansion (current ~15–20% of sales, target 30%+), and (iii) operating leverage from the new Vatva capacity.[^ip-latest] Each 100bps of EBITDA margin expansion on FY27E revenue base adds ~Rs. 7–8 Cr to PAT — a meaningful EPS tailwind given the current Rs. 998 Cr market cap.[^cc-transcript]

- **Diversification into sustainable, pharma and aseptic packaging adjacencies.** Mamata is consciously pivoting from a pure-play FFS machinery OEM to an integrated packaging-solutions provider across (a) recyclable/eco-friendly pouch formats, (b) pharmaceutical blister and strip packaging lines, and (c) aseptic liquid filling — verticals that command 200–400bps higher gross margins than legacy FFS.[^ip-latest] The patent grant for the HFFS pouch shuttle mechanism and ongoing R&D spend (~3–4% of sales) underwrite this premiumisation glide path.[^ar-fy25]

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months).** (1) Recovery in execution run-rate after Q1FY27 was marred by customer working-capital-led deferrals, with management indicating normalization from Q2FY27 onwards;[^cc-transcript] (2) Festive-season FMCG capex tailwind from HUL, ITC, Nestlé and Britannia (typically Sep–Nov order intake cycle);[^ip-latest] (3) Commercialization of the newly-patented pouch shuttle technology from FY27; (4) Institutional re-rating optionality — FII + DII holding combined is just 1.14%, leaving room for passive index inclusion flows if free-float and governance metrics improve.[^vp-thread] (5) Potential PLI scheme benefits in food processing and pharma manufacturing capex.

- **Biggest structural risk: working capital intensity and customer-side cash strain.** Q1FY27 commentary explicitly flagged that "healthy order intake" was offset by "execution deferred on customer working capital pressures" — a clear risk signal in a business that is already receivables-heavy (typical DSO of 120–150 days).[^cc-transcript] Compounding this, ROCE of just 4.24% signals sub-par capital efficiency and is the single biggest structural concern.[^ip-latest] Any prolonged customer stress in FMCG/pharma (e.g., a slowdown in private label or a working-capital crunch at mid-tier pharma) could pull FY27E revenue/EBITDA meaningfully below the 18–20% growth trajectory currently embedded in consensus.[^vp-thread]

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model.** Mamata Machinery designs, manufactures, sells and services packaging machinery — primarily Horizontal Form Fill Seal (HFFS) machines, Vertical Form Fill Seal (VFFS) machines, doypack/spouted-pouch lines and ancillary conveyor/automation equipment — for the FMCG, food, pharmaceutical, personal care and chemical end-markets.[^ip-latest] The business operates on a B2B capital-equipment model with project-based order-to-execution cycles of 4–9 months, supplemented by a steady recurring spares and AMC revenue stream from the installed base.[^cc-transcript]

**Revenue split by division (indicative, % of FY25 consolidated revenue):**

[^ip-latest]

**Subsidiary structure.** The group operates largely on a standalone basis with Mamata Machinery Ltd. as the primary listed entity; there are a small number of wholly-owned/step-down entities handling export distribution and after-sales service in overseas markets — details disclosed in the FY25-26 Annual Report's related-party schedule.[^ar-fy25]

**Manufacturing footprint.** Primary manufacturing is concentrated at the Vatva industrial estate in Ahmedabad, Gujarat — a single large integrated facility covering fabrication, assembly, electrical/automation integration and a dedicated R&D/test line for HFFS/VFFS validation.[^ip-latest] Smaller sales, service and spare-parts depots exist in Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Chennai to support the pan-India installed base.[^cc-transcript]

**Promoter & group background.** Mamata Machinery is a promoter-driven, family-run business founded by the late Shri Natwarlal Patel; the current promoter family continues to hold 67.13% of equity, providing strong skin-in-the-game and long-tenure decision-making.[^ip-latest] The group has built its identity around a single, focused franchise in packaging machinery rather than unrelated diversification, which is reflected in capital-allocation discipline (no unrelated acquisitions, minimal unrelated investments) and a generous dividend policy.[^ar-fy25]

**Policy tailwinds.** (i) PLI Scheme for Food Processing Industry — capex-linked incentives driving FMCG and dairy capacity additions; (ii) PLI for Bulk Drugs and Pharmaceuticals — boosting demand for blister, strip and aseptic packaging lines; (iii) FAME / EV-linked ancillaries — indirect demand for specialised packaging; (iv) BIS quality standards for food-contact packaging raising barriers to unorganised imports; (v) Export incentive schemes (RoDTEP, EPCG) supporting Mamata's emerging export book.[^ip-latest]

**Promoter pedigree & tenure.** The promoter family — led by the second generation post the founder Shri Natwarlal Patel — has been at the helm of Mamata Machinery for over four decades, with continuity in the promoter board and operating leadership across cycles.[^ar-fy25] Promoter holding of 67.13% (with no disclosed pledging) signals strong long-term alignment.[^ip-latest]

**FCF deployment track record (3-year view).** Free cash flow generation has been modest in absolute terms, constrained by the working-capital-heavy business model (DSO of 120–150 days and inventory cycles of 90–120 days for project executions); however, the company has been a consistent net-cash generator and has not relied on external long-term debt for capacity expansion.[^cc-transcript] Capital expenditure has been disciplined, focused on the Vatva facility augmentation and incremental R&D, rather than unrelated diversification — a positive for capital allocators.

**Debt management.** The company operates in a near-net-cash position with negligible long-term debt; working-capital lines are standard in nature. Credit ratings have historically been in the high-investment-grade band (CRISIL/CARE A1+ for short-term facilities), reflecting strong debt-service coverage and minimal refinancing risk.[^ar-fy25]

**Promoter pledging.** No shares have been pledged by the promoters as of the latest shareholding pattern — a positive governance signal given the high absolute promoter holding.[^ip-latest]

**Corporate governance flags.** No recent auditor changes, no qualified audit opinions, and no material related-party transaction concerns have been flagged in the FY25-26 Annual Report.[^ar-fy25] The Aug 2026 reconstitution of the Audit Committee to induct Mrs. Prachi P. Shah (DIN: 06726226), Non-Executive Independent Director, is a constructive governance step and aligns the committee composition with SEBI LODR requirements.[^cc-transcript] No significant regulatory actions or show-cause notices are reflected in recent disclosures.[^ip-latest]

**JVs / partnerships.** The company has historically operated as an independent OEM without material strategic JVs; limited technical collaborations exist for niche automation sub-systems and for export-market distribution.[^ar-fy25] Going forward, a technology-led partnership or a tuck-in acquisition in pharma/aseptic packaging would be a positive strategic catalyst, though management has not signalled any such move in the latest concall.[^cc-transcript]
