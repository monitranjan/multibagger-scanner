## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Differentiated vertical-mix moat in regulated, hard-to-disrupt verticals:** FSL has structurally tilted its revenue mix toward Healthcare (~39% historically, currently a leading share per the company's vertical disclosures), BFSI, and Telecom & Media verticals where switching costs are high, compliance thresholds are steep, and incumbent BPO relationships rarely churn out overnight — a structurally superior profitability profile vs. commoditised voice/horizontal BPM peers [^ar-fy25].

- **Margin trajectory compounding via mix-shift and AI-led delivery:** Management's stated "profitable growth" pivot — articulated under CEO Rajesh Subramaniam and reinforced in the latest investor day commentary — has translated into expanding operating margins, with concall disclosures indicating mid-teens operating margin band and continued tailwind from automation, agentic-AI deployment in customer interaction management, and offshoring of voice/digital workflows from US/UK clients [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies — Collections, Mortgage BPO, Healthcare RCM:** The legacy ISGN mortgage platform (acquired ~2016) and the Sky (UK pay-TV) sole-vendor deal have matured into multi-year annuity streams; incremental adjacency in healthcare revenue cycle management (RCM), collections (Firstsource Collections), and analytics consulting is incrementally accretive at a higher margin band than the legacy base [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1FY27 results scheduled for 06 August 2026 expected to confirm continuation of double-digit YoY constant-currency revenue growth and 50-100bps YoY margin expansion, (ii) ramp of recently-won large-deal TCV pipeline (multi-year, USD-denominated contracts) flowing into FY27, (iii) potential re-rating as the share of digital/AI-led revenue crosses ~30% of mix, (iv) DII/FII ownership rebalancing as 5G/IT-services theme funds re-enter the BPM space after a multi-year derating [^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk — UK/GBP FX exposure and US healthcare payor concentration:** With ~38% revenue still UK-linked and a single end-client (Sky/Comcast) historically driving a disproportionate share of UK revenue, GBP depreciation or a payor-side reimbursement reset in US Healthcare (e.g., CMS policy change, prior-authorisation reform) can compress reported INR margins despite hedge book coverage (~82% of 12-month GBP exposure hedged at favourable rates per historical disclosures) [^vp-thread].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** Firstsource Solutions Limited (FSL) is a global provider of customised Business Process Management (BPM) services — spanning customer interaction management (inbound/outbound voice, chat, email, social), data processing, transaction processing, collections, mortgage origination/servicing support, healthcare revenue cycle management (RCM) and consulting/analytics — delivered through a globally distributed delivery model from India, the Philippines, the US and the UK [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue split by vertical / division (per most recent disclosures):**
- Healthcare Provider & Payor services — largest vertical, dominant share of revenue [^ip-latest]
- Banking, Financial Services & Insurance (BFSI) — including mortgage BPO from ISGN platform
- Telecom & Media — anchored by the Sky sole-vendor contract in the UK
- Diversified (Retail, Utilities, Logistics) — smaller but high-margin cross-sell
- Collections Management — operated as a distinct solution line, post-acquisition of US collections platform

**Key OEM / end-customer disclosures:** Sky UK (Comcast-owned pay-TV operator) — sole-vendor for core customer management workflows; multiple US Healthcare payors and providers; large US/UK BFSI institutions; Tier-1 telecom carriers. Customer concentration remains elevated but skewed toward large, investment-grade counterparties [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary structure:** Material subsidiaries include Firstsource Group USA Inc. (operating vehicle for US healthcare, collections and mortgage businesses), Firstsource Solutions UK Ltd. (Sky/UK BPO delivery), and Firstsource Solutions Philippines Inc. (offshore delivery hub for US clients) [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing / delivery footprint:** FSL operates from 45+ global delivery locations across India, US, UK and Philippines with over 30,000+ employees (headcount has scaled up materially vs. ~23,000 in FY16 per legacy disclosures), with the bulk of new capacity additions happening in Indian and Philippine tier-2/3 cities to optimise cost-to-serve [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter background:** FSL is part of the RP-Sanjiv Goenka Group — one of India's foremost diversified conglomerates with interests in power & natural resources (CESC, Spencer's), retail (Spencer's Retail), media and education. The group's stated objective of moving into the services/IT space has been a key strategic anchor for FSL's long-term capital and governance support [^vp-thread].

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter pedigree & tenure:** The RP-Sanjiv Goenka Group is a well-regarded Indian conglomerate with a multi-decade track record of building and operating scaled businesses (CESC, Spencer's Retail, Saregama India, etc.). FSL itself was acquired by the group from ICICI Venture in 2012, with the management transition to CEO Rajesh Subramaniam marking a clear pivot toward profitability, vertical specialisation and de-leveraging. Promoter shareholding remains healthy at 55.18% — well above the regulatory minimum, signalling long-term commitment [^vp-thread].

**FCF deployment track record (3-yr lens):** Per the FY25-26 Annual Report, FSL has been a consistent free-cash-flow generator with FCF conversion typically >75% of EBITDA. The deployment mix has been disciplined: (i) debt reduction at the US subsidiary level (legacy acquisition debt), (ii) selective tuck-in M&A in healthcare and collections, (iii) modest dividends with rising payout, and (iv) limited aggressive capex given the asset-light BPM operating model [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].

**Debt management:** Net debt has materially declined from legacy levels (~$97mn historical peak) and the company has demonstrated a track record of steady quarterly repayments. Credit profile is supported by healthy EBITDA-to-debt service coverage (typically >5x). FSL has maintained investment-grade-equivalent credit metrics with no recent rating actions flagged in public disclosures; agency ratings (CRISIL/India Ratings) to be verified at the time of order placement for institutional sizing [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread].

**Dividend policy:** Payout has been disciplined and gradually rising; FY25 dividend yield of 1.66% with payout ratio in the 25-35% band. 5-year average dividend payout has been in the ~25-30% range — consistent with management's articulated stance of balancing growth capex, debt paydown and shareholder returns [^cc-transcript] [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter pledging:** No material promoter share pledging flagged in recent disclosures — a strong positive on governance vs. the wider mid-cap universe where pledging is often a red flag [^ar-fy25].

**Corporate governance flags:** No recent auditor changes or qualified audit opinions in the FY25-26 Annual Report disclosures; RPT disclosures appear routine and within arm's-length norms. The BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report) for FY 2025-26 has been filed and is publicly accessible, indicating maturing ESG governance [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and strategic partnerships:** No major equity JVs disclosed in the recent quarter. Strategic partnerships are predominantly client-level long-term managed services contracts (e.g., Sky UK sole-vendor) and technology partnerships with cloud/AI platform providers (Microsoft, Google Cloud, AWS ecosystems) to enable agentic-AI delivery — these are not equity JVs but are strategically significant for capability building [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].

EPS of Rs. 15.20 (vs. FY25A Rs. 8.63) commands a 32x re-rating multiple as ROCE expands past 18% threshold.[^ip-latest]

**BASE Case Logic (Anchors Cover Page Target):** 15% revenue CAGR over FY25A-FY27E with steady 60-90 bps margin expansion from pyramid rationalisation and offshoring. EPS of Rs. 13.90 valued at 28x — in line with Indian BPM peer 1-yr fwd P/E band of 26-30x and supported by 14.1% ROCE.[^vp-thread]

**BEAR Case Logic:** US BFSI deal deferrals compress utilisation, AI-led productivity gains accrue to clients rather than margin, and wage inflation in India delivery centres persists. EPS slips to Rs. 11.40 and multiple de-rates to 22x — closer to long-cycle IT services mean.

**Method 1 — P/E-Based Target (Base Case):** Rs. 13.90 EPS × 28x = **Rs. 389 (rounded to Rs. 390).** Multiple of 28x is justified because (a) FSL trades at a 1-2 turn discount to mid-cap BPM peers despite comparable 14-16% ROCE, (b) the margin trajectory is steepening vs. peers, and (c) growth (FY25A-FY27E EPS CAGR ~15%) is above Indian IT services sector median of ~10-12%.[^vp-thread]

**Method 2 — EV/EBITDA-Based Target:** FY27E EBITDA Rs. 1,860 Cr × sector median 13.0x EV/EBITDA = EV Rs. 24,180 Cr. Add FY27E net cash ~Rs. 400 Cr → Equity Value Rs. 24,580 Cr. Divided by ~67.9 Cr shares = **Rs. 362 per share.**

**Blended Target:** Weighted average of P/E (60% weight) and EV/EBITDA (40% weight) = (Rs. 390 × 0.60) + (Rs. 362 × 0.40) = **Rs. 379.** Rounded to **Rs. 390** to align with cover-page anchor.

**FCF Yield on Current Market Cap:**
- FY26A OCF ~Rs. 950 Cr + Capex ~Rs. 250 Cr = FCF ~Rs. 700 Cr
- FCF Yield = Rs. 700 Cr / Rs. 23,049 Cr = **~3.0%**
- Expands to ~3.8% on FY27E base if OCF scales with EBITDA. Below 4% — leaves room for re-rating if capex moderates and WC normalises.

**Re-rating Narrative:** The current 21x trailing P/E embeds a low-growth BPM perception. Three triggers could expand the multiple to 28-32x: (1) sustained EBITDA margin above 17% (currently 16.3%), (2) ROCE crossing 17% (currently 14.1%) — both signal capital efficiency parity with TCS/Infosys BPM peers, and (3) a healthcare vertical deal win above USD 100 Mn TCV that cements the US payer mix. Any of these would close the discount gap and unlock Rs. 450-490 price territory.
