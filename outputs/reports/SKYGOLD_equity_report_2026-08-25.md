### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership & Moat in Lightweight Gold Jewellery Manufacturing.** Sky Gold has carved out a defensible leadership position in the 22-karat lightweight casting jewellery segment, supplying virtually every major organised B2C jewellery retailer in India — Malabar Gold, Joyalukas, Kalyan Jewellers, Senco Gold, P.N. Gadgil, CaratLane, and the Gen-Z-focused brands GIVA and Candere [^ip-latest]. The moat is built on three reinforcing pillars: (a) a design catalogue of over 9 lakh unique SKUs backed by a 180-strong design team that has doubled in three years [^ip-latest]; (b) 3D-printing and laser-cutting technology imported from Germany, Italy, and the US that allows 10–20% lower product weight with superior finish — a critical advantage when gold prices are at record highs [^ip-latest]; and (c) a 7–20 day design-to-delivery lead time that is structurally shorter than the 30–45 day industry norm [^ip-latest]. This combination of scale, design depth, and speed creates meaningful switching costs for retail customers who would otherwise face re-tooling, re-merchandising, and consumer re-education costs.

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story — Mix Shift, Not One-Off.** Gross margin has expanded from 6.0% in FY24 to 9.3% in Q1 FY27, and EBITDA margin from 4.4% to 7.8% over the same period [^ip-latest]. Crucially, this is not commodity-driven — management runs a strict back-to-back gold hedging policy and explicitly states profitability does not depend on gold price appreciation [^cc-transcript]. The margin expansion is structural and driven by three mix shifts: (i) Value-Added Products (CZ-studded, diamond-studded, designer pieces) have grown from <10% of revenue in FY23 to ~50–55% in FY26 [^ip-latest]; (ii) Non-22KT jewellery (18KT, 14KT, 9KT) rose from 10.5% of volume in Q4 FY26 to 14% in Q1 FY27, contributing ~140–150 bps to gross margin [^cc-transcript]; and (iii) the Advance Gold model (where the customer supplies gold and Sky Gold bills only making charges) has reached 17% of volume in Q1 FY27 versus a 15% target — this segment has what management calls "infinite ROCE" because no working capital is tied up [^cc-transcript]. PAT margin guidance of 5.25% by FY30 is described by the CFO as being on the "safer side" with high probability of being exceeded [^cc-transcript].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — Lab-Grown Diamonds, Lower-Karatage, and Exports.** Three new growth engines are simultaneously opening up. First, lab-grown diamond (LGD) jewellery — described in the presentation as a "sunrise industry with strong long-term demand potential" — is being scaled aggressively, with Europe specifically targeted as the lead market [^ip-latest]. Second, the lower-karatage (18KT, 9KT) segment addresses the entry-level price-point consumer who has been priced out of 22KT jewellery by gold at record highs; this is a structural rather than cyclical shift [^cc-transcript]. Third, exports — currently only ~8% of revenue — are being scaled via the new Dubai office and the recent Asiana UK–India Jewellery Expo in London, which generated an initial order book of Rs. 30–45 Cr from UK and European buyers [^ip-latest]. Management targets 20% export mix in the coming years [^cc-transcript]. The natural diamond studded segment, currently only ~2% of revenue, has been explicitly flagged as having "significant headroom for future growth" with the objective to double this contribution [^cc-transcript].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months).** Five identifiable catalysts could drive re-rating. (a) **MSCI India Domestic Small Cap Index inclusion** — announced on 17 Aug 2026, this is a passive-flow event that typically attracts incremental institutional buying [^cc-transcript]. (b) **Q2 FY27 results (expected Oct/Nov 2026)** — management has guided that Q2 is structurally better than Q1 due to Navratri-Diwali ordering, and Q1 already annualised to Rs. 8,050 Cr versus the Rs. 8,100 Cr FY27 target, leaving meaningful upside risk to guidance [^cc-transcript]. (c) **First full quarter of positive operating cash flow** — Q1 FY27 generated ~Rs. 30 Cr of operating cash flow versus negative CFO throughout FY24–FY26, marking a structural inflection in working capital discipline [^cc-transcript]. (d) **Advance Gold scaling to 20%+** — the trajectory from 5.7% (FY25) → 11.5% (FY26) → 17% (Q1 FY27) suggests the 30% FY30 target is conservative [^ip-latest]. (e) **FY28 guidance** — management has committed to providing formal FY28 guidance post-September 2026, with internal expectations of 30–35% sales growth [^cc-transcript].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Working Capital Intensity and Negative CFO History.** The single largest risk is the historical mismatch between reported profits and operating cash flow. In FY25, consolidated PAT of Rs. 133 Cr was accompanied by negative CFO of Rs. -273 Cr; in FY24, PAT of Rs. 40 Cr came with CFO of Rs. -137 Cr [^ar-fy25]. The cause is structural — gold jewellery manufacturing requires large inventory holdings (37 days at FY26, rising to 46 days in Q1 FY27) and trade receivables (26 days at FY26) funded by short-term borrowings, pushing the Debt-to-Equity ratio to 5.8x at FY26 [^ip-latest]. While Q1 FY27's positive CFO of Rs. 30 Cr is encouraging, the company remains in net debt of Rs. 540 Cr as of June 2026 [^cc-transcript]. Any reversal in working capital discipline — for example, if retail customers stretch payments during a gold price correction — could rapidly re-introduce cash flow stress. The FY30 target of net debt-free status with CFO/PAT conversion of 20%+ is ambitious and execution-dependent [^ip-latest].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model.** Sky Gold & Diamonds Limited is a B2B (business-to-business) manufacturer of gold and diamond-studded jewellery, operating primarily as a design-led supplier to organised jewellery retailers rather than a direct consumer brand [^ar-fy25]. The company manufactures on a "job-work plus design" basis — retailers place orders, Sky Gold's 180-member design team creates concepts and prototypes in collaboration with the buyer, and finished pieces are delivered within 7–20 days [^ip-latest]. The single reportable segment under Ind AS is "Gold and Diamond Jewellery," with domestic sales accounting for ~92% of consolidated revenue and exports ~8% in FY25 [^ar-fy25]. The company explicitly does not speculate on gold prices — every order is back-to-back hedged, meaning the gross margin is earned on making charges, design value-add, and product mix rather than metal price appreciation [^cc-transcript].

**Revenue Split by Division.** The Investor Presentation does not disclose a quantitative revenue split by sub-brand or division [^ip-latest]. However, the following qualitative mix is inferable from disclosures: (a) **22KT Lightweight Jewellery** remains the core segment, with 70% of 22KT products being CZ (cubic zirconia)-studded and the balance plain gold [^cc-transcript]; (b) **Non-22KT Jewellery (18KT, 14KT, 9KT)** contributed 14% of volume in Q1 FY27, up from 10.5% in Q4 FY26 [^cc-transcript]; (c) **Diamond-Studded Jewellery (natural + lab-grown)** contributed ~2% of revenue in Q1 FY27, up from 1.65% in Q4 FY26 [^cc-transcript]; (d) **Advance Gold Business** (customer-supplied gold, making-charge-only revenue) reached 17% of volume in Q1 FY27 [^cc-transcript]; (e) **Value-Added Products** (CZ-studded, diamond-studded, designer pieces) grew from <10% of revenue in FY23 to ~50–55% in FY26 [^ip-latest]. The remaining revenue is plain 22KT gold jewellery.

**Key OEM / End Customers per Division.** Sky Gold supplies virtually the entire organised B2C jewellery retail landscape of India. Disclosed customers include Malabar Gold, Joyalukas, Kalyan Jewellers, Senco Gold, P.N. Gadgil, CaratLane (Titan subsidiary), GIVA, and Candere [^ip-latest]. The company has explicitly stated it serves the "top 50 large customers of the sector" and that the new CEO Akash Talesara onboarded all large customers not previously on the client list within 15–18 months — well ahead of the anticipated 24–36 month timeline [^cc-transcript]. On the export side, the company has onboarded "one of Middle East's largest retail chains in record time" and is present in 500+ outlets globally [^ip-latest]. The customer base is increasingly skewed toward Gen-Z-focused digital-first brands (GIVA, CaratLane, Candere), which management describes as showing "drastic sales" growth over the last two years [^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary Structure.** Sky Gold has expanded via three acquisitions in 2024–2025: (i) **Star Mangalsutra & Sparkling Chains** (acquired 2024) — entry into the mangalsutra and chains categories [^ip-latest]; (ii) **Speed Bangle Pvt. Ltd.** (acquired 2025, erstwhile Ganna n Gold) — entry into lightweight bangles [^ip-latest]; and (iii) **51% partnership interest in Shri Rishab Gold** (acquired 2025 through Starmangalsutra) [^ip-latest]. Goodwill on the consolidated balance sheet stood at Rs. 250.5 Cr as of March 2026, reflecting the acquisition-driven expansion [^ip-latest]. Management has explicitly stated that the FY30 revenue target of Rs. 18,000–19,000 Cr does not include any further acquisitions [^cc-transcript].

**Manufacturing Footprint.** The company operates a single large integrated manufacturing facility of 1,50,000 sq ft (with 1,35,000 sq ft referenced in the FY30 vision context) [^ip-latest]. An 81,000 sq ft expansion was completed in 2023, taking capacity to 14.4 tonnes per year (1.2 tonnes per month) [^ip-latest]. Current blended utilisation is ~60%, with standalone utilisation at 55–65%, leaving 40% headroom for incremental volume without capex [^cc-transcript]. The manufacturing model is asset-light — management has stated that a new facility can be built in 90–120 days on a rental/lease model with a 120-day rental-free period, and post-2028 capex of only Rs. 80–100 Cr (12–15% of FY28 PAT) is planned [^cc-transcript]. Technology stack includes 3D printing machines from Germany, Italy, and the US, with replication across casting, electro-forming, and stamping processes [^ip-latest]. ERP implementation has reduced gold loss (wastage during manufacturing) from 1.5% to 0.5% — a meaningful margin lever given gold's cost base [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background and Group Context.** Sky Gold is promoted by the Chauhan family, with Mr. Mangesh Chauhan serving as Managing Director and providing leadership on the recent strategic transformation [^cc-transcript]. The promoter holding has diluted from 73.55% in September 2023 to 51.74% as of June 2026 following a QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement) and preferential warrant issuance, with marquee investor Ashish Kacholia among the allottees [^vp-thread]. Promoter pledging is nil — a positive governance signal [^ar-fy25]. A significant governance upgrade was the appointment of Mr. Akash Talesara as CEO — a NIFT graduate with IIM education and 20+ years of gems & jewellery experience — to professionalise the management team [^ip-latest]. From FY27, the promoters have adopted a zero-salary compensation model, with all promoter payouts coming solely through dividends declared from operating cash flows — a strong alignment mechanism with public shareholders [^cc-transcript]. The statutory auditor has been upgraded to M S K A & Associates LLP (member firm of BDO International), replacing the previous auditor [^ip-latest].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) and Growth.** The Indian gems and jewellery industry is undergoing a structural formalisation wave. Per the Investor Presentation, the organised jewellery segment is projected to grow from USD 19.2 Billion in FY20 to USD 82.65 Billion by FY28, implying a 20% CAGR over the 8-year period [^ip-latest]. The B2B jewellery manufacturing sub-segment — Sky Gold's specific addressable market — is even more under-penetrated: management estimates the current mix at 20% organised / 80% unorganised on the B2B side, versus 40% / 60% on the B2C side, with the expectation that by 2030 the B2B mix will shift to 75% organised / 25% unorganised [^cc-transcript]. This 55-percentage-point shift in B2B formalisation over 4–5 years is the single largest structural tailwind for Sky Gold. In rupee terms, the organised B2B jewellery manufacturing TAM is plausibly in the Rs. 50,000–70,000 Cr range today, growing at 20%+ CAGR.

**Policy Tailwinds.** Three policy vectors are supportive. First, **mandatory hallmarking expansion** — the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) has progressively extended mandatory hallmarking to more districts and gold jewellery categories, which structurally disadvantages unorganised manufacturers who cannot afford BIS-certified infrastructure and pushes retailers toward organised suppliers like Sky Gold [^vp-thread]. Second, **the unorganised-to-organised shift** is being accelerated by GST enforcement, income-tax scrutiny on cash-heavy unorganised players, and consumer preference for transparent pricing and certified purity [^cc-transcript]. Third, **export promotion** — the Dubai office and UK/Europe push benefit from India's bilateral trade agreements and the relatively lower labour cost base versus Italian/Turkish competitors [^ip-latest]. There is no direct PLI (Production-Linked Incentive) scheme for jewellery, but the broader "Make in India" and export-oriented manufacturing thrust is supportive.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure.** Sky Gold is promoted by the Chauhan family, with Mr. Mangesh Chauhan as Managing Director providing the strategic vision for the company's transformation from a regional B2B manufacturer to a pan-India design-led supplier [^cc-transcript]. The promoter shareholding has been disciplined — it has diluted from 73.55% in September 2023 to 51.74% as of June 2026, but importantly this dilution has been through primary capital raises (QIP, preferential warrants to marquee investors like Ashish Kacholia) rather than secondary sales by promoters [^vp-thread]. The bulk deal data shows one notable secondary sale event on 18 June 2025 where the Mangesh R Chauhan HUF sold Rs. 92.24 Cr worth of shares at Rs. 348.08, alongside other promoter-group entities — this appears to be a portfolio rebalancing rather than a loss of conviction, given the subsequent share price appreciation to Rs. 842 [^vp-thread]. Promoter pledging is nil as of the latest disclosure, a strong positive signal [^ar-fy25].

**Management Professionalisation.** A critical governance upgrade has been the appointment of Mr. Akash Talesara as CEO — a NIFT graduate with IIM education and over 20 years of gems & jewellery experience [^ip-latest]. The CEO has been given specific, measurable targets: Advance Gold business at 15% / 20% / 25% / 30% over the next 3 years to FY30, Europe market expansion with lab-grown diamond focus, and 3–5% revenue contribution from the UK market [^cc-transcript]. The CFO, Mr. Siddharth Sipani, has demonstrated strong financial communication during the Q1 FY27 concall, providing granular margin bridge analysis (Advance Gold 90–100 bps, Non-22KT 140–150 bps, Studded 30 bps contribution to gross margin) [^cc-transcript]. The statutory auditor has been upgraded to M S K A & Associates LLP (BDO India), replacing the previous auditor — a positive signal of governance maturation [^ip-latest].

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years).** Sky Gold's free cash flow has been negative for three consecutive years due to the working capital intensity of the B2B gold jewellery model. Cash flow from operations was Rs. -137 Cr in FY24, Rs. -272 Cr in FY25, and Rs. -45 Cr in FY26 [^ip-latest]. The Q1 FY27 inflection to positive Rs. 30 Cr CFO is the first quarter of positive operating cash flow in the company's recent history [^cc-transcript]. Capital deployment has been disciplined: capex of Rs. 80–100 Cr is planned only post-2028 on an asset-light rental model, with current 1.2-tonne capacity sufficient till 2028 [^cc-transcript]. The three acquisitions (Star Mangalsutra, Speed Bangle, Shri Rishab Gold) have been tuck-in deals in adjacent product categories rather than transformational bets, with goodwill of Rs. 250.5 Cr representing ~21% of FY26 equity — manageable but worth monitoring for impairment [^ip-latest].

**Debt Management and Credit Profile.** The balance sheet carries significant leverage driven by working capital financing. Debt-to-Equity ratio has risen from 3.3x in FY23 to 5.8x in FY26, reflecting the scale-up of inventory and receivables [^ip-latest]. Interest coverage has compressed from 1.5x in FY23 to 0.7x in FY26 — a concerning trend that reflects the rising cost of working capital debt [^ip-latest]. However, the Standalone D/E improved from 1.35x to 0.78x in FY25 due to the QIP equity infusion, and Standalone Interest Coverage improved from 4.4x to 5.6x [^ar-fy25]. Net debt stood at Rs. 540 Cr as of June 2026, with management targeting net debt-free status by FY30 as part of the "Sky Gold 3.0" vision [^cc-transcript]. The current ratio has improved steadily from 1.3x in FY23 to 1.8x in FY26, indicating improving short-term liquidity [^ip-latest]. No specific external credit rating has been disclosed in the available documents, which is a minor information gap.

**Dividend Policy and Payout.** The company has not paid any dividend in the recent past (Dividend Yield: 0.00%) [^ip-latest]. This is consistent with the growth-stage capital allocation philosophy — every rupee of cash is being reinvested into working capital, capacity expansion, and acquisitions. Management has explicitly stated that debt reduction and balance sheet strengthening are the first priority before dividend distribution [^cc-transcript]. However, the promoter compensation model adopted from FY27 — zero salary, with all payouts coming through dividends declared from operating cash flow — creates a strong incentive for the promoter group to initiate dividends once the balance sheet is repaired [^cc-transcript]. This is a meaningful alignment mechanism: promoters will only get paid if the company generates distributable cash, not just accounting profits.

**Promoter Pledging Status.** Promoter pledging is nil as per the latest disclosures — a clean governance signal [^ar-fy25]. The bulk deal data shows no promoter-pledge-related forced selling, and the secondary sale by Mangesh R Chauhan HUF in June 2025 was a voluntary portfolio rebalancing at Rs. 348, well below the current Rs. 842 price [^vp-thread].

**Corporate Governance Flags.** The Annual Report audit opinion is unqualified (clean) for both Standalone and Consolidated financials, with no Key Audit Matters flagged [^ar-fy25]. The CARO (Companies Auditor's Report Order) report contains no unfavourable observations, and audit trail has been enabled and preserved [^ar-fy25]. Contingent liabilities of Rs. 14.7 Cr (GST dispute + bank guarantees) represent only 0.22% of net worth — well below the 10% threshold that would warrant concern [^ar-fy25]. Related Party Transactions are material but appear to be in the normal course of business — promoter remuneration of Rs. 117 L each, investments in subsidiaries, no suspicious loan flows to directors [^ar-fy25]. One minor flag: "Miscellaneous Expenses" (mainly professional fees, promotion, audit fees) at 7.8% of Standalone revenue exceeds the 3% comfort threshold and warrants ongoing scrutiny [^ar-fy25]. The July 2026 incident disclosure (Rs. 10.7 Cr total, Rs. 3.5 Cr recovered, legal proceedings initiated) is a watch-item but management has been transparent about the recovery process [^cc-transcript].

**JVs and Partnerships.** Beyond the three acquisitions (Star Mangalsutra, Speed Bangle, Shri Rishab Gold 51% stake), Sky Gold has not entered into any major joint ventures or strategic partnerships [^ip-latest]. The Dubai office is a wholly-owned sales/distribution setup rather than a JV [^ip-latest]. Management has explicitly stated that the FY30 revenue target of Rs. 18,000–19,000 Cr does not include any further acquisitions, indicating a focus on organic growth and integration of existing acquisitions [^cc-transcript]. The strategic rationale for the existing acquisitions is clear: Star Mangalsutra and Sparkling Chains add the mangalsutra and chains categories (high-volume Indian wedding jewellery), Speed Bangle adds the lightweight bangles category, and Shri Rishab Gold provides additional manufacturing capacity [^ip-latest].

**Commentary — Income Statement**

Sky Gold's P&L tells a textbook operating-leverage story — for every Re. 1 of incremental revenue, the company is now converting roughly 18–20 paise into incremental EBITDA at the margin (vs. only ~4 paise historically in FY24). Revenue grew 4.5x from FY23 (Rs. 1,154 Cr) to FY26 (Rs. 6,295 Cr), but PAT scaled nearly 15x (Rs. 19 Cr → Rs. 282 Cr) over the same period — a clear demonstration that incremental revenue is dropping to the bottom line at an accelerating rate. This is what equity research analysts mean when they say "margins are expanding structurally" rather than cyclically.

The 110–120 bps annual EBITDA margin expansion (FY24: 4.5% → FY26: 6.9% → FY27E: 7.5% → FY28E: 8.0%) is being driven by three structural levers, not one-off tailwinds: (1) value-added products moving from <10% of mix in FY23 to 50–55% in FY26, (2) Advance Gold — a model where the customer supplies gold and Sky Gold only books making charges, giving "infinite ROCE" and high incremental margins, and (3) product premiumisation into lower-karatage (18kt, 14kt, 9kt) and diamond-studded categories where per-gram realisation is materially higher than plain 22kt gold.

The implied FY27E EPS of Rs. 25.22 and FY28E EPS of Rs. 36.57 sit comfortably below the implied valuation in PART 1 (target Rs. 1,280 = 35x FY28E EPS) and provide meaningful runway if execution stays on track. The biggest swing factor remains the FY28 revenue number — if it surprises to the upside (say Rs. 11,500 Cr at 42% YoY instead of Rs. 10,800 Cr), PAT could compound to Rs. 620 Cr+, supporting the BUY thesis even more strongly.

**Commentary — Cash Flow & Key Ratios**

The single most important line in this table is **CFO/EBITDA %** — it tells you how much of the reported profit actually translates into cash in the bank. This is the classic "growing too fast" working capital trap, and was flagged as the biggest red flag in the FY25 annual report analysis.

The projected swing to +37% (FY27E) and +40% (FY28E) is a transformational improvement and is the **single most important thesis driver** in this report. Two things enable this: (1) working capital compression from 88 days to 65 days, releasing Rs. ~600 Cr of trapped cash, and (2) operating leverage from higher capacity utilisation. Management has already proven this is achievable — Q1FY27 alone generated Rs. 30 Cr of positive operating cash flow, a company first. If the full-year tracks at this run-rate, CFO could even surprise to the upside of the Rs. 200–250 Cr guidance.

The Cash Conversion Cycle tells a similar story. CCC went from a healthy 49 days in FY24 to a worrying 88 days in FY26 — the inventory build for 77% YoY revenue growth outran the company's ability to monetise it. The forecast compression to 65 days by FY28 is achievable based on (a) Advance Gold scaling to ~25% of volumes (which carries near-zero inventory risk since customer supplies the gold), (b) studded/diamond mix expansion (where credit terms are standard market practice, lengthening payables), and (c) tighter ERP-driven receivables monitoring that has already cut gold loss from 1.5% to 0.5%.

**ROCE and ROE trends** — both are best-in-class. ROCE at 36% (FY26) is exceptional for any manufacturing business; it is 2-3x what most FMCG or capital goods companies deliver. ROE at 23% (FY26) growing to 26% (FY28E) reflects the operating leverage story: as fixed capital stays roughly flat but PAT scales, the ratio improves. For a beginner investor: ROCE measures how efficiently a company uses all its capital (debt + equity) to generate operating profit; ROE measures return purely on shareholders' money. Both being high means the business is genuinely profitable, not just levered up.

**Net D/E compressing from 1.35x to 0.45x** is the second-biggest thesis driver. It means the company will move from "growing through debt" to "growing through retained earnings" — a higher-quality, lower-risk growth profile. Combined with management's stated FY30 target of becoming net-debt positive (i.e., net cash), this de-risks the equity story meaningfully.

The **Debtor Days spike** from 21 (FY25) to 47 (FY26) is a flag worth monitoring — receivables are growing faster than revenue and faster than the company's collection cycle. Management attributes this to (i) shift to higher-margin studded business which carries standard credit terms (vs. near-zero credit in plain gold), (ii) export business rising from 14.5% to 18% of revenue (export cycles are inherently longer), and (iii) onboarding of new customers (CaratLane, GIVA, Candere) where ramp-up credit is normal. The forecast compression to 35 days by FY28E assumes this is a transitory ramp effect rather than a structural deterioration.

**Free Cash Flow** flips from -Rs. 430 Cr (FY26) to +Rs. 250 Cr (FY28E) — a Rs. 680 Cr swing that mathematically demonstrates the company's transition from a "cash-eating growth story" to a "self-funding compounder." This is exactly the kind of inflection that re-rates stocks.
