## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Exclusive global BlackBerry licensee creates a defensible niche moat:** Optiemus holds the exclusive worldwide rights to design, manufacture, and distribute BlackBerry-branded smartphones, accessories, and IoT/security products. With BlackBerry's brand equity in enterprise security and the company's own QNX-derivative software stack, Optiemus is the only Indian player with a globally-licensed premium handset brand — a structural advantage no domestic peer (Dixon, Lava, Micromax) can replicate.

- **Structural margin expansion from backward-integration into tempered glass (Optiemus Uniglobal):** In-house manufacturing of tempered glass and screen protectors at the Noida facility has shifted Optiemus from a low-margin distribution model (3–5% EBITDA) toward a higher-margin components play (12–15% EBITDA on Uniglobal). We estimate Uniglobal contributes ~Rs. 350–400 Cr revenue at FY26E with EBITDA margins of 14–16%, expanding 200–300 bps over the consolidated margin profile.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies — drones (Aero360) and IT services:** Aero360 is positioned to capture India's drone PLI scheme (Rs. 120 Cr allocation) targeting Rs. 30,000 Cr TAM by 2030. Combined with the IT/enterprise services vertical (BlackBerry-secured MDM, cybersecurity), Optiemus is transitioning from a single-line telecom distributor to a multi-vertical EMS player — a rerating catalyst.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) BlackBerry smartphone refresh cycle with global rollout in EU/SEA markets, (ii) Aero360 commercial order wins from defence and agriculture segments under Drone PLI, (iii) Uniglobal capacity expansion from 25 mn units/yr to 50 mn units/yr by Q3FY27, (iv) potential entry into wearable/IoT categories under BlackBerry license, (v) Q1FY27 results expected to show first full quarter of consolidated drone revenue.

- **Biggest structural risk — Xiaomi concentration in telecom distribution:** Telecom distribution (Xiaomi, Honor, Nokia, OnePlus) still accounts for ~55–60% of consolidated revenue. Any deterioration in Xiaomi's India market share (currently ~15% and declining) or margin compression from channel partners could shave 150–200 bps off consolidated EBITDA. Regulatory action on Chinese OEMs (similar to 2020–22 restrictions) remains the single largest tail risk.
