### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Ceat is the #3 player in the Indian tyre industry by domestic revenue (~13% OEM share) with leadership in the 2W replacement segment and a fast-scaling OEM position across PV, CV and 2W — a portfolio breadth that only MRF and Apollo can match, supporting a defensible mid-teens ROCE through cycles.[^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25]

- **Structural margin story:** Consolidated EBITDA margin expanded from 11.8% in FY22 to ~16.5% in FY25, driven by the Halol (Gujarat) and Chennai greenfield ramps, specialty mix in TBB (Truck-Bus-Bias) PCR, and RM softening; management guides 17–18% sustained EBITDA margin by FY28E as premium product share (WAM, Run-Flat, EV-spec) crosses 30% of revenue.[^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript]

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** The company is pivoting decisively from a pure 2W/CV cyclical into (a) PCR — capacity scaling from 0 to 30% of PCR revenue mix, (b) Exports — share already at 14% of revenue with targets of 20% by FY28E, and (c) Off-Highway/OTR — Srinagar facility (J&K) supplies CEAT-specialty radial OTR tyres to global mining majors.[^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript]

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1 FY27 results expected to show 220–250 bps YoY EBITDA margin expansion led by Chennai radial PCR utilization; (ii) EU and US anti-dumping duty extensions in FY26 create an export window — CEAT is one of the few Indian players with TBR/PCR homologations in EU; (iii) Replacement demand tailwind from monsoon-led MHCV utilization uptick; (iv) Potential re-rating on inclusion in broader NSE indices given FII float expansion.[^ip-latest] [^vp-thread]

- **Biggest structural risk:** Sustained RM volatility (natural rubber, carbon black) combined with intense pricing pressure from Chinese PCR imports in the replacement market can compress gross margins by 150–200 bps; CEAT's premium positioning is its hedge, but a sustained 2W OEM volume softness (Honda, Hero destocking) would offset the PCR diversification gains.[^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript]

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Ceat Limited, the flagship of the RPG Group, is one of India's top-three tyre manufacturers with a comprehensive product portfolio spanning Two-Wheeler (2W), Passenger Car Radial (PCR), Truck-Bus-Bias (TBB), Truck-Bus-Radial (TBR), Off-Highway Tyres (OHT/OTR), and Farm tyres. The company operates on a hybrid OEM + replacement-channel business model, with replacement contributing ~62% of FY25 standalone revenue (a structurally higher-margin pocket) and OEM ~38%.[^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25]

**Revenue split by division (FY25, consolidated):**
- 2W tyres: ~32% of revenue (leadership position; key OEM customers include Honda Motorcycle & Scooter India, Hero MotoCorp, Bajaj Auto, TVS Motor, Royal Enfield)
- TBB + TBR (Commercial vehicles): ~28% (OEM: Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, Mahindra Truck & Bus, VE Commercial Vehicles; Repl. dealers across India)
- PCR (Passenger Car Radials): ~18% (OEM: Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors PV, Hyundai, Mahindra & Mahindra, Stellantis; growing presence in Kia and MG)
- Off-Highway / OTR / Farm: ~12% (mining, construction, agriculture)
- Exports: ~10% of standalone revenue, with key markets being the US, EU, Latin America, Africa, and SAARC[^ip-latest]

**Subsidiary structure:** Ceat Limited (the listed entity) consolidates Ceat Specialty Tyres Ltd. (the OHT/OTR and farm radial business — the Srinagar JV, now wholly owned post-FY24 restructuring) and 100%-owned Ceat International FZE (Dubai-based distribution arm for the Middle East and Africa). The RPG Group parentage provides access to KEC International, Zensar Technologies, and Saregama within the broader ecosystem, though there are no direct operational cross-holdings within the tyre business.[^ar-fy25]

**Manufacturing footprint:** Ceat operates 6 manufacturing plants across India — (i) Bhandup, Mumbai (2W, specialty, HQ), Maharashtra; (ii) Nashik (TBB, TBR), Maharashtra; (iii) Halol (TBR, PCR), Gujarat; (iv) Chennai (PCR greenfield), Tamil Nadu; (v) Morai (TBB, farm), Gujarat; and (vi) Srinagar (OHT/OTR radials), Jammu & Kashmir. Combined installed capacity stands at ~5,000 MT/day (~15 lakh tyres/month equivalent), with Chennai capable of scaling to 30,000 tyres/day at full ramp.[^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25]

**Promoter / group background:** Ceat was founded in 1958 by the RPG Group, founded by Rama Prasad Goenka. The promoter entity RPG Enterprises Ltd. and related parties hold 48.50% (per the Header Block), with Harsh Goenka and Anant Goenka representing the second-generation promoter lineage. The RPG Group's diversified interests (infrastructure EPC via KEC, IT services via Zensar, entertainment via Saregama) provide strategic stability and access to capital markets, though the tyre business is operationally independent and has never relied on group cross-subsidization.[^ar-fy25]

The Indian tyre industry is a ~Rs. 95,000 Cr (FY25) TAM, growing at 9–10% CAGR with structural tailwinds from rising vehicle parc, replacement-driven demand mix (~65% of industry revenue), and increasing radialization. Industry revenue pool is expected to reach Rs. 1,40,000–1,50,000 Cr by FY28E (CAGR ~11%).[^ip-latest]

**TAM sizing:**
- Industry revenue: ~Rs. 95,000 Cr (FY25) / ~USD 11.4 Bn

- Volume CAGR (FY25–FY28E): 8–9%
- Value CAGR (FY25–FY28E): 10–11% (premium/mix tailwind)

**Policy tailwinds:**
- **PLI Scheme for Auto & Auto Components:** Rs. 25,938 Cr outlay; advanced automotive technology (including specialty tyres, EV-grade tyres) qualifies — Ceat's Halol and Chennai plants are in the process of applying.

- **RE (renewable energy) targets:** Sustainability mandates push OE buyers to source lower-carbon tyres; CEAT's BRSR FY26 disclosures show 38% renewable power mix at Halol — a differentiator vs. peers.[^ar-fy25]
- **US/EU anti-dumping duty extensions (FY25–26):** AD/CVD measures on Chinese PCR/TBR continue — Indian exporters Ceat, MRF, Apollo, and JK Tyre benefit.
