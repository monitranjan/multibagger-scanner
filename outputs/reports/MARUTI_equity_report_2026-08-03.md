### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Undisputed market leadership with structural moat:** MSIL commands ~41% market share in the domestic passenger vehicle (PV) segment and has held the #1 slot for over four decades [^ip-latest]. The breadth of the portfolio across entry-level (Alto, WagonR), compact (Swift, Baleno), mid-size (Dzire, Brezza) and UV (Grand Vitara, Jimny) provides unmatched reach across price points — a competitive moat we rate **Strong**. The 7.5 lakh unit annual Kharkhoda plant, which commenced phased production in FY25, addresses the chronic capacity bottleneck that has historically forced MSIL to refuse bookings on top-selling models [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin expansion story (12.0% EBITDA → 14.0%+ trajectory):** EBITDA margin expanded from 9.6% in FY20 to 12.0% in FY25, and management has guided to a path toward 14% by FY28E [^cc-transcript]. The drivers are: (i) operating leverage on Kharkhoda (currently dragging margins at ~10%), (ii) richer mix as SUVs rise to 25%+ of portfolio, (iii) reduction in warranty and commodity tailwinds, and (iv) ~150-200 bps cumulative benefit from PLI scheme on advanced automotive technology components [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** (i) **SUVs** now contribute ~22% of volumes vs. ~12% three years ago, with eVX (born-EV) launching FY26 targeting the Rs. 15-25 lakh EV segment [^ip-latest]. (ii) **Exports** scaled to ~3 lakh units in FY25; the first Africa CKD assembly contract in Egypt is operational and Mozambique is being commissioned — diversifying away from a single-geography concentration risk [^ar-fy25]. (iii) **Suzuki Motor Gujarat (SMG)** fully consolidated on a profit-share basis from FY25, removing the earlier minority leakage.

- **Near-term catalysts (next 6-12 months):** (1) eVX launch in late FY26 — first true born-EV from MSIL, critical to defend share in metro markets where ICE volumes are already softening [^cc-transcript]. (2) Kharkhoda ramp to full utilization (currently ~60%) — every 10% utilization step adds ~50 bps to EBITDA margin and ~Rs. 700 Cr to annualized EBITDA [^ip-latest]. (3) Margin uplift from price hikes taken in Q4FY25/Jimny anniversary edition. (4) Operating leverage from a normalized chip and commodity environment. (5) Potential re-rating from reclassification into the large-cap EV thematic basket.

- **Biggest structural risk — EV disruption and Suzuki royalty overhang:** The Suzuki group is reportedly preparing to directly manufacture EVs in India via Suzuki Motor Corporation, potentially bypassing the listed entity, which has historically been a minority-shareholder-unfriendly friction point [^vp-thread]. Concurrently, the ~17.09% FII holding is below historical 22%+ levels — partly reflecting this structural overhang. Compounded by intensifying competition from Tata Motors (Nexon/EV), Hyundai (Creta EV), and Mahindra (XUV.e8), the EV transition execution risk is the single most-watched variable for the stock.
