### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

*   **(1) Strong Deposit Franchise & Improving Asset Quality:** Bank of Maharashtra (BoM) benefits from a robust retail deposit franchise, particularly its CASA ratio of 52.73% (FY24), among the best in the PSU banking space, providing a low-cost funding advantage. Asset quality has significantly improved, with GNPA at 2.04% and NNPA at 0.20% as of Q4 FY24, driven by stringent recovery mechanisms and proactive bad loan management. This reduces credit costs and supports profitability.
*   **(2) Sustainable NIM Expansion & Profitability:** The bank has demonstrated consistent Net Interest Margin (NIM) expansion, reaching 3.98% in FY24, supported by its strong CASA base and a shift towards higher-yielding retail and MSME advances. This, coupled with controlled operating expenses (Cost-to-Income ratio at 43.15% in FY24), is driving sustainable growth in Net Interest Income (NII) and bottom-line profitability.
*   **(3) Focused Retail & MSME Growth:** BoM is strategically expanding its high-growth, high-margin retail and MSME loan book, which now constitute a significant portion of its advances. The retail segment grew by 21.04% YoY in FY24, and MSME by 19.82% YoY, indicating a strong focus on granular and diversified credit growth. This reduces concentration risk and enhances yield.
*   **(4) Near-Term Catalysts: Government Support & Digital Push:** The Indian government's continued focus on strengthening PSU banks, potential capital infusions (though less critical now given improved financials), and a push for digital banking adoption (e.g., ONDC integration, digital lending platforms) will act as catalysts. BoM's strong operational metrics position it well to leverage these initiatives for further market share gains and operational efficiencies over the next 6-12 months.
*   **(5) Biggest Structural Risk: PSU Bank Headwinds:** Despite improvements, BoM remains exposed to broader PSU banking sector risks including potential government policy interventions, slower credit growth in specific cycles, and competition from large private sector banks. While asset quality has improved, any systemic stress in specific sectors (e.g., agriculture, certain MSME segments) could lead to an uptick in fresh slippages, impacting profitability.
