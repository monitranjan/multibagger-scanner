### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Global Leadership in Small-Tractor 3PL Systems — A Genuinely Sticky Moat**

Uniparts is the world's largest manufacturer of Three-Point Linkage (3PL) systems for small tractors (under 70 horsepower) [^ip-latest]. To put 3PL in simple terms: imagine the metal triangular hitch assembly on the back of a tractor that connects it to ploughs, rotavators, and trailers — that is exactly what 3PL is. The 3PL category represents ~48.8% of Uniparts' finished goods revenue and is the foundation of the company's global market position [^ip-latest]. The moat is genuine for three reasons. First, **design-in lock-in**: once Uniparts' 3PL is engineered into a tractor platform, switching mid-cycle is prohibitively expensive for the OEM because it requires re-engineering and re-homologation. Second, **tooling amortization**: every new platform requires Uniparts to invest crores of rupees in dies and fixtures, and OEMs typically amortize these costs over 5–7 year platform lives. Third, **scale advantage**: Uniparts services all 10 leading global Off-Highway Vehicle (OHV) OEMs in agriculture, with four of the top five customers associated for over a decade [^ip-latest]. Top customer concentration is high (Top 1 = 31%, Top 5 = 71%, Top 10 = 84% of Q1 FY27 sales [^ip-latest]), but this is actually evidence of design-in stickiness rather than a vulnerability, since these relationships have survived multiple downcycles.

**2. Structural Margin Story — Channel Mix Shift + Operating Leverage = Sustained >20% EBITDA**

Uniparts has decisively exited the FY25 cyclical trough. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin surged to **25.3%**, up from 20.7% in Q1 FY26 — a 460 basis points (bps) expansion [^ip-latest]. To explain bps in plain English: a "basis point" is 1/100th of a percentage point, so 460 bps means 4.6 percentage points of margin gain in a single year, which is exceptional. The margin expansion is not a one-off; management has consistently guided for a cycle EBITDA margin of ~20%, and current levels are above this target because of two structural drivers. **Driver 1 — Channel mix shift**: warehouse-managed sales (where Uniparts ships finished goods to a warehouse in the customer's geography, holding inventory locally and delivering just-in-time) rose from 50–52% of revenue in Q1 FY26 to ~56% in Q1 FY27 [^cc-transcript]. Warehouse sales are the highest-margin product because Uniparts captures both manufacturing margin and inventory/installation margin. **Driver 2 — Operating leverage**: with revenue growing 27% YoY in Q1 FY27, fixed costs (depreciation, factory overheads) are being spread over a larger base, dropping through to EBITDA [^ip-latest]. Material costs are guided in the 34–37% of revenue range, depending on product and channel mix [^cc-transcript]. Even after normalizing for the favorable Q1 mix, we believe Uniparts can sustain 21–22% EBITDA margins through the cycle — significantly above peers in the precision engineering space.

**3. Diversification into Large Ag, Construction, and System Adjacencies — Tamping Down Cyclicality**

Uniparts is in the middle of a deliberate strategic broadbasing. Construction & Forestry/Mining (CFM) already accounts for 46.2% of Q1 FY27 revenue (up from 36.0% in FY25 per the Annual Report), and this segment is in a multi-quarter recovery driven by US tech/infrastructure capex and European government infrastructure spending [^cc-transcript]. Within agriculture, Uniparts is extending from its stronghold in small tractors (<70 HP) into **large agricultural equipment** (>70 HP) — a natural adjaceny given its existing customer relationships at major OEMs [^ip-latest]. The new product platforms include Power Take-Off (PTO) shafts, Hydraulic Cylinders, and Fabrication — these are synergistic system offerings that allow Uniparts to bid for larger content-per-tractor contracts [^ip-latest]. Geographically, Mexico nearshoring is live: the first warehouse in Mexico has commenced operations, with first customer deliveries expected in Q3 FY27, capturing the structural US-China decoupling tailwind [^cc-transcript]. The Trailing Twelve-Month (TTM) new business order book stands at over Rs. 225 Cr, spanning segments and geographies [^ip-latest]. This diversification roadmap directly addresses Uniparts' historical vulnerability to small-tractor cyclicality in the Americas.

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months) — Q1 FY27 Beat Sets the Tone**

The Q1 FY27 print is the catalyst: Revenue grew 27.0% YoY to Rs. 347.4 Cr, EBITDA grew 55.4% YoY to Rs. 89.8 Cr, and PAT grew 64.1% YoY to Rs. 56.6 Cr [^ip-latest]. Basic EPS for Q1 stood at Rs. 12.54, with TTM EPS at Rs. 39.97 [^ip-latest]. Importantly, management has indicated that FY27 top-line growth will be "a couple of percentage points better" than FY26's 21% YoY growth, with H2 FY27 expected to be stronger than H1 due to the agriculture recovery [^cc-transcript]. Specific near-term milestones to track: (a) **Mexico warehouse first customer deliveries in Q3 FY27** — this validates the nearshoring thesis; (b) **Ludhiana finishing shop restoration completion** — already on schedule with customer supply uninterrupted [^ip-latest]; (c) **M&A closures** — management is actively evaluating about half a dozen acquisition targets in hydraulics, PTOs, and large fabrication, with the stated discipline of being value-accretive and ROCE/ROE-accretive within 18–30 months [^cc-transcript]; (d) **ESOP grant of 4,623 stock options in Aug 2026** signals management's confidence in sustained upside; (e) **Cycle trough confirmation** for large ag in FY26 with recovery expected in CY2027/FY28 [^cc-transcript].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Customer Concentration + Cyclical Ag Demand + Contingent Liabilities**

While the bull case is compelling, the bear case cannot be ignored. **Top 1 customer at 31% of Q1 FY27 revenue** [^ip-latest] — meaning if Uniparts loses or is de-contented by this customer (likely one of the global majors like John Deere, AGCO, or CNH), revenue could fall by ~Rs. 400 Cr annualized, which would be a 30%+ top-line hit. The Americas account for 60.6% of revenue [^ip-latest], exposing Uniparts to (a) US tractor cyclicality, (b) USD-INR forex volatility (a 1% INR appreciation ~ Rs. 4 Cr PAT hit on rough math), and (c) any US-China trade decoupling whiplash. The FY25 Annual Report flags **contingent liabilities of Rs. 162 Cr on a net worth of Rs. 574 Cr — 28%** — almost entirely comprising income tax demands [^ar-fy25]. To explain contingent liabilities in simple terms: these are potential future outflows if the company loses pending tax disputes — they are not on the balance sheet but are disclosed in the footnotes. A 28% ratio is a meaningful red flag and warrants monitoring of the actual tax assessments. Finally, the **related-party transaction (RPT) footprint is large** — standalone sales to subsidiaries were ~46% of standalone revenue in FY25, and loans to subsidiary Gripwel Conag were Rs. 286 Cr [^ar-fy25] — though management has stated these are arm's length. Investors must triangulate RPT disclosures carefully.

**Valuation Methodology & 12M Target Price Derivation:**

We apply a target P/E of 30x to our FY27E EPS estimate of Rs. 45–47, derived from (a) Q1 FY27 annualized EPS adjusted for cycle normalization, (b) management's guidance of FY27 growth being a couple of percentage points above the 21% FY26 growth, and (c) recovery in large ag in H2 FY27. We also cross-check against TTM EPS of Rs. 39.97 at current P/E of 24.2x — applying 30x to FY27E gives a 56.7% upside to CMP of Rs. 893.45. The 30x target multiple is justified by Uniparts' net-cash balance sheet (Rs. 190 Cr), >27% TTM ROCE [^ip-latest], and structural margin expansion. **12M Target Price: Rs. 1,400 | Upside: 56.7% | Rating: BUY**.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model — What Does Uniparts Actually Do?**

Uniparts India is a precision engineering company that designs, manufactures, and supplies critical drivetrain and linkage components to global Off-Highway Vehicle (OHV) OEMs — primarily tractor and construction equipment manufacturers. To translate the jargon: "drivetrain" refers to the parts that transmit power from the engine to the wheels/working parts, while "OHV" means vehicles not used on regular roads — tractors, harvesters, backhoe loaders, skid steers, and similar machines. The company runs a hub-and-spoke manufacturing model: it produces precision components in its Indian factories (mainly Ludhiana), ships semi-finished or finished goods to strategically located warehouses in the Americas and Europe, and then dispatches to OEM assembly lines on a just-in-time basis.

The company's five product verticals are:

- **Three-Point Linkage (3PL) — 48.8% of Q1 FY27 finished goods sales** [^ip-latest]. This is the triangular hitch on the back of a tractor that lifts and connects implements.
- **Precision Machined Parts (PMP) — 48.6%** [^ip-latest]. Broad basket of high-tolerance machined components for tractors and construction equipment — gears, shafts, knuckles, hubs.
- **Power Take-Off (PTO) — 1.1%** [^ip-latest]. The shaft that transfers engine power to attached implements (rotavators, pumps). A synergistic system offering.
- **Fabrication — 1.5%** [^ip-latest]. Welded assemblies; an emerging platform being developed organically with meaningful revenue expected in 18–24 months [^cc-transcript].
- **Hydraulic Cylinders — captured within PMP/Fabrication but being scaled as a standalone system offering** [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) and Growth Drivers**

The global Off-Highway Vehicle (OHV) market — comprising agricultural tractors, construction equipment, mining machinery, and forestry equipment — is estimated at **USD 100–120 billion in 2025**, growing at a 4–6% CAGR through 2030. Within this, Uniparts's relevant served addressable market (3PL systems, precision machined drivetrain parts, PTOs, and hydraulic cylinders for tractors and CE) is approximately **USD 8–10 billion**. India's auto components industry, more broadly, is targeted by the Government of India to reach **USD 100 billion by 2030** from current ~USD 70 billion, implying a ~7% CAGR [^vp-thread]. For Uniparts specifically, the addressable segments are:

- **Global tractor market**: ~USD 130 billion, ~5% CAGR. India is the largest tractor market by volume (over 900,000 units annually).
- **Global construction equipment market**: ~USD 250 billion, ~5–6% CAGR, with strong cyclical recovery post-2024.
- **Relevant precision engineering adjacencies** (PTOs, hydraulics, fabrications): ~USD 20–25 billion globally.

**Policy Tailwinds**

1. **Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme for Auto and Auto Components**: The Indian government's PLI scheme provides incremental sales incentives for advanced automotive technology components. While Uniparts exports primarily, its India manufacturing base could benefit from PLI for specialty / critical components.
2. **FAME and EV transition — limited direct relevance**: Uniparts' OHV exposure is largely ICE-based (tractor and CE), so FAME-driven EV tailwinds do not apply directly. However, electrification of off-highway equipment is multi-year away, providing visibility.
3. **US Infrastructure Investment & Jobs Act**: Drives US construction equipment demand — directly relevant to Uniparts's CFM segment (46.2% of revenue) [^cc-transcript].
4. **European Green Deal & Recovery Fund**: Drives European construction equipment demand — relevant for the 21.1% Europe revenue exposure [^cc-transcript].
5. **India Agricultural Mechanization push**: Government subsidies and Minimum Support Price (MSP) increases support Indian tractor demand — supports the 12.3% India revenue exposure [^cc-transcript].
6. **Mexico Near-shoring**: Structural shift in North American manufacturing supply chains benefits the Mexico warehouse strategy [^cc-transcript].

**Valuation Discount/Premium vs Peers**

Uniparts trades at a **24.2x TTM P/E**, which is a substantial discount to the precision engineering / auto-component peer average of ~38x P/E [^vp-thread]. On EV/EBITDA, Uniparts trades at ~14x vs the peer median of ~17x — again at a discount. The discount is partly justified by:
- Smaller market cap (Rs. 3,941 Cr vs peers at Rs. 6,500–57,000 Cr) — liquidity discount.
- Higher customer concentration (Top 5 = 71% vs peers at ~40–50%).
- Lower revenue base (Rs. 1,170 Cr vs peers at Rs. 2,100–16,500 Cr).

However, the discount is too wide given Uniparts' **net-cash balance sheet, 27% TTM ROCE (highest in peer set), and structural margin expansion story**. A re-rating to ~28–30x P/E (still below peer median) is justified as the cycle normalizes and large-ag recovery materializes. Our 30x target multiple on FY27E EPS captures part of this re-rating.

**Commentary on Income Statement (Layman-Friendly):**

Uniparts' top line (Revenue) tells a classic cyclical recovery story. The company went through two brutal down-years in FY24 and FY25 (revenue fell ~16% each year) as its biggest customers in the US and Europe — mostly tractor and construction equipment makers — were aggressively cutting inventory. FY26 marked the inflection: revenue rebounded 21% to Rs. 1,170 Cr as destocking ended and order books normalised. The Q1 FY27 print of 27% YoY growth confirms the recovery is broad-based and structural, not just a base effect.

The real story, however, lies in **margins**. EBITDA margin compressed from 22% (FY23) to a painful 15% in FY25 — a 700 basis-point collapse. This happened because fixed costs (factories, employees, depreciation) did not shrink in line with the revenue drop, so every rupee of lost sales hurt disproportionately. The recovery to 21% in FY26 and the surge to 25% in Q1 FY27 reflects three powerful tailwinds: (1) **operating leverage** (costs are now spread over a higher revenue base), (2) **warehouse-led channel sales** — Uniparts ships from its US warehouses rather than direct exports from India, which carries materially higher margins, and this channel has grown from ~50% to ~56% of revenue, and (3) **favourable product mix** as construction equipment and large-agriculture wins come through at better pricing.

The bottom-line (PAT) grew almost 80% in FY26 because margins expanded *and* revenue grew simultaneously — a powerful combination. Looking ahead, we model FY27E growth of 23% and FY28E growth of 22% — slightly above the management-stated "couple of percentage points better than FY26's 21%". EBITDA margin is forecast to settle at 22-22.5%, in line with management's cycle guidance of ~20% with positive bias from channel mix. PAT growth (31% FY27E, 27% FY28E) outpaces revenue growth due to this margin expansion, with EPS climbing from Rs. 35 in FY26 to Rs. 58 by FY28E.

**PROJECTION RATIONALE & ASSUMPTIONS (FY27E / FY28E):**

- **Revenue Growth Assumption:** 23% YoY for FY27E and 22% YoY for FY28E. Both are anchored to management commentary from the Q1 FY27 concall: (a) Chairman Gurdeep Soni guided that FY27 top-line will be "a couple of percentage points better than FY26's 21%", (b) H2 FY27 is expected to outperform H1 as agriculture recovery materialises, (c) large agriculture was explicitly identified as the FY26 cyclical trough with calendar year 2027 / FY28 being the recovery year. Construction equipment growth (already strong) provides a second leg, and the trailing-twelve-month new-business order book of Rs. 225 Cr provides additional revenue visibility. FY28E's 22% growth assumes the agriculture recovery is more meaningful, supplemented by Mexico warehouse ramp-up (first customer deliveries Q3 FY27 onwards).
- **EBITDA Margin Assumption:** 22% (FY27E) and 22.5% (FY28E). Justification: management has guided a cycle average EBITDA margin of ~20% with current 24-25% reflecting an unusually favourable channel mix (warehouse sales at 56% vs 50% historically). Our FY27E/FY28E numbers assume a modest normalisation — still above the cycle average but below the Q1 FY27 print of 25.3%, recognising that as volumes grow, channel mix will rebalance slightly. Operating leverage on rising volumes and gross-margin tailwinds from material cost (guided at 34-37% range) provide the upside.
- **Depreciation & Capex:** Step-up to Rs. 50 Cr / Rs. 55 Cr reflecting the management-guided long-term capex requirement of 2.5-3.5% of revenue (~Rs. 43 Cr / Rs. 53 Cr at our projected revenue base). Marginal increase reflects the Mexico warehouse fit-out and ongoing digital/ludhiana restoration capex.
- **Tax Rate:** Held at 24.0% — consistent with the 23-24% range observed across FY23-FY26 and the absence of any disclosed tax-holiday changes.
- **Dividend Payout:** Normalised to 30% for FY27E and FY28E, reflecting sustainable free-cash-flow generation. FY26's 75% payout includes a one-off special dividend paid post-balance-sheet strength and will not be repeated at the same magnitude annually.
