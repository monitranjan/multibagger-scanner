### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Balu Forge Industries Ltd (BFIL) has built a narrow but deepening moat (a moat is a business’s durable competitive advantage that protects profits from rivals, like a castle’s water-filled ditch) in precision forged and machined components across 11 end-user industries, with 180+ product approvals from 25 global OEMs (original equipment manufacturers – the big brands that assemble final products) spread over 80 countries [^ip-latest]. The company’s integrated forging-plus-machining model (forging is shaping metal using compressive force; machining is cutting metal to exact dimensions using CNC machines) creates switching costs because an OEM must re-qualify a new supplier’s metallurgy and tolerances, a process that can take 12–18 months. This approval wall is the core moat, rated Moderate because scale is still below large peers but approval depth is strong [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin story:** BFIL’s EBITDA margin expanded from ~21.3% in FY24 to 27.0% in FY26 and 28.2% in Q1 FY27, a gain of roughly 580 bps (bps = basis points, where 100 bps = 1.0 percentage point, so 580 bps = 5.8 points) over two years [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. The structural driver is a shift in revenue mix toward high-margin defence, aerospace and railway (Def/Aero/Rail) segments which rose from 5% of revenue in FY24 to 14% in Q1 FY27, and which management labels “premium pricing” [^ip-latest]. Lower raw-material intensity (raw material cost as % of revenue fell to 32.9% in Q1 FY27 from higher levels earlier) and operating leverage on new 46-acre Belagavi plant support a sustainable 27–29% EBITDA margin band [^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** The company is deliberately reducing dependence on cyclical agriculture (Agri share fell from 40% in FY25 to 34% in Q1 FY27) by entering aerospace and defence energetics [^ip-latest]. Concrete steps: maiden aerospace order from Alpha Aircraft Systems Inc., USA [^ip-latest]; 5-year binding MoU with a NATO-affiliated entity for large-calibre ammunition [^ip-latest]; subsidiary Quantum Energetics Pvt Ltd moving into TNT/RDX/HMX (these are high-energy explosive compounds used in munitions) forward integration subject to approvals [^ip-latest]; and commercialisation of 360,000 shells per annum automated empty shell line at Belgaum [^ip-latest]. These adjacencies address global defence re-stocking and China+1 supply-chain shift, offering revenue visibility beyond FY28E.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Receipt of multi-month contract for 155mm ERFB BB/BT (extended range base-bleed boat-tail – a shell design that flies farther) shells announced 17 Aug 2026, adding to order book where Def/Aero/Rail already constitutes 50% of Q1 FY27 order book [^ip-latest]; (ii) Commissioning of 25T hydraulic hammer and 8,000T press (heavy forging equipment that allows bigger components) under way, lifting forging capacity to 150,000 MTPA (metric tonnes per annum) and machining to 80,000 MTPA by FY27 [^ar-fy25][^ip-latest]; (iii) Possible NATO supply-chain induction and European/Middle East expansion [^ip-latest]; (iv) Analyst meet on 20 Aug 2026 may clarify FCCB (foreign currency convertible bond – a loan that can turn into shares) deployment and preferential pricing concerns [^vp-thread]. Each can re-rate the stock over the next 3–4 quarters.

- **Biggest structural risk:** The principal structural risk is customer and geographic concentration in export markets (export share >70% per BRSR) combined with related-party transaction concentration (RPT loans were 54% of loan book at subsidiary level) which, while explained, creates governance overhang [^ar-fy25][^vp-thread]. Additionally, a slowdown in global capital expenditure for earth-moving and heavy engineering (17% of Q1 FY27 revenue) or adverse FX (foreign exchange rate) moves could compress the 28% margin by 200–300 bps [^ar-fy25]. The defence margin is also subject to import substitution pricing pressure if global surplus artillery shells emerge post-Ukraine conflict [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

BFIL operates a single integrated reportable segment of precision forged and metal products; the core business model is to take steel/aluminium/titanium billets, heat and hammer them into near-net shapes (forging), then precisely cut them to OEM specs (machining), and supply directly to factories that build trucks, tractors, trains, ships, planes and guns [^ar-fy25]. This is a B2B (business-to-business) model where the company is paid per piece after quality sign-off, meaning revenue is tied to industrial production volumes rather than consumer sentiment.

Revenue split by end-user industry for Q1 FY27 is: Agriculture 34%, Commercial Vehicles (CV) 20%, Heavy Engineering 17%, Power 10%, Defence/Aerospace/Railway 14%, Oil & Gas 5% [^ip-latest]. For FY26 the mix was Agri 36%, CV 20%, Heavy Eng 16%, Power 10%, Def/Aero/Rail 13%, O&G 5% [^ip-latest]. The parent BFIL runs four manufacturing units: Unit 1&2 at Belgaum/Kakti (Karnataka) with machining 22,000 TPA and 15,000+ powertrain units PA; Unit 3 at Hattargi (Karnataka) with machining 58,000 TPA and forging 100,000+ MTPA; Unit 4 in UAE (1 acre footprint) [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. A greenfield 46-acre Belagavi facility is being commissioned to add 50,000 TPA forging and 22,000 TPA machining by FY27 [^ip-latest].

Key OEM/end customers per division are not individually named in the presentation due to confidentiality, but the document states 25 OEMs across 80 countries and explicitly cites Alpha Aircraft Systems Inc. (US aerospace) and an Indian energetics player for 30,000 units of 152mm artillery shells [^ip-latest]. Subsidiary structure includes Quantum Energetics Pvt Ltd (defence materials, pre-revenue) and associate JV Swan Balu Heavy Industries (incorporated Apr-2025 for defence) [^ar-fy25]. Promoter background: the Chandock family (Jaspalsingh Prehladsingh Chandock and sons Jaikaran and Trimaan) has led the firm for decades, with MD letter emphasising “Make in India” and precision diversification [^ar-fy25]. Group context is closely held, with promoter stake 64.17% per Screener data, and no pledged shares disclosed [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Indian specific TAM for defence forgings is Rs. 25,000 Cr with 12% CAGR under PLI (Production Linked Incentive – a government scheme that pays firms a percentage of incremental sales) and “Make in India” push [^ar-fy25]. Policy tailwinds: PLI for auto components, FAME (Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of Electric Vehicles – subsidy scheme for EVs) indirectly lifting e-mobility forgings, and defence capital procurement exceeding Rs. 1.5 lakh Cr annually [^ar-fy25].

Commentary: Balu Forge has delivered a sharp profitability curve — EBITDA margin jumped from 21% to 27% over two years as the company shifted from commodity forgings to precision defence/aerospace components. The FY27E/FY28E estimates extend this but at a slower top-line pace because the easy post-Covid rebound is behind us. The key margin driver is “operating leverage”: once the expensive new hammers and CNC lines (explained later) are installed, each extra rupee of sales bears less new fixed cost, lifting profits faster than revenue. Free cash flow has been negative historically due to heavy capex (writing cheques for factories before they earn), but our model shows turnaround as capex tapers. For a beginner: think of the firm building a bigger, smarter workshop; short-term pain (cash out) for long-term gain (higher margins).
