## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Niche market leadership with high-moat engineering consumables**: THEJO is the dominant domestic player in specialty conveyor belts, pulley lagging, and engineered polymer products used in bulk material handling across mining, power, cement, steel, and ports, commanding an estimated 35–40% domestic market share in its core belt segment with limited organized competition[^ip-latest]. The company has a near-duopoly/oligopoly positioning in select product categories (e.g., ceramic pulley lagging, heat-resistant belts) where it competes primarily with global majors like ContiTech and Fenner, giving it strong pricing power on specialty SKUs.

- **Structural margin expansion story driven by mix shift**: EBITDA margins have structurally expanded from ~12% in FY18 to ~18–20% in FY25, driven by (i) higher contribution from proprietary engineered products (screening media, chute liners, polymer screens) where realization is 1.8–2.0x commodity conveyor belts, (ii) a successful shift towards domestic value-addition vs. import-dominated product mix historically, and (iii) operating leverage from utilization in the new Sikkim and Orissa plants[^ar-fy25]. Management has guided to sustainable 19–21% EBITDA margins through FY28E[^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth mining and infra adjacencies**: THEJO has successfully expanded into (a) engineered screening media (growing at 18–20% CAGR), (b) wear-resistant liners and chute liners, and (c) specialized hose and sheet rubber products, which now contribute ~30% of revenue versus ~10% five years ago[^ip-latest]. The recent entry into screen media for the iron ore and coal segments, and growing traction with global OEMs in Australia, South Africa, and South America, opens a multi-decade export opportunity.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months)**: (1) Capacity ramp-up at the new Sikkim plant to drive 200–300 bps operating leverage, (2) Order book from Coal India and SCCL replacement cycle worth Rs. 150–200 Cr, (3) Possible re-rating from sustained margin expansion and incremental institutional investor interest, and (4) Continued special dividends / buybacks given strong free cash flow conversion (~70% of PAT) and net cash balance sheet[^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk**: Cyclicality of the mining and bulk material handling capex cycle, with ~55–60% revenue exposed to coal, iron ore, and cement end-markets, which are sensitive to commodity prices and government capex allocations[^ar-fy25]. A sharp slowdown in mining capex (e.g., a coal pricing downturn) could pressure volumes by 8–12% YoY. Additionally, the inability to scale the export business beyond 20–25% of revenue remains a key monitorable.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

THEJO Engineering Ltd, founded in 1986 and headquartered in Chennai, is a specialty engineered products company focused on bulk material handling solutions across mining, power, cement, steel, ports, and aggregate industries[^ar-fy25]. The company operates through a vertically integrated model that includes design, compounding, manufacturing, and on-site service of conveyor belts, pulley lagging systems, screening media, and engineered polymer products[^ip-latest].

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25, estimated):**
- **Conveyor Belts & Belt Accessories (~55–60%)**: Heat-resistant belts, fire-resistant belts, steel cord belts, and textile belts; the core legacy business
- **Screening Media & Engineered Products (~20–25%)**: Polyurethane screens, rubber screens, trommel screens — the highest-growth and highest-margin segment
- **Pulley Lagging & Wear Protection (~10–12%)**: Ceramic, rubber, and polyurethane pulley lagging; chute liners; wear liners
- **Hoses, Sheets & Other Rubber Products (~5–8%)**: Industrial hoses, rubber sheets, custom-molded products
- **Service & Site Work (~3–5%)**: Installation, jointing, splicing, and condition monitoring services

**Key End Customers by Division:**
- **Mining/Coal**: Coal India Ltd (CIL and subsidiaries), Singareni Collieries, NMDC, Vedanta, Adani Enterprises, Hindustan Zinc
- **Power**: NTPC, Tata Power, Adani Power, Reliance Power
- **Cement & Steel**: Ultratech, ACC, Ambuja, JSW Steel, Tata Steel, SAIL
- **Ports & Bulk Handling**: Adani Ports, JSW Infrastructure, various private berths
- **International**: BHP, Rio Tinto (indirectly via tier-1 OEMs), Glencore suppliers, South African mining majors[^cc-transcript]

**Subsidiary Structure:**
- **Thejo Hatcon International FZE** (UAE) – Middle East distribution
- **Thejo Brasil Servicos De Instalacao Ltda** (Brazil) – Latin American operations
- **Thejo Australia Pty Ltd** – Australian market presence
- **JV / Associates**: Strategic partnerships with global technology providers for specialized product categories[^ar-fy25]

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
- **India**: 6 plants across Tamil Nadu (Chennai – corporate HQ and main plant), Karnataka, Sikkim (new), Orissa (new), and one in Maharashtra; total installed capacity utilization at ~75–80% in FY25
- **International**: Small assembly/service centers in UAE and Brazil
- **Employee count**: ~1,500–1,800 (FY25)

**Promoter & Group Context:**
- Originally promoted by the Jose family (V.A. Jose and family), with the second-generation professional management now leading operations
- The company does not have a formal "promoter" classification under SEBI's promoter framework as shareholding is widely distributed, with promoter/family holding reported at 0% under the formal promoter definition — a structural feature worth flagging from a governance perspective[^ar-fy25]
- Chairman: V.A. Jose (founder); MD & CEO: Manoj Joseph (professional management with 25+ years tenure)
- Group has no other significant listed entity; THEJO is the flagship
