### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

*   **Market Leadership & Moat in Apparel Manufacturing:** Gokaldas Exports (GOKEX) is India's largest apparel manufacturer and exporter, with a strong focus on woven and knit garments for global fashion brands. Its moat stems from deep relationships with marquee international clients (e.g., GAP, H&M, Levi's, Columbia Sportswear), diversified product portfolio across categories (outerwear, activewear, fashionwear), and significant scale (over 20 manufacturing units). This allows for efficient large-volume production, adherence to stringent quality standards, and compliance with complex buyer requirements, creating high switching costs for customers.
*   **Structural Margin Improvement Story:** The company is undergoing a structural shift towards higher-value-added products, increased operational efficiencies through automation, and strategic backward integration (e.g., fabric processing). This, coupled with favorable government policies like the PLI scheme for Textiles, is expected to drive EBITDA margin expansion by 150-200 bps over the next 3-5 years from current levels, moving towards a sustainable 10-11% range. The recent acquisition of Atraco Group further enhances its capabilities and margin profile in complex, higher-margin categories.
*   **Strategic Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** GOKEX is strategically expanding its geographical footprint and product offerings. The acquisition of Atraco Group in FY24 provides a significant entry into the high-growth knitwear segment and strengthens its presence in the US and European markets, reducing reliance on a few key geographies. Furthermore, the company is investing in technical textiles and performance wear, catering to the increasing demand for functional apparel, which typically commands higher margins.
*   **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** Key catalysts include: (1) Robust order book visibility post the Atraco acquisition, expected to reflect in strong revenue growth in H2 FY25 and FY26. (2) Continued benefits from the China+1 strategy adopted by global brands, driving increased sourcing from India. (3) Stabilisation of cotton prices and freight costs, which will support margin recovery. (4) Operationalisation of new capacities and efficiency initiatives contributing to improved asset turnover and profitability.
*   **Biggest Structural Risk:** The primary structural risk is the high dependence on global macroeconomic conditions and consumer discretionary spending. A significant slowdown in key export markets (US, Europe) could lead to reduced order volumes, pricing pressure from clients, and inventory build-up. Additionally, intense competition from other low-cost manufacturing hubs (e.g., Vietnam, Bangladesh) and potential trade policy changes or tariffs pose ongoing competitive threats.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

The business model revolves around receiving orders from international clients, sourcing raw materials (primarily fabrics), manufacturing garments in its own facilities, and exporting finished products. The company operates on an OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) model, producing garments based on client designs and specifications.
