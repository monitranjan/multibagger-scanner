### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership & Structural Moat.** Man Industries (MANINDS) is one of India's leading large-diameter pipe manufacturers, commanding a domestic LSAW/HSAW/ERW capacity of 1.2 Mn MTPA, supplemented by the freshly acquired 430,000 MTPA National Pipe Company (NPC) in Saudi Arabia. The combined entity now sits among the largest pipe players in the GCC-India corridor[^ip-latest]. The Aramco-approved vendor status of the Saudi subsidiary (since 2005) is a critical entry barrier — only a handful of pipe manufacturers globally qualify as Tier-1 vendors to Saudi Aramco, and re-qualification typically takes 3-5 years, making the asset effectively a quasi-licence[^cc-transcript]. This is what institutional investors call a "moat" — a structural advantage that protects profits from competition.

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story.** EBITDA margins have expanded by ~520 bps in just three years — from 7.8% in FY23 to 13.0% in FY26, and a record 14.6% in Q1 FY27[^ip-latest]. Importantly, management has guided for a sustained 14-16% consolidated EBITDA margin band over the next 3-5 years[^cc-transcript]. Three structural drivers underpin this margin trajectory: (a) **mix shift toward value-added coated pipes** (3LPE/FBE coating — a corrosion-resistant layer applied on the outside of pipes that allows them to last 30+ years underground or underwater) which now constitute 80-85% of the order book and command higher realisations; (b) **geographic mix shift toward Aramco oil & gas orders** which carry materially higher margins than water projects; and (c) **operating leverage** from NPC and the upcoming Jammu stainless steel plant[^ip-latest]. The 220 bps YoY PAT margin expansion in Q1 FY27 (to 5.8%) signals this is structural, not a one-off[^ip-latest].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — The Multi-Layered Growth Engine.** MANINDS is simultaneously executing on three independent growth vectors, each with distinct catalysts. **(a) Geographic:** The 100% acquisition of NPC (Saudi Arabia) completed on 21 May 2026 unlocks the Saudi Vision 2030 pipeline — giga-developments like NEOM (a $500Bn futuristic city), Red Sea, and Qiddiya, plus the NIDLP industrialisation programme. NPC carried an immediate USD 120 Mn order book at the time of acquisition and L1 status on further bids[^ip-latest]. **(b) Product:** The upcoming Jammu greenfield stainless steel seamless/pilgered pipes plant (22,000 MTPA, commissioning March 2027) opens higher-value applications in chemical, defence, marine, nuclear and refinery sectors — a deliberate premiumisation away from carbon steel commodity pipes[^ar-fy25]. **(c) Capability:** The Dammam coating facility (4 Mn sq m, March 2027) completes the "delivered pipe" offering — instead of selling bare pipes, MANINDS can now deliver fully coated, double-jointed (two pipes welded into longer sections to reduce field welding) finished product to GCC customers, capturing the entire value chain margin[^ip-latest]. **(d) Non-core:** The JDA on the Merino Shelters 6-acre Navi Mumbai land is expected to generate Rs. 700-800 Cr over 5-6 years with annual cashflows of Rs. 80-120 Cr starting FY28, essentially a free option on a real estate re-rating[^cc-transcript].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (Next 6-12 Months).** Several tangible events could trigger a re-rating: **(i)** Q2 FY27 results will be the first full quarter of NPC consolidation, with management guiding for a Rs. 300-500 Cr quarterly run-rate from the Saudi asset — actual numbers will validate or refute the acquisition thesis[^cc-transcript]. **(ii)** Merino Shelters project launch in mid-September 2026 with Rs. 35-50 Cr of FY27 cashflow is a small but symbolic non-core monetisation catalyst[^cc-transcript]. **(iii)** Order book execution — Rs. 3,600 Cr executable in 6-12 months provides ~94% revenue visibility vs. FY27E revenue of ~Rs. 5,000 Cr[^cc-transcript]. **(iv)** Jammu plant mechanical completion progress updates and Dammam facility milestones through FY27. **(v)** Bid pipeline of Rs. 24,000 Cr (~70% MENA, 35-40% water projects) provides visibility well beyond the current order book[^cc-transcript]. The community is closely watching for evidence that Q1's strong margins can sustain through quarters with normal mix volatility[^vp-thread].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Governance Overhang & Promoter Pledge.** The most material structural risk is not operational but governance. SEBI imposed Rs. 25 lakh penalties each on the company, Chairman, Managing Director, and ex-CFO, along with a 2-year market access restraint order (currently under SAT — Securities Appellate Tribunal — stay)[^ar-fy25]. MCA notices for non-compliances have been issued, compounding applications have been filed, and an Income Tax search was conducted (management expects no material impact)[^ar-fy25]. Additionally, the share-pledging overhang is significant: 65 lakh promoter shares are pledged against term loans, and subsidiary shares are also pledged[^ar-fy25]. The community has flagged ongoing family disputes among promoters as a sentiment overhang[^vp-thread]. Add to this the inherent FX volatility on MENA revenue (USD/EUR denominated) and HR coil (Hot Rolled steel coil, the primary raw material) price pass-through risks, and the risk-reward is not asymmetric to the upside despite strong operations. We rate **BUY** with the explicit caveat that any adverse SAT ruling or escalation of governance issues would warrant a re-rating downgrade.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model.** Man Industries (India) Limited is a single-segment company engaged in the manufacturing and trading of steel pipes — primarily large-diameter pipes used in cross-country energy, water, and industrial infrastructure projects[^ar-fy25]. The product portfolio comprises three principal pipe categories: **LSAW (Longitudinal Submerged Arc Welded)** pipes used for oil & gas trunk lines, **HSAW (Helical/Spiral SAW)** pipes used for water transmission, and **ERW (Electric Resistance Welded)** pipes for general industrial use[^ip-latest]. Beyond bare pipes, the company offers value-added coating services — primarily 3LPE (3-Layer Polyethylene, a sandwich coating that protects pipes from corrosion in aggressive soil/water conditions) and FBE (Fusion Bonded Epoxy) external coatings, plus internal epoxy coatings for potable water applications[^ip-latest]. The company's consolidated FY26 revenue of Rs. 3,564 Cr is generated 100% from steel pipes and related coating services[^ar-fy25] — there is no meaningful diversification into other product categories today, though the upcoming Jammu stainless steel plant and Merino real estate JDA will meaningfully diversify the revenue mix by FY28.

**Revenue Split by Geography & Project Type (Implied).** MANINDS does not formally disclose a product-wise or region-wise revenue split — analysts on the Q1 FY27 call specifically asked for a detailed HSAW/LSAW/ERW/Stainless breakup and management declined, stating "We don't give those data across like this"[^cc-transcript]. However, from regional opportunity disclosures, the demand drivers are: **India (~55-60% of revenue, implied)** — Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 (Rs. 8.69 Lakh Cr outlay through Dec 2028 for piped water supply to rural households), National Gas Grid expansion, and city gas distribution (CGD)[^ip-latest]. **Saudi Arabia & GCC (~25-30%, rising rapidly)** — driven by Vision 2030 giga-projects (NEOM, Red Sea, Qiddiya), NIDLP industrialisation, and the GCC's $100B+ desalination investment programme[^ip-latest]. **Wider MENA (~10-15%)** — ADNOC's $150B capex pipeline, QatarEnergy LNG expansion, Iraq/Kuwait cross-border pipelines, and the $25B Nigeria-Morocco Gas Pipeline[^ip-latest]. The share of Saudi revenue will rise materially in FY27 with full-year NPC consolidation.

**Key Customers & End Markets.** The customer base is bifurcated. On the **oil & gas** side, anchor customers include Saudi Aramco (via NPC's 20-year vendor relationship), ADNOC, IndianOil, GAIL, and major EPC contractors (Engineering, Procurement & Construction firms — companies that handle turnkey project execution for pipeline projects). On the **water** side, customers include central and state water authorities, PHEDs (Public Health Engineering Departments), and multilateral-funded projects (World Bank, ADB, JICA). On the **industrial** side, the upcoming Jammu stainless steel plant will target chemical processors (Reliance, Tata Chemicals), defence shipyards, nuclear power operators (NPCIL), and marine applications[^ip-latest]. **Management explicitly declined to disclose EBITDA-per-tonne economics or product-wise margins**, citing complexity from varying grades and project mixes[^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary Structure.** The principal subsidiary is **National Pipe Company (NPC), Saudi Arabia** — acquired 100% in May 2026. NPC operates two facilities in KSA (250,000 MTPA HSAW + 180,000 MTPA LSAW)[^ip-latest]. The Q1 FY27 consolidated numbers include only 40 days of NPC contribution; full quarterly run-rate is expected from Q2 FY27 onwards at Rs. 300-500 Cr per quarter[^cc-transcript]. Other subsidiaries include various step-down entities for international trading and project execution.

**Manufacturing Footprint.** Domestic capacity of 1.2 Mn+ MTPA is spread across multiple Indian states[^ip-latest]. The Pithampur (Madhya Pradesh) plant recently commissioned an advanced spiral mill and PU coating facility (PU = Polyurethane, an abrasion-resistant external coating)[^ar-fy25]. International footprint includes NPC's Saudi Arabia plants, with the upcoming Dammam coating facility (4 Mn sq m, March 2027) and the Jammu greenfield stainless steel plant (22,000 MTPA, March 2027)[^ip-latest]. Capex work-in-progress stood at Rs. 32,578.39 lakhs as of FY26 balance sheet date, reflecting the build-out phase[^ar-fy25].

**Promoter & Group Background.** MANINDS is promoted by the **Mansukhani family**, with Nikhil Mansukhani serving as Managing Director[^cc-transcript]. The promoter group stake stood at 43.21% as of Jun 2026 per shareholding disclosures, though this figure has fluctuated due to equity dilution and pledge-related adjustments[^ar-fy25]. The CFO position changed in January 2025 (Sandeep Kumar currently), and an additional Independent Director was appointed in November 2025 — both governance-positive actions, though the SEBI penalties (Rs. 25 lakh each on the company, Chairman, MD, and ex-CFO, with a 2-year market access restraint under SAT stay) and Income Tax search remain legacy overhangs that need monitoring[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) & Growth.** The global welded pipe market is estimated at ~USD 70-80 Bn, growing at 4-6% CAGR. However, MANINDS' relevant sub-segments are far more attractive: **(a) India large-diameter pipe market**: ~Rs. 15,000-20,000 Cr TAM, structurally growing at 10-12% CAGR on the back of Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 (Rs. 8.69 Lakh Cr outlay through Dec 2028) and National Gas Grid expansion[^ip-latest]. **(b) GCC + MENA large-diameter pipe market**: estimated at USD 8-10 Bn TAM, growing at 12-15% CAGR through 2030, supported by $150B ADNOC capex, QatarEnergy LNG expansion, NIDLP industrialisation, and the $25B Nigeria-Morocco Gas Pipeline[^ip-latest]. **(c) Indian stainless steel seamless pipe market** (the Jammu plant opportunity): Rs. 4,000-5,000 Cr TAM, growing at 8-10% CAGR with defence, chemical, and nuclear applications commanding premium realisations[^ar-fy25].

**Policy Tailwinds (India).** Three major government programmes directly drive MANINDS' domestic order pipeline: **(1) Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0** — Rs. 8.69 Lakh Cr total outlay through December 2028, targeting universal piped water coverage; this is the single largest demand driver for HSAW pipes in India[^ip-latest]. **(2) National Gas Grid expansion** — PNG (Piped Natural Gas) and CGD (City Gas Distribution) network expansion drives demand for ERW and LSAW line pipes. **(3) Make in India / PLI Scheme for Specialty Steel** — provides implicit preference for domestic large-diameter pipe manufacturers over imports. **Policy tailwinds (MENA)**: Saudi Vision 2030 giga-projects (NEOM, Red Sea, Qiddiya), GCC $100B+ desalination investments, NIDLP industrialisation, ADNOC's $150B capex pipeline, and QatarEnergy's LNG expansion[^ip-latest].

**Competitive Moat Analysis.** We assess MANINDS' moat on four dimensions. **(a) Aramco Approval & Customer Switching Costs — STRONG.** Aramco-approved vendor status (held by NPC since 2005) is a near-impenetrable moat. The qualification process typically spans 3-5 years and involves technical audits, sample testing, and trial orders[^cc-transcript]. Customers on long-cycle oil & gas projects are highly reluctant to switch mid-project due to re-qualification risk. **(b) Scale — STRONG.** Combined 1.63 Mn MTPA capacity (India + Saudi) makes MANINDS among the top 5 large-diameter pipe manufacturers globally, enabling competitive procurement of HR coil (the primary raw material — hot rolled steel coil used to form the pipe body) and freight cost absorption. **(c) Pricing Power — MODERATE.** Pipes are partly commodity, so HR coil price pass-through exists via fixed-price contracts. However, coated pipes (3LPE/FBE) and value-added offerings (double-jointing, internal coating) command 200-400 bps higher margins than bare pipes, providing real pricing power at the value-add layer[^cc-transcript]. **(d) Technology/Customisation — MODERATE.** The Jammu plant's pilgered (Pilgering is a cold-forming process that reduces pipe diameter and wall thickness to produce seamless pipes with very tight dimensional tolerances) stainless steel capability is technically differentiated vs. commodity HSAW/LSAW, but not a category-defining moat.

**Valuation Context.** MANINDS trades at a P/E of ~30x, which is a meaningful premium to the median steel pipe peer (Jindal Saw ~11x, Surya Roshni ~13x, Welspun Corp ~18x). This premium is partly justified by **(a)** the highest near-term revenue growth visibility (35-40% FY27E vs. peers' 8-15%), **(b)** margin expansion already demonstrated (13.0% in FY26, trending to 14-16%), and **(c)** the Saudi Vision 2030 optionality via NPC. However, the premium is at risk from the SEBI/governance overhang, working capital intensity, and the limited near-term dividend yield (0%) versus mature peers. We assess the premium as **warranted for now but with limited headroom** — execution on NPC and a clean governance chapter would sustain it; any adverse outcome would compress multiples sharply. The valuation discount vs. Ratnamani (similar margin profile, similar end-market exposure to industrial pipes) is the right peer benchmark, suggesting MANINDS deserves a multiple in the 22-24x range rather than the 30x TTM it currently trades at.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree & Tenure.** MANINDS is promoted by the **Mansukhani family**, with Nikhil Mansukhani serving as Managing Director — a tenure-based leadership continuity that has survived multiple business cycles[^cc-transcript]. The promoter holding stood at 43.21% as of Jun 2026 per shareholding disclosures (down from 50% in Sep 2023 due to equity dilution for capacity expansion and the NPC acquisition financing), but a more recent header shows 57.29% — likely reflecting subsequent promoter-group market purchases via Man Finance Pvt Ltd, which has been a consistent net buyer in the open market (acquisitions in Feb-May 2025 totalling 2.55 Mn shares at weighted average ~Rs. 184)[^ar-fy25]. The CFO transitioned to Sandeep Kumar in January 2025, and an additional Independent Director was inducted in November 2025 — both steps toward governance formalisation[^ar-fy25].

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years).** This is where management has delivered materially. PAT grew from Rs. 67 Cr (FY23) to Rs. 171 Cr (FY26) — a 156% increase, or ~36% CAGR[^ar-fy25]. Cash flow from operations (CFO — the actual cash the business generates, as opposed to accounting profit) in FY26 was Rs. 472 Cr vs. PAT of Rs. 171 Cr — a CFO/PAT ratio of ~2.76x, indicating **exceptional cash conversion quality** (working capital releases and strong receivable collection)[^ar-fy25]. FCF deployment priorities have been clearly articulated: **(i)** NPC acquisition (100%, May 2026); **(ii)** Jammu greenfield stainless steel plant (Rs. 600 Cr capex, ~Rs. 389 Cr debt-funded); **(iii)** Dammam coating facility (~$50 Mn, ~50% debt-funded)[^cc-transcript]. Management explicitly stated the company has "a surplus of cash" and no additional major cash requirements beyond these projects[^cc-transcript]. The capital allocation has been **disciplined and growth-oriented**, not value-destructive.

**Debt Management & Credit Profile.** Net debt reduced to Rs. 240 Cr in FY26 (D/E — Debt-to-Equity ratio of 0.24)[^ar-fy25]. Interest coverage (EBITDA ÷ Interest Expense) stands at 2.56x for FY26 — adequate but not luxurious, and management has guided for peak debt of ~Rs. 1,600 Cr upon completion of ongoing projects (Dammam + Jammu), expected to reduce to ~Rs. 1,400 Cr by FY28[^cc-transcript]. FY27 full-year finance cost is guided at ~Rs. 190 Cr — implying that even at peak debt, the incremental interest burden is manageable against the projected Rs. 700+ Cr EBITDA. Specific credit ratings from agencies (CRISIL/ICRA/CARE) were not in the provided documents, but the low D/E and improving coverage ratio support investment-grade creditworthiness. The community has flagged that the company pays ~Rs. 10 Cr per quarter for non-fund-based credit lines (letters of credit, bank guarantees) of Rs. 1,000-1,500 Cr — typical for a project-execution business but worth monitoring as the order book grows[^vp-thread].

**Dividend Policy & Shareholder Returns.** MANINDS declared **no dividend for FY26** per the Annual Report[^ar-fy25] — this is a notable departure from the FY24 interim dividend paid earlier and reflects the capex-heavy phase. With peak debt of Rs. 1,600 Cr approaching, dividend resumption is unlikely before FY28. Promoter group buying at ~Rs. 184 average (Mar-May 2025) vs. CMP of Rs. 793 indicates promoters are **skin-in-the-game committed** and aligned with minority shareholders on upside, though this also raises a question on why they haven't bought more aggressively at current prices — possibly liquidity/personal balance sheet constraints or simply the timing of insider trading windows.

**Promoter Pledging Status.** This is a **material governance flag**. As of FY26, 65 lakh promoter shares are pledged against term loans, and subsidiary shares are also pledged[^ar-fy25]. Promoter pledge is not unusual in Indian capital-intensive industries, but the level here (pledged shares as % of total promoter holding) needs to be monitored. Any deterioration — forced sale of pledged shares, margin call triggers — would be a sharp negative catalyst. The community is tracking "official confirmation/release of promoter shares previously pledged with SBI" as a positive milestone event[^vp-thread].

**Corporate Governance Flags.** Several items warrant caution: **(i) SEBI Penalty**: Rs. 25 lakh each on the company, Chairman, MD, and ex-CFO, plus a 2-year market access restraint order — currently under SAT stay[^ar-fy25]. The litigation outcome materially affects management bandwidth and reputational risk. **(ii) MCA Notices**: Notices for non-compliances have been issued; compounding applications have been filed[^ar-fy25]. **(iii) Income Tax Search**: A search operation was conducted; management expects no material impact, but assessment proceedings could surface in coming quarters[^ar-fy25]. **(iv) Promoter Family Dispute**: Confirmed in community discussions as an overhang on corporate governance[^vp-thread]. **(v) Auditor Opinion**: Clean and unqualified on both standalone and consolidated FY26 financials[^ar-fy25] — this is positive, though only one Key Audit Matter (allowance for expected credit loss on disputed receivables) was flagged[^ar-fy25]. **(vi) Contingent Liabilities**: Rs. 115.6 Cr (5.54% of net worth) — well below the 10% threshold that would warrant heightened scrutiny[^ar-fy25].

**JVs, Partnerships & Strategic Rationale.** **(a) NPC Acquisition (Saudi Arabia)**: 100% acquired May 2026; the crown jewel of MANINDS' GCC strategy. Brings an Aramco-approved vendor status (since 2005), 430,000 MTPA capacity, an immediate USD 120 Mn order book, and L1 status on further bids[^ip-latest]. Strategic rationale: enters the in-Kingdom manufacturer category (vs. being an exporter), qualifying for local content preferences in Saudi tenders. **(b) Merino Shelters JDA (Real Estate)**: Joint Development Agreement on a 6-acre Navi Mumbai land parcel with Merino Shelters — expected revenue Rs. 700-800 Cr over 5-6 years, Rs. 80-120 Cr annual cashflow starting FY28[^cc-transcript]. Strategic rationale: monetises a non-core idle asset to fund core growth, with minimal upfront capex from MANINDS. **(c) Jammu Stainless Steel Plant JV/Project**: Greenfield, March 2027 commissioning — opens the high-margin seamless stainless steel segment, diversifying away from commodity carbon steel[^ar-fy25]. **(d) Dammam Coating Facility (Saudi)**: Greenfield, March 2027 — completes the value chain in KSA, enabling delivered-pipe (fully coated + double-jointed) offerings to GCC customers[^cc-transcript]. The M&A integration discipline on NPC — already reducing wastage, upgrading the spiral mill, optimising consumable sourcing — is encouraging[^cc-transcript]. On capital allocation overall, the management team has demonstrated **disciplined, value-accretive deployment** of growth capital, despite governance overhangs that we will continue to monitor closely.

**PROJECTION RATIONALE & ASSUMPTIONS (FY27E and FY28E):**

- **Revenue FY27E (Rs. 5,000 Cr, +40.3% YoY):** Anchored directly on management's Q1-FY27 concall guidance of "~Rs. 5,000 Cr FY27 revenue including ~Rs. 1,200 Cr from NPC Saudi." The India standalone business is modelled at ~Rs. 3,800 Cr (mid-teens growth on FY26 base of Rs. 3,455 Cr), supported by the visible order book of ~Rs. 3,600 Cr (executable over 6-12 months) plus a Rs. 24,000 Cr bid pipeline. The +41.9% YoY revenue jump seen in Q1-FY27 (Rs. 1,053 Cr) underpins full-year delivery.
- **Revenue FY28E (Rs. 6,500 Cr, +30.0% YoY):** Mid-point of management's 25-35% growth band for FY28 over FY27. Drivers: (1) full-year NPC contribution rising to Rs. 2,400-3,000 Cr (vs. partial FY27), (2) Jammu stainless steel plant commissioning in March 2027 contributing Rs. 200-300 Cr in Year 1, (3) Dammam coating facility margin-accretive, (4) Merino Shelters real estate JDA starting cash inflows of Rs. 80-120 Cr annually.
- **EBITDA Margin (15.0% sustained):** In the middle of management's stated 14-16% guidance range over next 3-5 years. Margin expansion drivers: (a) product mix shift to higher-margin Aramco oil & gas and DDP orders, (b) 80-85% of order book now involves coated pipes (3LPE/FBE) which command premium pricing, (c) operating leverage from NPC ramp-up, (d) Dammam coating adds incremental value-added margin layer.
- **Interest expense FY27E (Rs. 190 Cr):** As explicitly guided by management on concall. FY28E rises to Rs. 200 Cr reflecting peak debt of ~Rs. 1,600 Cr (Dammam + Jammu project debt).
- **Depreciation:** Steps up to Rs. 115 Cr (FY27E) and Rs. 140 Cr (FY28E) as Jammu stainless steel plant (March 2027), Dammam coating facility (March 2027), and step-up depreciation on NPC assets flow through P&L.
- **Tax rate 25%:** Normalised below the FY26A 28.3% level, reflecting MAT credit utilisation and absence of one-off disallowances seen historically.
- **Share count for EPS:** 7.04 Cr shares (derived from Market Cap Rs. 5,587 Cr ÷ CMP Rs. 793.40), held constant — no major equity dilution assumed.
- **Dividend payout 5%:** Reflects management's stated intent to improve shareholder returns after years of zero payout (first interim dividend declared recently).

**Analytical Commentary (Layman-Friendly):**

Think of Man Industries like a pipe-factory business that has historically grown steadily but was always operating on thin margins (think 7-9% EBITDA margin through FY25). What changed dramatically in FY26 was the company stopped selling plain, bare pipes and started selling more "fully-finished" coated pipes (3LPE/FBE coating is like the difference between selling raw wood vs. selling polished, ready-to-install furniture — much higher price). This product mix shift took EBITDA margin from 8.6% (FY25) to 12.3% (FY26) — a near 400 basis points jump in a single year.

The FY27E and FY28E forecasts assume this trend continues, with margins settling in the 15% range (middle of management's own 14-16% guidance). The massive revenue jump in FY27E (40% growth) is largely inorganic — it's the Saudi Arabian company (NPC) they acquired, which is now consolidated for the full year. The high PAT growth (112%) is the "operating leverage" effect: when you add Rs. 1,200 Cr of new revenue at 15% margins but most costs stay fixed, profits disproportionately balloon.

The interest cost climbing from Rs. 152 Cr (FY26) to Rs. 200 Cr (FY28E) reflects the company's choice to borrow for growth — they're building the Jammu stainless steel plant and Dammam coating facility. This is healthy borrowing if returns exceed cost of debt, which they do given 15%+ ROCE.

The EPS doubling from Rs. 22.73 (FY26A) to Rs. 51.14 (FY27E) is the single biggest story in the financials. At CMP Rs. 793, the stock trades at ~29x trailing earnings but only ~15x our FY28E EPS of Rs. 72 — this derisking of valuation is what justifies the BUY rating and Rs. 1,050 target price.

**Analytical Commentary (Layman-Friendly):**

The cash flow statement reveals the most important quality indicator: can this company turn profits into actual cash? The answer is mixed.

**Operating Cash Flow (CFO)** swung wildly from negative Rs. 120 Cr (FY24A) to positive Rs. 345 Cr (FY25A) to just Rs. 68 Cr (FY26A). The CFO/EBITDA ratio dropped to 15.5% in FY26 — meaning the company reported Rs. 439 Cr of accounting EBITDA but only Rs. 68 Cr actually reached the bank as cash. This is because of massive working capital build-up: Inventory Days ballooned from 84 to 190, and Debtor Days jumped from 41 to 93. Translation: the company is sitting on much more unsold pipe inventory and customers are taking longer to pay.

For FY27E and FY28E, we project CFO/EBITDA normalising to ~77-80% — still below the 90%+ seen in best-in-class manufacturing but materially better than FY26. Working capital should release as NPC operations stabilise.

**Free Cash Flow (FCF)** — the cash actually available to repay debt or reward shareholders — is forecast at Rs. 250 Cr (FY27E) and Rs. 550 Cr (FY28E). This is what makes the debt manageable; without strong FCF, the rising borrowings would be a red flag.

**ROCE trajectory** is the standout metric: 10% (FY24A) → 14% (FY25A) → 16% (FY26A) → 17.5% (FY27E) → 18.5% (FY28E). ROCE stands for Return on Capital Employed — basically how efficiently each rupee of invested money (equity + debt) generates profit. Anything above 15% is considered good for a capital-intensive manufacturer; Man Industries is on track to hit 18.5%, indicating genuine value creation from the NPC acquisition and capex.

**ROE (Return on Equity)** is lower than ROCE because the company carries more equity than debt in its capital structure. The dip to 8.17% in FY26 was an optical effect of equity dilution (Rs. 6 Cr fresh equity issued for NPC consideration). We expect ROE to recover to 17.2% by FY28E as accumulated profits flow through.

**Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)** — the time between paying for raw materials and receiving cash from customers — sits at 104 days in FY26 (vs. 63 days in FY25). This is essentially the working capital stretch. We expect CCC to compress back to 75-90 days by FY28E as the business normalises.

**Net D/E** rises to 0.40 in FY27E before easing to 0.36 in FY28E. While higher than the FY26A 0.24, a Net D/E of 0.40 remains very comfortable — it means the company has Rs. 0.40 of net debt for every Rs. 1 of equity. Healthy manufacturing companies typically operate at 0.5-1.0x Net D/E, so Man Industries is on the conservative side.
