### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Niche Domain Leadership with 37-Year Track Record:** TFCI is India's only listed specialized NBFC-ND-ML focused exclusively on tourism and hospitality financing, with a 37-year operating history since incorporation.[^ip-latest] The company commands an estimated 60–70% market share in organized tourism project finance in India, a niche that mainstream banks and NBFCs have historically avoided due to asset-cycle volatility and project complexity.[^ip-latest] This domain specialization creates a structural moat — TFCI has built proprietary underwriting expertise in hotel IRR modeling, hospitality cash-flow assessment, and tourism project feasibility that competitors cannot easily replicate.[^ip-latest] The company's advisory capabilities span tourism project feasibility reports, marketing, structuring/restructuring, and special situation financing, positioning it as a one-stop solution provider rather than a pure lender.[^ip-latest]

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story:** Net Interest Margin (NIM) expanded sharply from 5.07% in FY25 to 6.43% in FY26 — a 136 bps improvement that reflects the company's pivot toward higher-yielding non-hospitality segments (real estate, manufacturing, infrastructure) and improved cost of funds following the AA- rating upgrade.[^ar-fy25] Return on Average Equity (RoAE) improved to 9.83% in FY26 from 9.10% in FY25, with RoAA expanding to 5.47% from 4.93%.[^ar-fy25] Q1FY27 NII of Rs. 45.96 Cr (up 34% YoY from Rs. 34.30 Cr) and PAT of Rs. 72.12 Cr (up 29% YoY) demonstrate that margin expansion is sustaining into FY27.[^ip-latest] The structural drivers — shift toward LRD-backed real estate, higher-yielding manufacturing credit, and lower credit costs post-NPA cleanup — are durable rather than cyclical.[^ar-fy25]

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** TFCI has systematically reduced hospitality concentration from ~70% historically to 47% of the loan portfolio as of Q1FY27, with the balance deployed across Real Estate (24%), Manufacturing (12%), Infrastructure/Social Infra (7%), NBFC (6%), and ARC & Other Financial (4%).[^ip-latest] The company has committed Rs. 90 Cr across three Category-II AIFs — Holystone TFCI Hospitality Fund (Rs. 25 Cr), Certus TFCI Real Estate Fund (Rs. 50 Cr), and Oxyzo Credit Fund (Rs. 15 Cr) — creating fee-based income streams and co-investment optionality.[^ar-fy25] Lease Rental Discounting (LRD) in commercial office space and affordable/middle-income housing financing represent high-growth adjacencies aligned with India's urbanization and Grade-A office absorption trends.[^ip-latest]

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** (a) **Rating upgrade momentum** — Brickwork upgraded TFCI to AA-/Stable in July 2026 from A+, which should lower incremental cost of funds by 25–40 bps and expand lending headroom.[^ip-latest] (b) **Asset quality re-rating** — GNPA collapsed to 0.41% in Q1FY27 from 3.22% in FY26, with Net NPA at Nil; this should drive P/B re-rating from current 4.64x toward 5.5–6.0x over 12 months.[^ip-latest] (c) **IFCI stake-sale overhang removal** — the 26.09% IFCI divestment process (with Thomas Cook reportedly in the race at ~Rs. 250 Cr valuation) remains a binary catalyst; a credible strategic buyer would validate the tourism-finance thesis.[^vp-thread] (d) **CRAR deployment** — current CRAR of 55.53% (3.7x regulatory minimum) provides substantial headroom to grow the book 40–50% over 18 months without fresh equity.[^ar-fy25]

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Concentration:** Top 20 borrowers account for 69.17% of advances, and top 10 borrowings constitute 77.52% of total borrowings — both metrics represent material concentration risk.[^ar-fy25] A single large hospitality account turning stressed could materially impact GNPA, given the Rs. 2,002 Cr book size. Additionally, geographic concentration in Maharashtra (Rs. 599.87 Cr) and Uttar Pradesh (Rs. 477.07 Cr) — together ~54% of AUM — exposes the portfolio to regional economic shocks and state-level policy changes.[^ip-latest] The ECL overlay of Rs. 33.68 Cr (93% of total ECL provisions of Rs. 36.16 Cr) is judgment-heavy and suggests model-based provisioning may be insufficient if a tail event materializes.[^ar-fy25]

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Tourism Finance Corporation of India (TFCI) operates as a non-deposit-taking, systemically important non-banking financial company (NBFC-ND-ML) registered with the Reserve Bank of India.[^ip-latest] The company's primary business is providing term loans, project finance, and structured credit solutions to the tourism/hospitality sector and select non-hospitality sectors including real estate, manufacturing, infrastructure, and financial services.[^ip-latest] TFCI also earns fee income from tourism project advisory, debt syndication, and special situation financing, positioning it as a domain specialist rather than a generic lender.[^ip-latest] The company is promoted by public-sector institutions — primarily Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) and Oriental Insurance Company — which together hold 3.85% as of June 2026.[^ip-latest]

**Key OEM/End Customers per Division:** In hospitality, TFCI's client base spans mid-market and upscale hotel chains, resort developers, spa/health resort operators, and approved lodging/restaurant chains across India.[^ip-latest] The real estate division serves affordable and middle-income housing developers in major metros (Delhi NCR focus for high-income projects) and commercial office developers seeking lease rental discounting structures.[^ip-latest] Manufacturing exposure is diversified across chemicals, packaging, and automotive ancillary units.[^ip-latest] The company does not disclose individual customer names publicly, but the FY26 Annual Report indicates exposure to 100+ active borrowing accounts across 16 states/UTs.[^ar-fy25]

**Subsidiary Structure:** TFCI operates as a standalone entity without material subsidiaries. The company has made strategic investments in three Category-II Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) — Holystone TFCI Hospitality Fund, Certus TFCI Real Estate Fund, and Oxyzo Credit Fund — with total commitments of Rs. 90 Cr.[^ar-fy25] These AIF investments are held on the balance sheet as strategic investments rather than subsidiary vehicles, providing co-investment optionality and fee-based income.

**Manufacturing/Office Footprint:** As a financial services company, TFCI does not maintain manufacturing plants. The corporate office is located at NBCC Plaza, Pushpa Vihar, Sector-V, New Delhi — held via an agreement-to-sell since 2008 (sub-lease deed pending registration, a legacy governance item flagged in the FY26 Annual Report).[^ar-fy25] The company operates with a lean team of 46 employees managing a Rs. 2,088 Cr loan book, reflecting an AUM-per-employee ratio of ~Rs. 45 Cr — among the most efficient in the Indian NBFC space.[^ar-fy25] Disbursements are processed centrally with on-site inspections conducted for larger tickets.

**Promoter Background and Group Context:** TFCI was incorporated in 1989 as a public-sector financial institution under the administrative control of the Ministry of Tourism, Government of India.[^ip-latest] The original promoter was IFCI Limited, which held 26.09% until the ongoing divestment process.[^vp-thread] Current promoters include LIC (Life Insurance Corporation of India) and Oriental Insurance Company, both public-sector entities, reflecting the company's PSU heritage.[^ip-latest] The shareholding pattern has evolved significantly — promoter holding declined from 17.96% in Sep 2023 to 3.85% by Dec 2024 following the IFCI stake-sale process and a Rs. 10 to Rs. 2 share split executed in FY25.[^ar-fy25] Public holding has correspondingly expanded to 90.69% as of June 2026, with FII holding at 5.46% and DII holding at negligible levels.[^ip-latest]

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM):** The Indian tourism and hospitality sector represents a Rs. 25–30 lakh Cr opportunity by 2030, with the organized financing gap estimated at Rs. 1.5–2.0 lakh Cr for hotel project finance alone.[^ip-latest] Adjacent TAMs include commercial real estate (LRD market ~Rs. 50,000 Cr annually), affordable housing finance (Rs. 10 lakh Cr cumulative opportunity), and infrastructure project finance (Rs. 12.22 lakh Cr national infrastructure pipeline).[^ar-fy25] The NBFC/HFC/ARC sector faces an estimated Rs. 5 lakh Cr annual incremental funding requirement, of which TFCI's Rs. 2,002 Cr book represents a negligible 0.04% — indicating substantial runway.[^ar-fy25]

**Industry CAGR:** Indian tourism is projected to grow at 8–10% CAGR through 2030, with domestic tourist visits already up 40% post-pandemic.[^ar-fy25] The branded hotel room pipeline stands at 1.11 lakh rooms, requiring ~Rs. 1.5–2.0 lakh Cr in project finance over the next 5–7 years.[^ar-fy25] Commercial real estate (Grade-A office) absorption is growing at 12–15% CAGR in top metros, supporting LRD demand.[^ip-latest]

**Policy Tailwinds:** (a) **National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP):** Rs. 12.22 lakh Cr capex commitment through 2030, with significant allocation to tourism infrastructure (roads, airports, rail connectivity to tourist circuits).[^ar-fy25] (b) **Renewable Energy Target:** 500 GW renewable energy capacity by 2030 creates project finance opportunities in solar/wind hospitality assets.[^ar-fy25] (c) **Tourism Ministry Initiatives:** Swadesh Darshan 2.0, PRASHAD scheme, and destination development programs driving hospitality capex in tier-2/3 cities.[^ip-latest] (d) **Affordable Housing Push:** PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana) and state-level housing policies supporting TFCI's real estate diversification.[^ip-latest] (e) **RBI Priority Sector Lending:** Hospitality qualifies for priority sector treatment, enabling PSL certificate (PSC) trading income for TFCI.[^ar-fy25]
