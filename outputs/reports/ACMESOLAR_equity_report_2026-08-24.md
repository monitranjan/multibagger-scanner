### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership & Moat — First-Mover Dominance in India's BESS Market**

ACME Solar has established itself as India's largest battery energy storage system (BESS) operator with 3.62 GWh already commissioned, representing approximately 40% of India's total commissioned BESS capacity [^ip-latest]. This first-mover advantage is significant because BESS is the critical enabling technology for India's renewable energy transition, addressing the intermittency challenge of solar and wind power [^cc-transcript]. The company has secured battery supply orders for over 15 GWh from tier-1 suppliers including CATL and Hithium, ensuring execution certainty for its ambitious growth pipeline [^ip-latest]. With 10+ GWh BESS commissioning targeted by FY27 (revised upward from CY27), ACME is positioned to capture disproportionate market share in a segment where competitors are still in early-stage deployment [^cc-transcript]. The moat is reinforced by 10 GW of grid connectivity inventory already secured, which is a scarce resource in India's transmission-constrained RE market [^ip-latest].

**2. Structural Margin Story — Best-in-Class EBITDA Margins with Expansion Potential**

The company delivered an exceptional 87.1% consolidated EBITDA margin in Q1 FY27, with core renewable energy EBITDA margins (ex-BESS) running at approximately 91% [^cc-transcript]. This is structurally superior to most Indian IPPs because solar/wind power generation has near-zero variable costs once commissioned — sunlight and wind are free, and operating expenses are limited to land lease, O&M, and insurance [^ar-fy25]. The EBITDA margin expanded by approximately 500 basis points from FY24 (84.3% adjusted) to FY25 (89.2%) on operating leverage from the 1,200 MW Rajasthan project [^ar-fy25]. BESS operations contribute an 82% EBITDA margin, slightly lower than pure generation but still exceptional, with realizations of Rs. 8-10/unit locked in through short-term contracts [^cc-transcript]. The margin trajectory is supported by improving Capacity Utilization Factor (CUF), which hit a record 30.9% in Q1 FY27 versus 28.5% YoY [^ip-latest].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — From Pure Solar to Multi-Technology IPP**

ACME has strategically pivoted from a pure-play solar IPP to a diversified multi-technology renewable energy platform. The contracted portfolio now comprises 49% solar, 49% FDRE (Firm & Dispatchable Renewable Energy) and Hybrid, and 2% wind [^ar-fy25]. This shift is commercially significant because FDRE and Hybrid projects command tariffs of Rs. 3-5/kWh versus Rs. 2.5/kWh for conventional solar, reflecting the value of round-the-clock power supply [^ar-fy25]. The company has also entered wind energy with 150 MW operational and additional capacity under construction [^ip-latest]. The most important adjacency is BESS, which transforms ACME from a commodity power generator into a grid services provider capable of capturing peak power premiums, frequency regulation revenue, and demand-side management opportunities [^cc-transcript]. Management has guided for 10 GW generation + 25 GWh BESS by 2030, implying a generation capacity CAGR of approximately 35% [^ip-latest].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months) — Execution-Linked Re-Rating Triggers**

The next 6-12 months contain multiple execution catalysts that could drive re-rating. First, BESS commissioning of approximately 7 GWh is scheduled across Q2-Q4 FY27 at substations including Bikaner-III, Fatehgarh-I, and Neemuch, which would take cumulative capacity to 10+ GWh [^ip-latest]. Second, 1.5 GW of renewable energy capacity (FDRE and Hybrid projects) is targeted for commissioning in FY27, including the 250 MW ACME Surya Power and 320 MW ACME Sun Power projects at Fatehgarh-II [^ip-latest]. Third, the company has locked in Rs. 1,400+ Cr of short-term BESS revenue with realizations of Rs. 8-10/unit, providing strong near-term revenue visibility [^cc-transcript]. Fourth, the QIP completed in Q1 FY27 has strengthened the balance sheet (Net Debt/Equity improved to 1.7x from 2.6x), enabling accelerated capex deployment [^ar-fy25]. Fifth, the share of central offtakers (SECI, NTPC, SJVN, NHPC) is expected to increase to 84% from current 67%, which should further reduce DSO days and improve cash flow predictability [^ip-latest].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — MOOWR Contingent Liability and Related Party Transaction Complexity**

The single largest structural risk is the MOOWR (Manufacture and Other Operations in Warehouse Regulations) deferred import duty liability of Rs. 18,566 million, equivalent to approximately 41% of net worth [^ar-fy25]. Six subsidiaries imported solar modules under this duty deferment scheme; while the Delhi High Court quashed the CBIC's adverse instruction in May 2024, custom authorities have appealed to the Supreme Court [^ar-fy25]. If the SC reverses the HC order, the group could face a material cash outflow. The second risk is the complexity of related party transactions — the captive EPC model routes most construction spend through promoter-group entities, with proposed RPTs of Rs. 8,825 million with ACME Sigma and Rs. 2,640 million with ACSPL seeking AGM approval [^ar-fy25]. The 49/51 subsidiary structure, where the listed entity funds and operates projects while ACSPL holds the controlling 51% stake, creates a potential agency conflict that requires ongoing monitoring [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model**

ACME Solar Holdings Limited is a pure-play renewable energy Independent Power Producer (IPP) that develops, constructs, owns, and operates utility-scale solar, wind, and battery energy storage projects across India [^ip-latest]. The company sells electricity to central and state government-owned off-takers under long-term Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) with tenures of 25 years, providing highly predictable cash flows [^ar-fy25]. Unlike EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction) contractors who earn one-time construction margins, ACME is an asset owner that earns recurring returns over the project lifecycle — this is a fundamentally different and superior business model in terms of cash flow visibility [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue Split by Segment**

The company reports financials on both consolidated and standalone bases, which serve different purposes:

*Consolidated Revenue (Q1 FY27):*
- Renewable Energy (Solar + Wind) Generation: Rs. 728 Cr (~76% of revenue)
- BESS Operations: Rs. 226 Cr (~24% of revenue)
- Other Income: Rs. 96 Cr
- **Total Consolidated Revenue: Rs. 954 Cr** [^ip-latest]

*Standalone Revenue (Q1 FY27) — In-house EPC Business:*
- EPC Revenue: Rs. 2,266 Cr
- Other Income: Rs. 92 Cr
- **Total Standalone Revenue: Rs. 2,358 Cr** [^ip-latest]

The standalone EPC revenue is almost entirely inter-company, representing construction services provided to subsidiaries [^ar-fy25]. This captive EPC model allows ACME to capture construction margins internally rather than paying them to third-party contractors.

**Key OEM/End Customers per Division**

The company's customer base is concentrated among government-owned off-takers, which provides credit quality but also exposes the company to government payment delays:

- **Central Off-takers (84% of total portfolio):** SECI (Solar Energy Corporation of India), NTPC, SJVN, NHPC — these are central government entities with strong credit profiles [^ip-latest]
- **State Off-takers (14% of total portfolio):** GUVNL (Gujarat), and various state DISCOMs [^ip-latest]
- **BESS Customers:** Short-term contracts with state distribution companies and C&I (Commercial & Industrial) consumers including data centers [^cc-transcript]

**Subsidiary Structure**

ACME Solar Holdings operates through a complex structure of approximately 65 subsidiaries, each typically a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) holding individual power projects [^ar-fy25]. A notable structural feature is that several under-construction SPVs (ACME Sigma, ACME Alpha, ACME Renewtech) are 49% owned by the listed entity and 51% owned by ACSPL (promoter entity), with call options for the listed entity to acquire the remaining stake post-lock-in [^ar-fy25]. This structure allows the promoter to maintain economic interest while the listed entity funds development.

**Manufacturing Footprint**

ACME does not own solar module manufacturing facilities within the listed entity — a 1.2 GW ALMM-certified module plant exists under MKU Holdings, a promoter-group entity [^ar-fy25]. The company's operational footprint spans 11 states, with over 75% of operational capacity located in Rajasthan, which offers the highest solar irradiance in India [^ar-fy25]. Key substations for under-construction projects include Fatehgarh, Bikaner, Bhadla, Neemuch, and Ananthapuram [^ip-latest]. The company has acquired over 18,000 acres of land for its under-construction portfolio [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background and Group Context**

ACME Solar is promoted by Manoj Kumar Upadhyay, who serves as Chairman and Managing Director [^ar-fy25]. The ACME Group has been in the renewable energy business since 2003 and was among the early entrants in India's solar sector [^ar-fy25]. The group operates through multiple entities including ACSPL (holding company), MKU Holdings (module manufacturing), and VIPL, with the listed entity being the primary vehicle for utility-scale project ownership [^ar-fy25]. The promoter holding stood at 71.40% as of June 2026, down from 83.29% in March 2026 following a QIP (Qualified Institutions Placement) that raised institutional capital [^ip-latest].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM)**

India's renewable energy market is among the largest globally in terms of growth potential. The government has committed to 500 GW of non-fossil fuel capacity by 2030, up from approximately 200 GW currently installed [^cc-transcript]. The BESS segment is nascent but rapidly scaling — India had less than 1 GWh of operational BESS capacity before FY26, and ACME alone targets 10+ GWh by FY27 [^ip-latest]. Industry estimates suggest India will require 40-50 GWh of BESS capacity by 2030 to support grid stability and renewable integration [^cc-transcript]. In monetary terms, the RE + BESS opportunity represents a TAM of approximately Rs. 15-20 lakh crore over the next decade, with BESS alone representing a Rs. 2-3 lakh crore opportunity [^cc-transcript].

**Market CAGR**

- **Solar/Wind Generation Capacity CAGR:** 15-20% (India targeting 500 GW by 2030 vs ~200 GW today)
- **BESS Capacity CAGR:** 60-80% (off a small base, with policy mandates accelerating adoption)
- **ACME's Targeted Generation CAGR:** ~35% (10 GW by 2030 vs 2.99 GW operational) [^ip-latest]

**Policy Tailwinds**

Several regulatory developments create a favorable environment for ACME:

1. **ALMM Timeline Extension (MNRE, deadline 31.12.2026):** Provides flexibility to commission merchant BESS projects charged with co-located solar, enabling ACME to monetize BESS capacity before securing long-term PPAs [^ip-latest].

2. **ISTS Waiver Protection (MoP, Jul 2025–Jun 2028):** Benefits off-takers by allowing them to avail Interstate Transmission System waiver even where General Network Access (GNA) is delayed, reducing project execution risk [^ip-latest].

3. **GNA Connectivity Flexibility (CERC):** Free-exit option and merchant-route conversion for projects lacking PPA within 12 months of LoA, providing downside protection [^ip-latest].

4. **Accelerated BESS Deployment (CTUIL ROFR route):** Right of First Refusal-based connectivity allocation accelerates BESS project commissioning [^ip-latest].

5. **Grid Forming Inverters mandatory for BESS (CEA):** Creates structural demand for BESS as grid-forming capability becomes essential for grid stability [^ip-latest].

6. **Peak Power Demand Growth:** Q1 FY27 electricity demand grew 8.8% YoY with peak demand hitting 271 GW, driven by EV adoption, data center growth, and air conditioning penetration [^cc-transcript].

**Valuation Discount/Premium vs Peers**

ACME Solar trades at a P/E of 42.3x, which is at a premium to diversified peers like Tata Power (32x), JSW Energy (38x), and significantly above hydro-focused NHPC (22x) and SJVN (18x) [^ip-latest]. However, this premium is justified by:

1. **Higher Growth Profile:** ACME's targeted 35% capacity CAGR exceeds most peers, who are growing at 15-25%.
2. **BESS Optionality:** ACME is the only listed pure-play with material BESS exposure, which commands scarcity value.
3. **Best-in-Class EBITDA Margins:** At 89.2%, ACME's margins are superior to all listed peers.

The premium is partially offset by:
1. **Execution Risk:** ACME has a larger under-construction pipeline (5,080 MW) as a proportion of operational capacity (170%) versus more mature peers.
2. **Governance Complexity:** The 49/51 subsidiary structure and RPT density are higher than peers.
3. **Smaller Scale:** At Rs. 28,388 Cr market cap, ACME is smaller than Adani Green, Tata Power, and JSW Energy, resulting in lower liquidity.

On a P/B basis, ACME trades at 4.9x versus the peer average of approximately 4.0x, reflecting the market's pricing of its growth optionality [^ip-latest].

**PROJECTION RATIONALE & ASSUMPTIONS (FY27E and FY28E):**

FY28E growth moderates to **31% YoY** (base effect normalization) as the full-year run-rate of FY27 commissioning kicks in, plus incremental FDRE/Hybrid projects at higher tariffs of Rs 3.25-6.28/unit.

- **Other Income:** Stepped down to Rs 350-400 Cr from FY26A's Rs 486 Cr as QIP and IPO cash gets deployed in capex (lower interest on deposits), partially offset by MOOWR deferred revenue amortization.

- **Depreciation:** Climbs from Rs 468 Cr (FY26A) to Rs 1,100 Cr (FY28E) as 5,080 MW UC portfolio commissions. Useful life of 30 years (revised from 25 in FY24) keeps the per-MW depreciation rate moderate at ~Rs 0.25-0.30 Cr/MW/yr.
- **Tax Rate:** Held flat at 25% (standard MAT rate applicable to renewable energy IPPs). MAT credit of Rs 7,487 Cr (from FY24 exceptional gain) provides a buffer; hence effective rate slightly below statutory 25.2%.
- **PAT & EPS:** FY27E PAT of Rs 1,125 Cr (+126% YoY) and FY28E PAT of Rs 1,538 Cr (+37% YoY) reflect full operating leverage. EPS calculated on ~60.5 Cr share count (post-FY25 corporate actions). The target price of Rs 470 from PART 1 implies a forward FY28E P/E of ~18.5x, reasonable for a high-growth utility IPP.

**Analytical Commentary (Table 1):**

The income statement tells the classic story of a capital-intensive utility entering its "growth spurt" phase. Revenue growth accelerates from a modest 7% in FY25A to a staggering 108% in FY27E — driven primarily by the battery energy storage (BESS) business which is now commercialized at premium tariffs of Rs 8-10/unit (vs Rs 3-4/unit for plain solar). In plain English: imagine a farmer who used to grow only wheat (solar) and now has started growing strawberries (BESS) at 3x the price — the same land, but the revenue multiplies. EBITDA margins remain world-class at 86-88%, which is essentially saying that for every Rs 100 of power sold, Rs 87 flows down as gross profit before interest and depreciation — this is the magic of solar/wind PPAs where fuel cost is zero (sunlight and wind are free). The "pressure point" is the interest line: it nearly doubles from Rs 1,123 Cr to Rs 2,000 Cr as the company borrows heavily to fund new projects. Think of it as a family taking a bigger home loan to add rooms — short-term cash flow strain, but the rooms will generate rental income for 25 years under PPAs. PAT growth of 126% in FY27E looks spectacular but is mathematically driven by the low base of FY25A (which was depressed by commissioning delays) and FY26A (partial year of new capacity). The EPS trajectory of Rs 4.17 → Rs 8.23 → Rs 18.60 → Rs 25.42 forms the core of our investment thesis.

**Specific Watch-Points on AMBER/RED Items:**

- **RED — MOOWR Contingent Liability (Rs 18,566 Cr):** Six subsidiaries imported solar modules under the MOOWR (Manufacture and Other Operations in Warehouse) scheme that allows duty deferment. CBIC issued an adverse instruction in July 2022 challenging the eligibility; the Delhi High Court quashed this in May 2024, but the custom authorities have appealed to the Supreme Court. If the SC reverses the HC order, ACME could owe Rs 18.6 billion in import duties — equivalent to 41% of FY26A net worth. - **RED — Related Party Transactions:** The entire EPC supply chain (capital goods procurement, O&M, inter-corporate lending) runs through ACME Cleantech Solutions Pvt Ltd (ACSPL), the promoter holding company. RPT volumes of Rs 19,388 Cr on capital goods alone — in a single year — represent 9.5x consolidated revenue. The 49/51 subsidiary structure (where the listed entity funds 100% of project cost and holds 49%, while the promoter holds 51%) creates a potential agency conflict. The call option held by the listed entity to acquire the remaining 51% post-lock-in mitigates this partially, but investors should monitor RPT disclosures quarter-on-quarter.

- **AMBER — Other Income Quality:** Other income jumped from Rs 147 Cr (FY25A) to Rs 486 Cr (FY26A) — a 230% increase that is not from core operations. The components are: (a) interest on fixed deposits (Rs 62 Cr in Q1 FY27 alone) tied to DSRA/PBG accounts, (b) amortization of deferred revenue under MOOWR scheme (Rs 22 Cr in Q1 FY27), and (c) miscellaneous non-recurring items. The 72% PBT contribution from other income in FY26A is not sustainable — as QIP cash gets deployed in capex, interest income will decline. **Watch:** Decomposition of other income in quarterly results; if recurring components drop below 50% of PBT, the PBT quality improves.

- **AMBER — Capital Work-in-Progress Concentration:** CWIP of Rs 4,358 Cr (FY26A) and projected Rs 6,000 Cr (FY27E) represents ~15-16% of total assets. The impairment risk if any of the under-construction projects face GNA (grid connectivity) delays or PPA renegotiation is a meaningful tail risk, given the recent fire incident at Acme Suryodaya (July 2026). **Watch:** Quarterly commissioning progress vs. guidance; any slippage beyond Q1 FY28 would impact FY28E commissioning revenue.
