### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Anuh Pharma is one of India’s largest manufacturers of Macrolides (think erythromycin – a class of antibiotics that fight bacterial infections) and Anti-TB (tuberculosis) active ingredients, with leadership in Erythromycin salts, Pyrazinamide, Sulfadoxine, Ambroxol HCL and Gliclazide [^ip-latest]. An API (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient) is the pure chemical molecule that actually treats the disease – like the “flour” that goes into a medicine “cake”. The moat is built on regulatory switching costs: a customer cannot easily change supplier because each API plant must pass strict USFDA (US Food & Drug Administration) and EU GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) audits; in 2026 Anuh cleared USFDA inspection with zero 483s (deficiency letters) and got WHO pre-qualification for Amodiaquine, locking in institutional tender eligibility [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin story:** In Q1 FY27, EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation & Amortisation – a clean measure of core factory profit) margin rose to 9.99% from 7.34% a year earlier, an expansion of ~265 basis points (a basis point is 0.01%, so 265 bps = 2.65 percentage points) [^ip-latest]. ROCE (Return on Capital Employed – how efficiently all invested capital is turned into profit) improved 326 bps to 19.18% vs FY26’s 15.92% [^ip-latest]. This is structurally supported by scale-up of Tarapur capacity from 1,800 to 2,400 MTPA (Metric Tonnes Per Annum – the maximum physical output of the plant per year) and a richer mix of higher-value regulated-market products [^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** The company is pivoting from commoditised antibiotics to chronic-therapy APIs: Vonoprazon Fumarate (anti-ulcer/stomach acid), Pretomanid (new anti-TB), Edoxaban Tosylate (anti-coagulant/blood thinner), Bisoprolol Fumarate (beta-blocker for hypertension) are under development [^ip-latest]. Management explicitly calls high-value regulated-market (US/EU) growth the “primary engine” to reduce currency risk and lift realisations, alongside process innovation at its DSIR-approved Mahape R&D centre [^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Full utilisation of the new INT-1A block (200 MT + 40 KL reactor) commissioned Jul-2025, taking nameplate to 2,400 MTPA [^ar-fy25]; (ii) WHO PQ for Amodiaquine HCL in 2026 opens Global Fund tender volumes [^ip-latest]; (iii) Company-stated 15–20% p.a. revenue growth and 5–6 new API launches in FY27 [^ar-fy25]; (iv) Possible NSE listing progress (though historically evasive on timeline) could improve liquidity and re-rate visibility [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Approximately 46% of FY26 revenue is export-linked, exposing the P&L to rupee–USD movement and Chinese intermediate (raw material precursor) cost inflation [^ar-fy25]. Investor community flags a Sales/Net Fixed Asset ratio of ~22x vs peer range 2–4x, suggesting a trading/merchant-export blend that may dilute true manufacturing margin quality [^vp-thread]. Historical EBITDA% slid from 13.2% (FY24) to 8.5% (FY26) confirming weak pricing power in surplus global API capacity [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Anuh Pharma’s core business model is manufacturing bulk drugs, i.e., APIs, for domestic formulation companies and global distributors – it sells the “active ingredient” rather than the final tablet or syrup. In Q1 FY27, total operating revenue was ₹193.81 Cr [^ip-latest]. The revenue split by product group (absolute ₹ Cr and % of total) was: Erythromycin ₹63.6 (32.8%), Other Items ₹34.7 (17.9%), Corticosteroids ₹20.9 (10.8%), Higher Macrolides (Azithromycin) ₹19.7 (10.2%), Chloramphenicol ₹12.2 (6.3%), Sulphadoxine ₹11.0 (5.7%), Pyrazinamide ₹10.3 (5.3%), Ambroxol HCL ₹8.4 (4.3%), Gliclazide ₹5.1 (2.6%) [^ip-latest]. Geographically, Domestic contributed ₹124 Cr (64%) and Export ₹70 Cr (36%); export mix was Asia 35.7%, Europe 29.6%, Africa 27.9%, North America 4.2%, Latin America 2.6% [^ip-latest].

Key customers are largely anonymous formulation makers and tender agencies; top 5 customers account for 34% of sales (domestic-heavy 37% vs export 30%) and the firm serves 360 marketing partners/customers across 72+ countries [^ip-latest]. Subsidiary structure is not materially disclosed; the entity is part of a promoter cluster with alleged affiliate Wanbury Ltd per news co-occurrence, though no formal JV is filed [^vp-thread]. Manufacturing footprint is concentrated at Tarapur (Maharashtra) with 2,400 MTPA capacity featuring ZLD (Zero Liquid Discharge – a water treatment system that ensures no polluted liquid leaves the factory, meeting strict environmental norms) and a Mahape (Navi Mumbai) R&D lab recognised by DSIR (Department of Scientific & Industrial Research – a government body certifying in-house innovation) [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. Promoter background: Chairman A. Todarwal and Vice Chairman B. Shah steer strategy; Joint MDs Ritesh Shah and Vivek Shah are operating executives from the founding families, with promoter+group holding 71.8% post bonus issue [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian API market is approximately ₹60,000 Cr (~USD 7.2 bn at ₹83/$) growing at 8–10% CAGR, while global API TAM exceeds ₹16 lakh Cr (USD 200 bn) [^vp-thread]. Policy tailwinds include the government’s PLI (Production Linked Incentive – a subsidy paid on incremental sales) scheme for API self-reliance, which favours compliant, scaled players like Anuh amid China+1 diversification [^ar-fy25]. Competitive moat analysis: Switching Costs = Strong (regulatory filings like USFDA, EDQM CEPs take years and customers rarely requalify); Scale = Moderate (Anuh 2,400 MTPA vs peers >10k, but sufficient for niche macrolides); Pricing Power = Weak (global surplus capacity and price erosion noted by chairman) [^ar-fy25].

Anuh Pharma makes bulk drugs (called APIs – active pharmaceutical ingredients – the raw chemical compounds that become medicines). In FY24-FY26, sales grew but profits fell because margins shrank – the company spent more on compliance, raw materials, and built inventory.
