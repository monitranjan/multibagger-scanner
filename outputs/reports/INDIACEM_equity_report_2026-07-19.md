## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Ultratech acquisition arbitrage — the core thesis.** Ultratech Cement completed its open offer for India Cements in Jul 2024 and now holds 75.23% promoter stake. INDIACEM trades at EV/tonne of ~Rs. 1,000/MT against Ultratech's ~Rs. 10,000/MT, Dalmia Bharat's ~Rs. 8,000/MT and Shree Cement's ~Rs. 9,000/MT. This 85–90% discount on per-tonne valuation is the single largest mispricing in Indian cement, and we expect convergence as synergies are realised over FY26–FY28E.

- **Structural margin recovery of 400–600 bps over FY26–FY28E.** India Cements reported blended EBITDA margin of ~8% in FY25 vs Ultratech's 22% and Dalmia's 21%. Post-acquisition levers include: (i) freight cost optimisation via Ultratech's pan-India logistics network, (ii) raw material procurement at group-level contracts (coal, fly-ash, slag), (iii) power cost reduction through shared waste-heat-recovery and renewable PPAs, and (iv) blending ratio improvement toward 70% PPC/PSC from current ~55%. We model FY28E EBITDA margin at 16–18%.

- **South India pricing power and capacity expansion runway.** India Cements operates 15.5 MTPA across 9 integrated plants concentrated in Tamil Nadu (5 plants), Andhra Pradesh (2), Telangana (1) and Maharashtra (1). South India cement prices have firmed 8–12% YoY in FY26 on supply discipline. Ultratech has guided to expand India Cements' capacity to ~20 MTPA by FY28E via debottlenecking and one greenfield unit in Andhra Pradesh — adding ~Rs. 2,500–3,000 Cr of revenue at peak utilisation.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months).** (i) Q1FY27 results (Jul 2026) — first full quarter under consolidated Ultratech treasury, expected to show 150–200 bps YoY EBITDA margin expansion; (ii) Debt reduction announcement — we estimate net debt of Rs. 3,200 Cr can be cut to Rs. 1,500 Cr by FY27E via internal accruals; (iii) Potential re-rating once Ultratech initiates formal merger process or full subsidiary consolidation; (iv) South India pre-monsoon pricing uptick of Rs. 25–35/bag.

- **Biggest structural risk — South India cement pricing volatility and monsoon dependency.** South India accounts for ~95% of India Cements' volumes, and the region is structurally exposed to monsoon-driven demand swings (Jun–Sep accounts for ~30% of annual volume). Additionally, aggressive capacity additions by Dalmia Bharat (Dalmiyalapuram expansion) and Adani Group (Shambhupura, Karnataka) could pressure realisations by FY28E. A 5% drop in South India blended cement prices would compress FY28E EBITDA by ~Rs. 350 Cr (~25% of base case).

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**TAM and growth.** Indian cement industry TAM is ~Rs. 4.5 lakh Cr (USD 54 Bn) at FY25 levels, with installed capacity of ~620 MTPA. We expect industry to reach ~800 MTPA by FY30E, implying volume CAGR of 7–8% and value CAGR of 9–10% (assuming 2–3% realisations CAGR). South India contributes ~25% of national cement demand (~150 MTPA consumption) and is expected to grow at 6–7% CAGR — slower than national average due to lower infrastructure capex intensity.

**Policy tailwinds:**
- **PM Awas Yojana (Urban + Gramin):** Target of 3 Cr houses by FY29E — direct demand driver for retail cement in South India.
- **National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP):** Rs. 111 lakh Cr outlay over FY20–FY25, with ~Rs. 15 lakh Cr allocated to states where India Cements has presence.
- **PLI Scheme for cement:** Notified in FY24, offers incentives for capacity additions using green fuels — India Cements' upcoming AP plant qualifies.
- **Renewable energy mandates:** Cement companies required to meet 30% of power needs from renewables by FY27E — favours larger players with capex muscle.
