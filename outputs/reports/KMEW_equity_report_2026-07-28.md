### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Niche market leadership in private-sector Indian dredging with high asset productivity.** KMEW has built a fleet of 11+ self-propelled hopper barges and cutter suction dredgers and is one of the few listed pure-play players in Indian capital and maintenance dredging, executing works at Deendayal, Mangrol, Porbandar and other non-major ports [^ip-latest]. The company's stated internal threshold of 40% EBITDA on dredging contracts (with management commentary indicating actual margins trending at ~45–48%) places it structurally above diversified shipping peers operating at sub-25% EBITDA margins [^cc-transcript]. This is a moat rooted in equipment availability — KMEW's >95% fleet uptime vs. an industry average of 70–75% is the single biggest determinant of project economics in time-bound dredging awards [^vp-thread].

- **Asset-light, refurbishment-led capex model drives 200–300 bps structural margin advantage over fleet-heavy peers.** Rather than buying newbuilds at $15–20mn ticket sizes, KMEW acquires 2010–2017-built secondhand vessels at ~Rs. 3–5 Cr (as evidenced by the M.V. Abnco VI acquisition at Rs. 3.5 Cr) and uses its in-house maintenance team to refurbish them, cutting effective vessel cost by 60–70% [^vp-thread]. Combined with the absence of large debt service on the P&L, this drives a structurally higher ROCE than capital-heavy peers like Great Eastern Shipping or SCI, where ROCE sits in low-to-mid teens pre-impairment [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into high-growth maritime adjacencies is meaningfully de-risking the dredging concentration.** Beyond core dredging, the company has scaled into (a) fishing harbour capital dredging under the Sagarmala / PMMSY scheme, (b) hydrographic and magnetometer surveys for the Indian Navy and port trusts, and (c) ship repair & refit at its Mumbai and Goa facilities [^ip-latest]. The fishing harbour opportunity alone — Gujarat's plan for 10 new major ports over 25 years and ~200+ fishing harbours nationally — is multi-decade and currently underserved by listed players [^vp-thread]. This adjacency expansion should lift non-dredging revenue mix from current ~20% to 30–35% by FY28E.

- **Near-term catalysts over 6–12 months: order book conversion, post-EGM capex authorisation, and defence survey awards.** The order book of ~Rs. 600+ Cr (post recent Gujarat fishing harbour awards) provides 2.0–2.2x revenue cover on FY25A base and should support 35–40% YoY topline growth in FY26E [^ip-latest]. The 19-Jul-2026 EGM outcome (referenced in the recent postal ballot scrutiniser's report) is expected to unlock incremental vessel capex authorisation, which is a critical gating factor for revenue acceleration in FY27E [^cc-transcript]. Defence survey contract wins, where KMEW has historically bid alongside 1–2 PSU rivals, are likely to be a positive surprise given the Navy's indigenisation push [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk: customer concentration, charter rate cyclicality, and the regulatory dependency on port tender pipeline.** Top 5 customers (Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port, Deendayal Port, Gujarat Maritime Board, Indian Navy) likely contribute >70% of revenue, creating binary exposure to port trust budget cycles and political decision-making [^ar-fy25]. Charter rate inflation — global hopper barge day rates have eased ~15% from FY24 peaks — can compress margins if contracts are renegotiated mid-tenure. Additionally, environmental clearances for capital dredging (CRZ, EIA) routinely delay project commencement by 3–6 months, creating revenue lumpiness that the market is currently under-pricing at 45.7x trailing P/E [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** KMEW is a Mumbai-headquartered, single-location marine services company providing four integrated service lines: (1) **Capital & Maintenance Dredging** (largest revenue contributor at ~65–70% of FY25A revenue), (2) **Ship Repair & Refit** (~15%), (3) **Hydrographic / Magnetometric / Bathymetric Surveys** (~8–10%), and (4) **Technical manning, O&M and allied marine infrastructure works** (~7–10%) [^ip-latest]. The company operates under a BOQ/contract model with multi-year framework agreements (typically 3–5 years) with major and non-major port trusts, the Indian Navy, and state maritime boards [^ar-fy25].
