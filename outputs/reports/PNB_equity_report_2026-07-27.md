### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Moat: 2nd largest domestic branch network & PSU funding cost advantage.** PNB operates ~10,000+ branches with a deep semi-urban / rural moat that private peers cannot replicate at scale, anchoring a CASA-led low-cost liability franchise[^ar-fy25]. Government ownership ensures sovereign-rated funding access, keeping cost of deposits ~50–80 bps below private peers, a structural NIM tailwind that has persisted across cycles[^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin expansion: NIM trajectory + credit-cost normalization.** NIMs have expanded to ~3.45% in FY26 from sub-3% in FY23 as legacy bulk deposits re-price lower and RAM (Retail, Agriculture, MSME) share rises to >55% of advances[^ip-latest]. With credit costs normalizing to ~0.55% post NCL write-offs and slippages running at decade lows (~1.2%), we see 80–100 bps of further RoA expansion runway over FY26–FY28E[^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies: Digital, RAM & gold loans.** PNB One app has crossed 100 Mn+ registered customers, making it one of the largest digital banking platforms by user base among PSU banks[^ip-latest]. Gold loan portfolio (low-ticket, high-yield) and home loan (PNB Housing Finance stake monetisation optionality) provide high-CAR, low-LGD growth vectors[^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months): Q1FY27 results, further stake sale in PNB Housing, NIM guidance.** Q1FY27 board meeting is scheduled for 04 Aug 2026 — we expect another quarter of NIM expansion and stable asset quality[^ar-fy25]. Further divestment of PNB Housing Finance stake (currently ~28%+ holding) remains a re-rating catalyst with embedded value of Rs. 8–10/share[^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk: Asset quality cycle reversal in unsecured retail & MSME.** Unsecured retail (personal loans, credit cards on BoB co-branded book) and MFI exposure have grown >25% YoY — these segments are now showing early signs of stress industry-wide[^cc-transcript]. A sharp slowdown in unsecured retail could push slippage ratios back toward 2%, compressing ROA by 30–40 bps and derailing the re-rating thesis.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Punjab National Bank is India's second-largest public sector bank by business volume, with a consolidated balance sheet of Rs. 18+ lakh Cr, offering a full suite of banking services across retail, corporate, treasury, agriculture, MSME, and international banking segments[^ar-fy25].

**Revenue / Asset Mix:**
- **Advances (Net Interest Income driver):** Rs. 9.5 lakh Cr domestic + international book; RAM share ~55%, Corporate ~38%, overseas ~7%[^ip-latest].
- **Deposits:** Rs. 14+ lakh Cr, with CASA ratio ~42% — among the highest for PSU banks[^cc-transcript].
- **Fee Income:** Rs. 4,000+ Cr p.a., driven by transaction banking, processing fees, and digital payments[^ar-fy25].

**Key Customer Segments:**
- **Retail:** Home loans, vehicle loans, personal loans, education loans via direct branch channel[^ip-latest].
- **Corporate:** Large relationships in power, infrastructure, steel, and PSU discoms[^ar-fy25].
- **Government: Single-largest tax collection banker; salary account monopoly for central government employees**[^vp-thread].

**Subsidiary Structure:**
- PNB Housing Finance Ltd (PNB: 28%+ stake) — listed housing finance company[^ip-latest].
- PNB Gilts Ltd — debt trading arm.
- PNB Cards & Services — co-branded credit cards.
- JVs: IndiaFirst Life Insurance (with Bank of Baroda), Everest Bank (Nepal).

**Manufacturing / Distribution Footprint:**
- ~10,000+ domestic branches across all states/UTs[^ar-fy25].
- International presence in UK (PNB International), Dubai, Hong Kong, and Nepal[^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Background:** Government of India holds 70.08% — making PNB a true blue-chip PSU bank with policy leverage, sovereign support and direct linkage to central government welfare schemes (MUDRA, PMJDY, DBT)[^ar-fy25].
