### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Motisons Jewellers Ltd is a well-entrenched organised retailer in the Jaipur (Rajasthan) market with four large format showrooms and a digital storefront launched in 2018 [^ar-fy25]. The company’s moat is built on brand trust accumulated since 1997, deep local customer relationships, and a very large product catalogue of over 3.5 lakh SKUs (a SKU, or “stock-keeping unit”, is simply a unique product variant – like a specific gold ring design and weight) that allows a customer to find almost any jewellery piece under one roof [^cc-transcript]. While it is not a national leader like Titan, its hyper-local dominance in Jaipur and high repeat footfall constitutes a moderate economic moat. The recent redemption of preference shares and clean audit opinion further reinforce credibility [^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin story:** The net profit margin expanded from ~7% in FY23 to 9.3% in FY25 primarily because the company used IPO proceeds to slash debt, causing interest cost to collapse and interest coverage (a ratio showing how many times a firm’s operating profit covers its interest expense – higher is safer) to surge to 107x [^ar-fy25]. However, operating (EBITDA) margin slightly contracted to 14.4% due to gold price volatility and higher inventory carrying costs [^ar-fy25]. The structural story is therefore one of financial leverage normalisation rather than core business pricing power; we expect net margin to stabilise at 9-10% as the benefit of zero net debt fully flows through [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** The firm has deliberately avoided geographic or product-segment diversification; domestic jewellery sales account for ~99.9% of revenue with export negligible at ₹0.38 Cr [^ar-fy25]. The only adjacency is an online platform that contributes a small but growing channel, and a continuous new-collection launch cycle where 20-30% of SKUs are refreshed every 3-4 months to keep assortments trendy [^cc-transcript]. Long-term management ambition is “pan-India expansion” but no concrete store pipeline exists yet [^cc-transcript]. This lack of diversification is a double-edged sword: it keeps execution simple but exposes the firm to regional demand shocks.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** First, the recently approved Qualified Institutional Placement (QIP – a way for listed firms to raise equity from institutions) and preferential warrant issuance of ₹170 Cr (₹42.5 Cr already received) provide dry powder for store upgrades and possible new showrooms, which if deployed efficiently could re-rate the stock [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. Second, the festival calendar (Diwali, Akshaya Tritiya, wedding season) historically lifts topline by double digits as noted by the Joint MD [^cc-transcript]. Third, the stock’s sharp +10.65% single-day gain on 14 Aug 2026 and subsequent bulk deals indicate renewed institutional interest [^vp-thread]. We model FY27E revenue growth of 12-14% on the back of these catalysts.

- **Biggest structural risk:** The most glaring red flag is negative operating cash flow (₹22.6 Cr outflow in FY25) against a reported PAT of ₹43.2 Cr, driven by a massive inventory build of ₹65.4 Cr (inventory days ~430+) [^ar-fy25]. For a jeweller, inventory is both the product and the working capital trap; if gold prices correct or consumer demand softens, write-downs could erase profits. Compounding this, related-party transactions (director loan balances aggregating ₹69.3 Cr and group rent) disclosed in notes contradict the “nil material RPT” assertion in AOC-2, creating a transparency gap [^ar-fy25]. Bullion price volatility (30-50% of product cost) and US diamond market dependence are additional exogenous risks [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Motisons Jewellers operates a straightforward but inventory-intensive retail model: it buys gold, diamond, silver and other materials from vendors, outsources manufacturing/trading rather than owning large factories, and sells finished jewellery through four Jaipur showrooms and an e-commerce site [^cc-transcript]. The core business model is thus “buy–assemble–sell” with minimal owned manufacturing, which keeps fixed costs low but leaves margin exposed to vendor pricing and bullion swings [^ar-fy25].

Revenue split by product category for FY25 (latest full year) is approximately: Gold ₹374 Cr (81%), Diamond ₹47 Cr (10%), Silver ₹41 Cr (9%), and others minor, out of total domestic sales ₹461.7 Cr [^ar-fy25]. Export is trivial at ₹0.38 Cr [^ar-fy25]. There are no separate “divisions” as such; the firm is a single-reportable segment (retail jewellery). Key end customers are individual consumers (brides, festival shoppers, investors), not OEMs; the Vaishali Nagar showroom alone contributed ~₹40 Cr sales last year, illustrating the importance of each location [^cc-transcript].

Subsidiary structure is minimal – the company is a standalone listed entity with no material subsidiaries mentioned in the annual report [^ar-fy25]. Manufacturing footprint is essentially nil owned plant; the firm relies on vendor network, but it holds inventory in Jaipur showrooms and possibly back-end storage. The four showrooms are all in Rajasthan (Jaipur), indicating a concentrated geographic footprint [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. Promoter background: the Chhabra family (Sandeep, Sanjay, Namita, Kajal) controls ~62.75% post recent dilution, with the firm founded in 1997 and listed on NSE/BSE in December 2023 [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. The group context is a first-generation jewellery retail family that scaled organically in a highly unorganised local market.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian jewellery market is estimated at roughly ₹3,50,000 Cr (≈ USD 42 bn) in organised+unorganised retail, growing at a CAGR of ~11-13% over the next five years driven by rising disposable income, wedding culture, and gold-as-investment habit [^ar-fy25]. Policy tailwinds include mandatory hallmarking (improves trust for organised players), GST rationalisation, and PLI schemes for gem & jewellery exports, though Motisons’ export exposure is negligible [^ar-fy25]. The competitive moat analysis for Motisons:

- **Switching costs:** Weak. Customers can easily move between jewellers based on design, price, and festival offers; no lock-in [^cc-transcript].
- **Scale:** Moderate. Four showrooms and ₹462 Cr revenue is small vs national chains, but large within Jaipur [^ar-fy25].
- **Pricing power:** Moderate. Ability to pass on gold price changes exists, but competition from unorganised local goldsmiths limits aggressive mark-ups [^ar-fy25].
