### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

*   **Market Leadership & Moat:** GMR Airports operates some of India's largest and most critical airport infrastructure, including Delhi (IGIA) and Hyderabad (RGIA), which are key gateways for international and domestic traffic. The long-term concession agreements (Delhi till 2066, Hyderabad till 2068, Goa till 2065) provide a significant competitive moat with high barriers to entry, regulated asset base (RAB) model assuring stable returns, and strong pricing power through aeronautical tariffs. The company benefits from the hub-and-spoke model of airlines, solidifying its position as a dominant player in the Indian aviation ecosystem.
*   **Structural Margin Story driven by Non-Aero Growth:** While aeronautical revenues are regulated, GMR Airports is strategically expanding its non-aeronautical revenue streams (retail, F&B, duty-free, car parking, advertising, cargo, MRO, hospitality, real estate development in aerotropolis zones). We expect non-aero revenue contribution to increase from ~35% currently to ~45% by FY28E, driven by rising passenger footfall, enhanced retail offerings, and monetisation of significant land banks around its airports. This shift will lead to higher EBITDA margins, as non-aero segments typically command better profitability and are less regulated.
*   **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies (International & Logistics):** GMR's strategic expansion beyond India, with operational airports in Cebu (Philippines), Medan (Indonesia), and upcoming Bhogapuram (India), offers geographical diversification and exposure to high-growth emerging markets. Furthermore, the focus on integrated airport cities (aerotropolis) through GMR Aerocity Delhi and Hyderabad, developing logistics hubs, hospitality, and commercial spaces, provides a strong growth runway beyond core airport operations. The recent strategic partnership with Groupe ADP (28.49% stake) provides global expertise and capital for future expansions.
*   **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** Key catalysts include continued robust recovery in domestic and international passenger traffic exceeding pre-COVID levels, especially for Delhi and Hyderabad. The operationalization of the new terminal at Goa Mopa Airport (GMIA) and progress on the expansion of Delhi Airport's Terminal 1 and Terminal 3, along with the development of the aerotropolis projects, will drive revenue and profitability. Further, potential re-rating post-monetization of additional non-core assets or successful financial closures for upcoming projects could provide upside.
*   **Biggest Structural Risk:** The primary structural risk is the highly regulated nature of aeronautical tariffs, which are subject to periodic review by the Airport Economic Regulatory Authority (AERA). Adverse regulatory rulings on tariff revisions or unexpected changes in the regulatory framework could impact profitability and return on capital. Additionally, geopolitical events, pandemics, or global economic slowdowns can significantly depress air travel demand, affecting passenger volumes and revenue across all segments. High debt levels and ongoing large-scale capex requirements also pose a financial risk if traffic growth falters.
