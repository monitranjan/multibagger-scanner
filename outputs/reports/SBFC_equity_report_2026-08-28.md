### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** SBFC Finance is a niche-focused Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC – a type of lender that is not a full bank but still gives loans; think of it as a specialised money-lending institution regulated by the RBI). The company’s moat rests on a secured-lending model in the small-ticket segment of ₹5 lakh to ₹30 lakh for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME – basically tiny factories, shops, and businesses). This addressable market is huge at ₹4.61 lakh crore (₹461,000 crore) and has grown at a 23% compound annual growth rate (CAGR – meaning it roughly doubled every 3+ years) between FY18–FY26 [^ip-latest]. SBFC’s AUM (Assets Under Management – the total pool of loans still outstanding that the company has given to borrowers, like the sum of all IOUs it holds) stood at ₹11,922 crore in Q1 FY27, making it a meaningful player in this under-served band [^ip-latest]. The moat is reinforced by collateral (property or gold) which creates high switching costs: a borrower who has pledged his shop title deed or gold ornaments cannot easily move to another lender without repaying, and SBFC’s local branch underwriting (judging borrower character) is hard to replicate [^cc-transcript].

- **Structural margin story:** The core profitability driver is the “spread” – the gap between the interest rate SBFC charges borrowers (yield) and the rate it pays to borrow from banks/NCDs (cost of funds). In Q1 FY27, yield on AUM was 17.90% while cost of borrowing fell ~90 bps (basis points – 1 bp = 0.01%, so 90 bps = 0.90%) YoY to 8.42%, expanding spread to 9.48% (+81 bps YoY) [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. Net Interest Margin (NIM – similar to spread but adjusted for average assets and cash holdings) was 10.68% [^ip-latest]. Crucially, Cost-to-AUM (operating expense as % of loans) dropped 30 bps YoY to 4.29%, showing operating leverage (as branches mature, each branch generates more loan volume without proportional cost) [^ip-latest]. ROA (Return on Assets – profit divided by total loans, a measure of efficiency) improved to 4.53% (+3 bps YoY) and ROE (Return on Equity – profit divided by shareholders’ funds, i.e., how hard the owners’ capital is working) rose 120 bps to 14.73% [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. This margin expansion is structural because cost of funds benefits from repo-rate transmission and the branch network is now 256-strong, up 41 YoY [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Rather than launching unrelated products, SBFC is deepening within its two secured verticals: Secured MSME (78% of AUM, ₹9,271 cr) and LAG (Loans Against Gold – lending money by taking gold jewellery as pledge; 21% of AUM, ₹2,631 cr) [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. Gold loan AUM grew 11% QoQ, a higher-yield book that uplifts overall portfolio yield [^cc-transcript]. Management explicitly stated “no new products” but intends to get better at existing ones as it scales from ₹10,000 cr to ₹20,000 cr AUM [^cc-transcript]. This disciplined adjacency growth reduces concentration risk while avoiding execution slippage [^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Branch expansion of only 10–15 new branches in FY27 (vs 52 last year) means prior additions turn productive, driving cost-to-AUM below 4% by year-end [^cc-transcript]. (ii) The RBI circular on MSME <₹20 lakh collateral hit co-origination (a joint lending model with banks) mixing it from ~20% to 10%; management expects normalisation to run-rate this quarter, boosting disbursals [^cc-transcript]. (iii) Credit rating intimation on 26 Aug 2026 under Reg 30 suggests possible rating upgrade, lowering borrowing cost further [^ar-fy25]. (iv) Liquidity buffer of ₹1,864 cr and CAR (Capital Adequacy Ratio – a safety cushion of own funds vs risk assets, here 32%) supports uninterrupted growth to ₹15,000 cr AUM [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The sub-₹10 lakh ticket segment shows signs of over-leverage (borrowers taking too many loans) and household inflation stress; login-to-disbursal (the ratio of loan applications to actual sanctioned loans) fell to 34% from 42%, implying tighter underwriting [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, 0+ delinquency (loans where borrower missed even one EMI payment) rose 70 bps as “homework” (management’s candid term for self-identified weakness) [^cc-transcript]. A prolonged macro slowdown or oil-shock induced rate volatility could pressure credit cost (currently 1.45% of avg AUM) beyond the guided 1.4–1.5% range [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

SBFC Finance operates as a tier-3/4 focused secured NBFC. Its core business model is simple to understand: it borrows money wholesale from banks, NCD issuances (Non-Convertible Debentures – a type of bond that pays fixed interest), and securitisation (selling pools of its loans to other lenders for upfront cash), then lends this money to small businesses and gold borrowers at a higher rate, pocketing the spread. The company does not manufacture anything; its “factory” is its 256-branch network across 201 cities, 18 states and 2 union territories [^ip-latest].

Revenue split by division (Q1 FY27 total income ₹492 cr): Interest Income on Loans ₹454 cr (92.3%), Fee & Other Income ₹32 cr (6.5%), Other Interest ₹6 cr (1.2%) [^ip-latest]. By AUM mix: Secured MSME (primarily Small Ticket LAP – Loan Against Property, where a businessman pledges his home or commercial property) 78%; LAG (Loans Against Gold) 21%; Others 1% [^ip-latest]. Key end customers are micro-entrepreneurs (kirana store owners, small workshop operators) and gold-owning households needing working capital. Subsidiary structure is lean: the holding entity SBFC Holdings Pte Ltd (Singapore) is the promoter vehicle, with SBFC Employee Welfare Trust handling ESOPs (Employee Stock Ownership Plan – a scheme giving staff shares) [^ar-fy25]. Promoter background: Aseem Dhru (Exec Vice Chairman) and Mahesh Dayani (MD & CEO) have delivered 30 quarters of steady AUM growth, emphasising an “anti-fragile” approach (a term meaning the company benefits from disorder by being conservative) [^cc-transcript]. Group context: the firm listed recently and has seen promoter stake dilution from 64% (Sep 2023) to ~52% (Jun 2026) via secondary sales, yet public institutions (DIIs) rose to 20.7% [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Total Addressable Market (TAM – the full potential size of the niche SBFC serves) for the ₹5–30 lakh MSME secured loan segment is ₹4.61 lakh crore (approx $55 billion at 83 INR/USD), growing at 23% CAGR FY18–FY26 [^ip-latest]. Policy tailwinds include RBI priority-sector classification for small MSME credit, though a recent RBI circular restricting banks from taking collateral for <₹20 lakh MSME loans temporarily disrupted co-origination channels [^cc-transcript]. No PLI (Production Linked Incentive) or FAME (EV subsidy) relevance; however, financial inclusion infra capex indirectly aids branch reach.

Competitive moat analysis:
- Switching costs: **Moderate** – borrowers pledge collateral, but local moneylenders offer faster cash; SBFC’s documented underwriting builds stickiness [^cc-transcript].
- Scale: **Moderate** – 256 branches is smaller than large NBFCs but dense in target geographies [^ip-latest].
- Pricing power: **Strong** – spread held >9% despite competitor “birds on badminton court” (management’s analogy for chaotic pricing), proving ability to walk away from unprofitable loans [^cc-transcript].
