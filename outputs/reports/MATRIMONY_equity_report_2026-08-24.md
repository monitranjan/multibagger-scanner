### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Matrimony.com is India’s #1 online matchmaking company with the highest pan-India market share and dominant position in Southern India [^ip-latest]. Its flagship BharatMatrimony (launched 2000, 17 regional sites) and CommunityMatrimony (300+ community-specific sites including IIMIITMatrimony and DoctorsMatrimony) create a deep “moat” — a term we use for a durable competitive advantage that protects profits like a water-filled ditch protects a castle. The moat is strong because users build multi-year profiles, families trust the brand across generations, and switching to a rival means losing established connections and paid tenure. In simple words, once a bride’s family uploads verified biodata and pays for a 12-month plan, they rarely migrate to a new app. This lock-in is reinforced by vernacular reach (Jodii in 9 languages) and offline retail presence of 120+ stores [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin story:** The core matchmaking division delivered EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization — a proxy for operating profit excluding non-cash and financing items) margin of 26.9% in Q1 FY27, up sharply from 17.6% a year ago [^cc-transcript]. To explain to a beginner: if the company earns Rs.100 of revenue from matchmaking, it now keeps Rs.26.9 as core operating profit versus Rs.17.6 earlier — a 930 basis points (bps) jump, where 100 bps = 1% point. The improvement is driven by operating leverage (spreading fixed tech and admin costs over more subscribers) and disciplined marketing: ex-marketing margin reached 63% in Q1 FY27 [^cc-transcript]. Average Transaction Value (ATV — the average amount a paid subscriber spends per package) rose to Rs.5,032 in FY26 from Rs.4,379 in FY24, showing customers are buying higher-value Elite and Assisted plans [^ip-latest]. This structural uplift in profitability is the central reason we are constructive.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Management is building a “relationship and wedding ecosystem” beyond pure matchmaking (which is 99% of revenue) [^ip-latest]. New launches include Luv.com (serious relationships app with 100% verified profiles), MeraLuv.com (Indian-American dating), ManyJobs (frontline jobs platform with >1.5 mn seekers in TN), RainbowLuv (LGBTQIA+), MatchAstro (astrology), and MakeMyWedding [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. WeddingBazaar (100k+ vendors, 40+ cities) and Mandap (venue booking) are shifting from subscription to commission-led asset-light models, meaning the company earns a fee per transaction without owning inventory [^cc-transcript]. These adjacencies address younger demographics and diaspora, creating optionality for revenue beyond the matrimony-only TAM.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The CEO guided double-digit YoY billing and revenue growth for Matchmaking in Q2 FY27, with PAT (Profit After Tax — net profit) expected to grow triple-digit YoY and be at similar or slightly better level than Q1’s Rs.19.1 cr [^cc-transcript]. The company completed a Rs.58.5 cr share buyback in FY26, signaling capital discipline [^ar-fy25]. Legal overhang from Google Play Store billing fee dispute got a Madras HC breather, and community expects normalization to lift PAT toward Rs.65–70 cr annually [^vp-thread]. Additionally, the Rs.4 cr strategic investment in Bharat Ek Khoj (AI astrology) could yield product synergies [^cc-transcript]. These events should re-rate the stock over the next four quarters.

- **Biggest structural risk:** The model faces two intertwined threats: (a) platform dependency on Google Play Store where commissions of 11–26% on subscriptions can compress margins if litigation fails [^vp-thread][^cc-transcript]; (b) competitive intensity from free-chat models (e.g., rival Jeevansathi) that erode the paid subscription logic, and unauthorized tier-2/3 city brokers scraping profiles, undermining rural monetization [^vp-thread]. If ATV growth stalls and marketing must be stepped up to defend share, the impressive 26.9% EBITDA margin could revert toward historical teens, which is the key bear case we monitor.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Matrimony.com operates an online matchmaking and marriage-services platform. Think of it as a “digital shaadi bureau” where individuals or families create profiles, pay for access to contact details, and use assisted relationship managers for premium matchmaking. The core business model is a B2C subscription: users buy 3–12 month plans, revenue is recognized ratably over the period (explaining the billing-revenue gap of Rs.5.5 cr in Q1 FY27) [^cc-transcript].

Revenue split by division (Q1 FY27): Matchmaking Services 99.2% (Rs.1,295 Mn) and Marriage Services & Others 0.8% (Rs.10 Mn) [^ip-latest]. Within matchmaking, brands are segmented as BharatMatrimony (pan-India vernacular), CommunityMatrimony (caste/community niches like Brahmin, Chettiar, IIT/IIM alumni), Elite Matrimony (high-net-worth, airport kiosks), Assisted Matrimony (RM-led), Jodii (value, 9 languages), and Techie/RainbowLuv specialized [^ip-latest]. End customers are individual marriage-seekers and their families; there are no B2B OEMs as in auto components.

Subsidiary structure: The company is Chennai-headquartered (TVH Beliciaa Towers, MRC Nagar) with associate Astro-Vision (minority stake, small loss) and strategic startup investment Bharat Ek Khoj [^cc-transcript][^ar-fy25]. Manufacturing footprint is irrelevant (services), but physical presence includes 120+ retail stores, 140+ historical matrimony outlets, offices in 3 international cities, and Elite airport kiosks [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. Promoter group is led by Chairman & MD Murugavel Janakiraman (26 years domain leadership) with wife Deepa Murugavel on board; the group context is a first-generation internet entrepreneur family with zero pledged shares [^ar-fy25].
