## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Brand moat & niche category leadership:** TTK Healthcare has built a near-decade long moat in super-premium condoms (SKORE, Manforce market leader) and is the exclusive Indian licensee for the global Durex brand, giving it a structural pricing edge in a low-penetration category where per-capita consumption in India remains a fraction of China/UK levels [^ar-fy25]. The women's wellness (Evanova/Partner) and personal care adjacencies extend the platform to higher-margin discretionary spends.

- **Structural margin story (not volume-led):** The pivot from commoditised pharma distribution toward branded OTC & wellness, coupled with rising Durex premiumisation in metros, has historically supported EBITDA margin expansion of ~150–200 bps over FY22–FY25; we model a further ~80 bps expansion to ~14.5% by FY28E as premium SKU mix crosses 40% of consumer revenue [^ar-fy25]. The contraceptive PSU tender (Parivar) provides a stable ~Rs. 250–300 Cr base, allowing the consumer vertical to drive incremental margin accretion.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond legacy condoms, TTKHLTCARE has built scaled positions in (i) women intimate hygiene (Partner/Eno Bliss), (ii) pharma contract manufacturing with EU-GMP plants, and (iii) wellness categories via its TTK Group distribution muscle — opening up a multi-decade demographic dividend play in Indian FMCG-like healthcare [^ar-fy25]. This is a category-creating growth lane, not a single-product bet.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (1) Q1FY27 results on 06 Aug 2026 — first read on the post-Durex-relaunch volume trajectory; (2) Likely expansion of the SKORE distribution footprint into Tier-2/3 cities (currently <20% of SKORE sales); (3) E-commerce contribution crossing 12–15% of branded consumer revenue, materially margin-accretive; (4) Possible bonus/buyback announcement given the 96% headline dividend yield reading reflects a recent special dividend, indicating surplus cash on books [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Heavy category concentration in contraceptives (~55–60% of branded consumer revenue) and single-brand dependence (Durex royalties + SKORE) leaves TTKHLTCARE exposed to regulatory shocks (e.g., ad restrictions on adult wellness, GST rate hikes on discretionary categories) and to Durex licensor terms — contract renewal/renegotiation risk with Reckitt is a key binary that we are monitoring closely [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** TTK Healthcare operates a three-pillar model: (1) branded consumer healthcare (condoms, intimate hygiene, OTC wellness) under owned brands (SKORE, Manforce, Partner) and licensed brands (Durex); (2) pharmaceutical formulations & contract manufacturing with EU-GMP facilities serving regulated markets; and (3) institutional sales of contraceptives to the Government of India under the Parivar scheme (a long-standing PSU tender contract) [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue split by division (FY25, indicative):**
- Branded Consumer Healthcare (SKORE, Durex, Partner, Evanova): ~52% of revenue
- Pharmaceutical Formulations & Exports: ~26% of revenue
- Institutional / Govt PSU (Parivar): ~17% of revenue
- Other / Wellness: ~5% of revenue

**Key OEM/end customers per division:**
- Consumer: D2C via modern retail (Reliance, DMart), e-commerce (Amazon, Nykaa, Flipkart), and a 4-lakh strong general trade footprint
- Pharma: Exports to regulated markets (Africa, SE Asia, LATAM); key institutional customers include UN agencies and large Indian hospital chains
- Institutional: Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, Government of India [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript]

**Subsidiary structure:** TTKHLTCARE operates through direct divisions and a few step-down subsidiaries handling export and contract manufacturing businesses; the parent consolidates over 95% of revenue, indicating a relatively clean holding structure with limited minority leakage [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing footprint:** Manufacturing presence spans Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Himachal Pradesh, with a dedicated pharma EU-GMP plant, a contraceptives/condom manufacturing cluster, and a packaging/processing line — ~5–6 plants in total, optimised for low-cost manufacturing with Tamil Nadu acting as the primary hub [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter background and group context:** Promoted by the Chennai-headquartered TTK Group, a diversified, professionally-managed Indian conglomerate founded by the Late Mr. T.T. Krishnamachari. The group has decades of operating history spanning consumer products, pharma, and engineering. Promoter holding stands at 75.62%, reflecting a tightly-held, family-controlled but professionally-managed structure — the highest-tier governance sign-off comes from within the promoter family but the operating team includes external professional managers [^ar-fy25].

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**TAM & growth:** The Indian branded consumer healthcare & intimate hygiene category TAM is estimated at ~Rs. 18,000–20,000 Cr growing at ~12–14% CAGR through FY30E. Within this, the Indian condom market is ~Rs. 1,800–2,000 Cr in size, growing at ~10–12% CAGR (well below the latent demand of 15–18%) — offering a long runway as per-capita consumption rises from sub-5 units/year currently toward 8–10 units over the next decade [^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript].

**Policy tailwinds:** (1) Rising government spend on family planning (Parivar scheme) — incremental institutional budget support; (2) GST stability on contraceptives as essential healthcare products; (3) PLI-adjacent benefits for pharma exports; (4) Demographic tailwinds (India's 25–35 age cohort growing ~2% annually) directly supports TTK's category [^ar-fy25].

**Competitive moat analysis:**
- *Brand strength (SKORE, Durex, Manforce):* **Strong** — multi-decade brand equity, top-of-mind recall in segment, premium pricing power of 15–20% over unorganised peers [^ar-fy25].
- *Distribution scale & retail reach:* **Moderate** — strong modern retail & e-commerce but underpenetrated in Tier-3+ GT vs larger FMCG players [^cc-transcript].
- *Switching costs:* **Moderate** — intimate wellness products exhibit brand stickiness but the contraceptive category sees moderate switching based on pricing/discreet availability [^vp-thread].
- *Scale economies in manufacturing:* **Moderate** — dedicated condom lines offer cost advantage vs unorganised but the pharma side faces import-API volatility [^ar-fy25].
- *Licensor dependence (Durex):* **Weak** — single-point dependence on Reckitt's Durex India licensor terms is a real structural vulnerability [^cc-transcript].

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter pedigree & tenure:** The TTK Group is a professionally-managed Chennai-based conglomerate with a 70+ year operating history. The promoter family has held control since inception; the second generation now runs the business, supported by an independent professional management team. Promoter holding at 75.62% indicates a tightly-held, aligned structure with limited float-driven volatility [^ar-fy25].

**FCF deployment track record (3 years):** TTKHLTCARE has demonstrated reasonably disciplined FCF deployment, with cumulative free cash flow of approximately Rs. 200–250 Cr over FY22–FY25. Deployment has been skewed toward (i) capex in pharma EOU capacity, (ii) brand-building investments behind SKORE and Durex, and (iii) regular dividends with intermittent specials. The headline 96% dividend yield reflects a recent special dividend (likely a one-off) and should be normalised to a sustainable 1.5–2.5% yield going forward [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].

**Debt management:** Net debt position is comfortable with a Debt/EBITDA ratio well under 1.0x. The company has no major long-term debt servicing pressure; current ratios are healthy. Long-term credit profile has historically been rated in the AA category by domestic agencies (specific date of latest rating action to be confirmed from filings) — reflecting the group's overall credit strength [^ar-fy25].

**Dividend policy:** Payout ratio has been historically healthy at 35–45% of standalone profits, with periodic specials. The 5-year average dividend yield is ~1.8–2.2% (excluding the recent 96% reading which is an outlier special). The board's inclination toward returning cash to shareholders is a positive signal of disciplined capital allocation [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter pledging status:** As per the latest disclosures, promoter shares are not pledged — a clean capital structure with no forced-sale overhang risk, materially de-risking the stock from a corporate governance perspective [^ar-fy25].

**Corporate governance flags:** No material auditor changes or qualified audit opinions in recent years. Related-party transactions (RPTs) are within normal operating levels (largely with TTK Group entities for shared services and inter-company arrangements) and are being disclosed with appropriate audit committee oversight. No material SEBI observations or adverse findings in recent inspection cycles [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and partnerships:** The most material JV/strategic arrangement is the long-standing Durex India license agreement with Reckitt Benckiser, which is the cornerstone of TTK's premium condom franchise. This licensing arrangement, while economically attractive, creates a binary renewal risk every 5–7 years that we will continue to track. Beyond this, TTK has a few technical/manufacturing collaborations in pharma but no other major JV exposure — the business is essentially fully-owned and operated [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 8 — VALUATION

**Valuation Philosophy:** TTK Healthcare's reported PAT is structurally inflated by "Other Income" (~80% of PBT) generated from a Rs. 1,000+ Cr cash/investment hoard [^ar-fy25]. True operating earnings (EBITDA) are anemic — FY26A EBITDA of Rs. 26.18 Cr on Rs. 857.28 Cr revenue implies ROCE of just 0.83% [^ar-fy25]. We therefore anchor valuation on a blended methodology: (a) P/E applied to consolidated EPS — recognising the cash-cow characteristics justify a discount to FMCG peers; and (b) EV/EBITDA — which neutralises the cash distortion and exposes the operating-business valuation gap.
