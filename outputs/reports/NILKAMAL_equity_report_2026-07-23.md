### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Moat in B2B Material Handling & Moulded Furniture Leadership:** Nilkamal commands dominant share in India's organised plastic moulded furniture category with the flagship "Cleopatra" / "Softech" brands and a near-duopoly position in bulk polymer crates/pallets/bins, supplying large institutional customers across FMCG, auto, e-commerce and organised retail (BigBasket, Reliance Retail, Future Group, Coca-Cola bottlers) [^ar-fy25]. The combination of moulded-furniture brand equity built over 40+ years, pan-India mould manufacturing capability, and a fleet of 2,000+ SKUs in crates/pallets creates meaningful entry barriers for unorganised players [^ip-latest].

- **Structural Margin Recovery Underway (250–350 bps runway):** Consolidated EBITDA margin compressed from ~12% (FY10) to ~7.5–8% (FY24-25) on account of (a) @Home retail losses, (b) muted material handling volumes, and (c) high PP/HDPE raw material prices [^vp-thread]. With @Home crossing break-even in FY25, soft polymer prices, and operating leverage kicking in as material-handling volumes revive alongside GDP/industrial capex, we model a 250–350 bps EBITDA margin expansion over FY25-28E to ~10.5–11.0% [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into High-Growth Consumer Adjacencies:** Beyond the core 80% (Furniture + Material Handling), Nilkamal is building four growth engines — (a) Nilkamal Sleep (mattress launch gaining traction), (b) Nilkamal Home Ideas (DIY home organisation sold via modern trade and e-commerce), (c) the @Home retail network of 60+ large-format stores (modular kitchens + home furniture), and (d) exports to the Middle East, Africa and the US (currently 8–10% of revenues, target 15% by FY28E) [^ar-fy25]. Each of these carries 20%+ growth potential and is margin-accretive once scaled.

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** (1) Union Budget / capex revival driving industrial material-handling demand, (2) festive + wedding season boost for moulded furniture and mattresses, (3) PLI-led auto and consumer-durables component sourcing benefiting moulded plastics volume, (4) sustained decline in crude/PP prices supporting gross margins, (5) potential re-rating as @Home contributes profits in FY27E and de-merger optionality of the retail vertical is considered [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** Cyclicality of the material-handment business (40–45% of revenue), which is highly correlated to industrial capex, FMCG volumes, and automotive dispatches; a sharp slowdown in these or a spike in crude/HDPE/PP prices before pass-through could compress gross margins by 150–200 bps and stall the operating leverage thesis [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]. The @Home retail business, while turning profitable, remains capital-intensive with a long break-even gestation and could again turn loss-making if discretionary consumption slows.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Nilkamal Limited is India's largest integrated plastic products company, operating across moulded furniture, material-handling solutions (crates, pallets, bins, dunnages), mattresses, and organised home-retail under the @Home banner. The company is a polymer conversion play — converting polypropylene (PP) and high-density polyethylene (HDPE) into finished products sold through dealers, modern trade, e-commerce, and own retail [^ip-latest].

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25E – illustrative, based on disclosures):**
- Moulded Furniture: ~45% of revenue (consumer-facing, B2C, dealer + e-com)
- Material Handling: ~35% of revenue (B2B crates, pallets, bins for FMCG/auto/retail logistics)
- @Home (Retail / Home Interiors): ~12–14% of revenue
- Mattresses (Nilkamal Sleep) + Home Ideas + Others: ~6–8% of revenue [^ar-fy25]

**Key OEM / End Customers (Division-wise):**
- *Moulded Furniture:* Mass-market consumers via 5,000+ dealer network; modern trade (Reliance Smart, D-Mart, Home Centre); Amazon, Flipkart, Pepperfry [^ip-latest].
- *Material Handling:* BigBasket, Flipkart (Myntra/Jabong) logistics, Hindustan Unilever, Coca-Cola bottlers, Maruti Suzuki ancillary chain, IndianOil, ITC, Britannia, Parle, Future Group supply chain [^ar-fy25].
- *@Home:* Walk-in consumers in 12 Tier-1/2 cities (60+ stores); sells modular kitchens, wardrobes, sofas under private labels [^ip-latest].
- *Exports:* Middle East, Africa, the US for material handling crates and select furniture SKUs [^ar-fy25].

**Subsidiary Structure:**
- Nilkamal Storage Systems Pvt Ltd (parent-level material-handling operations)
- Nilkamal Lifestyle Real Estate Pvt Ltd (retail vehicle for @Home)
- Nilkamal Foundation (CSR arm)
- Step-down / joint subsidiaries handling mattress and Home Ideas operations [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint:** 15+ plants across India including Maharashtra (Tarapur, Vasai, Bhiwandi — large moulded furniture hubs), Gujarat, Tamil Nadu (Sriperumbudur), Karnataka, Himachal Pradesh (Baddi), Uttarakhand, Andhra Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh, with mould-making tooling facilities in-house — giving Nilkamal a structural cost and customisation moat vs. trade-only peers [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** Promoted by the late Mr Vamanrai V. Parekh (founder, 1985) and now led by the second generation — Mr Manish V. Parekh (Chairman & MD) and Mr Nayan S. Parekh (Executive Director) — alongside other family members. The promoter group is closely held (~70% via ELCOT Investments and direct family holding). The Vamanrai Parekh group is widely regarded as a conservative, professionally-run Indian industrial family with a long history in plastics and a reputation for clean governance and zero pledging [^vp-thread] [^ar-fy25].
