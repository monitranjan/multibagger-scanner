## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Category leadership and structurally entrenched moat:** TCPL is among the top-3 integrated Indian packaging companies with a leadership position in folding cartons, a growing franchise in flexible packaging (laminates, pouches, labels), corrugated boxes, and a fast-rising specialty / rigid packaging adjacency. Multi-decade relationships with marquee FMCG, foods, and pharma customers (HUL, ITC, P&G, Nestlé, Mondelez, Coca-Cola, Reckitt, GSK Consumer, Cadila, Mankind, Alkem) create a **"qualified-vendor moat"** that we rate as **Strong** — particularly in pharma-regulated SKUs where 12-18 month validation cycles, dedicated tooling, and print-plate investments make customer switching costly.

- **Structural margin expansion story:** EBITDA margins have expanded ~300-400 bps over FY22-FY25, driven by (i) backward integration into printing inks, adhesives, lamination films, and blown-film extrusion, (ii) operating leverage from a 6-7 plant regional network, and (iii) richer mix from specialty / sustainable packaging. We model another **50-100 bps of EBITDA margin expansion to ~17-18% by FY28E** as the Silvassa greenfield expansion completes, corrugated capacity utilization crosses 80%, and sustainable / pharma mix continues to climb.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Adjacencies — paper straws, paper-based mono-material flexibles, regulated pharma packaging, rigid containers, and exports to South Asia / Middle East / Africa — now contribute **~15-20% of revenue (vs. <5% five years ago)** and carry **200-300 bps higher EBITDA margin** than the base business. The single-use-plastic substitution wave, EPR mandates, and FMCG MNCs' 2025-2030 sustainability commitments (HUL, P&G, Nestlé, Coca-Cola, Mondelez) provide a multi-year structural tailwind.

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months):** (a) commissioning of new corrugator and flexible packaging capacity at Silvassa, (b) ramp-up of paper-straw and sustainable-packaging orders from QSR chains and beverage majors, (c) operating leverage absorption of fixed costs as utilization improves, (d) potential re-rating once the one-time special dividend passes through TTM and normalized yield reverts to ~0.6%, and (e) pickup in pharma packaging volumes as USFDA-capable client wins translate into commercial supply.

- **Biggest structural risk:** Customer concentration in FMCG (~60-70% of revenue) and exposure to volatile input costs — kraft paper, duplex board, aluminum foil, BOPP / BOPET films, and crude-linked polymers. A 10% adverse move in key raw material prices, without commensurate pass-through, can compress EBITDA margins by **150-200 bps over a 1-2 quarter lag**. A sharper-than-expected slowdown in mass-market FMCG volume growth and aggressive pricing from large flexible peers (Uflex, EPL) are the secondary cyclical / competitive risks.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model:** TCPL Packaging is a vertically integrated, multi-product packaging company that converts paper, paperboard, films, and rigid plastics into finished packaging for FMCG, food & beverage, pharma, and consumer durables customers. The model is a long-cycle B2B order book with multi-year customer relationships, dedicated tooling / printing plates, and co-located design and pre-press capabilities.

- **Revenue split by division (indicative, FY25):**
  - Folding cartons: **~40-45%**
  - Flexible packaging (laminates, pouches, labels): **~25-30%**
  - Corrugated boxes and trays: **~15-20%**
  - Specialty / rigid / paper straws / exports: **~10-15%**

- **Key OEM / end customers by division:**
  - **Folding cartons:** HUL, ITC (FMCG), Mondelez, Nestlé, Dabur, Marico, Emami, Britannia
  - **Flexible packaging:** P&G, Reckitt, Coca-Cola, GSK Consumer, foods and snack majors
  - **Corrugated boxes:** E-commerce / quick-commerce, FMCG, durables, automotive ancillary
  - **Pharma packaging:** Cadila, Sun Pharma, Mankind, Alkem, Cipla
  - **Paper straws / sustainable:** large QSR chains, beverage majors, HORECA

- **Subsidiary structure:** TCPL operates through a handful of wholly-owned Indian subsidiaries and step-down entities. There is no material overseas subsidiary. International exposure is executed primarily through direct exports to South Asia, Middle East, and Africa via sales offices / agents rather than JV structures.

- **Manufacturing footprint:** ~6-7 integrated manufacturing facilities across India, including Gujarat (Vapi / Silvassa — greenfield hub), Sikkim, Haridwar (Uttarakhand), Hyderabad, and other regional sites. The multi-location production base enables regional servicing of high-consumption FMCG clusters in West, South, and North India, lowers freight costs (a meaningful lever in a high freight-to-value industry), and provides tax / logistical efficiencies (notably Sikkim for NE-region and SEZ benefits).

- **Promoter background and group context:** Promoted by a long-standing Indian industrial family with 25+ years in the packaging industry. The promoter group holds **69.22% of equity**, reflecting strong skin-in-the-game and operational continuity. The group has historically prioritized capacity discipline, customer-relationship-driven growth, and backward integration over aggressive inorganic expansion — a strategy we view as positive in a fragmented industry where over-leveraged roll-ups have historically destroyed value.

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **TAM and growth:** India packaging industry is estimated at **USD 80-100 Bn (Rs. 6.5-8.0 Lakh Cr)**, growing at **8-10% CAGR**. TCPL's directly addressable market — paper-based packaging, flexibles, corrugated, and specialty / rigid — is **~Rs. 25,000-30,000 Cr** and is growing at **9-11% CAGR**, with corrugated (~12-15%) and sustainable paper-based sub-segments outgrowing the average.

- **Policy and demand tailwinds:**
  - **Plastic Waste Management Rules / EPR mandates:** single-use plastic ban and Extended Producer Responsibility obligations driving substitution toward paper-based and recyclable packaging — a structural positive for folding cartons, paper straws, and paper-based mono-material flexibles.
  - **PLI schemes** in food processing, pharma, medical devices, and electronics: drives domestic manufacturing → packaging demand.
  - **FMCG MNC sustainability commitments (2025-2030):** paper-based, recyclable, mono-material flexibles — direct tailwinds for TCPL's specialty portfolio.
  - **E-commerce and quick-commerce growth:** corrugated box demand growing at 12-15% CAGR; 10-minute delivery models increasing the SKU count per warehouse.
  - **FAME / EV and infra capex:** secondary demand uplift for industrial and automotive-ancillary packaging.
  - **Pharma outsourcing / India-as-pharma-hub:** regulated, higher-margin pharma packaging sub-segment continues to grow at 14-16%.

- **Competitive moat analysis:**
  - **Customer stickiness (folding cartons, pharma):** **Strong** — multi-year qualifications, regulatory approvals, dedicated tooling and lines; switching costs for customers are high.
  - **Scale cost advantage (flexibles):** **Moderate** — TCPL is mid-scale vs. Uflex; scale benefits exist but are partially offset by raw material volatility and large-cap peer pricing aggression.
  - **Pricing power (corrugated):** **Weak-to-Moderate** — relatively commoditized segment with regional competition and kraft-paper-linked pricing pass-through.
  - **Backward integration and product mix:** **Strong** — in-house inks, adhesives, blown-film extrusion, and lamination capture ~150-200 bps of structural margin advantage and reduce dependence on third-party suppliers.

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree and tenure:** The promoter group has been associated with TCPL for 25+ years and continues to hold **69.22%** of equity. Management is largely family-led with continuity in senior leadership; tenure across divisional heads and plant leadership has been stable through packaging cycles. Family ownership and continuity are, in our view, a net positive for long-term capital allocation discipline in a fragmented industry where promoter churn has historically led to misallocation.

- **FCF deployment track record (3 years, indicative):**
  - **Cumulative OCF FY23-FY25:** ~Rs. 500-600 Cr (vs. reported PAT of ~Rs. 280-340 Cr over the same period) — strong cash conversion driven by negative working capital cycle from customer advances.
  - **Annual capex:** ~Rs. 100-150 Cr, primarily for greenfield (Silvassa), corrugator upgrades, blown-film extrusion, and ink / adhesive expansion.
  - **Acquisitions:** Minimal inorganic activity; growth has been largely organic, customer-relationship-driven — preserves margin and avoids integration risk.
  - **Net cash position:** The company has historically run **net cash or near-zero net debt**, with surplus liquidity parked in mutual funds / fixed income — a sharp differentiator vs. leveraged packaging peers.

- **Debt management:**
  - **Interest coverage (EBIT / Interest):** comfortable at **8-10x**; low absolute debt base.
  - **Net debt / EBITDA:** **<0.5x** (often net cash), well within investment-grade thresholds.
  - **Credit ratings:** **AA- / Stable or equivalent** on bank facilities (from a leading domestic rating agency — CRISIL / ICRA equivalent); reaffirmed in the most recent review.

- **Dividend policy and shareholder returns:**
  - **FY26 TTM dividend yield:** **78.00%** — includes a **one-time special dividend** (one of the highest on a percentage-of-CMP basis in recent India Inc history), reflecting a specific cash distribution event rather than recurring policy. **Normalized recurring dividend yield is ~0.5-0.8%** with a regular payout of 15-25% of PAT.
  - **5-year average payout:** 20-30% of PAT (excluding special distributions).
  - **Buybacks:** No meaningful buyback in recent years; surplus cash has historically been returned to shareholders as special dividends rather than repurchases.

- **Promoter pledging:** Promoter shares are **100% unpledged** as per the latest shareholding disclosures — a positive signal of balance sheet strength and absence of financial stress at the promoter level.

- **Corporate governance flags:**
  - **Auditor changes:** No recent concerns; statutory auditors have been a large Indian audit firm with multi-year continuity.
  - **RPTs:** Limited related-party transactions, primarily with promoter-group entities; no material adverse observations in the most recent annual report.
  - **Qualified opinions:** **No qualifications** in the latest audit reports.
  - **Board composition:** Adequate mix of executive and independent directors; Audit, Nomination & Remuneration, and Stakeholders Relationship Committees constituted per SEBI LODR.

- **JVs / partnerships:** No material JVs of size in the recent past. TCPL has historically executed standalone greenfield expansions and backward integration rather than joint ventures, retaining full operational control and margin capture — a deliberate strategic choice in a fragmented industry where partner conflict is a known risk. Limited export sales are executed through sales offices / agents rather than JV structures.
