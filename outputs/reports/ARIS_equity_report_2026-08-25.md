### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Arisinfra has built what it calls an "orchestration layer" for India's highly fragmented construction materials supply chain, connecting 3,412+ customers with 2,229+ verified sourcing vendors across 1,000+ pin codes [^ip-latest]. To explain this in simple terms for a beginner: the construction industry in India is like a giant, messy neighbourhood bazaar where thousands of small suppliers sell sand, cement, steel, and bricks to builders who have no idea what the fair price is and no reliable way to get materials delivered on time. Arisinfra acts like the Uber or the organised aggregator of this chaos — it brings order, price transparency, and dependable logistics through software [^ar-fy25]. Its moat (a business term for a protective advantage that keeps competitors away, just like a wide water ditch protected medieval castles) comes from powerful network effects: more vendors on the platform attract more customers, and more customers attract more vendors, creating a self-reinforcing loop. On top of this, it owns a proprietary technology stack that digitises RFQs (Request-for-Quote — electronically asking many sellers "what is your best price?" instead of calling each one), logistics tracking, and an AI-driven credit assessment tool named "Cara AI" [^ip-latest]. The proof that this moat is real is the 82% repeat order rate in Q1-FY27 (up from 78% in Q4-FY26) — meaning customers keep coming back year after year rather than switching away [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin story:** The single most important reason we are positive on Arisinfra is the ongoing shift in its revenue mix away from low-margin pure trading toward higher-margin Contract Manufacturing (CM) and Developer-as-a-Service (DaaS) [^cc-transcript]. Let me explain EBITDA margin in plain language: EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, and Amortisation) is roughly the core operating profit — think of it as "the money left from sales after you pay for the raw materials and the direct cost of running operations, but before you pay bank interest or taxes." A 2% EBITDA margin means for every ₹100 of sales, only ₹2 is core profit. The company's B2B supply division carries a thin 2–2.5% EBITDA margin, while CM delivers a much healthier 9–9.5%, and DaaS (where Arisinfra manages entire real estate projects for a fee without owning the land) earns a stellar 55–60% EBITDA margin [^ip-latest]. Consolidated EBITDA margin expanded from 1.84% in FY24 to 9.43% in FY26, and reached 10.49% in Q1-FY27 (up 191 basis points year-on-year) [^ip-latest]. A basis point (often written as "bps") is one-hundredth of a percent, so 191 bps simply means a 1.91 percentage-point improvement. Management clearly stated in the concall that the CM+DaaS combined share moved from about 46% of revenue to 63% as of Q1-FY27, and this mix shift is the primary lever lifting group profitability [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Arisinfra is not standing still; it is aggressively expanding from its base of aggregates (crushed stone and sand used as the foundation material in construction) and RMC (Ready-Mix Concrete — concrete that is mixed in a factory and delivered ready to pour at the site, saving time and labour) into asphalt (the black road-grade topping material used for highways), tiles, CP fittings (Chrome-Plated taps and sanitary fixtures), sanitaryware, electricals, and plumbing supplies [^ip-latest]. The asphalt business is a standout: it scaled from ₹299 Mn in Q4-FY26 to ₹529 Mn in Q1-FY27, with transacting customers rising from 28 to 38 in just one quarter [^ip-latest]. Additionally, the DaaS pipeline includes a ₹6,500 Mn mandated contract from the Wadhwa Group, with GDV (Gross Development Value — the total expected sales value of a real estate project once completed) under execution at ₹18,391 Mn in Q1-FY27 versus ₹12,674 Mn in Q4-FY26 [^ip-latest]. In everyday terms, the company is moving from just supplying the bricks and cement to also managing the entire house-building project for a slice of the profit — a far higher-margin activity.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** Several concrete triggers should support the stock over the next year: (i) Continued capacity expansion in contract manufacturing from a ~9 Mn ton base to ~11 Mn tons annually, achieved via "recycling deposits" — meaning the company uses existing vendor advance funds rather than spending new shareholder capital [^cc-transcript]; (ii) The DaaS vertical is expected to contribute 9–11% of topline with 60–65% EBITDA margins and zero real estate risk, because Arisinfra is explicitly not a RERA promoter (RERA is the Real Estate Regulatory Authority; not being a "promoter" means it does not carry the legal land or building liability on its balance sheet) [^cc-transcript]; (iii) A ₹79.05 Cr GMLR Excavation Disposal order win announced in July 2026 triggered a +7% stock price reaction, signalling healthy order-book momentum [^vp-thread]; (iv) The subsidiary merger process is at its "last leg" with 3 of 4 regulators cleared, which will simplify the corporate structure and likely improve investor clarity [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The most serious vulnerability is extreme customer and geographic concentration combined with working-capital dependence. In Q1-FY27, Maharashtra alone contributed 65% of revenue, and the top 10 customers represent 45–50% of total business spread across various projects [^cc-transcript]. Furthermore, the B2B supply and CM model requires careful management of net working capital days (this is the number of days between when the company pays its suppliers and when it collects cash from its customers — think of it as how long its own money is "stuck" in the business cycle), which stood at 56 days [^ip-latest]. Although net debt/equity is a very low 0.02x, if large infrastructure or EPC (Engineering Procurement Construction — companies that win turnkey projects and build them end-to-end) customers delay payments, cash flow could strain [^ip-latest]. A sharp slowdown in Maharashtra real estate or a cut in state infrastructure spending would disproportionately hit Arisinfra relative to a more geographically diversified peer [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Arisinfra Solutions Limited operates as a tech-enabled construction materials and services company with an asset-light, network-driven model [^cc-transcript]. In plain language for a beginner investor: instead of spending billions to own cement factories or a fleet of trucks, the company uses software to connect two groups of people — those who need construction materials (real estate developers and contractors) and those who supply them (vendors and manufacturers) — and earns its margin on coordination, quality assurance, logistics tracking, and credit facilitation [^ar-fy25]. The company was incorporated in 2021 as Arisinfra Solutions Pvt. Ltd. in Mumbai, launched its digital platform to replace offline, paper-based buying, and by 2024 had scaled operations to 18+ states with over 10 million metric tonnes of materials delivered and zero manufacturing capex (Capex is Capital Expenditure — money spent on buying or building fixed assets like plants; "zero manufacturing capex" means it never built its own production facilities) [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue split by division (Q1-FY27):** The business is reported in three streams. Contract Manufacturing is 53% of revenue (₹1,540 Mn), B2B Supply is 37% (₹1,092 Mn), and Services/DaaS is 10% (₹277 Mn) [^ip-latest]. In CM, Arisinfra uses exclusive partner plants — meaning it reserves capacity at third-party factories rather than owning them — to produce RMC, Asphalt, and Aggregates. The reserved capacity breakdown is: 6.1 Mn tons across 6 aggregate plants, 2.7 Mn tons across 3 RMC plants, and 0.3 Mn tons in 1 asphalt plant [^ip-latest]. B2B Supply involves direct aggregation and doorstep delivery of steel, cement, tiles, electricals, plumbing, chemicals, and walling solutions such as AAC blocks (Autoclaved Aerated Concrete — a lightweight, factory-made building block that is easier to lift than traditional brick) [^ip-latest]. The Services vertical (DaaS) manages the full real estate project lifecycle for landowners and developers on a commercial model of fixed fee plus a percentage of construction cost plus a percentage of sales [^cc-transcript].

**Key customers per division:** The industry-wise revenue mix in Q1-FY27 shows Large Infra & EPC contractors at 58%, Real Estate Developers at 18%, Manufacturers at 17%, and Others at 7% [^ip-latest]. The most prominent named mandate is the Wadhwa Group ₹6,500 Mn DaaS contract [^ip-latest]. The group-wide repeat order rate of 82% indicates strong stickiness with both OEM-style (Original Equipment Manufacturer — here meaning large institutional buyers) and end-customers [^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary structure:** The company is in the process of simplifying its structure via a subsidiary merger, with management confirming on the concall that 3 of 4 required regulators have cleared the process and it is at the final stage [^cc-transcript]. The group context post-IPO is that of a unified operating company rather than a holding of disjointed units [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing footprint:** Although asset-light, Arisinfra controls effective production capacity through partner plants primarily serving Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu (22% of Q1-FY27 revenue), Karnataka (7%), and Others (6%) [^ip-latest]. Physical corporate and operational presence includes the Mumbai headquarters, plus Bangalore, Nashik, Noida, and Ranchi [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter background:** Chairman & Managing Director Ronak Morbia leads the strategic vision; the promoter group held 69.11% per the header data, with family member Dhanlaxmi Ramesh Morbia appearing in insider transaction disclosures (market sale of 200,000 shares at ₹102.31 in March 2026) [^ar-fy25]. The promoter group's stated ambition is to be the "orchestration layer" of Indian construction rather than a traditional supplier or contractor [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**TAM (Total Addressable Market):** India's construction material market in 2024 was approximately USD 216 Bn (roughly Rs. 1.8 lakh crore) split as follows: Steel USD 45 Bn, Cement & Concrete USD 69 Bn, and Other Materials USD 102 Bn [^ip-latest]. By 2029, this market is projected to expand to USD 724 Bn (around Rs. 6 lakh crore) — implying a CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate — the smoothed yearly growth rate over a period) of roughly 27% in USD terms, or a more conservative ~20%+ in Rupee terms allowing for currency movement [^ip-latest]. Critically, construction materials constitute 50–60% of total project cost, which makes procurement the single largest spend lever for any developer [^ip-latest].

**Policy tailwinds:** While there is no direct PLI (Production Linked Incentive — a government scheme that gives manufacturers cash rewards for incremental local production) for construction aggregates, the sector benefits enormously from sustained national infrastructure capex (union budget allocations to highways, railways, and smart cities), maturity of real estate regulation via RERA, and urbanisation driving Tier 1 and Tier 2 real estate demand [^ip-latest]. The deeply fragmented unorganised supply base — Aggregates are >80% unorganised, RMC is 55–60% unorganised — creates a massive substitution opportunity for an organised, tech-driven platform like Arisinfra [^ip-latest].

**Competitive moat analysis:**
- *Switching costs:* **Strong** — once a developer integrates Arisinfra's RFQ, logistics, and credit systems into daily project workflows, leaving means re-training procurement teams and losing years of price history; the 82% repeat rate is hard evidence of this lock-in [^ip-latest].
- *Scale:* **Moderate** — a network of 3,412 customers and 2,229 vendors is sizable, though private competitor Infra.Market is larger; however, Arisinfra's asset-light model means scale does not require proportional capital deployment, which is a structural advantage [^ip-latest].
- *Pricing power:* **Weak-to-Moderate** — in B2B supply, margins are thin (2–2.5%) because commodities like steel and cement have publicly quoted exchange prices, leaving little room to mark up. Pricing power strengthens materially in CM (9–9.5%) and DaaS (55–60%) where service quality and project execution differentiate the offering [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript].

Net debt/equity is a mere 0.02x, and net working capital days improved dramatically from 120 in FY24 to 66 in FY25 to 56 in Q1-FY27 [^ip-latest]. This disciplined, variable-cost model means cash is recycled into vendor deposits (to reserve plant capacity) rather than sunk into fixed factories, preserving financial flexibility [^cc-transcript].

**Debt management:** Interest coverage is strong given the low absolute debt load. Credit risk is managed through the "Cara AI" tool, keeping ECL (Expected Credit Loss — the estimated provision for bad debts) at approximately ₹22 Cr on ~₹3,800–4,000 Cr of lifetime revenue (about 0.5%), and this exposure is insured [^cc-transcript]. No external credit rating agency disclosure appears in the provided documents, but the near-zero leverage signals a balance sheet strength comparable to investment-grade borrowers [^ar-fy25].

**Dividend policy:** The company has paid no dividend (Div Yield 0.00%). As a growth-stage, asset-light platform, it rationally retains all earnings to fund network and category expansion [^ip-latest]. The 5-year average payout ratio is therefore 0% [^ar-fy25].

Insider data shows ESOP grants to employees at ₹2 face value and a minor promoter-family market sale, but no pledged shares [^ar-fy25].

**Corporate governance flags:** No auditor changes, no adverse RPT (Related Party Transaction — a deal with an entity linked to promoters or management) flags, and no qualified opinions in the independent auditor's report excerpt [^ar-fy25]. **JVs or partnerships:** The core strategic partnership model is exclusive partner plants for Contract Manufacturing (capacity reserved, not owned) [^ip-latest]. The Wadhwa Group DaaS mandate (₹6,500 Mn) is a landmark partnership proving the Services vertical at scale [^ip-latest].
