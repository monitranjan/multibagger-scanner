### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Regulatory Duopoly Moat with Network Effects:** CDSL operates in a SEBI-licensed duopoly alongside NSDL, holding ~70% of India's 19.2 Cr demat accounts (vs. NSDL's ~30%). Every IPO, rights issue, mutual fund folio, and corporate action in CDSL's books creates stickiness — switching costs for participants (brokers, AMCs) are near-prohibitive due to integrated back-office systems, KYC records, and settlement infrastructure. This is one of the strongest regulatory moats in Indian financial services.

- **Asset-Light, High-Incidence Margin Architecture:** EBITDA margins sustained at 58–62% over FY21–FY24, with ROCE of 31.05% and ROE of 23.22% — among the highest in Indian capital markets. Revenue scales on demat account additions (~2.0 Cr net adds in FY24), annual maintenance fees (Rs. 200–500/pa), and transaction-based charges, with negligible incremental capex. Operating leverage is structurally favourable: every 1 Cr new accounts adds ~Rs. 50–60 Cr in annualised revenue at >80% incremental margin.

- **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacent Verticals:** Beyond core depository, CDSL has built four fintech rails — (i) CDSL eKYC (~3.5 Cr verifications/month), (ii) CDSL eSign (digital signature), (iii) CDSL eLocker (digital document vault, 1.2 Cr+ users), and (iv) CDSL Online Exams (online proctoring for NISM/NCFM). Combined non-core revenue grew from Rs. 78 Cr in FY22 to Rs. 142 Cr in FY24 (CAGR 35%) and is targeted at Rs. 220 Cr by FY27E — providing optionality without diluting the core thesis.

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** (a) T+1 settlement cycle implementation (Apr 2023) has increased per-account transaction velocity by ~18%; (b) GIFT City IFSC depository license expected to unlock NRI/foreign investor flows (currently addressable market ~$2.5 Bn); (c) Account aggregator (AA) framework live since 2022 — CDSL Ventures subsidiary positioned as a leading AA; (d) FY25 dividend visibility strong at Rs. 22–24 DPS (90% payout policy reaffirmed); (e) SEBI's discussion paper on fractional ownership/REITs could expand addressable asset classes.

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** Concentration risk in the NSDL duopoly is the primary tail risk. Any SEBI decision to license a third depository (proposed in 2021 discussion paper, dormant since) would compress pricing power. Secondary risks: (a) regulatory fee caps on annual maintenance charges (currently unregulated but under SEBI review); (b) technology disruption — blockchain-based settlement rails (RBI's CBDC pilot, Project Nexus) could disintermediate traditional depositories over a 7–10 year horizon; (c) demat account growth deceleration — FY24 net adds of 2.0 Cr vs. FY23's 2.7 Cr suggests saturation in Tier-1/Tier-2 cities.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** CDSL is a SEBI-registered Depository under the Depositories Act, 1996, providing post-trade infrastructure for securities — equity, debt, mutual funds, ETFs, and corporate bonds. Revenue is generated from three streams: (i) Issuer services (one-time listing fees, annual custody charges), (ii) Participant/Business Correspondent services (annual maintenance, transaction, settlement fees), and (iii) Online data dissemination and ancillary services. The model is asset-light, with revenue per employee of ~Rs. 1.4 Cr — among the highest in Indian financial services.

#### Monitoring Watch-Points (Even on GREEN Items)

- **Metric 1 — Revenue Recognition:** Monitor SEBI circulars on fee rationalisation; any shift to T+1 settlement or fee-cap could compress recognition timing. Watch the FY27 annual report for any change in BO conversion fee accounting policy.
- **Metric 2 — Receivables:** FY26 saw receivables grow ~22% YoY vs revenue growth of 5.8% — minor uptick, but still within tolerance. Flag if debtor days exceed 30 days in any quarter.
- **Metric 3 — CCC:** Range is tight; any breach above 35 days would warrant deeper investigation into AMC collection efficiency from DP (Depository Participant) channel.
- **Metric 4 — Contingent Liabilities:** Track quarterly — any new SEBI penalty, cyber-incident claim, or tax demand above Rs. 25 Cr would be a RED trigger.
- **Metric 5 — Auditor Tenure:** Confirm next rotation cycle in FY27 AGM disclosures; any auditor change post-rotation should be evaluated for continuity of accounting policies.
- **Metric 6 — Other Income:** Largest non-operating lever. A 100 bps cut in repo rate would compress treasury yields by ~Rs. 10–12 Cr annually. Watch RBI policy stance.
- **Metric 7 — Tax Rate:** Stable, but monitor any change in Section 80-IAC tax holiday status or MAT applicability if CDSL's investment subsidiary structure evolves.
- **Metric 8 — RPT:** With zero promoter holding, RPT risk is structurally low. However, BSE (legacy shareholder with residual board representation) continues as a counterparty — ensure all transactions remain at arm's length and board-approved.
