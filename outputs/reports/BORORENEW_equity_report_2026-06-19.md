### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

*   **Market Leadership & Moat:** Borosil Renewables (BRL) is India's sole manufacturer of solar glass, holding a near-monopoly in the domestic market with over 90% share. This leadership provides a significant competitive moat due to high capital intensity, stringent quality requirements, and long gestation periods for new entrants. The company benefits from 'Made in India' preference and anti-dumping duties on imported solar glass, ensuring sustained demand and pricing power.
*   **Structural Margin Story:** The company is undergoing a significant capacity expansion from 1000 TPD to 1550 TPD by FY27E, which, coupled with backward integration efforts and operational efficiencies, is expected to drive a structural improvement in EBITDA margins by 200-300 bps over the next 2-3 years. Increased economies of scale and better absorption of fixed costs will be key drivers.
*   **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** BRL is strategically diversifying its product portfolio beyond standard solar glass into high-value, niche segments like textured solar glass, anti-glare glass for airport applications, and ultra-thin glass for advanced PV modules. These products command higher margins and cater to evolving technological demands in the renewable energy sector, positioning BRL for future growth.
*   **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** Key catalysts include the commissioning of the new 550 TPD furnace by Q4FY27, which will significantly de-risk supply chain dependencies and boost production volumes. Additionally, the government's continued focus on domestic solar manufacturing through schemes like ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers) and PLI for solar modules will create a robust demand environment for BRL's products.
*   **Biggest Structural Risk:** The primary structural risk is the potential for a significant influx of cheap imported solar glass from China if anti-dumping duties are relaxed or removed. This could lead to severe pricing pressure and impact BRL's profitability and market share. Another risk is the rapid technological obsolescence in solar module design requiring frequent R&D investments.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Borosil Renewables Ltd. (BRL) is primarily engaged in the manufacturing and sale of solar glass, a critical component for photovoltaic (PV) modules and solar thermal applications. The company is India's pioneer and only manufacturer of solar glass.

*   **Core Business Model:** BRL's core business revolves around producing high-quality, low-iron textured solar glass for use in solar panels. This glass is designed to maximize light transmission and enhance the efficiency of solar modules. The business model is B2B, supplying directly to solar module manufacturers.
*   **Revenue Split by Division (% of Revenue):**
    *   Solar Glass: ~100% (BRL is a pure-play solar glass manufacturer).
*   **Key OEM/End Customers per Division:**
    *   **Solar Glass:** Major Indian solar module manufacturers such as Waaree Energies, Adani Solar, Vikram Solar, Goldi Solar, and other domestic and international module assemblers.
*   **Subsidiary Structure:** BRL has a wholly-owned subsidiary, Geosil Renewables Private Limited, established to explore and develop new opportunities in the renewable energy sector, particularly in advanced materials.
*   **Manufacturing Footprint:** BRL's sole manufacturing facility is located in Bharuch, Gujarat. This plant currently has a capacity of 1000 TPD (tonnes per day) and is undergoing expansion to 1550 TPD.
*   **Promoter Background and Group Context:** Borosil Renewables is part of the larger Borosil Group, which has a heritage of over six decades in India. The group is well-known for its consumer glassware (Borosil Glassware) and scientific/laboratory products (Borosil Scientific). The promoters, led by Mr. Pradip Kheruka (Chairman) and Mr. Ashok Kumar Jain (Whole-time Director), have a strong track record in manufacturing and have successfully steered BRL to become a market leader in solar glass, demonstrating foresight in entering the renewable energy sector early on. The group maintains a strong focus on quality, innovation, and sustainable practices.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

*   **TAM in Rs. Cr and USD, CAGR:** The global solar glass market size was estimated at approximately USD 6.5 billion in 2023 and is projected to grow at a CAGR of 15-18% to reach USD 15-18 billion by 2030. India's domestic solar glass market, currently estimated at Rs. 2,500-3,000 Cr, is expected to grow at a significantly higher CAGR of 20-25% over the next five years, driven by ambitious renewable energy targets and domestic manufacturing push.
*   **Relevant Policy Tailwinds:**
    *   **PLI Scheme for Solar PV Modules:** The Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme for solar PV modules (Tranche-I & II) incentivizes integrated domestic manufacturing, creating a captive demand for indigenous solar glass.
    *   **ALMM (Approved List of Models and Manufacturers):** This mandate requires government projects and projects availing government support to procure solar modules from ALMM-listed manufacturers, indirectly boosting demand for components from domestic suppliers like BRL.
    *   **Renewable Energy Targets:** India's target of 500 GW of non-fossil fuel energy capacity by 2030 necessitates massive solar power deployment, ensuring sustained demand for solar glass.
    *   **Customs Duties:** Imposition of basic customs duty (BCD) on imported solar modules and cells, and potentially on solar glass in the future, further supports domestic manufacturing.
*   **Competitive Moat Analysis:**
    *   **Switching Costs: Moderate.** While solar module manufacturers generally prefer consistent quality and established suppliers, switching glass suppliers is not prohibitively expensive if a new supplier offers significant cost or technological advantages. However, BRL's consistent quality and technical support create stickiness.
    *   **Scale: Strong.** BRL's current capacity and ongoing expansion provide significant economies of scale, allowing for cost-efficient production. The high capital expenditure required for solar glass manufacturing acts as a strong barrier to entry for new players.
    *   **Pricing Power: Moderate to Strong.** As the sole domestic manufacturer, BRL enjoys relatively strong pricing power. However, this is tempered by the availability of imported solar glass (albeit with duties) and the competitive nature of the global solar industry, which influences module pricing and, consequently, component pricing.
