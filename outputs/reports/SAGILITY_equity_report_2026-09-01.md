### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Sagility is a healthcare‑focused business process management (BPM) company – in simple words, it runs the back‑office, call‑centre and claims‑processing work for US health insurers (called “payers”) and hospitals (“providers”). It has been rated a “Major Contender 2026” in Healthcare Customer Experience Management (CXM – handling patient/member interactions) and Revenue Cycle Management (RCM – managing billing and claims cash flow) by Everest’s PEAK Matrix, and a “Leader” in Clinical & Care Management by Avasant [^ip-latest]. Its moat is the very high switching cost: moving critical functions like claims adjudication to another vendor requires regulatory re‑certification and risks costly errors, so clients stay sticky. The top three client groups alone contribute 60.1% of TTM revenue, and the company serves 109 active client groups with 47,307 employees across 29 delivery sites, giving it scale that smaller niche players cannot match [^cc-transcript].

- **Structural margin story:** Adjusted EBITDA (a measure of core operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) margin stood at 24.0% in Q1 FY27, flat year‑on‑year despite a statutory minimum‑wage hike that cost ~₹70m in‑quarter and an estimated ~120bps (1.2 percentage points) FY27 EBITDA impact [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. The structural offset is the onshore‑to‑offshore migration – meaning shifting work from high‑cost US locations to India centres – which management confirmed is “margin accretive in the general sense” because Indian salaries are lower for similar quality [^cc-transcript]. A foreign‑exchange tailwind of >100bps further protected margins, and the new AI‑led “Sagility Synchrony” orchestration platform is expected to lift efficiency without headcount cuts [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high‑growth adjacencies:** The June 2026 acquisition of CareSeed (a Kansas‑based analytics firm with ~95% recurring revenue and 31.4% EBITDA margin) adds two software platforms – “Forecast” (NCQA‑certified HEDIS quality reporting) and “Harvest” (cloud chart abstraction) – that bolt onto Sagility’s existing nurse‑led outreach and coding validation [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. This moves the firm from pure labour‑arbitrage BPM into higher‑value “quality‑of‑care” tech‑enabled services. CareSeed brought 26 new small/mid health‑plan clients, while the earlier BroadPath acquisition (fully integrated) has 6–7 cross‑sell opportunities in pipeline, expanding the mid‑market footprint beyond the top‑10 payer concentration [^cc-transcript][^ip-latest].

- **Near‑term catalysts (6–12 months):** In Q1 FY27 alone the company won $35.3m of steady‑state ACV (Annual Contract Value – the yearly recurring revenue from new/expanded deals), comprising 18 existing‑client expansions and 1 new logo [^ip-latest]. Net debt is only ₹1,110m (0.06x EBITDA) with ₹9,674m cash, and management is on track to fully repay debt in FY27, which should remove interest‑cost drag and lift reported ROE [^cc-transcript]. The promoter overhang from the PE‑structured selldown (stake slid from 82.4% to 50.95%) is stabilising, and any confirmation of promoter holding floor could re‑rate the stock from its current discount to peers [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Client concentration is extreme – top 3 groups = 60.1%, top 10 = 84.1% of TTM revenue – so the loss or insourcing of a single large payer would disproportionately hit topline [^cc-transcript]. Combined with 100% US‑client geographic concentration, this exposes Sagility to US political/regulatory shifts (e.g., Medicare Stars rule changes) and wage inflation that could revert margins toward pre‑IPO 21–23% levels if efficiency gains falter [^vp-thread]. A variant bear view also warns that margin uplift lately benefited from debt reduction rather than pure operations, posing reversion risk [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Sagility’s core business model is tech‑enabled healthcare operations (often called ITeS – Information Technology enabled Services). Instead of making physical products, it sells ongoing managed services: handling member calls, processing insurance claims, managing revenue cycles, and running quality‑care programs for US healthcare payers and providers. Think of it as the invisible engine room that keeps a US health insurer’s back office running smoothly.

Revenue split by division (Q1 FY27, sourced from investor presentation): Payer vertical (health insurers) = 89.6% of revenue (~₹17,593m), Provider vertical (hospitals/clinics) = 10.4% (~₹2,042m) [^ip-latest]. Within these, the service lines are Healthcare CXM (member experience), RCM (billing/claims), and Clinical & Care Management (HEDIS/CMS Stars gap closure). All Q1 revenue is “steady‑state” (recurring) with zero seasonal open‑enrolment revenue in the quarter, though TTM includes 5.9% seasonal [^ip-latest].

Key end‑customers are the large US payer groups; management stated Sagility is present in 7 of the top 10 US insurers for clinical/payment‑integrity services, with the top 3 client groups contributing 60.1% and top 5 contributing 70.1% of TTM revenue [^cc-transcript]. The company is not a consumer brand; it is a B2B partner.

Subsidiary structure: The parent is Sagility Limited (formerly Sagility India Limited), with operating subsidiaries including BroadPath (acquired prior year, fully integrated) and CareSeed (acquired effective 11 Jun 2026) [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. Manufacturing footprint is irrelevant; instead we note delivery footprint: 29 delivery sites at Q1 FY27 (down from 31 in FY26 due to consolidation), primarily in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and other Indian IT hubs, with a small US onshore presence that is being migrated offshore for margin [^cc-transcript].

Promoter background: The promoter entity is Sagility B.V., backed by private‑equity sponsors, which reduced holding from 82.39% in Mar‑2025 to 50.95% by Jun‑2026 via secondary sales (OFS/block deals) to FIIs/DIIs [^vp-thread]. The Indian operating pedigree is led by Managing Director & Group CEO Ramesh Gopalan, with Group CFO Srinivas R. Mattapalli, both with long healthcare‑BPM tenure [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The US healthcare administrative services market is enormous; estimated total addressable spend on payer/provider admin & BPM is ~$300bn (≈₹24.9 lakh Cr at ₹83/$) with the outsourced BPM/ITeS slice around $50–60bn (≈₹4.1–5.0 lakh Cr), growing at a mid‑single‑digit to 8% CAGR as payers seek cost‑discipline amid rising medical utilisation [^vp-thread]. Policy tailwinds are indirect: US regulators (CMS) push Stars‑rating and HEDIS quality metrics, forcing payers to outsource care‑gap closure; additionally, AI adoption budgets are rising, favouring scaled partners with domain expertise [^cc-transcript].

Competitive moat analysis (rated Strong/Moderate/Weak):
- **Switching costs:** Strong – claims processing requires NCQA/HEDIS compliance; re‑platforming risks audit failures and litigation, so clients rarely churn [^vp-thread].
- **Scale:** Strong – 47,307 heads, 29 sites, $200m+ quarterly revenue base enables pan‑US payer coverage that mid‑caps lack [^cc-transcript].
- **Pricing power:** Moderate – clients are large insurers with procurement leverage; management notes “no pricing lever” used, but value‑based outcome deals allow modest annual uplift [^cc-transcript].

Promoter pedigree and tenure: The operating promoter group (Sagility B.V. / PE sponsor) has governed since pre‑IPO; Indian MD Ramesh Gopalan has driven the payer‑focused strategy for years, and the board re‑appointed Non‑Exec Director Hari Gopalakrishnan at the Aug‑2026 AGM, signalling stability [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript].

FCF (Free Cash Flow – the cash left after running operations and buying needed assets) deployment track record (3 years): In Q1 FY27 alone, operating cash flow was ₹3,161m (70% of EBITDA), capex (fixed asset additions) ₹745m, M&A cash ₹1,360m, yielding FCF of ₹2,416m [^cc-transcript]. Corporate governance flags: No auditor changes, no qualified opinions, no related‑party transaction (RPT) red flags noted in the FY25‑26 Annual Report; AGM conducted virtually with proper e‑voting [^ar-fy25].

JVs / partnerships: Strategic acquisitions serve as inorganic partnerships – BroadPath (payer‑provider connectivity) and CareSeed (quality analytics) are integrated as subsidiaries, with rationale to build an “End‑to‑End Quality Operations Continuum” combining tech with Sagility’s nurse workforce [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript].
