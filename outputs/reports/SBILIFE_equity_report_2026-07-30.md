## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

1. **Distribution moat anchored to SBI's parentage is the single largest competitive advantage in Indian life insurance.** SBI Life is the only insurer with the State Bank of India distribution engine behind it — 22,000+ SBI branches serve as bancassurance points, contributing 56% of total APE in FY25 [^ip-latest]. No private peer can replicate this counter footprint. The bancassurance channel is structurally lower-cost than agency or online acquisition and gives SBILIFE a sustained ~150-200 bps cost advantage on new business strain [^ar-fy25]. Q1FY27 disclosure of 64.5% banca share in individual APE underscores continued execution [^cc-transcript].

2. **Margin expansion story is structural, not cyclical, driven by product mix shift toward protection and non-par savings.** VNB margin expanded from 24.6% in FY21 to 30.0% in FY25, and management has guided for 32-34% by FY28E driven by: (a) growing weight of non-linked savings (now 50% of APE vs 38% in FY22), (b) credit life attach rates doubling, and (c) focus on higher-margin group protection [^ip-latest]. This is a 300-400 bps structural margin expansion over a 3-4 year horizon — the kind of operating leverage that is rare in financial services [^vp-thread].

3. **Diversification into high-margin adjacencies is de-risking the traditional ULIP-heavy book.** Protection APE grew at 24% CAGR over FY22-FY25 vs 12% for total APE, and now constitutes 9% of mix vs 5% three years ago [^ar-fy25]. Annuity APE — a 100% capital-friendly line with long-duration liability tail ideal for SBI's pension-distribution synergies — crossed Rs. 4,500 Cr in FY25, up 2.1x YoY [^ip-latest]. The group business is being re-positioned toward credit life and term, both of which are stickier and more economically attractive than the historical group fund-based book.

4. **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months) include (a) Q1FY27 results showing APE growth re-acceleration, (b) product changes in capital-light non-par, (c) potential increase in ownership cap raise by SBI from current ~58.7%, and (d) Q3FY27 reset of the persistency curve.** Q1FY27 APE grew 14% YoY to Rs. 4,970 Cr with VNB margin holding above 31% — the strongest quarterly print in 6 quarters and a clear signal that the FY25-26 mix shift is now flowing through to P&L [^cc-transcript]. The 13th-month persistency improved to 87.5% (vs 84.2% in FY24), validating the multi-year investment in customer servicing [^ip-latest].

5. **The biggest structural risk is concentration in SBI's banca channel combined with regulatory exposure on surrenders and IRDA-mandated product changes.** Banca at 56%+ of APE means any SBI-driven strategy shift, internal staff realignment, or renegotiation of the bancassurance agreement could materially impair new business flows [^vp-thread]. Additionally, IRDA's ongoing scrutiny of non-par guarantee products and the Insurance Amendment Bill provisions on composite licensing could reshape distribution economics. An 18-24 month IRDAI cycle on ULIP surrenders remains a tail risk on persistency assumptions baked into the EV.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** SBI Life is a private sector life insurance company offering a comprehensive suite of individual and group life, health, pension, and annuity products. The company operates through four main product categories: (i) Individual Savings (Unit-Linked Insurance Plans / ULIPs, Participating / Par, and Non-Participating / Non-Par savings), (ii) Individual Protection (term, health, and rider solutions), (iii) Annuity, and (iv) Group Business (group term, group savings, group credit life, and group fund-based schemes). Premium is collected from customers, managed through two funds — Policyholders' funds and Shareholders' funds — with profits emerging from mortality experience, investment yields, expense loadings, and persistency bonuses on par products.

**Revenue Split by Product (APE basis, FY25):**

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Non-Par Savings | 28% | Saral Pension, Smart Guaranteed Savings, Guaranteed Income Builder | Banca (SBI + 7 banca partners) |
| Participating (Par) | 14% | Smart Annuity Plus, Platina Life | Bancassurance |

*Source: SBI Life Investor Presentation FY25 and Q1FY27 disclosure [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript]*

**Key Customers by Division:**

- **Bancassurance:** Anchor partner is State Bank of India (22,000+ branches, 48 Cr+ customer accounts). Supplementary banca partners include Karur Vysya Bank, South Indian Bank, Federal Bank, Karnataka Bank, and select regional rural banks — adding 6,000+ distribution points.
- **Agency:** ~50,000+ advisors as of Q1FY27, with top 100 branches contributing ~22% of agency APE.
- **Group Business:** Major credit life clients include HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, SBI Cards, and 20+ NBFC partners. Group term clients span large corporates and PSUs.
- **Annuity:** Distributed primarily through SBI's pension and senior citizen customer base.

**Subsidiary / Investment Structure:** SBI Life is a 58.7% subsidiary of State Bank of India, with BNP Paribas Cardif holding the residual foreign joint venture stake of ~5% (post IPO dilution). The remaining ~36% is held by public shareholders (institutional and retail) [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing / Operations Footprint:** HQ in Mumbai. The company has 1,122+ branch offices across India as of Q1FY27, with the highest density in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities, leveraging SBI's pre-existing branch network. Operations are largely central — no separate "manufacturing plants" since this is a service business. IT infrastructure is hosted in Mumbai and Hyderabad, with full digital claims processing enabled across 850+ branches.

**Promoter / Group Background:** Promoter is State Bank of India — the largest commercial bank in India by deposits, assets, and customer reach. SBI is majority-owned by the Government of India (57.5%). SBI Life was established in 2001 and listed in 2017 (one of India's largest IPOs at the time). The group context is critical: SBI has a strategic mandate to maximize bancassurance yield to its 48 Cr+ customer base, and SBI Life is the primary vehicle for monetization of life insurance.

### SECTION 8 — VALUATION

**Approach note:** SBI Life is best valued on EV/EBITDA and EV/VNB frameworks rather than standalone P/E — life insurer accounting inflates revenue (policyholder investment income) while suppressing EBITDA, making traditional P/E optically distorted[^ip-latest]. #### Method 1 — P/E-Based Target (Cross-Check)

- **Justified P/E range:** 60–70x for FY28E EPS, in line with listed life-insurance peer band (HDFC Life, ICICI Pru, Max Financial trade at 55–75x on FY1 forward earnings).
- **ROCE linkage:** SBI Life's operating RoEV (~14–15%) sits at the top of the listed peer set; a premium P/E is warranted, but not extreme — we apply 65x base-case.
- **Target = 28.90 × 65x = Rs. 1,879** — broadly aligns with CMP, suggesting market is fairly priced on P/E lens. P/E is therefore a sanity check, not the anchor.

#### Method 2 — EV/EBITDA-Based Target (Primary Anchor)

EV/EBITDA is the standard sector multiple for life insurers because it adjusts for the distorted revenue/EBITDA profile of underwriting-heavy businesses[^ar-fy25].

- **Sector median EV/EBITDA (FY1):** ~28–30x (HDFC Life, ICICI Pru, Max Financial cluster)
- **SBI Life justification for premium:** Higher RoEV, bancassurance distribution moat with SBI parent, consistent top-2 APE share — supports 30–32x multiple
- **EBITDA (FY28E):** Rs. 1,025 Cr (BASE) — note EBITDA understates true earnings power for insurers; EV-based valuation uses Embedded Value (EV) framework
- **Implied EV (FY28E):** 1,025 × 30x = Rs. 30,750 Cr
- **Add: Invested Assets backing Shareholders' Equity** (approx. Rs. 1,85,000 Cr at market value) → Equity Value ≈ Rs. 2,15,750 Cr
- **Per share: Rs. 2,15,750 / 101.25 Cr shares = Rs. 2,131**
- EV/VNB cross-check: FY27E VNB ≈ Rs. 6,800 Cr × 30x sector EV/VNB multiple ≈ Rs. 2,04,000 Cr EV (sanity-check band)
