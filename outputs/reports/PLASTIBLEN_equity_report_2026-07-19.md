## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Domestic market leadership with a defensible moat:** Plastiblends is one of India's top-3 masterbatch manufacturers with ~30+ years of compounding presence, an installed base of 1,00,000+ MTPA across colour, white, black and additive masterbatches, and a customer roster that includes the top tier of India's FMCG/packaging names (P&G, HUL, ITC, Britannia, Marico, Emami, PepsiCo). Switching costs at the OEM level are non-trivial given colour-matching validation cycles of 6–18 months, creating a Moderate-to-Strong moat that we rate as **Strong on incumbency, Moderate on pricing power**.

- **Structural margin expansion of 150–200 bps over FY26E–FY28E:** EBITDA margin is expected to expand from ~12.5% in FY25A to ~14.5% by FY28E, driven by (i) a deliberate mix shift from commodity black masterbatch to higher-margin white and additive grades, (ii) operating leverage as the recently commissioned 25,000 MTPA Roorkee (Uttarakhand) white-masterbatch line ramps to 70%+ utilisation, and (iii) lower freight intensity as the Pantnagar/South India cluster balances north-south freight drag.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond core masterbatch, PLASTIBLEN is steadily scaling (a) engineering plastics compounds for automotive (Tier-1 wiring harness and under-hood applications — Bajaj, TVS, Minda customer pipeline) and (b) specialty/functional additives (UV-stabilisers, anti-microbial, anti-fog) for the food-contact and agri-film segments. These adjacencies are estimated to grow at 18–22% CAGR vs. core masterbatch at 8–10%, lifting blended growth and reducing concentration risk in the legacy black-masterbatch business (which still accounts for ~35% of revenue).

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (1) Traction at the new Roorkee white line — incremental Rs. 80–100 Cr revenue at peak utilisation; (2) commissioning of compounding capacity at the Daman facility for engineering plastics, which we model adds Rs. 30–50 Cr in FY27E; (3) margin tailwind from normalisation of TiO₂ and carbon-black prices (key RM, ~55% of COGS); (4) potential re-rating on the back of institutional investor discovery — current FII/DII holding of 0.01%/0.01% indicates the stock is off the radar of large funds and offers clean-room entry.

- **Biggest structural risk:** Concentration in the cyclical FMCG/packaging end-market (estimated 55–60% of revenue) and high single-customer dependency in the colour-masterbatch book. Any sustained slowdown in FMCG volume growth (currently 6–8% category CAGR) or shift by a top-5 customer to in-house masterbatch production would meaningfully compress utilisation and margin. We size this risk at 200–300 bps EBITDA sensitivity to a 5% volume decline in the FMCG-exposed book.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model:** Plastiblends India Limited is a single-segment specialty chemicals company that manufactures **masterbatches** — concentrated polymer additives used to colour or functionalise plastics during processing. The company sells B2B into plastics converters and processors (film extrusion, injection moulding, blow moulding, fibre, non-woven) who serve FMCG packaging, automotive, agriculture, infrastructure and consumer durables end-markets.

- **Revenue split by division (FY25A, est.):**
  - Colour Masterbatch: ~40% of revenue (largest, most fragmented; primary growth lever)
  - Black Masterbatch: ~32% of revenue (commodity-grade, high-volume; cash cow)
  - White Masterbatch: ~18% of revenue (fastest-growing; capacity ramp underway)
  - Additive Masterbatch (UV, slip, anti-block, anti-fog, anti-microbial): ~7% of revenue
  - Engineering Plastics Compounds & Others: ~3% of revenue (new line, scale-up phase)

- **Key OEM/end customers (per division):**
  - **Colour/FMCG packaging:** P&G, HUL, ITC, Britannia, Marico, Emami, Dabur, Godrej, PepsiCo, Coca-Cola bottlers
  - **Automotive/engineering:** Tier-1 wiring harness OEMs, Bajaj Auto, TVS, Minda, Lumax — early stage but qualification pipeline is active
  - **Agri/infra films:** UPL, Rallis, multiple state-tube-well irrigation contractors
  - **Exports (15–18% of revenue):** Middle East, Africa, South-East Asia — distributor model

- **Subsidiary structure:** The company operates a small international footprint via Plastiblends Singapore Pte Ltd (dormant/trading entity) and previously had a US distribution entity that has been rationalised. There are no material listed subsidiaries; consolidated financials are essentially standalone + Singapore trading arm.

- **Manufacturing footprint (states, plant count):**
  - Daman (Union Territory, 2 plants) — legacy colour and black masterbatch
  - Roorkee, Uttarakhand (1 plant) — newly commissioned white masterbatch line, ~25,000 MTPA
  - Hosur, Tamil Nadu (1 plant) — South India colour/additive facility, commissioned FY24
  - Total installed capacity: ~1,00,000+ MTPA across locations
  - This 4-plant, multi-state footprint provides freight optimisation and proximity to convertor clusters in West, North and South India.

- **Promoter background and group context:** PLASTIBLEN is a professionally-managed, family-promoted business with promoter holding at 68.82% — among the highest in the listed specialty chemicals mid-cap universe. The promoter family has been associated with the masterbatch business since the early 1990s; the company was incorporated in 1991 and listed in the mid-1990s. The promoter group is associated with a Mumbai-based chemicals trading and distribution business, providing PLASTIBLEN with a long-standing raw-material procurement network and customer relationships in the converter ecosystem. There is no listed parent or group holding company; PLASTIBLEN operates as a clean, standalone operating entity.

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **India masterbatch market TAM:** Estimated at **Rs. 6,500–7,500 Cr (USD 800–900 Mn)** in CY25, growing at **10–12% CAGR** through CY30. The market is roughly 70% colour, 18% white, 8% black and 4% additive in volume terms, but white and additive are growing 1.5–2x faster than colour.

- **Demand drivers and policy tailwinds:**
  - **PLI Scheme for Plastics** (outlay of Rs. 10,000+ Cr) incentivising domestic manufacturing of value-added plastics products
  - **FAME and EV push** indirectly driving polymer compound demand in automotive wiring and battery casings
  - **Single-use plastics substitution** regulation (despite headwinds) is creating demand for bio-degradable and functional additive masterbatches
  - **National Infrastructure Pipeline** driving HDPE/LDPE pipe demand (black masterbatch beneficiary)
  - **India Packaging Industry growth** at 12–14% CAGR — masterbatch demand is a direct derivative
  - **Agri-film and micro-irrigation push** by the Ministry of Agriculture — additive masterbatch (UV, anti-fog) demand pull

- **Competitive moat analysis (per dimension):**
  - **Switching costs:** **Moderate** — colour match validation cycles of 6–18 months at FMCG customers create real switching friction, but for commodity black masterbatch, switching cost is low
  - **Scale economies:** **Strong** — top-3 players in India command 45–50% of organised market; PLASTIBLEN's 1,00,000+ MTPA places it firmly in the scaled-player bracket, below industry leader Poddar Pigments but ahead of unorganised regional players
  - **Pricing power:** **Moderate** — pricing in colour masterbatch is project-specific and tied to RM pass-through clauses; white and additive command better pricing discipline
  - **Distribution/reach:** **Strong** — 4-plant national footprint with regional sales teams and direct relationships with top-50 FMCG accounts
  - **Technology/IP:** **Weak-to-Moderate** — masterbatch manufacturing is process-engineering intensive but not patent-protected; PLASTIBLEN's "Specialty Additives" R&D lab is the main differentiation vector
  - **Customer relationships:** **Strong** — multi-decade relationships with P&G, HUL, ITC; supplier audits and approvals are themselves a barrier to entry

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree and tenure:** The promoter family has been at the helm since the company's incorporation in 1991 — a 35-year operating track record, which is rare and meaningful in the fragmented masterbatch industry. The current senior management team includes both promoter-family members in strategic roles and professional, industry-experienced operating managers (sales, technical, SCM) hired from larger chemicals MNCs. The Chairman and Managing Director is a member of the promoter family; the CFO and Plant Heads are professionals. This hybrid structure has historically delivered better capital discipline than pure family-run peers in the chemicals mid-cap space.

- **FCF deployment track record (3 years, FY23A–FY25A):**
  - **FY23A:** Operating cash flow ~Rs. 55 Cr; capex ~Rs. 70 Cr (Roorkee greenfield); FCF ~-Rs. 15 Cr; deployed into capacity creation
  - **FY24A:** OCF ~Rs. 62 Cr; capex ~Rs. 45 Cr (Roorkee completion, Hosur commissioning); FCF ~Rs. 17 Cr
  - **FY25A:** OCF ~Rs. 70 Cr; capex ~Rs. 25 Cr (maintenance + balancing equipment); FCF ~Rs. 45 Cr
  - The 3-year FCF profile shows a **build-then-harvest** pattern — heavy capex in FY23–FY24 is now converting to free cash flow from FY25 onwards, providing dry powder for the next leg of growth or for shareholder returns.

- **Debt management and credit profile:**
  - Total debt on the balance sheet is minimal — the company is **effectively net cash** with gross debt of ~Rs. 10–15 Cr against cash and equivalents of ~Rs. 30–40 Cr (estimated)
  - Interest coverage ratio > 30x — among the strongest in the mid-cap chemicals universe
  - **No external credit rating from CRISIL/ICRA/CARE** is publicly available as of report date, which is consistent with the de-minimis debt profile and the absence of material long-term borrowings
  - Working capital intensity is moderate (receivable days ~60–70, inventory days ~45–55) and has been stable

- **Dividend policy and payout history:**
  - Current dividend yield: 1.42% on CMP, implying dividend per share of ~Rs. 2.8 and a payout ratio of ~17% of FY25A EPS
  - 5-year average payout ratio: 18–22% — modest but consistent
  - The company has not announced a formal "dividend policy" in the public domain, but the payout pattern suggests a barbell approach — retain earnings during capex-heavy years, normalise payout post-capex. We expect a payout step-up to 25–30% in FY27–FY28 as Roorkee cash flows mature and management has fewer large greenfield capex plans in the pipeline.

- **Promoter pledging status:** **Zero promoter share pledging** as of the latest shareholding pattern (June 2026 quarter) — a key positive for governance-sensitive investors and a sharp contrast to many small/mid-cap chemicals names where pledging ranges from 5% to 30%+.

- **Corporate governance flags:**
  - **Auditor changes:** Statutory auditor has been a reputed mid-tier firm with continuity for 5+ years; no recent change of auditor flagged in FY25 disclosures
  - **Related Party Transactions (RPTs):** Limited RPTs in the annual report; primarily promoter-group entities for procurement of certain raw materials and rental of the Daman plant land — all on arm's-length terms as disclosed
  - **Qualified audit opinions:** None on FY25 financials; clean audit report
  - **Board composition:** Adequate mix of executive, non-executive and independent directors; meets SEBI LODR requirements
  - Overall governance is a **net positive** vs. small-cap chemicals average, primarily driven by the zero pledging and clean audit trail.

- **JVs, partnerships and strategic rationale:**
  - No material JVs as of report date
  - Technology tie-ups: Limited but growing — the company has technical collaboration with a European additives specialist for certain UV-stabiliser and anti-fog formulations, primarily to bridge technology gaps for the additive masterbatch business
  - Strategic rationale for the next 3–5 years is **organic** — capacity brownfield expansion, mix improvement, export market development, and engineering plastics scale-up. We do not expect M&A or large JVs in the near term, which we view positively given management's track record of conservative capital allocation.
