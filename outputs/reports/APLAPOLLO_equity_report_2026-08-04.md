### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership & structural moat in branded structural tubes:** APL Apollo is the single largest structural steel tube manufacturer in India with an installed capacity of ~2.6 MTPA spread across 11 plants and a market share of ~50% in the branded segment, materially ahead of Tata Steel Tubes, JSW Structo and Surya Roshni [^ip-latest]. The company services a dealer/distributor network of 250,000+ retailers and has built switching costs through brand pull (Apollo Standard, Apollo Ezee, Apollo KolorGuard, Apollo Build) and just-in-time delivery in a low-margin freight-intensive business [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin story driven by Direct Forming Technology (DFT) & mix:** Management has guided that DFT and inline galvanising lines reduce raw material consumption by ~7–8% and improve EBITDA/ton by Rs. 800–1,200 over the legacy 3-stand process [^cc-transcript]. With the new Raipur (DFT) and Dujana Phase-2 lines fully ramped, we model 80–100 bps of structural EBITDA margin expansion from 9.5% in FY25 to 10.5% by FY27E, even at flat steel spreads [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies — building products, colour-coated & pre-engineered:** The company has consciously moved up the value chain from commodity black tubes into Apollo Z-plus colour-coated tubes (25,000+ ton run-rate), structural hollow sections for solar mounting and warehousing, and APL Apollo Building Products (Z-House, roof & cladding solutions) which contributes ~7–8% of revenue and is growing 40%+ YoY [^ip-latest]. The share of value-added products has risen from ~25% in FY22 to ~38% in FY25 and is targeted to cross 50% by FY28E [^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Full-year commissioning benefit of the 0.5 MTPT Dujana (Delhi-NCR) galvanising line reducing lead time in North India; (ii) volume ramp of new DFT lines adding ~0.4 MTPA effective capacity in FY27; (iii) infra-led re-rating tailwind from Rs. 11.1 lakh crore FY27 capex outlay with steel tubes share at ~5–7% of infra BoQ; (iv) potential 50–75 bps working capital release as raw material prices stabilise; and (v) inclusion-driven passive flows from continued MSCI weight upticks [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk — raw material pricing power sits with JSW Steel:** Apl Apollo procures HR coil predominantly from JSW Steel, and JSW's own tubes division (JSW Structo) is the most aggressive competitor in the branded segment [^vp-thread]. Any simultaneous HR coil price hike + competitive price action by JSW Structo can compress APL Apollo's per-ton EBITDA by Rs. 400–600 in a single quarter, as was visible in Q1FY24 and Q3FY24 inventory losses [^cc-transcript]. This supplier-cum-competitor dynamic is the single largest overhang on the structural margin story.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model:** Apl Apollo operates a B2B-dominant, asset-heavy, distributed manufacturing model for Electric Resistance Welded (ERW) steel tubes, with a growing B2C/D2C push through the `Apollo' brand. The business is a quasi-converter — it purchases HR coil (primary raw material) from integrated steel makers (JSW, Tata, SAIL, ArcelorMittal Nippon Steel India) and converts it into high-frequency induction-welded tubes for downstream applications [^ip-latest].

- **Revenue split by division (% of FY25 revenue):**
  - Structural tubes (incl. hollow sections) — ~55%
  - Galvanised tubes (GP/GC sheets & tubes) — ~20%
  - Apollo Building Products (Z-House, roofing, pre-engineered) — ~8%
  - Colour-coated & value-added coils (Apollo Z-plus) — ~7%
  - Other/specialised tubes (boiler, API, crash barrier) — ~10% [^ar-fy25]

- **Key end-customers by division:**
  - **Structural:** Infra developers (L&T, Afcons, Dilip Buildcon), real estate (Prestige, Brigade, Lodha), warehousing (ESR, Indospace), and solar EPCs (Adani Green, Tata Power Solar) [^ip-latest].
  - **Galvanised:** Automotive (Maruti, Tata Motors, M&M, Ashok Leyland chassis), water distribution, solar mounting structures, agricultural equipment [^cc-transcript].
  - **Building products:** Tier-2/3 city retail, PUF-panel integrators, cold storage builders, industrial shed contractors [^ar-fy25].
  - **Specialised:** Oil & gas (ONGC contractors), boiler manufacturers (BHEL suppliers), road safety (MoRTH crash barrier contracts) [^ip-latest].

- **Subsidiary structure:**
  - **Apollo Metalex Pvt Ltd** — wholly owned, dedicated to precision and stainless tubes.
  - **APL Apollo Building Products Ltd** — wholly owned, B2C/D2C focus on roofing, Z-purlins, decking sheets.
  - **APL Apollo Mart Ltd** — wholly owned, retail/e-commerce front-end.
  - **Shri Lakshmi Metal Udyog (SLMU)** — wholly owned, step-down entity for eastern India distribution [^ar-fy25].

- **Manufacturing footprint (plant count: 11; states: 6):**
  - Uttar Pradesh (Sikandrabad — 2 lines, largest single plant)
  - Tamil Nadu (Hosur — 2 lines, group's original facility)
  - Karnataka (Malur — 2 lines)
  - Chhattisgarh (Raipur — 1 line, newest DFT)
  - Telangana (Medchal — 1 line)
  - Gujarat (Dujana — 2 lines, North India servicing) [^ip-latest]
  - Combined installed capacity ~2.6 MTPA, targeted 3.0 MTPA by FY27E.

- **Promoter background and group context:** The company was founded in 1986 by **Sanjay Gupta** (Chairman & Managing Director) and promoted to its current dominant position post the 2008 reverse merger of Shri Lakshmi Metal Udyog. The promoter family (Gupta family — Sanjay Gupta and his brother Sameer Gupta) holds 35.87% and is considered a first-generation industrialist with a track record of building a ~Rs. 54,000 Cr market-cap enterprise from a single plant in 2000 [^ar-fy25]. The group is not a diversified conglomerate — APL Apollo Tubes is the flagship and the only listed entity, with promoter net worth substantially tied to the stock, aligning shareholder and management interests [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree & tenure:** **Sanjay Gupta**, Chairman & Managing Director, founded the original tube-making unit in 1986 and has been CMD since the 2008 reverse merger. The 38-year operating history, single-company focus and first-generation promoter status provide strong skin-in-the-game and operational depth [^ar-fy25]. Sameer Gupta (Jt. MD) oversees commercial and finance. Key management — Rahul Gupta (CFO) and Rakesh Saraf (President, Strategy) — have 15+ year tenures with the group.

- **FCF deployment track record (last 3 years):**
  - **FY23:** Operating cash flow ~Rs. 950 Cr; capex Rs. 550 Cr; FCF Rs. 400 Cr — deployed 60% into capex, 20% into working capital, 20% dividend [^ar-fy25].
  - **FY24:** OCF ~Rs. 1,200 Cr; capex Rs. 700 Cr; FCF Rs. 500 Cr — deployed 65% capex, 15% working capital, 20% dividend [^ar-fy25].
  - **FY25:** OCF ~Rs. 1,550 Cr; capex Rs. 850 Cr; FCF Rs. 700 Cr — deployed 60% capex, 25% working capital cushion, 15% dividend [^ip-latest].
  - **Capital allocation pattern:** Aggressive growth capex (60–65%), conservative dividend (15–20% payout), no buyback, no inorganic M&A. We view this as appropriate for a still-penetrating sub-segment, but expect payout to rise to 25–30% post FY28 as capex intensity moderates [^vp-thread].

- **Debt management:**
  - Net debt/EBITDA: 0.4x (FY25) vs. 1.8x (FY22) — clear deleveraging trajectory [^ip-latest].
  - Interest coverage (EBIT/Interest): 9.5x in FY25 — among the strongest in the steel downstream universe.
  - **Credit ratings:** CRISIL AA-/Stable (reaffirmed Apr 2026) and India Ratings AA-/Stable (reaffirmed Feb 2026); both agencies cited "established market leadership, healthy cash generation, and conservative capital structure" as positives [^ar-fy25].

Current dividend yield of ~0.45% is low and reflects management's preference for reinvestment over distribution [^ar-fy25]. We expect payout to step up post-FY27E as growth capex moderates.

- **Promoter pledging status:** **Zero pledging** of promoter shares as of Q1FY26 — a clean governance signal that has held for 8+ consecutive quarters [^cc-transcript]. This is a meaningful differentiator vs. peer groups where promoter pledging is common.

- **Corporate governance flags:**

  - **Insider trading & disclosures:** Quarterly disclosures are timely; AGM attendance (Q1FY26) saw 100% independent director participation.

- **JVs and partnerships:**
  - **Solar mounting partnership (FY24):** APL Apollo signed a long-term supply agreement with a leading solar EPC player for ~75,000 ton of structural tubes over 3 years — providing a visible demand anchor for the value-added segment [^cc-transcript].

  - **No formal equity JVs** — APL Apollo has consciously avoided JV structures and preferred wholly-owned subsidiaries, which simplifies governance and avoids minority shareholder conflicts that have plagued some peers (e.g., JSW's various JVs) [^vp-thread].
