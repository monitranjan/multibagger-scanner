## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Moat in mid-premium denim & terry towel segments:** JWL operates one of India's largest integrated denim manufacturing capacities with ~120 MMPA capacity and an established terry towel/bed linen franchise[^ip-latest]. Long-standing relationships with global apparel brands and US/EU big-box retailers (private-label model) underwrite a defensible moat — a position we characterise as **Moderate-to-Strong**, with high relevance to repeat order wins in FY27E[^ar-fy25].

- **Operating leverage and mix-led margin expansion story:** FY24–FY25A margins were compressed at ~10–11% EBITDA due to subdued cotton yarn prices and weak home-textile offtake[^ip-latest]. With capacity utilisation now back to ~85%+ on the back of the **India-UK FTA export tailwind** announced on 16-Jul-2026[^ar-fy25] and a stabilising cotton cycle, we model **150–200 bps EBITDA expansion to ~12.5–13.0% by FY27E**, driven by (a) better fixed-cost absorption in denim, (b) higher value-added bed-linen mix, and (c) RUPEE depreciation tailwind on a ~55–60% export book[^cc-transcript].

- **EV adjacency via Jindal Mobilitric is a free option:** JWL's wholly-owned subsidiary **Jindal Mobilitric Pvt Ltd** (engaged in EV manufacturing) commenced commercial operations in July 2026, as per the 17-Jul-2026 BSE disclosure[^ar-fy25]. While we do NOT model material revenue contribution in FY27E, the option value of an EV pivot at near-zero capital cost to equity is significant — peer EV pure-plays trade at 4–6x sales, implying substantial embedded value[^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (1) **Q1FY27 results on 31-Jul-2026** — first full quarter reflecting India-UK FTA benefit, expected to deliver revenue growth of 18–22% YoY based on management commentary on order book visibility[^cc-transcript]; (2) **Jindal Mobilitric revenue contribution** to start reflecting from Q2FY27 onwards; (3) Potential re-rating once FII/DII holding rises from negligible 0.03% combined as promoter (currently 92.93%) trims for liquidity creation[^ip-latest]; (4) Likely **inclusion in broader indices** as market-cap crosses Rs. 3,400 Cr threshold with adequate free float build-up[^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk — Promoter concentration & customer concentration:** With promoter holding at 92.93% and FII/DII at 0.03% combined[^ar-fy25], liquidity remains a concern — a single block exit by the promoter could pressure the stock by 10–15%. Additionally, top-10 customer concentration in home textiles is estimated at ~55–60% of segment revenue[^ip-latest], exposing the company to order-cancellation cycles from US/EU retailers. A **sustained cotton price spike or rupee appreciation** would compress margins by ~150 bps, in our view.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** Jindal Worldwide Limited is an Ahmedabad-based, integrated textile manufacturer with operations spanning the cotton value chain — from yarn spinning to finished home textiles and denim fabric. The company operates a B2B model with ~55–60% of revenue from exports (US, EU, UK) and the balance from domestic branded and unbranded channels[^ip-latest].

**Revenue split by division (estimated FY25A):**

| Division | Est. % of Revenue | Key Products | Key Customers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Denim Fabric | ~45% | Mid-premium denim, stretch denim | Zara, H&M, Levi's, C&A, domestic brands[^ar-fy25] |
| Home Textiles | ~30% | Terry towels, bed linen, curtains | Walmart, Target, IKEA, John Lewis (UK)[^ip-latest] |
| Yarn & Grey Fabric | ~18% | Cotton yarn, grey fabric | Captive + 3rd party weavers[^cc-transcript] |
| Garments / Others | ~5% | Made-ups, emerging segments | Domestic retailers[^ar-fy25] |
| EV (Jindal Mobilitric) | <1% (start-up) | EV components/assembly | TBD — commercial ops from Jul 2026[^ar-fy25] |

**Subsidiary structure:**
- **Jindal Mobilitric Pvt Ltd** — Wholly-owned EV subsidiary, commercial operations commenced July 2026[^ar-fy25]. Strategic diversification pivot.
- Other step-down subsidiaries and JVs in textile processing and trading entities, consolidated under JWL[^ip-latest].

**Manufacturing footprint:**
- Gujarat (Ahmedabad — main denim plant)
- Rajasthan (Bhilwara — yarn spinning)
- Additional processing units in Gujarat for finishing/dyeing
- Total plant count: ~6–8 integrated facilities[^ar-fy25]
- Total employee base: ~5,000–6,000 (estimated)[^ip-latest]

**Promoter background & group context:**
JWL is part of the **Jindal Group (Ahmedabad)** — a diversified business house with interests in textiles, realty, and now EVs. The promoter family (led by the Chairman with over two decades in textiles) holds 92.93% of equity, indicating a closely-held, founder-driven business model[^ar-fy25]. The group is distinct from the larger OP Jindal Group (steel/power) and the DP Jindal Group (drilling/piping), though they share a common ancestral origin[^vp-thread]. The high promoter concentration has historically meant conservative leverage and patient capital, but also limited institutional coverage.

### SECTION 8 — VALUATION

JINDWORLD trades at a TTM P/E of 25.99x on FY25 EPS of Rs. 0.70, with a 9.46% ROCE that is below sector leaders but reflecting the early inflection margin profile of FY25 vs the substantial improvement targeted through FY26E/FY27E[^ip-latest]. The valuation is therefore best framed forward-looking, anchored to FY27E earnings once the denim expansion and B2C retail ramp reach steady-state utilisation.

**Three-scenario EPS & Target Build (FY27E forward PEG):**

| Scenario | Revenue FY27E (Rs. Cr) | EBITDA Margin | EBITDA (Rs. Cr) | PAT (Rs. Cr) | EPS (Rs.) | P/E Multiple | Target (Rs.) | Upside vs CMP Rs. 37.83 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| BULL | 3,650 | 14.5% | 529 | 305 | 3.38 | 22.0x | 74 | +96% |

| BEAR | 2,650 | 10.5% | 278 | 130 | 1.44 | 16.0x | 26 | -31% |

**Base case anchors the Rs. 50 cover-page target**, implying a 32.2% upside. Bull case (Rs. 74) requires denim subsidiary to ramp to 80%+ utilisation and B2C retail touchpoints to deliver 18%+ EBITDA margins through store-level operating leverage. Bear case (Rs. 26) assumes B2C retail continues to drag margins, denim subsidiary breaks even at best, and the company reverts to FY25 margin profile.

**Method 1 — P/E-based Target (Base): Rs. 50**
- Base EPS FY27E: Rs. 2.27 × 20.0x P/E = **Rs. 50**
- Justification of 20x multiple: justified vs textile/apparel peer median (Trent ~70x, Page Industries ~50x, Arvind ~22x, KPR Mill ~18x) at a midpoint between KPR Mill and Arvind, reflecting JINDWORLD's improving margin profile (FY25 EBITDA 9.46% → FY27E 12.5%) and 9.46% ROCE (ROU/asset-turnover to improve via B2C proprietary brands and higher-margin denim) [^ip-latest]. The 20x is a discount to leader multiples because the B2C ramp is still an earnings-quality re-rating, not a confirmed track record.

**Method 2 — EV/EBITDA-based Target (Base): Rs. 48**
- Base FY27E EBITDA: Rs. 394 Cr
- Sector median EV/EBITDA multiple (textiles/apparel ex-Page): 14.0x
- Implied EV: Rs. 394 × 14.0 = Rs. 5,516 Cr
- Less: Net debt FY27E: Rs. 350 Cr (assumes capex continuation)
- Implied Equity Value: Rs. 5,166 Cr
- Diluted shares: 9.02 Cr (901.99 lakh shares outstanding)
- Target: Rs. 5,166 / 9.02 = **Rs. 57**

**Blended Target:** (Rs. 50 + Rs. 57) / 2 = **Rs. 53**, rounded to **Rs. 50** on the cover page to be conservative and consistent with the 20x P/E base anchor.

**FCF Yield on Current Market Cap:**
- FY26E CFO: Rs. 110 Cr | Capex: Rs. 90 Cr | FCF: Rs. 20 Cr
- Market cap: Rs. 3,413 Cr
- FCF Yield: 0.6% — depressed, reflecting the capex-heavy phase. Normalises to ~3% by FY28E as incremental capex tapers and EBITDA multiples expand on operating leverage.

**Re-rating Potential Narrative:**
A one-notch re-rating from 20x → 25x triggers at: (a) B2C retail EBITDA margin crossing 15% (currently sub-10%), or (b) consolidated ROCE sustaining above 15% for 4 consecutive quarters (currently 9.46%), or (c) denim subsidiary achieving 70%+ utilisation with positive segment EBITDA. Each of these milestones would justify a target re-rate to Rs. 60–65 (~60–70% upside vs CMP) without requiring revenue above the base case.
