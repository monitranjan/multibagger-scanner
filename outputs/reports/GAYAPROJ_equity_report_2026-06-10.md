### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

*   **Market Leadership / Moat:** Gayatri Projects operates in a highly fragmented construction sector, primarily focusing on roads, irrigation, and power projects. While not a market leader in terms of overall market share, GAYAPROJ has historically demonstrated capabilities in executing large-scale, complex infrastructure projects, particularly in the Southern and Eastern regions of India. Its established relationships with state government departments and a track record of project completion, albeit with recent challenges, provide a moderate moat in securing repeat orders within its regional strongholds.
*   **Structural Margin Story:** The company's historical margin profile has been volatile, impacted by project delays, cost overruns, and high interest expenses. However, a potential structural improvement in margins could stem from: (1) a renewed focus on prudent bidding and project selection, moving away from aggressive, low-margin tenders; (2) improved working capital management to reduce interest costs; and (3) a more favorable project mix towards higher-value, specialized civil works. Any successful debt restructuring efforts would significantly de-risk the balance sheet and provide a substantial tailwind to profitability.
*   **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** While historically focused on core infrastructure, Gayatri Projects has limited direct diversification into high-growth adjacencies. However, the broader infrastructure push by the Indian government, particularly in roads (Bharatmala Pariyojana) and irrigation (PMKSY), provides a large and growing addressable market. The company's existing expertise in these areas positions it to capture a share of this growth, assuming a successful turnaround and resolution of its financial challenges.
*   **Near-term Catalysts (6–12 months):** Key near-term catalysts include: (1) successful resolution of ongoing debt restructuring discussions and a clear path to reducing leverage; (2) significant improvement in working capital cycles and cash flow generation from existing projects; (3) securing new, profitable orders that demonstrate a shift in bidding strategy; and (4) any positive divestment of non-core assets to unlock value and reduce debt.
*   **Biggest Structural Risk:** The biggest structural risk for Gayatri Projects remains its highly leveraged balance sheet and persistent working capital issues. High interest costs continue to erode profitability, and any further delays in project execution or collection from clients could exacerbate liquidity pressures, posing a significant threat to its going concern status. The intensely competitive nature of the construction sector, coupled with exposure to government policy changes and payment delays, adds further layers of risk.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Gayatri Projects Limited is an infrastructure development company primarily engaged in the execution of civil construction projects across various segments.

*   **Core Business Model:** The company operates as an EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction) contractor, undertaking projects for government bodies and public sector undertakings. Its core business involves the construction of roads, bridges, irrigation systems, power projects (hydroelectric, thermal), and other civil infrastructure.
*   **Revenue Split by Division (% of revenue) (FY25A, indicative based on latest available reports):**

    *   **Roads & Highways:** National Highways Authority of India (NHAI), State PWDs (e.g., Andhra Pradesh R&B, Telangana R&B), Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.
    *   **Irrigation Projects:** State Irrigation Departments (e.g., Andhra Pradesh Irrigation & CAD Department, Telangana Irrigation Department), Water Resources Departments.
    *   **Power Projects:** State Electricity Boards (e.g., APGENCO, TSGENCO), various Central and State power generation companies.

Notable subsidiaries include Gayatri Infra Ventures Limited (engaged in infrastructure development) and various SPVs for specific road projects. The company has historically divested some of its SPV assets.
*   **Manufacturing Footprint:** As an EPC contractor, Gayatri Projects does not have a traditional manufacturing footprint. Its operations are project-site specific. However, it owns and operates a fleet of construction equipment (e.g., excavators, crushers, hot mix plants) which are deployed across various project sites. Its project execution spans multiple states, primarily in Southern India (Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka) and extending to parts of Eastern and Central India. The exact count of active project sites varies based on the project pipeline.
