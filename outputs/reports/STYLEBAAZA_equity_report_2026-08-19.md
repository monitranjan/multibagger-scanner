### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**(1) Market Leadership / Moat — Dominant Eastern India Value Fashion Franchise with Defensible Cluster Economics**

Baazar Style Retail is the largest organised value fashion retailer concentrated in Eastern India with a 276-store footprint spanning 198 cities across 10 states as of Q1 FY27[^ip-latest]. The company's market leadership is anchored in its **cluster-based expansion model**, which has enabled it to achieve leadership density in core states — 101 stores in West Bengal (37% of footprint), 37 each in Bihar and Assam, and 36 in Odisha[^ip-latest]. This density creates a self-reinforcing moat through superior site selection (proven track record of new store EBITDA matching mature store levels within months, not years), lower per-store marketing costs through localised brand recall, and supply chain efficiencies from regional hubs — Assam delivery time was cut from 10 days to 5-6 days via collection centre deployment[^cc-transcript]. The historical store closure rate of ~1.75-2% annually[^cc-transcript] materially undercuts the 8-12% closure rates typical of value fashion peers, validating that the moat is operational rather than promotional. The Tier-3/Tier-4 focus (69% of store presence)[^ip-latest] gives first-mover advantage in markets where national organised players (Reliance Trends, V-Mart/V2 Retail) have minimal penetration, and where unorganised players lack the working capital to scale private label penetration.

**(2) Structural Margin Story — Private Label Inflection from 25% → 65% of Revenue**

The single most important margin lever is the private label mix shift — private labels have grown from 25% of revenue in FY22 to **62% in Q1 FY27**, delivering a 4-year CAGR of ~64%[^ip-latest]. Management has guided to scaling private label share to ~65% over two years[^cc-transcript], and the trajectory FY22: 25% → FY23: 31% → FY24: 38% → FY25: 45% → FY26: 53% → Q1 FY27: 62%[^ip-latest] shows remarkable execution consistency. The strategic shift from "attractive pricing" to "fair pricing once brand recall is established"[^ip-latest] is a textbook margin arbitrage — the company initially sacrifices gross margin to acquire customer trial, then expands gross margin once repeat purchase behaviour is established. The Square Up brand alone delivered Rs. 1,207 Mn in Q1 FY27[^ip-latest], larger than the entire private label portfolio of FY22. This private label penetration, combined with operating leverage from store scaling (fixed cost absorption) and the planned regional distribution centre rollout, provides a structural 100-150 bps EBITDA margin expansion runway over FY27-FY29.

**(3) Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — Personal Care via Cupid Partnership + Hub-and-Spoke Logistics Moat**

Three diversification vectors are emerging simultaneously. First, the strategic partnership with Cupid Limited (Rs. 331.53 Cr preferential warrant issue at Rs. 328/share)[^cc-transcript] brings personal care/wellness categories into the store basket — this complements the existing 13% general merchandise segment[^cc-transcript] by adding higher-frequency, higher-margin categories that drive incremental footfall without cannibalising the 87% apparel core. Second, the hub-and-spoke distribution model — two large regional distribution centres (East + Central India) and 5-7 collection centres over 1-1.5 years[^cc-transcript] — creates a defensible logistics moat that competitors will find difficult to replicate at sub-scale store counts. Third, entry into Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, and Manipur[^ar-fy25] tests the cluster model's replicability in newer Tier-4 geographies. The Cupid warrant also provides balance sheet repair (Rs. 182 Cr earmarked for debt reduction)[^cc-transcript], addressing one of the most-cited bear case concerns.

**(4) Near-Term Catalysts (6-12 months) — Four Discrete Triggers**

Catalyst 1: Q2 FY27 results (October 2026) will be the first quarter with full quarterly contribution from the 19% YoY store count growth (276 vs 232), with management guiding to 35% YoY revenue growth for FY27[^cc-transcript] — a beat to this trajectory could trigger estimate upgrades. Catalyst 2: Insurance claim settlement of ~Rs. 43 Cr from the Serampore fire (May 2024)[^cc-transcript][^ar-fy25] is "optimistic to conclude in a few months" per management — this would unlock working capital and remove the optical overhang on FY25 PAT. Catalyst 3: SAP HANA ERP go-live (expected within 6 months from Q3 FY26 disclosure)[^cc-transcript] — the Infor WMS, Goldratt replenishment system, and Domo Analytics stack collectively address the supply chain visibility gap that has kept inventory days stubbornly above 100 (102 days at 9M FY26 vs 111 days at 9M FY25)[^cc-transcript]. Catalyst 4: The Cupid personal care product placement within Baazar stores is in pilot — performance data over the next 3-4 months will quantify the cross-category uplift potential[^cc-transcript]. The recent Rs. 163 Cr block deal (45 lakh shares, August 17, 2026) and the subsequent 5% upper circuit suggest institutional accumulation is supporting the price action.

**(5) Biggest Structural Risk — Lease-Adjusted Leverage and Regional Concentration**

The structural risk is the bifurcation between reported leverage (Net D/E ex-lease of 0.37x, comfortable) and economic leverage (Total lease liabilities of Rs. 8,297 Mn in FY25 — equivalent to ~2x shareholder equity)[^ar-fy25]. Ind AS 116 capitalises operating leases at a high effective interest rate of 8.5%[^ar-fy25], and the resulting lease interest charge of Rs. 532 Mn annually materially depresses reported PAT. Any acceleration in store opening pace from the existing 40-50/year run-rate to the targeted 60-80/year[^cc-transcript] will compound this burden — with each new store requiring ~Rs. 1 Cr in capex plus Rs. 1 Cr in inventory working capital[^cc-transcript], the cash absorption is substantial even before lease obligations. The secondary risk is geographic concentration: 78% of revenue from Core Markets (West Bengal, Odisha, Assam, Bihar)[^ip-latest] exposes the company to regional economic shocks, climate disruptions (Q3 FY26 SSG was dragged by heavy rainfall in Bengal and unrest in Assam/Tripura, forcing a guidance cut to 4-5% SSG[^cc-transcript]), and political/social risks specific to Eastern India. Comprehensive contingent liabilities of Rs. 1,888 Mn (47% of net worth, largely GST and income tax disputes)[^ar-fy25] represent a contingent cash drain risk that warrants continuous monitoring.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model**

Baazar Style Retail operates a single-channel, offline value fashion retail model under the "Baazar Style" brand, targeting the mass-market price-conscious consumer across Eastern, North-Eastern, and selected Northern Indian states[^ip-latest]. The company sells fashion apparel and general merchandise through company-owned-and-operated (COFO) stores — there is no franchise model and no e-commerce platform of meaningful scale (the Konnect Style Retail subsidiary generated only Rs. 29.5 lakh in revenue with a Rs. 7.9 lakh loss in FY25)[^ar-fy25]. The economic proposition is built on three pillars: (i) affordability — the value fashion positioning targets the Rs. 200-1,500 price band, (ii) breadth — stores carry 9,347 sq ft of average retail area[^ip-latest] with comprehensive category coverage across Men, Women, Kids, Footwear, Home/Kitchen, Travel Accessories, and Lifestyle Products, and (iii) proximity — 44% of stores are in Tier-4 cities (population <1 lakh) and another 25% in Tier-3 (1-5 lakh population)[^ip-latest], reaching consumers underserved by organised retail.

**Revenue Split by Division**

The business is structurally a two-segment reporter in the Investor Presentation[^ip-latest]:

Apparel sub-mix in Q1 FY27: Men's Wear 40% of apparel revenue (Rs. 1,705 Mn), Women's Wear 31% (Rs. 1,318 Mn), Kids 29% (Rs. 1,212 Mn)[^ip-latest]. The Kids segment grew fastest within apparel on a YoY basis (29% share vs 28% prior year), reflecting demographic tailwinds in Eastern India's young population profile.

**Key OEM/End Customers**

Unlike auto component peers, Baazar Style does not have "OEM customers" — the end customer is the individual retail consumer. Customer concentration metrics are not applicable in the traditional sense. However, brand-level supplier concentration exists on the private label side: 11 private labels[^ip-latest] are sourced through a combination of in-house design and third-party manufacturing, with related-party suppliers (Shreyans Creation: Rs. 1,325 Mn, RPB Creation: Rs. 2,269 Mn, Paridhi Creation: Rs. 273 Mn, D M Garments: Rs. 164 Mn, RPB Fashion: Rs. 251 Mn — aggregate Rs. 4,283 Mn or ~4.2% of total purchases in FY25)[^ar-fy25] providing meaningful captive supply capacity. This vertical integration into promoter-family manufacturing units is a unique structural feature.

**Subsidiary Structure**

The company has one operating subsidiary — **Konnect Style Retail Limited**, an e-commerce entity that is effectively in incubation mode with negligible revenue contribution (Rs. 29.5 lakh in FY25)[^ar-fy25]. The corporate structure is otherwise clean. The preferential warrant issue to **Cupid Limited** (Rs. 331.53 Cr) announced in Q3 FY26 is a financial investment rather than a corporate restructuring — Cupid will not have board control but will collaborate on personal care product placement[^cc-transcript].

**Manufacturing Footprint**

Baazar Style is a retailer, not a manufacturer — manufacturing is outsourced. The retail footprint comprises 276 stores across 10 states covering 198 cities[^ip-latest]. Store distribution by state: West Bengal 101, Uttar Pradesh 39, Bihar 37, Assam 37, Odisha 36, Jharkhand 16, Andhra Pradesh 5, Tripura 3, Madhya Pradesh 1, Arunachal Pradesh 1[^ip-latest]. Distribution infrastructure is being upgraded with a hub-and-spoke model — current base of one large West Bengal warehouse supplemented by collection centres in Assam and Bihar, with two Regional Distribution Centres (East and Central India) and 5-7 collection centres planned over 1-1.5 years[^cc-transcript]. Total rented warehouse area added ~1.22 lakh sq ft in FY25 (cumulative ~3.12 lakh sq ft)[^ar-fy25], reflecting aggressive supply chain scaling to support the 500-store target by FY29[^cc-transcript]. Tier-wise presence: Metro Kolkata 4%, Tier-1 18%, Tier-2 9%, Tier-3 25%, Tier-4 44%[^ip-latest] — the 69% Tier-3/Tier-4 combined share is a defining characteristic of the model.

**Promoter Background and Group Context**

Baazar Style Retail Limited was founded by the Surana family with Mr. Shreyans Surana as Managing Director[^cc-transcript]. The promoter group, led by the Surana family along with related entities (Shreyans Creation, RPB Creation, Paridhi Creation — all promoter-controlled manufacturing/purchasing entities)[^ar-fy25], holds 44.80% of equity as of Jun 2026[^ip-latest]. The promoter identity is rooted in Kolkata with extensive garment trading and manufacturing heritage in Eastern India. Following the September 2024 IPO and the Q3 FY26 preferential warrant issue to Cupid Limited, promoter holding has stepped down from 62.68% (pre-IPO peak) to 44.80% as of June 2026[^ip-latest] — this represents legitimate dilution to fund growth rather than pledge or distress selling (no promoter pledge has been disclosed)[^ar-fy25]. The MD Mr. Shreyans Surana is the primary strategic voice in concalls, with Mr. Nitin Singhania as CFO providing financial discipline — the management's tone in concalls has consistently been confident, metric-driven, and growth-oriented while acknowledging operational challenges transparently[^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM)**

The Indian value fashion retail TAM is substantial. The broader Indian apparel market is estimated at ~USD 100 Bn, with the organised apparel retail penetration still below 15% (vs 35-40% in developed markets). Within this, the value fashion sub-segment (price points below Rs. 1,500) is estimated at Rs. 1.5-1.8 lakh Cr (~USD 18-22 Bn) and growing at 12-14% CAGR — driven by rising aspirational consumption among the bottom-of-the-pyramid middle class, increasing female workforce participation, and the post-pandemic formalisation tailwind. Eastern India specifically is structurally underpenetrated by organised retail — regional organised apparel density is 30-40% lower than the national average, providing a greenfield runway. While Baazar Style has not disclosed explicit market share figures in the Investor Presentation[^ip-latest], management describes the company as "One of the Leading Value Fashion Retailers in Eastern India"[^ip-latest] with dominant presence in West Bengal specifically.

**Industry CAGR**

The value fashion retail segment in India has compounded at 12-15% CAGR over FY19-FY24 (pre-IPO comparable data), with Baazar Style itself delivering a 4-year revenue CAGR of ~35% (significantly outpacing the industry)[^ip-latest]. The 4-year store count CAGR of ~26%[^ip-latest] confirms that growth has been a balanced mix of new store contribution plus like-for-like same-store sales growth (SSSG of 7% in Q1 FY27, 12.7% in FY25, 4-5% guided for FY26 reflecting regional disruptions)[^ip-latest][^cc-transcript][^ar-fy25]. Industry growth is supported by structural demographic tailwinds — India's median age of 28 years, rising Tier-3/Tier-4 disposable income, and the accelerating shift from unorganised kirana stores to organised branded retail.

**Policy Tailwinds**

Multiple policy frameworks support the organised retail ramp-up: (i) **GST rationalisation** — the equalisation of GST rates across fibres (post the 2021 rationalisation from 5-12% differential to 5-12% standard slabs) has reduced working capital intensity for organised players versus the unorganised sector; (ii) **PLI Scheme for Textiles** — though Baazar Style is a retailer rather than a manufacturer, the broader PLI support for textile manufacturing reduces input cost inflation pressure; (iii) **Infrastructure Capex** — the Eastern India focus states (Bihar, Odisha, West Bengal, Assam) are major beneficiaries of central infrastructure spending (roads, rail, urban development) that drives Tier-3/Tier-4 disposable income growth; (iv) **ONDC and Digital Public Infrastructure** — though Baazar is offline-only currently, the broader DPI rollout (UPI, Account Aggregator) reduces customer acquisition costs for organised retailers entering smaller cities; (v) **State-level retail policies** — West Bengal, Assam, and other focus states have been progressively simplifying retail operating licences, though Eastern India still has comparatively more fragmented retail regulation than Maharashtra or Karnataka.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure**

The Surana family, led by Managing Director Mr. Shreyans Surana[^cc-transcript], has been the founding promoter of Baazar Style with a multi-decade presence in the Eastern Indian garment trade and manufacturing ecosystem. The promoter group includes related entities (Shreyans Creation, RPB Creation, Paridhi Creation, D M Garments, RPB Fashion)[^ar-fy25] that have been operating in the garment supply chain well before the formal incorporation of the retail company. Post the September 2024 IPO and the Q3 FY26 Cupid preferential warrant issue, promoter shareholding has stepped down from a pre-IPO peak of 62.68% to 44.80% as of June 2026[^ip-latest] — this dilution is strategic (growth capital) rather than distress-driven (no pledging disclosed)[^ar-fy25]. Promoter tenure extends through multiple economic cycles, providing institutional knowledge of Eastern India consumer behaviour, supplier networks, and state-level regulatory nuances that is difficult for national competitors to replicate. Two key functional heads — Mr. Hirak Banerjee (VP-Supply Chain) and Mrs. Sushmita Banerjee (CHRO) — resigned on May 14, 2025[^ar-fy25], which is a moderate yellow flag worth tracking for management continuity; however, the top management (MD + CFO) remains stable with Mr. Nitin Singhania as CFO providing financial discipline[^cc-transcript].

However, the consolidated FY25 OCF of Rs. 527 Mn and FY26 OCF of Rs. 1,255 Mn (down substantially from standalone reporting)[^ip-latest] suggests the consolidated picture captures lease repayments and working capital build. **Dividend Policy**

Baazar Style has paid no dividend since listing in September 2024[^ar-fy25]. The 5-year average dividend payout ratio is effectively 0% — given the company was unlisted pre-FY24, the dividend track record cannot be fully assessed. Management's stance is that all internal accruals are being ploughed back into store expansion (60-80 stores/year vs original 40-50 plan)[^cc-transcript], warehouse infrastructure (Rs. 20-25 Cr per year)[^cc-transcript], and technology (Rs. 7-10 Cr in FY26 for SAP/Infor/Goldratt stack)[^cc-transcript]. For a sub-scale value retailer in expansion mode, the dividend skip is justified, but **investors requiring dividend yield need to look elsewhere** — at current price, dividend yield is 0.00%[^ip-latest]. Dividend initiation is not expected before the company reaches scale economies (likely FY28-FY29 timeframe).

**Promoter Pledging Status**

**No promoter pledging disclosed**[^ar-fy25] — this is the cleanest indicator that the dilution in promoter stake (from 62.68% peak to 44.80% as of June 2026)[^ip-latest] has been through legitimate capital-raising and not through loan collateralisation. The promoter family's continued substantial economic interest in equity (44.80% even after dilution) provides alignment with minority shareholders.
