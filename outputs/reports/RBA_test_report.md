### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **India Burger King at structural EBITDA inflection — QSR turnaround is finally showing up in numbers.** RBA's India Burger King business (master franchisee of Restaurant Brands International) has been the standout performer on same-store sales growth (SSSG) versus listed QSR peers, with management commentary on the Q1 FY27 concall flagging sustained double-digit SSSG driven by value-engineered platforms like the Rs. 99 King Saver Menu and aggressive digital couponing through the BK App [^cc-transcript]. The Investor Presentation underscores India Burger King has crossed the store-level cash break-even threshold and is now contributing meaningfully to consolidated EBITDA, marking a clear pivot from the historical narrative of perpetual store-level losses [^ip-latest].

- **Margin story is being driven by royalty rationalisation and supply chain localisation, not just topline.** As per the Annual Report, RBA has renegotiated the master franchise royalty rate with RBI and is on a glide path to reduce royalty outflows by ~150–200 bps over the medium term [^ar-fy25]. Combined with backward integration into buns, sauces and patty manufacturing via captive/partner facilities, gross margin has expanded materially versus FY24 levels [^ip-latest]. This is critical because royalty is the single largest fixed drain on a master-franchisee P&L and the prior management had been unsuccessful in extracting concessions.

- **Diversification into Popeyes (Indonesia) and BK Cafe (India) creates a multi-brand platform, though execution risk remains elevated.** The 2022 rebrand to Restaurant Brands Asia and the acquisition of exclusive master franchise rights for Burger King and Popeyes in Indonesia was the strategic pivot to build a South/Southeast Asia QSR platform [^ar-fy25]. BK Cafe, launched in late 2021, provides a higher-margin day-part (morning) and improves asset utilisation without incremental real estate [^ip-latest]. However, Indonesia remains a drag and is the largest single swing factor in the consolidated P&L — the value of the platform thesis hinges on Indonesia turning contribution-positive by FY28 [^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months): festive season SSSG, Indonesia store rationalisation, and possible strategic stake / promoter infusion.** Q2 FY27 (current quarter) opens with Onam, Ganesh Chaturthi and the build-up to the festive window — historically RBA's strongest quarter on dine-in mix and average transaction value [^cc-transcript]. The concall also flagged a deliberate slowdown in Indonesia new-store openings to focus on AUV and breakeven of existing stores, which should narrow segment losses [^cc-transcript]. Separately, the Agrawal Family Office (promoters of Ajanta Pharma) entry as a strategic investor, as flagged in the family office press release, is a constructive signal on promoter-level confidence and could be a precursor to further primary capital infusion at attractive valuations [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk: equity dilution overhang and Indonesia cash burn.** RBA's share count has expanded materially over the last 5 years through multiple rights issues and QIPs to fund Indonesia capex and India losses, which is a key reason the stock has underperformed the broader QSR index despite a strong underlying India franchise [^vp-thread]. Until Indonesia demonstrates credible unit economics and the consolidated entity generates positive PAT, RBA remains a "show me" story; any disappointment on SSSG or another equity raise would re-rate the stock sharply lower. Sustained Indonesia losses and another dilution event is the single largest tail risk to our BUY thesis [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Business model — Asset-light master franchisee model with two distinct geographic segments.** RBA operates Burger King restaurants in India and (since 2022) in Indonesia, plus Popeyes restaurants in Indonesia, under exclusive master franchise agreements with Restaurant Brands International (RBI). Revenue is generated from dine-in, takeaway, delivery (own-app and aggregators) and drive-through, with the standard QSR cost stack of food & paper costs, restaurant-level operating expenses, marketing, and a royalty / advertising fund contribution back to RBI [^ip-latest]. The BK Cafe extension, launched in late 2021, leverages existing restaurant real estate to add a higher-margin morning day-part with coffee, breakfast sandwiches and bakery items [^ar-fy25].

- **Revenue split by division (FY25A consolidated).** Based on the Annual Report disclosures, the consolidated revenue mix is approximately India Burger King (~78–82% of total), Indonesia Burger King (~12–15%) and Popeyes Indonesia (~3–5%), with the residual from inter-company and other items [^ar-fy25]. The India business is the cash-generative engine and is now driving consolidated EBITDA positive territory; Indonesia is the key swing factor and is currently a drag on consolidated margins [^ip-latest]. The 2022 strategic shift to a multi-geography platform was explicitly designed to diversify away from single-country QSR risk [^ar-fy25].

- **Key OEM/end customers — B2C QSR, no OEM exposure.** Unlike auto component peers, RBA's customers are end-consumers across dine-in, takeaway, delivery (Swiggy, Zomato) and drive-through channels. The "customers" in the institutional sense are the digital aggregator platforms (Swiggy, Zomato, ONDC) and the RBI franchisor (royalty counterparty) [^ip-latest]. The Indian business is anchored in Tier-1 and Tier-2 metro and mini-metro markets, while Indonesia is concentrated in Greater Jakarta, Bandung, Surabaya and Bali [^ar-fy25].

- **Subsidiary structure — Promoted by QSR Asia Pte Ltd, with Indonesian operations housed in PT Sari Burger Indonesia and PT Pop Indonesia.** QSR Asia Pte. Ltd., a Singapore-incorporated JV between Everstone Capital and RBIH (Restaurant Brands International's emerging markets arm), holds the controlling 48.35% stake in RBA as promoter [^ar-fy25]. The Indonesian master franchise rights are held through step-down subsidiaries — PT Sari Burger Indonesia (Burger King) and a separate vehicle for Popeyes — giving the group a clean holding structure for future capital raising at the subsidiary level if required [^ar-fy25].

- **Manufacturing footprint — Asset-light, real estate on long-lease, central commissaries.** RBA operates a central commissary / cloud-kitchen supply infrastructure in India that supplies buns, sauces, patties and other inputs to Burger King restaurants, enabling tight food cost control and standardisation [^ip-latest]. Restaurant-level real estate is largely on long-lease (typically 15–20 year tenure) in high-street and mall locations. Indonesia operations run a similar asset-light model, with commissaries in Jakarta. The India restaurant network stands at over 400 Burger King restaurants across 80+ cities as of Q1 FY27 [^cc-transcript], with continued runway for 50–70 net additions per year.

- **Promoter background and group context.** RBA is a professionally-managed QSR platform, not a family-promoted business. The promoter QSR Asia Pte. Ltd. is a JV between Everstone Capital (a leading India-focused PE firm with prior QSR experience via its earlier Burger King South Asia exposure) and RBIH, giving the issuer direct alignment with the global Burger King franchisor and access to operating playbook and supply chain know-how [^ar-fy25]. This is a meaningful structural advantage versus standalone franchisees, and explains why RBA has been able to negotiate royalty reductions and tap RBI's international supply chain [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **Indian QSR TAM and growth.** The Indian organised QSR market is estimated at ~Rs. 55,000–60,000 Cr as of FY25 and is projected to grow at a 15–18% CAGR through FY30, driven by urbanisation, rising disposable incomes, nuclear family structures, and the formalisation of "away-from-home" food spending [^vp-thread]. Within this, the burger sub-segment is estimated at ~Rs. 8,000–10,000 Cr and growing at ~20%+ CAGR, well above the broader QSR average, supported by India's young demographic skew and the relatively under-penetrated burger category versus Domino's-led pizza or Cafe Coffee Day-led cafe chains [^vp-thread].

- **Relevant policy and consumption tailwinds.** While the QSR sector does not directly benefit from PLI or FAME (auto EV) schemes, indirect tailwinds include (1) sustained real wage growth and discretionary consumption recovery post-FY25, (2) GST stability for restaurant services, (3) state-level incentives for food processing / cold-chain capex that RBA's commissary expansion can access, and (4) infrastructure capex (metro rail, highways, airport modernisation) creating high-quality real estate at attractive rentals in Tier-2 cities — RBA's key white-space expansion corridor [^ip-latest]. The RBI rate-cut cycle, if it extends through CY26, would also support discretionary consumption.

- **Competitive moat analysis — Moderate, with select pockets of strength.**
  - *Brand & franchise IP (Moderate to Strong):* The Burger King brand has strong global recall, but in India the brand lags Domino's (Jubilant FoodWorks) and McDonald's (Westlife) on unaided awareness. Defensible but not dominant [^vp-thread].
  - *Switching costs (Weak):* QSR has near-zero consumer switching cost; loyalty is purely price / convenience driven. RBA's investment in the BK App and loyalty programme is an attempt to build a soft moat [^cc-transcript].
  - *Scale economics (Moderate):* Royalty and advertising fund contributions are fixed percentages but deliver scale benefits; RBA's commissary model delivers food cost leverage at higher store counts [^ip-latest].
  - *Real estate and location (Moderate):* Long-lease mall and high-street locations are partially defensible against new entrants, though not unique.
  - *Pricing power (Moderate, conditional on value perception):* RBA has used the Rs. 99 King Saver Menu to drive transactions but cannot materially push pricing without volume risk — a structural constraint versus Domino's loyalty model [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree — QSR Asia JV between Everstone Capital and RBIH, professional management.** The promoter QSR Asia Pte. Ltd. is a Singapore JV combining Everstone Capital (a credible India-focused PE franchise with prior QSR operating experience) and RBIH (the franchisor's emerging markets vehicle). This is meaningfully different from a family-promoted business and brings (a) direct access to RBI's global supply chain and playbook, (b) institutional governance and (c) operating depth that a typical franchisee would lack [^ar-fy25]. The Indian CEO and management team have been in seat through the post-IPO phase and retain continuity into the multi-geography phase [^cc-transcript].

- **FCF deployment track record — Reinvesting for growth, no dividend, no buyback.** FCF has been negative across FY22–FY25 reflecting Indonesia capex and India store additions; the Annual Report shows cumulative capex of over Rs. 800 Cr over the period, primarily into Indonesia new stores and India restaurant fit-outs [^ar-fy25]. There has been zero dividend payout (consistent with a growth-stage QSR) and no buyback. Capital has been deployed into (1) India store expansion, (2) Indonesia greenfield, and (3) commissary / supply chain infrastructure — all defensible uses, though the ROI on Indonesia is the open question [^ar-fy25].

- **Debt management — Conservatively levered at India level, leveraged at consolidated level.** India standalone is essentially net-debt-free or modestly net cash, with debt limited to lease liabilities under Ind AS 116 [^ar-fy25]. Consolidated leverage is higher due to Indonesia-funded debt and operating lease obligations. RBA has not raised material long-term rupee debt — the funding mix has been equity (rights issues, QIPs), internal accruals and short-term working capital lines. Credit ratings have remained stable in the AA-equivalent / A+ range for bank lines at the India subsidiary level [^ar-fy25]. Debt service coverage is comfortable at the India level but the consolidated entity's negative EBITDA in prior years constrained conventional debt capacity — which is why RBA has leaned on equity.

- **Dividend policy — Nil payout, growth reinvestment.** RBA has not paid any dividend since listing, consistent with peer QSR operators at similar stages (Westlife and Devyani also pay nominal / no dividend). Payout ratio is 0%; 5-year average dividend yield is 0.00%. We do not model any dividend through FY28E given reinvestment requirements [^ar-fy25].

- **Promoter pledging — Nil.** As per the latest shareholding pattern (Q1 FY27), promoter QSR Asia Pte. Ltd.'s 48.35% stake is unencumbered, with zero pledging. This is a meaningful positive for an entity that has gone through multiple equity rounds — there is no shadow overhang from forced selling [^ip-latest].

- **Corporate governance flags — Largely clean with one dilution concern.** Auditor has been consistent (Big-4 firm continuing across the period), no qualified audit opinions flagged in the Annual Report, and related-party transactions (RPTs) are limited to the standard royalty / advertising fund flows to RBI (a franchisor relationship, not arm's length concern) [^ar-fy25]. The single governance concern is the historical equity dilution — share count has expanded materially through rights issues and QIPs to fund Indonesia expansion, which has weighed on per-share metrics and is the structural reason RBA trades at a discount [^vp-thread]. The recent Agrawal Family Office strategic investment is a constructive signal and arguably the start of a more disciplined capital allocation phase.

- **JVs and partnerships — RBI master franchise, Everstone/RBIH promoter JV, Indonesia JV partners.** The defining partnership is the master franchise agreement with Restaurant Brands International, which gives RBA the exclusive rights to the Burger King and Popeyes brands in India (BK) and Indonesia (BK + Popeyes), plus access to RBI's menu innovation, supply chain and digital infrastructure [^ar-fy25]. The promoter-level Everstone–RBIH JV brings operating depth and franchisor alignment. At the restaurant level, RBA has selectively partnered with regional developers for Tier-2 / Tier-3 expansion to accelerate real estate sourcing without bearing 100% of capex — a capital-efficient model we expect to expand [^cc-transcript].
