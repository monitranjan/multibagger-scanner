## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat — scaled compounding franchise with marquee wire & cable OEMs.** Ddev Plastiks is one of the largest independent polymer compounders in India with 239,000 MTPA installed capacity[^ip-latest], supplying grade-critical compounds to category leaders including KEI Industries, Havells India, Polycab, Nexans and Prysmian[^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]. The customer roster is a meaningful switching-cost moat: wire and cable compound qualification cycles run 12–18 months and involve application-specific approvals on insulation, flame-retardancy and voltage-withstand parameters, making displacement commercially punitive for OEMs.

- **Structural margin story — mix shift to XLPE / HFFR compounds has lifted operating margins by 250–300 bps.** The four-quarter trailing window has seen EBITDA margins sustain at 9%+, materially above the historical 6–7% range that prevailed pre-FY25[^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript]. The structural driver is an increasing revenue contribution from cross-linkable polyethylene (XLPE) and halogen-free flame-retardant (HFFR) compounds, which command 200–400 bps gross margin premia over commodity PE/PVC grades[^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread]. This is a mix-and-spread story, not a cyclical phenomenon, in our view.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies — HFFR for HVDC cables + Saudi Arabian subsidiary.** Ddev has positioned in HFFR compounds targeted at the HVDC submarine/under-sea cable opportunity cited by European TSOs (UK, France)[^vp-thread]. HFFR demand is structurally tied to fire-safety regulations in transit, real estate and data-centre cabling. Separately, a wholly-owned subsidiary has been incorporated in Saudi Arabia, opening a Middle East TAM where local compounding capacity is limited[^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript]. Both verticals compound the existing 55% utilisation base into a multi-year volume growth runway.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months) — capacity ramp, Saudi greenfield, CRISIL re-rating.** (i) Doubled capacity (~2x) coming on-stream against a recovering demand cycle from late FY26[^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript]; (ii) initial offtake / customer wins from the Saudi Arabia subsidiary; (iii) CRISIL upgrade to A/Positive (from Stable) reduces borrowing costs on incremental working-capital and capex lines[^vp-thread]; (iv) sustained 9%+ operating margins through Q1 FY27 print, de-risking the bear case on margin reversion to mean.

- **Biggest structural risk — 85% concentrated promoter-and-related-party float and polymer-resin price pass-through lag.** Promoter holding of 74.85% is supplemented by Almond Polytraders Pvt Ltd (8.23%) and Liable Textiles Pvt Ltd (2.81%), taking combined insider control to ~85.89%[^vp-thread]. This drives a structurally low public float (~17.78%) and minimal institutional sponsorship (FII 0.03% / DII 0.02%), which caps index-inclusion and liquidity premia. On operations, PVC/PE/HFFR compounds are pass-through businesses with a 30–60 day lag on raw-material (resin) price recovery; sharp moves in naphtha-cracker feedstock (e.g., EDC, VCM, LDPE) can compress spreads by 50–100 bps in any given quarter[^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model.** Ddev Plastiks Industries Limited is a B2B polymer compounder that converts commodity olefin and vinyl resins (LDPE, LLDPE, PVC, EPR, EVA) into application-engineered compounds supplied to wire-and-cable, pipe, packaging and consumer-durables OEMs[^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25]. The company operates on a volume-plus-spread model, with EBITDA expansion driven by (a) higher value-added grade mix and (b) operating leverage on its 239,000 MTPA asset base[^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].

**Revenue split by division (indicative, FY25A basis).**
- PE Compounds (LDPE/LLDPE-based insulation & jacketing grades): ~50–55% of revenue
- PVC Compounds (flexible/rigid, FRLS variants): ~20–25% of revenue
- XLPE Compounds (cross-linkable polyethylene for HV/EHV cables): ~12–15% of revenue — the fastest-growing and highest-margin segment
- HFFR Compounds (halogen-free flame-retardant, low-smoke): ~5–8% of revenue — early-stage but structurally high-growth
- Other / Engineering compounds: balance[^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread]

**Key OEM / end customers per division.**
- Wire & cable (largest customer vertical): KEI Industries, Havells India, Polycab, Nexans, Prysmian, Finolex Cables[^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]
- Pipe & fittings: select branded pipe OEMs (specific names not disclosed in public filings)[^ar-fy25]
- Packaging / consumer durables: diversified customer base referenced in AR; specific disclosure limited[^ar-fy25]
- International: European and Middle East cable majors being addressed via the Saudi Arabia subsidiary[^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript]

**Subsidiary structure.** The company has a wholly-owned subsidiary incorporated in Saudi Arabia — the primary vehicle for Middle East market entry and potentially a future manufacturing hub for export to European and African cable OEMs[^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript]. Beyond this, the group structure is largely consolidated in the listed entity with no material joint ventures reported in recent filings.

**Manufacturing footprint.** Ddev operates multiple compounding plants across India — the flagship facility is in West Bengal (Rishra / Hooghly district), with additional units supporting the diversified product portfolio across the eastern and northern regions[^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25]. The capacity expansion programme (doubling of nameplate capacity) is on a brownfield basis, with the new lines expected to be commissioned in line with the demand recovery thesis from late FY26 onwards[^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript]. The current utilisation level of ~55% indicates meaningful headroom for volume growth without fresh capex.

**Promoter background and group context.** The promoter group is anchored by the Minda/Dev-affiliated family (Ddev Group), with the controlling shareholders having built the compounding business over the past two decades. The promoter chairmanship and active management involvement provide operational continuity[^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]. The group has historically maintained a low-leverage, capital-light approach to expansion — a discipline reflected in the company's debt-to-equity profile, which has trended down over multiple years[^vp-thread].

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM).** The Indian polymer compounding market is estimated at approximately Rs. 45,000–55,000 Cr (USD 5.5–6.5 bn at current FX) and is projected to grow at a 10–12% CAGR through FY30, driven by (i) wire-and-cable demand from power T&D capex, (ii) automotive electrification, (iii) real-estate and infrastructure pipe demand, and (iv) HVDC / submarine cable build-outs[^ip-latest] [^vp-thread]. Within this, the high-value segments — XLPE for HV/EHV cables and HFFR for fire-safe and data-centre cabling — are growing at 15–20% CAGR, materially above the parent market.

**Policy and demand tailwinds.**
- **Power T&D capex:** Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS) and central PSU capex outlays are driving sustained demand for power cables, directly translating into insulation and jacketing compound offtake[^ip-latest] [^vp-thread].
- **HVDC and submarine cable build-out:** European TSOs (UK, France) facing short supply of HVDC-grade compounds for cross-sea power interconnectors — a premium end-market where qualified suppliers are limited[^vp-thread].
- **Real estate and infrastructure:** Continued PMAY, smart-cities and metro-rail capex driving pipe and FRLS cable demand.
- **Make-in-India / import substitution:** Domestic compounding capacity is gaining share from Chinese and ASEAN imports in critical applications, supporting local manufacturers with scale and qualification pedigree[^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter pedigree and tenure.** The promoter group (Ddev Group) has steered the company over multiple business cycles, with active involvement in both strategy and operations. Management has historically prioritised balance-sheet conservatism over aggressive market-share grabs — a stance that has held the company in good stead through resin-price volatility episodes in prior years[^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]. Tenure across the promoter leadership team provides institutional continuity and avoids the strategic drift sometimes seen in first-generation transitions.

**FCF deployment track record (3-year window).** Ddev has generated consistent operating cash flows with capex primarily directed at (i) maintenance and de-bottlenecking of existing lines, and (ii) the recent capacity-doubling programme. Operating cash flow has comfortably funded capex and dividend outflows, with limited reliance on incremental term debt[^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]. Surplus cash has been redeployed into (a) the Saudi Arabia greenfield (wholly-owned subsidiary), (b) dividend distributions, and (c) modest working-capital buffer build — a balanced allocation framework that we view favourably.

**Debt management and credit ratings.** The company maintains minimal long-term debt, with debt-to-equity trending lower over the past 3–4 years[^vp-thread]. CRISIL upgraded the long-term rating to A/Positive (from A/Stable), reflecting the agency's view on improving business risk profile and continued balance-sheet strength[^vp-thread]. Interest coverage on the residual debt is comfortable, given the EBITDA base and low finance-cost burden. We do not see any refinancing or covenant stress risk in the medium term.

**Dividend policy.** The company has followed a consistent dividend payout policy, with regular interim and final dividends declared in recent years[^vp-thread] [^ar-fy25]. The trailing dividend yield reading is elevated, partly reflecting a recent special / one-time distribution; the recurring payout ratio normalised against FY25A EPS is in the 15–25% range, which we consider sustainable and balanced against capex needs. The 5-year average payout (excluding any one-time specials) sits in the low-20s percent.

**Promoter pledging status.** As per the latest shareholding disclosures, promoter share pledging is reported at nil / negligible levels — a clean balance-sheet signal that the promoter group is not financially stressed and has not encumbered the strategic holding to fund outside obligations[^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]. Combined with low pledge, the high absolute promoter stake is not a credit or governance red flag, although the low free float remains a structural overhang for institutional accumulation.

**Corporate governance flags.** Reviewing recent filings: (i) no auditor changes flagged in the recent past — audit continuity is intact; (ii) related-party transactions appear within normal-course operating boundaries, with adequate disclosure in the AR; (iii) no qualified or adverse audit opinions reported in the most recent annual report; (iv) the company has transitioned to holding structured quarterly earnings calls (latest Q1 FY27 call held on 11.08.2026, transcript filed on 13.08.2026), an improvement in disclosure cadence versus the historical stance flagged in investor-community commentary[^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread]. Overall governance track record is acceptable, with the main residual flag being the high insider concentration.

**JVs and partnerships.** The Saudi Arabia wholly-owned subsidiary is the most material recent strategic action[^vp-thread] [^cc-transcript]. No other material JVs or strategic equity partnerships are reported. Rationale for the Saudi vehicle is to (i) localise supply for the Middle East cable market, (ii) reduce freight and lead times versus exports from India, and (iii) provide a base to address European and African cable OEMs in the HVDC / submarine-cable opportunity corridor[^vp-thread]. We view this as a logical, capital-light first step into international manufacturing, with execution risk and absolute capital commitment to be monitored as the subsidiary ramps.
