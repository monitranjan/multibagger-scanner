### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Sui Generis Moat — The Only Domestic EPC Player in the "Big-Ticket" Power Equipment Space with Government Backing**
BHEL is the only Indian engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) major that manufactures the entire BoP (Balance of Plant) and BTG (Boiler-Turbine-Generator) train for thermal power plants domestically [^ip-latest]. To put it simply: think of BHEL as the country's only "one-stop shop" for building a power plant — from the giant boiler that burns coal, to the turbine that spins, to the generator that produces electricity. It enjoys "sole Indian supplier" status in nuclear turbine generators and is the largest domestic player in super-critical boilers [^cc-transcript]. This positioning is virtually impossible to replicate — the capex required to set up even a single heavy forgings shop exceeds Rs. 5,000 Cr, and BHEL has 16 such manufacturing units spread across 9 states [^ip-latest]. Its market share in identified FGD (Flue Gas Desulphurization) installations is 30% of the ~1,67,000 MW opportunity — FGD systems are pollution-control "scrubbers" attached to coal plants to remove harmful sulphur dioxide from exhaust gases [^cc-transcript]. This regulatory + scale moat is structurally defensible against private players like L&T Power or Thermax who lack end-to-end BTG capabilities.

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story — From Sub-5% EBITDA to Double-Digit Run-Rate**
EBITDA margins have expanded from a depressed 4.90% in FY24 to 9.21% in FY26 [^ar-fy25]. In plain terms, on every Rs. 100 of revenue, BHEL now keeps Rs. 9.2 as operating profit (before interest, tax, and depreciation) versus Rs. 4.9 two years ago. Three structural drivers underpin this re-rating: (a) Engineering optimization savings of Rs. 600-700 Cr from in-house value-engineering initiatives [^cc-transcript]; (b) Manufacturing, administrative and selling expenses compressed from 9% to 7% of turnover, with Q4 FY22 hitting just 5.5% [^cc-transcript]; (c) Better product mix from higher-margin spares, services, and FGD retrofits (spares are replacement parts sold to existing customers — a stickier, higher-margin revenue stream). Notably, material costs at 72% of revenue (an "all-time high" per management) limits further near-term upside, but with order book size now 7.1x annual revenue, BHEL can selectively walk away from low-margin commodity contracts [^cc-transcript]. PAT tripled from Rs. 260 Cr in FY24 to Rs. 1,578 Cr in FY26 — a 6x jump in two years [^ar-fy25]. ROCE improved from 7.06% to 12.77% — the metric that measures how efficiently a company generates profit from its capital base [^ar-fy25].

**3. Diversification Beyond Thermal — Coal Gasification, KAVACH, Defence, and Hydrogen Optionality**
The single biggest narrative change in the BHEL story is the credible diversification away from the "single-product thermal power plant" tag. Coal gasification — converting India's abundant domestic coal into methanol/ammonia/hydrogen feedstocks instead of burning it directly — secured a maiden Rs. 8,288 Cr order from the BCGCL JV in FY26 [^ar-fy25]. Coal gasification is part of India's National Coal Gasification Mission to reduce import dependency on natural gas and crude oil. Rail signalling ("KAVACH" — India's indigenous train collision-avoidance system, akin to the European ETCS) — BHEL secured a maiden prototype development order [^ar-fy25]. Defence — delivered 4 upgraded SRGMs (Super Rapid Gun Mounts, the large-caliber naval artillery used on Indian Navy warships) ahead of schedule, with management calling the segment "bullish" and growing [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript]. Hydrogen — a dedicated business group set up; in Aug 2026, BHEL signed an SCA (Specific Cooperation Agreement) with Norway's Hystar AS for PEM (Proton Exchange Membrane) electrolyser technology to manufacture green hydrogen production equipment domestically. These adjacencies could account for ~15-20% of incremental revenue over FY27E-FY29E, materially de-risking the "thermal is dying" bear case.

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6-12 Months): Rs. 2.4 Lakh Cr Order Book + Visible Inflows**
The order book stands at an all-time high of Rs. 2,40,000 Cr as of March 2026, providing ~7.1 years of revenue cover at FY26 run-rate [^ip-latest]. Recent order inflows of Rs. 21,000 Cr were reported in early Jun 2026, providing near-term re-rating ammunition [^ip-latest]. Specifically: (i) The 7,700 MW coal-based pipeline (~Rs. 45,000-50,000 Cr opportunity) — projects at Talabira (3x800 MW for NLC), Bhandora (2x800 MW for Adani), Talcher (2x660 MW), Singrauli (2x800 MW), and Lara Phase-2 (2x800 MW) are in various stages of bidding [^cc-transcript]. (ii) Order inflow of Rs. 75,916 Cr in FY26 was a sharp jump — the historical norm was Rs. 20,000-30,000 Cr annually [^ip-latest]. (iii) Working capital release of Rs. 586 Cr despite 24% revenue growth, plus CFO of Rs. 5,837 Cr significantly exceeding PAT of Rs. 1,578 Cr — strong cash conversion implies no near-term equity dilution risk [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript]. (iv) Credit rating upgrade by India Ratings & Research in Aug 2026 — a structural positive for borrowing costs [^ip-latest].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Thermal Power Capex Cyclicality and Execution Slippage**
The bear case is real: India's stated renewable targets (500 GW non-fossil by 2030) and nuclear scale-up (100 GW by 2047) could structurally erode the addressable thermal market over a 5-7 year horizon [^ar-fy25]. Even with the visible 7,700 MW pipeline, the long-tail thermal order book beyond FY28 is uncertain. The second risk is execution: BHEL historically suffers from project delays, and contingent liabilities (potential future obligations depending on how lawsuits or disputes resolve) of Rs. 6,610 Cr — ~25% of net worth — are dominated by liquidated damages (pre-agreed penalty payments if BHEL misses delivery deadlines, currently Rs. 3,673 Cr) and tax disputes [^ar-fy25]. Material cost at 72% of revenue with index-linked but capped Price Variation Clauses (PVC — contractual mechanisms that allow some pass-through of input cost changes to customers) compresses margins if commodity prices spike again [^cc-transcript]. Finally, board composition is currently non-compliant with SEBI's requirement for adequate independent directors and the absence of a female independent director — a governance red flag that could trigger regulatory penalties or shareholder activism [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model — Heavy Engineering EPC Contractor with Manufacturing Backbone**
BHEL operates as a vertically integrated EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction) contractor for power and industrial projects, with end-to-end in-house manufacturing capability across 16 production units, 4 regional offices, 8 service centres, and 15 regional marketing centres [^ip-latest]. In plain English, this means BHEL doesn't just design a power plant — it builds every major component in its own factories, then assembles it at the customer's site. The company earns revenue across three streams: (a) Equipment supply (boilers, turbines, generators, transformers); (b) EPC contracts for full plant construction (including civil works, erection, and commissioning); and (c) Spares & Services (aftermarket revenue from existing installed base). FY26 revenue from operations was Rs. 33,782 Cr (up 19% YoY), with PAT of Rs. 1,578 Cr (up ~6x vs. FY24) [^ar-fy25] [^ip-latest].

**Revenue Split by Division (% of Revenue, FY26 Basis):**
- **Power Segment (~75% of revenue):** This is the legacy thermal/hydro/nuclear business. Comprises (i) Thermal BTG (Boiler-Turbine-Generator sets, the core "engine" of a coal-fired power plant); (ii) Hydro sets; (iv) Nuclear sets (BHEL is the sole Indian supplier of nuclear turbine generators); (v) FGD retrofits (Flue Gas Desulphurization — pollution control equipment retrofitted to older coal plants); and (vi) Spares & Services [^ar-fy25]. Power segment contributed Rs. 17,931 Cr to order inflow in FY22, with order book of Rs. 85,664 Cr (and grown substantially by FY26) [^cc-transcript].
- **Industry Segment (~25% of revenue):** Includes (i) Transportation (railway locomotives, traction motors, propulsion systems for Vande Bharat trains — India's indigenous semi-high-speed rail service); (ii) Transmission (transformers, switchgear — high-voltage electrical equipment used in power distribution networks); (iii) Defence (SRGMs for Indian Navy warships, aerospace components); (iv) Captive Power (small power plants built for individual industrial customers); and (v) Renewables (floating solar, where BHEL holds ~67% market share of India's commissioned capacity) [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].
- **Export (~1% of Order Book):** Rs. 3,362 Cr as of 31-Mar-2026, growing but still a small fraction [^ip-latest].

**Key End Customers per Division:**
- **Power:** NTPC (India's largest power producer, central PSU), state electricity boards (state-owned distribution utilities like Uttar Pradesh Power Corporation, etc.), private players like Adani Power, Neyveli Lignite Corporation (NLC) — a government-owned lignite mining and power generation company. Receivables mix: 36% Central PSU, 41% state utilities, 14% private, 8% export — heavy concentration in government customers (77% combined) which provides credit quality but also exposes BHEL to government capex cycles and payment delays [^cc-transcript].
- **Defence:** Indian Navy (sole supplier of upgraded SRGMs), DRDO (Defence Research and Development Organisation), Indian Army [^cc-transcript].
- **Railways:** ICF Chennai (Integral Coach Factory — the government's railway coach manufacturer), for Vande Bharat propulsion systems [^cc-transcript].
- **Nuclear:** NPCIL (Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited — government-owned, monopoly operator of India's nuclear power plants), in fleet mode procurement (NPCIL bulk-orders multiple units of the same reactor design to drive standardization and cost efficiency) [^cc-transcript].
- **Industry/Captive:** Various private industrial customers including steel, cement, and petrochemical plants.

**Subsidiary & Joint Venture Structure:**
BHEL has multiple JVs, the most notable being:
- **BHEL-GE Gas Turbine (BGGT):** JV with GE for gas turbine manufacturing
- **NTPC-BHEL Power Projects (NBPPL):** JV with NTPC for power project execution
- **Latur Power (under resolution):** Stressed SPV (Special Purpose Vehicle — a subsidiary created for one specific project)
- **Raichur Power Corporation Ltd:** JV with KPCL (Karnataka Power Corporation Limited) — operational
- **Bina Power Supply Co. Ltd.:** JV — operational
Sales to JVs were ~Rs. 365 Cr in FY26 (~1.13% of revenue) — minimal RPT (Related Party Transaction) risk [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
16 manufacturing units across 9 states, including heavy fabrication plants at Bhopal, Hyderabad, Haridwar, Jhansi, Ranipet, Trichy, and Bangalore. R&D spend at ~2.4% of revenue [^ip-latest]. 6,068 patents and copyrights cumulative up to FY26 — a meaningful IP portfolio, though not directly monetizable. 12 manufacturing units have received GreenCo certification (an Indian rating for manufacturing sustainability and environmental performance) [^ip-latest]. Total installed capacity-equivalent: BHEL has cumulatively commissioned ~8.9 GW of power capacity in FY26 alone [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background and Group Context:**
BHEL is a CPSE (Central Public Sector Enterprise — a company majority-owned by the Government of India) under the administrative control of the Ministry of Heavy Industries. The Government of India (GoI) is the promoter with 58.17% stake as of June 2026 (down from 63.17% pre-Feb 2025 following an OFS — Offer for Sale where the government sold some of its shares to the public to raise capital and reduce its holding — of 17.42 Cr shares at Rs. 256.59, raising Rs. 4,470 Cr) [^ip-latest]. The GoI stake has been progressively declining as part of the broader disinvestment programme, but the government remains the majority shareholder. CMD Dr. Nalin Shinghal has been at the helm since the Q4FY22 concall (May 2022), bringing techno-managerial experience from the power sector. Director Finance Subodh Gupta has handled financial disclosures during his tenure [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) — India Power and Heavy Engineering**
The Indian power equipment TAM is estimated at Rs. 4-5 lakh Cr over FY25-FY30, considering: (a) Thermal capex of Rs. 2-2.5 lakh Cr (315 GW target by FY32 implies ~80-100 GW new build at Rs. 6-8 Cr/MW); (b) Nuclear at Rs. 1-1.5 lakh Cr (100 GW by 2047 target); (c) Hydro and Pumped Storage at Rs. 50,000 Cr (100 GW target); and (d) Transmission at Rs. 80,000-1,00,000 Cr (inter-state and intra-state upgrades). At a conservative blended growth rate, this implies a ~12-15% CAGR in addressable power equipment spend over FY25-FY30. Globally, the heavy electrical equipment market is ~USD 250-280 Bn (Rs. 20-22 lakh Cr), growing at ~5-6% CAGR with India being the fastest-growing geography [^ar-fy25].

**Policy Tailwinds — PLI, Aatmanirbhar Bharat, FAME, RE Targets**
- **Coal-to-Chemicals Mission:** Government commitment to gasify 100 MT (million tonnes) of coal by 2030 — direct beneficiary of BHEL's coal gasification technology [^cc-transcript].
- **Defence Indigenisation:** Rs. 50,000 Cr defence export target and 'Make in India' mandates favour BHEL as a sole/lead domestic supplier [^vp-thread].
- **Nuclear Expansion:** Fleet mode procurement by NPCIL (10 indigenous reactors in fleet mode) — BHEL has secured the Rs. 10,800 Cr order for 6 units of 700 MWe TG package (Turbine Generator — the equipment that converts nuclear steam energy into electricity) [^cc-transcript].
- **Rail Modernisation:** KAVACH deployment across 68,000 km of Indian Railways network — BHEL has prototype order [^ar-fy25].
- **Net Zero by 2047:** National commitment driving investment in coal gasification, FGD retrofits, and clean coal technologies — all BHEL domains [^ar-fy25].
- **FAME / EV:** Limited direct impact on BHEL (which is B2B heavy industrial), but indirectly drives transmission capex.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure**
The Government of India (through the Ministry of Heavy Industries) is the promoter with a current stake of 58.17% as of June 2026 — down from 63.17% pre-Feb 2025 OFS, with 17.42 Cr shares divested at Rs. 256.59 to raise Rs. 4,470 Cr [^ip-latest]. There is no pledging of promoter shares (typical for a CPSE, but nonetheless a positive signal). The CMD (Chairman & Managing Director) Dr. Nalin Shinghal has been at the helm since 2022 (as confirmed in the Q4FY22 concall transcript), bringing techno-managerial domain expertise [^cc-transcript]. Director Finance Subodh Gupta has handled financial disclosures during his tenure. Director Power U.S. Matharu oversees the power segment. Recent corporate announcement dated 25-Aug-2026 disclosed a "Change in Management" — indicating possible senior management reshuffle which we will monitor closely for directional implications [^ip-latest]. Board composition has been non-compliant with SEBI's requirement for adequate independent directors and absence of a female independent director — a governance red flag that requires attention [^ar-fy25].

**Free Cash Flow Deployment Track Record (3-Year)**
This is where BHEL's story has materially improved. CFO (Cash Flow from Operations — the actual cash a company generates from its core business activities) for FY26 was Rs. 5,837 Cr — a remarkable 3.7x PAT (Profit After Tax) conversion, indicating high-quality earnings (cash collected from customers significantly exceeds accounting profit). Cash from Operations grew 176% YoY [^ar-fy25]. Working capital release of Rs. 586 Cr was achieved despite revenue rising 24% in FY22 alone [^cc-transcript]. Capex has been modest at Rs. 671 Cr in FY26 (vs. Rs. 536 Cr in FY25, and a planned Rs. 231 Cr for FY23) — funded entirely internally, indicating abundant self-funded growth capacity [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript]. Long-term committed capex areas: ISRO tankages (Rs. 200+ Cr), upgraded SRGMs (Rs. 140 Cr), and "substantial CAPEX" for coal gasification. Asset monetization (sale of non-core land/properties) was flagged as a near-term potential catalyst but lacked clear timelines — a watch-out for FY27 [^cc-transcript].

**Debt Management and Credit Profile**
BHEL's balance sheet is **net cash positive** (Cash & Bank: Rs. 11,867 Cr vs. Borrowings: Rs. 7,950 Cr) — net cash position of ~Rs. 3,917 Cr [^ar-fy25]. This is a structurally positive factor for a CPSE in a working-capital-intensive business. Total Debt/Assets stands at 0.11 — virtually no leverage. The company is debt-free on a long-term basis. Interest coverage ratio (EBITDA ÷ Interest expense — a measure of how easily a company can pay interest on outstanding debt) of ~4.2x (EBITDA Rs. 3,189 Cr / Interest Rs. 756 Cr) is healthy [^ar-fy25]. Credit rating upgrade by India Ratings & Research announced on 12-Aug-2026 is a structural positive — lower borrowing costs going forward [^ip-latest]. This net cash position provides optionality for buybacks, special dividends, or inorganic acquisitions — though management has not indicated any specific plans.

**Dividend Policy and Capital Return**
Historically, BHEL has paid modest dividends (typical payout ratio of 30-40% of PAT), consistent with CPSE norms. The Screener.in figure of 32% dividend yield appears to be a data anomaly (possibly reflecting cumulative TTM distributions against current market price or a one-time special dividend); we treat this with caution and note that the actual TTM dividend yield is likely in the 0.5-1% range based on historical payout patterns [^ip-latest]. Google news flow confirms an "upcoming dividend" was flagged as a potential near-term catalyst in May-Jun 2026 — supportive of the cash-rich balance sheet narrative [^ip-latest]. The GoI, as majority shareholder, typically prefers dividends over buybacks.

**Corporate Governance Flags**
- **Auditor Opinion:** Unqualified on FY26 financials — a clean audit is a positive signal for a CPSE [^ar-fy25].
- **CARO Report (Companies Auditor's Report Order):** No qualifications or adverse remarks [^ar-fy25].
- **RPTs (Related Party Transactions):** Minimal — sales to JVs at ~1.13% of revenue, no material suspicious flows [^ar-fy25].
- **Contingent Liabilities (potential future obligations if certain lawsuits/disputes go against the company):** Rs. 6,610 Cr — ~25% of net worth, dominated by liquidated damages (Rs. 3,673 Cr) and tax disputes [^ar-fy25]. This is material and warrants monitoring.
- **Working Capital Watch-out:** Inventory days increased to 150 (from 132), while payable days reduced to 165 (from 201) — partly indicating build-up for execution, but also signalling tightening of supplier terms [^ar-fy25].
- **Board Composition:** Non-compliant with independent director norms, no female independent director [^ar-fy25].
- **Management Remuneration:** Total KMP (Key Managerial Personnel — typically CMD, CFO, and Directors) remuneration of Rs. 4.76 Cr (~0.01% of revenue) — extremely low, typical for a CPSE [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Partnerships — Strategic Rationale**
BHEL has several operational and stressed JVs:
- **BHEL-GE Gas Turbine (BGGT):** JV with GE for gas turbine manufacturing — strategic positioning in gas-based power.
- **NTPC-BHEL Power Projects (NBPPL):** JV with NTPC for project execution — leverages NTPC's customer access and BHEL's manufacturing.
- **Hystar AS SCA (Aug 2026):** Specific Cooperation Agreement with Norwegian firm Hystar for PEM electrolyser technology — entry into green hydrogen equipment manufacturing, directly addressing India's National Green Hydrogen Mission [^ip-latest].
- **Discussions with Global OEMs (Rail, Defence, Aerospace):** Management has signalled active partnership exploration in rail transportation, defence, and aerospace for technology tie-ups [^cc-transcript].

**Capital Allocation Verdict**
Management has demonstrated improving capital allocation discipline over the past 3 years — capex funded internally, debt reduction, working capital release, and modest dividend payouts. However, the absence of buybacks (despite net cash position and the GoI likely favouring dividends for fiscal revenue) is a mild negative for minority shareholders. The FY27-FY28 watch-points are: (i) asset monetization execution (potential Rs. 1,000-2,000 Cr proceeds); (ii) coal gasification capex deployment; (iii) any inorganic moves in defence or rail signalling; (iv) dividend payout ratio in the next AGM [^vp-thread].
