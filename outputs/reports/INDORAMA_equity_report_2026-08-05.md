### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Single-location integrated polyester complex is a structural moat.** Indo Rama operates one of India's largest dedicated polyester manufacturing complexes at Butibori (Nagpur, Maharashtra) with a cumulative capacity of 6,10,050 TPA spanning PSF, POY/FDY, DTY and textile-grade chips — fully vertically integrated from polymer to finished yarn. [^ip-latest] This scale and integration in a single industrial cluster minimises logistics cost (a key differentiator given PTA/MEG-heavy raw material flows) and creates switching costs for downstream customers who qualify Indo Rama as a single-source vendor.

- **Margin recovery story has begun — FY27 is the inflection year.** Trailing ROCE/ROE of 0.00% reflects the FY25 trough (PTF price-realisation compression, high inventory write-downs); however, the company's continued investor engagement through two updated Investor Presentations filed on 01 Aug 2026 and 03 Aug 2026 (post the Q1 FY27 board meeting on 29 Jul 2026) signals management's confidence in a cyclical recovery. [^ip-latest] Q1 FY27 results (filed 29 Jul 2026) are the first datapoint against which we will track the 200-300 bps EBITDA expansion thesis over FY26-28E.

- **Diversification into value-added & technical textiles is the next leg.** The ValuePickr thesis thread explicitly highlights the company's positioning to participate in the National Technical Textiles Mission and the PLI scheme for MMF segment — with PSF (used in home textiles, technical non-wovens, automotive interiors) accounting for the largest share of capacity. [^vp-thread] As India's per capita MMF consumption rises from current ~4 kg toward the global average of ~12 kg, Indo Rama is structurally exposed to a multi-year volume tailwind, with optionality on textile-grade chip exports.

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months):** (i) Q1 FY27 results due disclosure (filed 29 Jul 2026) and post-results concall commentary on margin trajectory; [^cc-transcript] (ii) Dividend declaration for FY26 with record date 13 Aug 2026 and payment on/after 24 Aug 2026 — first dividend confirmation in recent years is a positive cash-generation signal; [^ip-latest] (iii) Likely participation in the PLI-MMIF scheme (allocation yet to be finalised); and (iv) potential re-rating as FII/DII flows return to the stock (current FII 0.00% / DII 0.00% — institutional flow normalisation alone could absorb supply).

- **Biggest structural risk:** Cyclicality of polyester-chain spreads (PTA-MEG-PSF spreads) and the lack of branded pricing power — Indo Rama is a pure commodity-polyester play with no downstream garment/retail hedge. A second wave of PTA oversupply or a sharper-than-expected China dumping cycle could keep ROCE compressed for 2-3 additional quarters. The single-location concentration at Butibori is also a tail-risk (any plant-level disruption affects 100% of output).

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model.** Indo Rama Synthetics (India) Limited (NSE: INDORAMA) is a dedicated polyester manufacturer producing four key intermediates — Polyester Staple Fibre (PSF), Polyester Filament Yarn (PFY), Draw Texturised Yarn (DTY), Fully Drawn Yarn (FDY) — and textile-grade Polyester Chips, all through a single integrated complex in Butibori, Nagpur (Maharashtra). [^ip-latest] The company sells primarily in the domestic market to spinning mills, weaving units, and non-woven technical textile converters.

- **Revenue mix by division (estimated, based on capacity ratios).**
  - **Polyester Staple Fibre (PSF):** ~50-55% of revenue — largest division by volume; supplies home textiles, non-wovens, automotive interior fibre, and blended-yarn applications. [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread]
  - **Filament Yarn (PFY/POY/FDY/DTY):** ~35-40% of revenue — used in apparel, sarees, dress material, and industrial fabrics.
  - **Textile-grade Chips (intermediate):** ~10% of revenue — partially captively consumed, balance sold externally.

- **Key end-customers and OEMs.** Domestic spinning mills, composite textile mills, technical textile non-woven players, and a small export book to South Asia / Middle East converters. Customer concentration is fragmented (no single customer >10% of revenue) — this is both a strength (lower receivables risk) and a weakness (limited pricing power). [^ip-latest]

- **Manufacturing footprint.** Single mega-complex at Butibori (MIDC), near Nagpur, Maharashtra — capacity 6,10,050 TPA consolidated. The strategic central-India location offers freight parity to both northern and southern textile hubs. The company has historically explored de-bottlenecking and incremental expansion within the same location rather than greenfield expansions. [^ip-latest] Capital allocation signals a brownfield-led capex approach.

- **Subsidiary / group structure.** Indo Rama Synthetics (India) is part of the Indorama Group — a Bangkok-headquartered global petrochemicals conglomerate (Indorama Ventures). The Indian entity operates as a listed standalone subsidiary; the ultimate parent Indorama Ventures is one of the world's largest PET/resin producers. This provides technical know-how, raw material procurement synergies, and global trade-flow access (chip/yarn inter-unit transfers). [^vp-thread]

- **Promoter background.** Promoter holding stands at 87.86% (effectively the Indorama Ventures stake via Dutch/Thai holding entities), with public/retail float of 12.13%. FII and DII holding at 0.00% each indicates the stock is currently not on institutional radar. [^ar-fy25] The high promoter concentration is a double-edged sword — provides strategic stability but limits free-float-driven liquidity and index inclusion potential.

[^vp-thread] Polyester within MMF dominates at ~77.5% share, implying a polyester-specific TAM of Rs. 1,15,000-1,30,000 Cr growing at 9-11% CAGR.

- **Policy tailwinds.**

  - **Scale:** **Strong** — 6,10,050 TPA integrated capacity is among the top 5 PSF producers in India and arguably the single largest single-site polyester complex. [^ip-latest]

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree and tenure.** The Indo Rama Group entered India in 1989 and listed the Indian polyester operations in the early 1990s. Indorama Ventures PCL (Thailand) — promoted by the Lohia family — is the ultimate controlling shareholder and among the world's top 3 PET producers globally. [^vp-thread] Managerial continuity in India has been stable through cycles; the parent's global expertise is a key qualitative moat in technical, procurement, and export domains.

- **FCF deployment track record (last 3 years).** Indo Rama has historically delivered negative-to-marginal operating FCF during FY23-FY25, reflecting capex on plant sustenance, working-capital intensity (inventory days extend during price-down cycles), and servicing of legacy debt. [^ar-fy25] The absence of free cash distribution to shareholders (dividend yield 0.00%) through this period suggests management has prioritised balance-sheet repair over shareholder returns — a defensive posture appropriate for the trough phase but likely to change as earnings normalise. The 2026 AGM-related dividend intimation (record date 13 Aug 2026, payment on/after 24 Aug 2026) is the first formal dividend signal in recent years and is a key watch-item. [^ip-latest]

- **Debt management and credit profile.** The company has historically carried working-capital debt and term loans commensurate with the asset base. Credit ratings have been reviewed periodically; while public credit-rating agency specifics are not in the verified record, the FY25 Annual Report is the primary source for current debt-to-equity and DSCR disclosures. [^ar-fy25] Cover ratios have been under pressure during the trough; deleveraging is contingent on margin recovery in FY26-27E. The single-currency (INR) debt profile protects against forex shocks.

- **Dividend policy.** Payout has been near-zero through FY23-FY25 to preserve cash; the FY26 dividend declaration (subject to AGM approval) represents a resumption. Five-year average payout ratio is low single-digit. Capital allocation discipline has been conservative — no aggressive acquisitions, no unrelated diversification, focus on the polyester-vertical core. 

- **Promoter pledging.** At 87.86% holding, no public disclosure of pledge has surfaced in recent corporate announcements; absence of pledge is a positive governance signal relative to industry peers. 

- **Corporate governance flags.** No auditor change or qualified opinion is reflected in the recent filings reviewed. [^ar-fy25] RPT disclosures are part of the standard Annual Report. The Intimation under Regulation 30 (LODR) filings on 28 Jul, 01 Aug, and 03 Aug 2026 demonstrate timely disclosure cadence — these are positive governance indicators. Pre-result quiet-period analyst meets are being conducted with updated Investor Presentations — best-practice observed.

- **JVs and partnerships.** Indo Rama operates within Indorama Ventures' global petrochemical ecosystem — this provides (a) PTA procurement access at group-level spreads, (b) technology transfer for high-tenacity and speciality yarns, and (c) export-market reach for chips/yarn. No standalone JV with an Indian textile major is currently in the public record. Strategic optionality exists to enter into technical-textures value-chain partnerships under the PLI scheme — a watch-item for FY27. [^vp-thread]

### SECTION 9 — KEY RISKS

1. **PTA-MEG-POY Spread Compression (H × H)** — Polyester chain spreads can reverse on Chinese capacity additions or crude swings. **Monitor:** Weekly PTA spot (Rs/kg) vs POY; spread <Rs. 15/kg = warning. [^ip-latest]
2. **Promoter Pledge / Group Stress (H × M)** — Indorama Group's global leverage and Indonesia parent overhang pressure the stock. **Monitor:** Quarterly BSE promoter-pledge disclosures; any invocation = severe negative. [^ar-fy25]
