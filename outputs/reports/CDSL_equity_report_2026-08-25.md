### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** CDSL is the largest securities depository in India by number of demat (dematerialized) accounts—think of a demat account as a digital locker where your shares are stored electronically instead of as paper certificates. As of Q1 FY27 (ended June 2026), it crossed 18.59 crore (185.9 million) BO (Beneficial Owner) accounts, representing roughly 80% market share, a leadership position held continuously since 2019 [^cc-transcript]. This scale creates a powerful network effect: because most Indian retail investors already park their securities with CDSL, brokers (called Depository Participants) and companies (called issuers) naturally default to CDSL, making customer switching costs (the hassle and expense of moving your locker to a competitor) very high. We rate this competitive moat as Strong.

- **Structural margin story:** CDSL operates a highly scalable infrastructure model. Once the technology platform is built, adding a new account or processing an extra transaction costs almost nothing. Standalone EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation and Amortization—a proxy for core operating profitability before non-cash and financing items) margins have historically been very high; for example, in Q1FY26 operating income was Rs 221 cr with EBITDA Rs 210 cr implying ~95% margin, though Q4FY26 saw lower reported EBITDA due to one-offs [^ip-latest]. The core annuity income from annual issuer charges (fees paid by companies whose securities are held) and transaction charges yields high Return on Capital Employed (ROCE—a measure of how efficiently a company uses all its capital, including debt and equity, to generate profits; TTM ROCE is 16.64% but excluding the recent Rs 62 cr building purchase incremental ROCE is higher as per investor community analysis) [^vp-thread]. The structural shift from paper to electronic holdings and rising retail participation supports margin durability.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Through subsidiaries, CDSL has expanded beyond core depository. CDSL Ventures Ltd (CVL, 54.25% held) provides KYC Registration Agency (KRA—a regulated entity that stores investor identity and address proofs digitally) services and Registrar to Issue (RTA—the entity that maintains shareholder records for companies) for 3,242 companies, processing 10.35 cr KYC records [^ip-latest]. Centrico Insurance Repository (CIRL, 52% held) manages 20.70 lakh policies in electronic insurance accounts [^ip-latest]. Countrywide Commodity Repository (CCRL, 100% held) handles electronic receipts for 9 non-agri commodities notified by WDRA [^ip-latest]. These adjacencies tap underpenetrated insurance and commodity demat Total Addressable Markets, reducing reliance on equity market cycles.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** Several regulatory tailwinds could re-rate the stock. SEBI's potential mandate for ~65,000 unlisted public limited companies to dematerialize their shares (currently optional) would create a massive new account and annual maintenance fee stream [^vp-thread]. Additionally, CDSL received SEBI approval to invest in ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce) on 13 Aug 2026, opening fintech ecosystem options [^cc-transcript]. The introduction of ISIN (unique 12-character code assigned to a security) issuance capability (currently in active engagement/testing) and unified KYC with CKYC are expected to go live within quarters, adding transaction revenues [^cc-transcript]. Also, a possible depository tariff hike by SEBI could lift realizations by 10–15% [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The primary risk is regulatory pricing intervention. In FY26, SEBI mandated cuts in KYC fetch charges from Rs 35 to Rs 28 (20% drop) and creation charges from Rs 20 to Rs 5 (75% drop) at CVL, which compressed subsidiary margins (PAT down 5% despite revenue +22%) [^cc-transcript]. If similar caps are extended to core depository transaction or annual issuer charges, CDSL's pristine margin profile could erode. Additionally, competition from NSDL in mutual fund folios and fintech-driven account opening could reduce incremental share gains (community notes CDSL lost ~420 bps incremental demat share to fintechs in a quarter) [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Core business model: CDSL is a securities depository—imagine a central digital bank for shares and bonds where instead of holding physical certificates, investors hold electronic records. It earns recurring fees from issuers (companies whose securities are held), transaction charges per demat debit/credit, IPO/company action processing, and e-services like e-voting, e-nomination, eKYC [^ip-latest]. The business is asset-light; revenue is earned in Indian Rupees and recognized largely on accrual basis.

Revenue split by division (% of revenue): For standalone Q4FY26 (latest detailed quarter in investor presentation), total income Rs 114 cr comprised Annual Issuer Income Rs 59 cr (~52%), Transaction Charges Rs 17 cr (~15%), IPO/CA (Corporate Action) Income Rs 47 cr (~41% though overlapping with other classifications in layout), Dividend from subsidiary Rs 25 cr, and other income residual [^ip-latest]. On a consolidated basis Q4FY26: Online Data Charge Rs 70 cr (largest, ~37%), Transaction Charges Rs 47 cr (~25%), Annual Issuer Income Rs 36 cr (~19%), IPO/CA Rs 12 cr (~6%), Other Income Rs 23 cr (~12%) [^ip-latest]. The standalone figures emphasize issuer and IPO linked fees; consolidated adds subsidiary-driven online data charges.

Key OEM/end customers per division: In core depository, customers are Depository Participants (DPs—banks and brokers like HDFC Bank, Zerodha, ICICI Securities) who interface with end investors; Issuers—live companies (35,922 in FY25 growing to 48,103 in Q4FY26) [^ip-latest]; and retail investors (BO accounts 1,801 lakh in Q4FY26) [^ip-latest]. For CVL, customers are SEBI-regulated intermediaries requiring KYC verification and corporates needing RTA services. For CIRL, customers are insurance companies and policyholders. For CCRL, customers are commodity warehouses and traders.

Subsidiary structure: Parent CDSL 100% consolidates CCRL (commodity repo), holds 54.25% of CVL (KYC/RTA), and 52% of CIRL (insurance repo) [^ip-latest]. No manufacturing footprint; operations are technology-driven from Mumbai HQ at Marathon Futurex, Lower Parel, with pan-India pin code coverage 98% via DP network of 574 participants [^ar-fy25].

Promoter background and group context: CDSL was incorporated in 1997, originally promoted by a consortium of banks and financial institutions; today promoter holding is nil (0.00%) as per latest shareholding, meaning it is professionally managed with widespread public ownership (85.26% public) [^ar-fy25]. The group context is that of a market infrastructure institution regulated by SEBI under the Depositories Act, akin to a utility for capital markets.
