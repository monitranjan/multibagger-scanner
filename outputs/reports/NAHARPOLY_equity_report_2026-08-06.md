### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Specialty-mix shift is the structural margin lever.** Nahar Poly Films is repositioning its product mix from commodity BOPP films towards higher-margin specialty films (thermal lamination, metallised high-barrier, label face stock, and coated films) which command 200–400 bps gross margin premium over standard BOPP and represent >35% of FY25 revenue versus ~20% three years ago [^ar-fy25]. Management has guided that specialty share will scale to 45–50% by FY28E, providing a durable EBITDA margin floor even in soft BOPP spreads [^cc-transcript].

- **Vertically integrated BOPP manufacturing with cost leadership in India.** With ~165,000 MTPA installed BOPP capacity across plants in Madhya Pradesh (Mandideep) and one international subsidiary footprint, Nahar Poly sits among the top-5 BOPP producers in India and operates at an asset utilisation of ~85% in FY25, well above the industry average of 70–75% [^ip-latest]. Vertical integration into in-house metallisation, coating, and converting gives ~300 bps EBITDA margin uplift versus pure-play BOPP peers [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into polyester (BOPET), CPP films, and flexible packaging adjacencies.** The company's expansion into BOPET (36,000 MTPA) and Cast Polypropylene (CPP, 18,000 MTPA) lines opens an addressable market pegged at Rs. 25,000 Cr in India growing at 11–12% CAGR, driven by FMCG, pharma, and e-commerce packaging demand [^ip-latest]. The recent commissioning of a 5-layer BOPET line is expected to contribute Rs. 180–200 Cr revenue at full utilisation by FY27E [^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months): (i) Q1FY27 results on 5 Aug 2026 — first full quarter reflecting new BOPET line output, (ii) dividend record date on 5 Aug 2026 signalling a healthy cash distribution cycle, (iii) potential re-rating as ROCE normalises post the FY25 special dividend payout, and (iv) commissioning of value-added coating capacity by Q4FY27E which should lift blended realisations by 3–4%** [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk: BOPP spread compression from Chinese dumping and PP raw material volatility.** BOPP is a cyclical, commodity-like business where realisations are tightly correlated to polypropylene (PP) prices; any sharp spike in crude-linked PP or aggressive Chinese BOPP exports into India can compress spreads by 150–200 bps and trigger inventory write-downs [^vp-thread]. The cyclicality is real — peers like Cosmo Films have demonstrated that EBITDA per kg can swing ±25% across cycles [^vp-thread].
