### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

1.  **Market Leadership in Critical Aerospace Components:** Unimech Aerospace holds a dominant position in manufacturing high-precision machined components and sub-assemblies for both commercial and military aircraft. The company is a Tier-1 supplier for specific critical aero-structure components for major global OEMs like Airbus and Boeing, and a key partner for HAL in various indigenous defence programs. Its specialized capabilities in advanced materials (e.g., Titanium alloys, composites) and complex geometries provide a significant competitive moat, making it difficult for new entrants to replicate its technical expertise and certifications.
2.  **Structural Margin Expansion Driven by Indigenization & Scale:** We anticipate a structural improvement in EBITDA margins from 22.5% in FY25 to ~26-27% by FY28E. This will be primarily driven by increased indigenization of components under 'Make in India' initiatives, higher value-added manufacturing with proprietary designs, and operating leverage from scaling up production for large, multi-year defence orders (e.g., Tejas Mk1A, AMCA). Furthermore, a favorable shift in revenue mix towards high-margin MRO (Maintenance, Repair, and Overhaul) services and upgrades for existing platforms will contribute an additional 150-200 bps to margins.
3.  **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies (Space & UAVs):** Unimech is strategically diversifying beyond traditional aircraft components into the burgeoning Indian space sector and Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). The company has secured initial contracts for propulsion system components for ISRO's upcoming missions and is developing proprietary sub-systems for military-grade drones. This diversification into high-growth, technology-intensive adjacencies, which currently contribute <5% of revenue, is expected to grow to 15-20% by FY28E, providing new revenue streams and reducing dependency on legacy aerospace cycles.
4.  **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** Key catalysts include the finalization of a significant order for structural components for the Indian Navy's Multi-Role Carrier-Borne Fighter (MRCBF) program (estimated Rs. 800-1000 Cr over 5 years), expected within H2 FY27. Additionally, the commissioning of its new advanced composites manufacturing facility in Bengaluru by Q4 FY27 will enable higher in-house production of lightweight components, improving cost efficiencies and opening avenues for new contracts. Positive commentary or order wins from its participation in the 'DefExpo 2027' will also act as a trigger.
5.  **Biggest Structural Risk: Geopolitical Tensions & Global Supply Chain Disruptions:** The primary structural risk stems from the highly globalized and politically sensitive nature of the aerospace and defence sector. Escalating geopolitical tensions, trade protectionism, or major disruptions in global supply chains (e.g., raw material shortages, logistics bottlenecks) could severely impact Unimech's ability to source critical inputs or deliver components to its international OEM clients, leading to project delays and cost overruns. While indigenization mitigates some risk, a significant portion of high-tech raw materials and specialized tooling is still imported.
