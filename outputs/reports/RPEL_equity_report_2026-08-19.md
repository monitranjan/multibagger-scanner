### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Dominant Niche Leadership in India's Organized Silica Ramming Mass Market with Structural Formalization Tailwind.**
RPEL is the largest organized-sector player in India's silica ramming mass segment, commanding a 14.0% domestic market share with an explicit target to scale this to 30% by FY30 [^ip-latest]. The ramming mass market has historically been fragmented and dominated by unorganized regional players; however, GST-driven formalization, combined with RPEL's ability to offer customized, energy-efficient formulations (delivering higher furnace lining longevity and lower energy cost per heat for induction furnace operators), is enabling sustained market share gains [^vp-thread]. The company's 10-year volume CAGR of 28% — materially ahead of the underlying steel sector growth — validates this share-shift narrative [^ip-latest]. We view RPEL's nationwide logistics infrastructure for a product that has traditionally been supplied locally as a unique structural moat that is difficult for regional unorganized players to replicate [^vp-thread].

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story with Operating Leverage and Mix Improvement.**
RPEL's consolidated EBITDA margin expanded by 235 bps YoY to 29.26% in FY26, reflecting a combination of operating leverage from volume growth (+29% YoY in ramming mass sales to Rs. 254 Crs), improved pricing power on customized formulations, and a richer mix from value-added silica products [^ar-fy25]. The 10-year EBITDA CAGR of 32% — outpacing the 28% volume CAGR — demonstrates that margin expansion has been a consistent feature, not a one-off [^ip-latest]. With Q4 FY26 capacity utilization at 87% and the brownfield expansion (414 KMTPA → 534 KMTPA, ~30%) commissioning in phases by Oct 2026, we expect further operating leverage as fixed costs are absorbed over higher volumes [^ip-latest]. ROCE improved to 28% in FY26 (from 24.2% in FY25), and ROE expanded to 25%, both well above the company's 10-year averages of 24% and 19% respectively [^ar-fy25].

**3. Diversification into High-Margin Silica Adjacencies — Semiconductor Crucibles and Artificial Marble.**
While silica ramming mass remains the core revenue driver (100% of FY26 revenue at Rs. 254 Crs), RPEL's R&D pipeline is explicitly targeting two high-value silica adjacencies: (a) **semiconductor-grade crucibles** — a high-purity application with significant import substitution potential given India's semiconductor manufacturing build-out, and (b) **artificial marble** — a construction materials adjacency leveraging the same quartz mineral base [^ip-latest]. The company's strategy of integrating with mine owners for raw material security and leveraging in-house R&D for grade customization positions it to capture premium pricing in these adjacencies [^ip-latest]. Additionally, the foundry segment scale-up (currently under-penetrated vs. steel plants) represents a domestic growth lever, and exports (80 KMT in FY26, 10-year CAGR of 56%) provide geographic diversification across 39 countries [^ip-latest].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months): Capacity Commissioning, Q1 FY27 Print, and Patent Grant.**
The most immediate catalyst is the **phased commissioning of the 30% brownfield capacity expansion** (414 → 534 KMTPA) by Oct 2026, which will unlock volume growth from FY27 onwards [^ip-latest]. The **Q1 FY27 results** (announced 15 Jul 2026) provide the first read on post-expansion demand absorption and pricing power; we will be watching for volume growth, EBITDA margin trajectory, and working capital normalization [^cc-transcript]. ### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model.**
Raghav Productivity Enhancers Limited (RPEL) is a single-product, niche-manufacturing company that produces **silica ramming mass** — a refractory consumable made from quartz mineral and binder chemicals — used as a protective lining and thermal insulator in the inner lining of **induction furnaces (IFs)** [^ip-latest]. The product is a critical consumable in IF-based steel plants, foundries, and casting units, where it directly impacts furnace lining longevity, energy consumption per heat, and overall productivity [^ip-latest]. RPEL's value proposition is **customization**: the company offers tailored ramming mass formulations that deliver higher furnace lining life and lower energy costs vs. generic alternatives, enabling customers to achieve measurable productivity enhancements [^ip-latest]. This customization capability — rare in a segment dominated by unorganized regional suppliers — is the cornerstone of RPEL's pricing power and customer stickiness [^vp-thread].

**Revenue Split by Division.**
RPEL's revenue is **100% concentrated in silica ramming mass sales**, which stood at Rs. 254 Crs in FY26 (+29% YoY) [^ip-latest]. There is no segmented revenue breakdown by sub-product or geography in the disclosures, reflecting the single-product nature of the business [^ip-latest]. However, the company has indicated that **exports** (80 KMT in FY26, 10-year CAGR of 56%) contribute a meaningful and growing share of volumes, with the company now present in 39 countries [^ip-latest]. The **foundry segment** is identified as a key under-penetrated domestic growth lever, and **value-added silica products** (semiconductor crucibles, artificial marble) are in the R&D pipeline but have not yet contributed material revenue [^ip-latest].

**Key OEM/End Customers per Division.**
RPEL does not disclose specific customer names in its public filings, but the end-customer profile is well-defined by the product application: (a) **IF-based steel plants** (the primary customer segment, including both integrated steel producers and secondary steelmakers using induction furnaces for billet/ingot production), (b) **foundries** (ferrous and non-ferrous casting units using IFs for melting), and (c) **casting units** (job-work foundries supplying automotive, engineering, and infrastructure castings) [^ip-latest]. The customer base is fragmented across India's secondary steel and foundry cluster, with no single customer likely contributing more than 5–10% of revenue given the diversified geographic and segment spread [^vp-thread]. The export customer base spans 39 countries, providing geographic diversification [^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary Structure.**
RPEL operates through a **subsidiary — Raghav Productivity Solutions Private Limited (RPSPL)** — which holds a significant portion of the group's manufacturing capacity [^ar-fy25]. As of FY26, the consolidated capacity split was: RPEL (standalone) at 144,000 MTPA and RPSPL at 270,000 MTPA, totaling 414,000 MTPA at the group level [^ip-latest]. Post-expansion (by Oct 2026), the split will be RPEL at 180,000 MTPA and RPSPL at 354,000 MTPA, totaling 534,000 MTPA [^ip-latest]. Material related-party transactions exist between RPEL and RPSPL (loans, dividends, and sales), but these have been disclosed transparently and do not raise forensic concerns per the auditor's unqualified opinion [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint.**
RPEL operates a **multi-location manufacturing footprint** across India, with the brownfield expansion underway at existing plant locations (no greenfield capex) [^ar-fy25]. The group's total capacity of 414 KMTPA (FY26) is distributed across RPEL's standalone facilities and RPSPL's facilities, with the expansion adding 120 KMTPA (~30%) at existing sites by Oct 2026 [^ip-latest]. Q4 FY26 capacity utilization stood at 87%, indicating that the existing base is well-absorbed and providing operating leverage headroom for the expansion [^ip-latest]. The multi-location strategy provides logistical advantages — ramming mass is traditionally a local product due to transportation cost sensitivity, and RPEL's pan-India footprint enables nationwide supply, a structural moat vs. regional unorganized players [^vp-thread].

**Promoter Background and Group Context.**
RPEL is promoted by the **Raghav family**, with the promoter group holding 62.89% of equity as of Jun 2026 (down marginally from 62.92% in Sep 2023, indicating minimal dilution) [^ip-latest]. The company celebrated its **decade of listing** in FY26, during which market share grew from 3.5% to 14% — a 4x expansion that reflects the promoters' execution capability [^ar-fy25]. The management's stated strategic priorities include: (a) multi-location manufacturing, (b) increasing domestic market share to 30% by FY30, (c) cross-selling new refractory products via R&D, and (d) global export expansion [^ar-fy25]. Promoter pledging is nil, and there have been no KMP resignations of concern (one independent director, Mr. Praveen Totla, completed his tenure in Dec 2025 per the standard rotational cycle) [^ar-fy25].
