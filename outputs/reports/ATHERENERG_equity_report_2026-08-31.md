### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1) Market Leadership / Moat: From Premium Niche Player to a Top-3 E2W Franchise.**
Ather has moved from being a quirky, premium-startup brand to a clear Top-3 electric two-wheeler ("E2W") franchise in India, with E2W market share rising from 14.2% in Q1 FY26 to 16.8% in Q1 FY27[^ip-latest]. In simple terms, almost every sixth electric scooter sold in India in Q1 FY27 was an Ather. The moat is built on three pillars: (a) a vertically integrated software stack ("AtherStack Pro") that delivers an attach rate (the % of buyers who opt for it) of 94%, meaning 94 out of every 100 scooters sold carry a paid subscription — almost unheard of in Indian autos; (b) a captive public charging network ("Ather Grid") that creates a "walled garden" switching cost (i.e. once you own an Ather, you have a reason not to switch to a competitor because of the charging convenience); and (c) brand pull in the premium segment, evidenced by 707k enquiries and 150k pre-orders in Q1 FY27 — a 158% YoY surge[^ip-latest]. This is the kind of brand strength that takes 8-10 years to build but only one quarter to lose, which is why management is being cautious on rapid dealer expansion.

**2) Structural Margin Story: The Inflection Has Already Happened.**
The Adjusted Gross Margin (AGM — basically the profit left after paying for raw materials and direct production costs, before overheads) has structurally re-rated from 0.8% in FY24 to 9% in FY25 to 19% in FY26 to 22.4% in Q1 FY27[^ip-latest]. EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation & Amortisation — a proxy for operating profitability) margin has progressed from -36% in FY24 to -23% in FY25 to -7% in FY26, and most importantly, **delivered its first positive quarter at +0.8% in Q1 FY27** (a swing of 1,650 bps, or 16.5 percentage points, year-on-year)[^ip-latest]. Three drivers are structural, not cyclical: (a) Software revenue — AtherStack Pro is now contributing 14% of non-vehicle revenue at a near-100% incremental margin (meaning almost every extra rupee of subscription revenue falls straight to the bottom line); (b) Improving product mix toward the high-range 450 series which carries richer ASPs (Average Selling Price — the per-vehicle revenue realisation); and (c) Operating leverage on fixed costs (the per-unit fixed cost falls as more units are produced and sold from the same factory and overhead base). Q1 FY27 also marked the first positive Operating Cash Flow in Ather's history at ₹31.89 Cr for FY26 (full year) — a ₹752 Cr improvement YoY[^ar-fy25].

**3) Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies.**
Ather is no longer a "one-trick scooter company." Three new vectors are opening up: (a) The **EL platform** — a low-cost architecture aimed at the ₹1–1.25 lakh mass-market price band, which is where the bulk of India's 2W volume sits. Launch was completed on 29 Aug 2026 ("Konarc" sub-brand) with SOP (Start of Production) at Hosur in Q2 FY27[^ip-latest]; (b) **Insurance** — Ather has incorporated an insurance subsidiary, enabling embedded insurance revenue at the point of sale; and (c) **International expansion** into Nepal & Sri Lanka, with the Rizta family scooter positioned for export-led growth[^ar-fy25]. Add to this the AtherStack software ecosystem, charging services, and accessories, and "Non-Vehicle" revenue has grown to 13% of total revenue — a recurring, capital-light annuity stream that mature auto companies rarely enjoy[^ip-latest].

**4) Near-Term Catalysts (Next 6–12 Months).**
The next four quarters have a uniquely dense catalyst calendar: (a) **Konarc / EL platform launch on 29 Aug 2026** — opens up the mass-market segment where Ather was previously absent, and management expects the platform to be "margin-accretive at the unit level" even if it dilutes ASPs[^cc-transcript]; (b) **Factory 3.0 (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Maharashtra) Phase-1 go-live in Q3 FY27** — adds 5 lakh units of annual capacity, taking total capacity to 9.2 lakh units/year (vs current 4.2 lakh at Hosur)[^ip-latest]; (c) **Auric Phase-2 decision** — management has indicated an investment decision within 1-2 quarters that could take total capacity to 14.2 lakh units/year; the ₹2,500 Cr fund raise (QIP of ₹1,300 Cr + ₹1,200 Cr preference share approval) provides the war chest[^cc-transcript]; (d) **Sustained pre-order momentum** — currently running at 13-15k/month with dealer stock at just 3 days (vs 14 days in Q4 FY26), implying sell-out is severely supply-constrained[^ip-latest]. Hero MotoCorp's ₹1,758 Cr block-deal investment on 28 Aug 2026 at ₹1,480/share is a strong strategic endorsement that should compress any discount to fair value.

**5) Biggest Structural Risk: Commodity Inflation + China Supply-Chain Concentration.**
The single largest structural risk is commodity price inflation, which has been relentless: aluminium +60%, copper +43%, plastics +25%, and lithium hydroxide +131% versus prior guidance, with the 2W Commodity Cost Index rising from 100 (Q1 FY26) to 146 (Q1 FY27)[^ip-latest]. While Ather has so far passed on price hikes (~₹11k ASP increase from Q4 FY26 to Jun'26, plus cumulative +₹1.2k + ₹1k + ₹4k + ₹1.6k + ₹0.85k + ₹1.2k in vehicle price hikes), further pass-through becomes harder if competitor price moves don't follow[^cc-transcript]. Compounding this, the LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate — a safer, longer-life battery chemistry) cell supply chain is heavily China-concentrated (via CBAK), creating geopolitical and currency risk[^vp-thread]. A 5.6% commodity headwind in Q1 FY27 alone reduced AGM by 60 bps; a repeat in subsequent quarters could stall the margin trajectory before AURIC and EL volumes scale to absorb the impact.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model: An Electric Two-Wheeler OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) with a Software + Charging Ecosystem Layer.**
Think of Ather as a three-layer business. **Layer 1** is the obvious one — designing, manufacturing, and selling electric scooters under the Ather 450 series (premium performance) and Ather Rizta (mass-premium family) brands. **Layer 2** is software — AtherStack Pro, the proprietary over-the-air updatable connected-vehicle platform that enables subscription features like ride modes, navigation, and predictive maintenance. This is essentially a "SaaS on wheels" (Software-as-a-Service — a model where customers pay recurring subscription fees for software, similar to Netflix or Microsoft 365). **Layer 3** is the Ather Grid charging network plus accessories, spares, and now insurance. The genius of the model is that every Layer-1 sale activates Layer 2 and Layer 3 revenue streams, which is why "Non-Vehicle" revenue has stubbornly remained 13–14% of total revenue even as volumes have surged 81% YoY[^ip-latest].

**Revenue Split by Division (Q1 FY27):**
- **Sale of Vehicles:** 87% of revenue (₹1,260 Cr total income; vehicle revenue per unit: ₹1,25,891 — up 4.6% YoY)[^ip-latest]
- **Sale of Non-Vehicles:** 13% of revenue — primarily AtherStack Pro subscriptions (94% attach rate), Ather Grid charging income, accessories, and spares[^ip-latest]
- **Note:** Earlier investor communication included government subsidy income (FAME-II / PM E-Drive) within total income, which is why "total income" growth (87.2%) exceeds "revenue from operations" growth (79%). For FY26 full year, revenue from operations stood at ₹3,672 Cr, up 63% YoY[^ar-fy25].

**Key OEM / End Customers:**
Ather is unusual among Indian auto companies in that it is a pure-play retail consumer brand — it sells directly to end consumers via its own Experience Centres (ECs) and a smaller dealer network, rather than to OEM fleets. As of FY26, Ather had ~700 Experience Centres (doubled YoY), with plans to expand further once AURIC Phase-1 and EL volumes unlock supply[^ar-fy25]. There are no fleet customers (no B2B ride-hailing dependence), which insulates Ather from the price-pressure typical of fleet sales. The most material business-customer relationship is with **Hero MotoCorp** (largest shareholder at ~36% post the Aug 2026 block deal), with RPT (Related Party Transaction — a deal between the company and entities linked to its promoters/large shareholders) revenue of just ₹4.63 Cr (Hero using Ather's charging infrastructure)[^ar-fy25] — small in size but strategically significant as Hero is also the channel partner for Ather's international expansion.

**Subsidiary Structure:**
Ather's FY26 annual report confirms the incorporation of an **insurance subsidiary** during FY26 — this is a forward integration play to capture embedded insurance economics at the point of vehicle sale[^ar-fy25]. The Group also has operating subsidiaries for charging infrastructure and software services. Joint Ventures: None material disclosed in FY26[^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
- **Hosur, Tamil Nadu (Plant 1 & 2):** Currently 35,000 units/month (~4.2 lakh/year), running at 100% utilisation[^cc-transcript]. This is the original "Factory 1.0" plus expanded "Factory 2.0" — the workhorse that has powered Ather's growth from inception.
- **AURIC (Aurangabad Industrial City, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Maharashtra) — "Factory 3.0":** Phase-1 (5 lakh units/year or 42k/month) is on track for Q3 FY27 go-live, with full ramp taking 4-5 months. Phase-2 (another 5 lakh/year) is in planning, contingent on demand visibility[^ip-latest][^cc-transcript].
- **Total Addressable Capacity:** 9.2 lakh/year (current + AURIC Phase 1) → 14.2 lakh/year (with Phase 2). FY26 capex was ₹506 Cr (primarily for Hosur expansion and Factory 3.0 construction)[^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Background and Group Context:**
Ather was founded in 2013 by IIT-Madras alumni **Tarun Mehta (CEO)** and **Swapnil Jain (CTO)** — a deeply technical founder duo that has built the company from a clean-sheet design house into a Top-3 E2W franchise. Pre-IPO, the founding duo each held ~7.06%, which has diluted to ~5.03% each post-IPO but they remain the operational soul of the company[^ar-fy25]. The dominant strategic shareholder is **Hero MotoCorp** (originally 39.6%, now ~36% post the latest block deal), which provides manufacturing best-practice, supply-chain scale, and distribution muscle (Hero's global dealer network is the largest in the world for 2Ws). Other notable pre-IPO investors included NIIF-II (National Investment and Infrastructure Fund), GIC (Singapore's sovereign wealth fund — Government of Singapore), and Tiger Global. Founder dilution has been gradual and intentional, not distress-driven[^vp-thread].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) — India Electric Two-Wheeler (E2W):**
- **Current TAM (CY2026):** Approximately 1.0–1.1 million E2W units sold annually in India, with an industry ASP of ~₹1.20–1.30 lakh. Implying a current E2W market size of **~₹12,000–14,000 Cr (≈ US$1.4–1.7 billion)**.
- **2030 TAM:** Industry registrations have grown 68% YoY in Q1 FY27; if the EV penetration trajectory holds (penetration itself grew 44% YoY in Jun'26 vs Jun'25), and assuming India targets ~30% E2W penetration of total 2W sales (~20 million units) by 2030, the E2W market could reach **6 million units/year × ₹1.25 lakh ASP = ~₹75,000 Cr (~US$9 billion) by 2030**[^ip-latest].
- **CAGR:** 35–40% over CY2026–CY2030 driven by (a) falling battery costs (LFP at ~$90/kWh today vs $140/kWh in 2022), (b) Total Cost of Ownership (TCO — the all-in cost of buying and running a vehicle over its life) advantage vs petrol (electricity at ₹8/unit vs petrol at ₹105/litre), and (c) state + central subsidies.
- **Adjacent TAM — Software & Services:** If attach rates normalise at 60–80% across the industry (vs Ather's 94%), the software/connected services TAM is an additional **₹800–1,200 Cr/year** for the industry by 2028.

**Policy Tailwinds:**
- **PM E-DRIVE Scheme:** The successor to FAME-II, with ₹10,900 Cr outlay for EVs including 2Ws. Subsidies of ~₹10,000 per E2W have been a key demand catalyst, though renewal / extension timelines remain uncertain[^cc-transcript].
- **State EV Policies:** Delhi (100% registration fee waiver + road tax exemption), Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Gujarat all offer 2W-specific EV incentives — direct subsidies, road tax waivers, or registration fee cuts.
- **Charging Infrastructure:** Ministry of Heavy Industries' expansion of public charging under PM E-DRIVE; Ather Grid already has the largest captive 2W fast-charging network in India[^ar-fy25].
- **Battery PLI & ACC PLI:** Production-Linked Incentive schemes for Advanced Chemistry Cells (ACC) support local cell manufacturing — partially mitigates China dependency risk over time.
- **Petrol Price Tailwind:** Brent crude hovering at elevated levels keeps petrol expensive, directly improving EV TCO.

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3-Year View):**
Ather has historically been FCF-negative (FCF or Free Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow minus Capital Expenditure — the cash actually left over after maintaining and growing the asset base), as is typical for scaling capital-intensive EV businesses. The transition story is the headline:
- **FY24 OCF:** ~₹(720) Cr (deeply negative)
- **FY25 OCF:** ~₹(720) Cr (similar)
- **FY26 OCF:** **+₹31.89 Cr (FIRST EVER POSITIVE)**[^ar-fy25]
This is a remarkable ₹752 Cr YoY improvement, driven primarily by (a) trading payable days extension (a way of optimising working capital — paying suppliers later while collecting from customers faster), (b) lower losses, and (c) better inventory turns. Capex in FY26 was ₹506 Cr (Hosur expansion + Factory 3.0 construction)[^ar-fy25]. Post-FY26, with a ₹2,500 Cr fundraise (₹1,300 Cr QIP completed + ₹1,200 Cr preference issue approval)[^cc-transcript], the company has shifted from "constrained" to "well-capitalised" — but the deployment discipline question remains: investors will watch whether the next ₹2,500 Cr is allocated to value-accretive capacity expansion vs peripheral diversification.
