### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership in greige consolidation:** Borana Weaves (BWL) is one of the largest organised water-jet loom weavers in the greige fabric (unprocessed/unbleached fabric) value chain, displacing fragmented/unorganised powerloom operators who lack scale and on-time delivery[^ip-latest]. Water-jet looms deliver 7–8x the output of conventional powerlooms[^vp-thread], and the company's integrated yarn-preparation (in-house texturising + warping) creates a structural cost advantage versus pure weaving units that outsource these steps[^vp-thread]. This positions BWL as a direct beneficiary of the ongoing shift from unorganised to organised greige capacity in India.

- **Structural margin story underwritten by mix shift and integration:** Grey fabric already contributes ~90% of revenue (vs. PTY yarn at ~10%), and management has guided to a sustainable 25–30% OPM band at full ramp-up[^vp-thread]. Q1FY27 demonstrated resilience: despite an industry-wide LPG-linked labour disruption in the opening two months and elevated POY/PFY realisations, BWL delivered a better-than-expected print, aided by advance raw-material booking from Chinese suppliers and disciplined pass-through[^vp-thread][^cc-transcript]. Vertical integration (POY texturising in-house) plus captive solar/renewable commissioning should sustain 200–300 bps of structural margin advantage over the next 24 months[^ar-fy25].

- **Capacity expansion driving 25–30% revenue CAGR visibility:** Units 6 & 7 are scheduled for Oct/Nov 2026 commissioning, with an additional 192-machine phase announced for Dec 2026 and a further 600–700 machine expansion expected by Mar 2027[^vp-thread]. At a fully commissioned run-rate, the VP-thread base case estimates Rs. 475–500 Cr of annual revenue and ~Rs. 150 Cr of operating profit by FY28E — implying a doubling vs. FY25 base[^vp-thread]. Phased, debt-funded-but-light capex is the preferred mode, with infrastructure already pre-built (incremental capex largely on looms)[^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1FY27 results already in; watch for Q2FY27 print which should reflect full ramp of Units 6 & 7 plus crude stabilising in the $70–75 band easing POY costs[^vp-thread]; (ii) Anti-dumping probe on imported polyester textured yarn and partial yarn — explicit policy support for domestic players[^vp-thread]; (iii) India–EU FTA tailwind opening export optionality in technical textiles (currently <5% of mix)[^ar-fy25]; (iv) Incremental capex announcement likely every quarter through FY27 as infrastructure readiness allows phased machine additions[^vp-thread]; (v) Renewable energy commissioning expected to drop power cost by 100–150 bps of OPM[^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk — governance and raw-material volatility:** (a) SEBI raid on promoter-group entity R&B Denims (joint venture between Borana family and Rawatkhedia/Dalmia family; Amit Dalmia is CMD) is a meaningful corporate governance overhang; the search was linked to suspected price manipulation and the management's response was deemed "vague" by informed observers[^vp-thread]. While Borana Weaves itself is not named, the promoter overlap is non-trivial at 74.71% holding; (b) POY/PFY constitute 60–70% of cost structure and are crude-linked, with limited pricing pass-through given the fragmented customer base and supplier concentration with Reliance Industries / Garden Silk Mills / Bhilosa[^vp-thread]; (c) customer concentration in local traders and processors means BWL has limited brand/IP moat — a sharp POY spike or a slowdown in downstream dyeing/processing could compress OPM by 300–400 bps before mitigation.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model**

- BWL manufactures **greige (unprocessed/unbleached) fabric** and **Polyester Textured Yarn (PTY)** as a vertically integrated upstream player in the man-made textile (MMF) value chain[^ip-latest]. The greige fabric is supplied to local processors/traders who dye, finish and convert it into garments, lining fabric, home furnishings, technical textiles (medical bandages, industrial canvas, agro-textiles, automotive airbags and seat covers) and coated fabrics[^ar-fy25][^vp-thread].
- **Revenue split (estimated FY25):** Greige Fabric ~90% | PTY Yarn ~10%[^vp-thread]. Management is steadily increasing the greige share; PTY is increasingly consumed captively as yarn-preparation for the looms[^cc-transcript].
- **Operating model:** Buy POY (Partially Oriented Yarn) from Reliance Industries, Garden Silk Mills, Bhilosa Industries, and (selectively) Chinese/imported sources → in-house texturising (weft) + warping (warp) → water-jet weaving → folding/packing[^vp-thread]. In-house yarn preparation is the key "integrated weaver" differentiator vs. pure weavers.
- **Key end-customers (per division):**
  - *Greige Fabric:* Local fabric traders, dyeing/processing units, garment manufacturers, home-textile converters, technical-textile players — predominantly domestic with nascent export exposure[^ar-fy25].
  - *PTY Yarn:* Captive consumption (~80–90%) + merchant sales to weaving units in the cluster[^cc-transcript].

**Manufacturing Footprint**

- Primary cluster: **Silvassa (Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu)** with 5 operational units; Units 6 & 7 under commissioning (Oct/Nov 2026) and an additional 192-machine phase announced (Dec 2026)[^vp-thread][^cc-transcript]. Further 600–700 machine expansion expected by Mar 2027 with infrastructure already pre-built[^vp-thread].

**Subsidiary / Group Structure**

- Listed entity: Borana Weaves Limited. Promoter-group associates/related parties include R&B Denims (JV with Rawatkhedia/Dalmia family) — flagged for governance reasons in Section 5[^vp-thread]. Detailed subsidiary list to be confirmed from the latest annual report[^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Background**

- The Borana family has long-standing operations in the Silvassa textile cluster, with BWL representing the listed flagship. Amit Borana (and family members) hold 74.71% of equity[^ip-latest]. The company recently transitioned to a listed entity (IPO prior to 2025), with post-IPO institutional holdings now reportedly exited as the lock-in expired[^vp-thread].

**Market Size & Growth**

- Indian textile and apparel industry: **~USD 165 Bn (FY25)**, of which technical textiles is **~USD 40 Bn and growing at 10–12% CAGR**, while the broader MMF/fabric segment grows at 8–10% CAGR[^ar-fy25]. In Rs. Cr terms, the addressable fabric market relevant to BWL is estimated at **~Rs. 1,40,000 Cr (FY25)** with greige/grey fabric being a sub-segment of ~Rs. 25,000–30,000 Cr[^vp-thread].
- Indian per-capita textile consumption is rising from a low base, supported by demographic dividend and rising disposable incomes, while China+1 diversification is incrementally directing global MMF sourcing to India[^ar-fy25].

**Policy Tailwinds**

- **PLI Scheme for Textiles:** Rs. 10,683 Cr outlay incentivising MMF, technical textiles and apparel — supports capacity addition at organised players[^ar-fy25].

- **India–EU FTA:** Reduces tariff barriers to one of the largest textile import markets, opening export optionality especially in technical textiles[^vp-thread].
- **National Technical Textiles Mission:** Rs. 1,480 Cr outlay for R&D, export promotion and education — relevant to BWL's diversification thesis[^ar-fy25].
