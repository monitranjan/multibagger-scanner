## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Niche global publishing-platform moat:** MPS is one of the top-3 independent STM (Scientific, Technical & Medical) journal production houses globally, with multi-decade relationships with 6 of the world's top-10 publishers including Wolters Kluwer, Springer Nature, RELX/Elsevier, Wiley and Taylor & Francis — switching costs are high because production workflows are deeply embedded into publishers' editorial systems and digital delivery platforms [^ip-latest]. Management has stated on the Q1 FY27 concall that renewal rates on the top-20 client cohort are tracking at ~100%, which we read as a structural moat indicator [^cc-transcript].

- **Structural margin story — operating leverage from platformisation:** The shift from labour-arbitrage (BPO/offshoring) to a platform-and-tech-led delivery model has expanded EBITDA margins by ~600–800 bps over the last four years, and the Q1 FY27 earnings deck shows continued expansion led by the proprietary MagPlus and MPS Interactive platforms [^ip-latest]. With ~70% of delivery now flowing through internally-built platforms, incremental margins are running materially above the ~25% reported blended margin [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond journals, the company has built three emerging verticals — (i) **eLearning & K-12 content** (~12% of revenue, growing 25%+), (ii) **research intelligence / data products** for institutional investors, and (iii) **subscription management & access solutions** for libraries — which together now contribute ~22% of revenue and are growing at 2x the core business [^ar-fy25]. Management has guided that the platform-led businesses should cross 35% of revenue by FY28E [^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (a) Outcome of the **NCLT-convened shareholders' meeting scheduled 22 August 2026** for the proposed Scheme of Arrangement, which we believe is value-accretive and could trigger a re-rating [^ip-latest]; (b) **Q2 FY27 print** is expected to reflect the full benefit of the cost-rationalisation programme announced earlier; (c) Continued **special dividend announcements** — the 3.14% trailing yield masks a history of multiple special dividends that often double the regular payout [^vp-thread]; (d) Potential **buyback announcement** if cash continues to build, given the low capex intensity (<3% of revenue).

- **Biggest structural risk — concentrated promoter shareholding and succession overhang:** With **73.46% promoter holding** and negligible FII/DII institutional presence (combined 1.90%), the float is thin (~24.6%) and **exit/liquidity risk** is non-trivial if the family ever decides to monetise; we also flag the **Scheme of Arrangement** as a binary near-term event risk — adverse tax or structural outcomes at the 22 Aug 2026 NCLT meeting could compress multiples by 15–20% [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript]. Secondary risk: AI-led content disruption to journal production economics, although MPS's workflow IP and customer integration are likely to insulate it through FY27E [^vp-thread].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model:** MPS Limited (formerly Macmillan India Limited) is a B2B **content and platform solutions** provider to global publishers, ed-tech companies, and research institutions. The company operates an integrated delivery model combining (a) content production and editorial services, (b) proprietary technology platforms (MagPlus, MPS Interactive, TOPS), and (c) subscription/access management for libraries and aggregators [^ip-latest]. Revenue is largely USD-denominated (~75–80% of revenue) with delivery centres in India serving global clients — making this effectively a **'services export' platform play** rather than a domestic e-commerce company, despite the broad sector classification.

- **Revenue split by division (FY25A, indicative):**

*Source: Annual Report FY25, Investor Presentation Q1 FY27* [^ar-fy25] [^ip-latest]

- **Key end-customers per division:** Top journal production clients include **Wolters Kluwer, Springer Nature, RELX (Elsevier), Wiley, Taylor & Francis, and SAGE Publications**; eLearning clients include large K-12 publishers and ed-tech firms in the US and UK; subscription management clients include **EBSCO, ProQuest/Clarivate** and university consortia [^ar-fy25]. **Customer concentration is moderate** — top-10 clients account for ~55–60% of revenue but no single client is >10%, which we view as structurally de-risked.

- **Subsidiary structure:** The group operates through MPS Interactive Systems (technology platform arm), MPS North America LLC (US sales/delivery), and MPS Europe Ltd (UK delivery) — along with the recently consolidated MagPlus Technologies platform entity [^ar-fy25]. The pending **Scheme of Arrangement** is aimed at simplifying this structure and consolidating the platform businesses into a single entity, which we believe will improve disclosure quality and enable cleaner valuation.

- **Manufacturing footprint (delivery centres):** India operations span **Bengaluru (Karnataka) — flagship campus, ~1,500+ staff**, **Noida (UP), Dehradun (Uttarakhand) and Chennai (TN)**; international presence includes offices in **Hoboken (NJ, USA) and Oxford (UK)**. Total headcount is in the ~2,500–2,800 range, of which ~85% are based in India, providing the labour-arbitrage backbone of the business [^ar-fy25] [^ip-latest].

- **Promoter background and group context:** Promoted by the **Nishith Arora family (73.46%)** — Mr. Nishith Arora is a St. Stephen's and IIM Ahmedabad alumnus who took over the company in 2011–12 when it was the loss-making Indian arm of Macmillan Publishers; under his tenure the company has executed a sharp turnaround, exited unprofitable legacy businesses, and pivoted to a platform-led model [^vp-thread]. The promoter family also controls the well-known wellness brand **VLCC** (founded by Mr. Arora's sister Vandana Luthra), giving the group strong entrepreneurial DNA and access to a wider professional network [^vp-thread].

The subset that MPS addresses — **journal production, platform-based content services, and library/access management** — is a **USD 12–14 Bn** slice growing at **9–11% CAGR** [^ip-latest].

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree and tenure:** Mr. **Nishith Arora (St. Stephen's / IIM-A)** has run the business for **~14 years** since taking control in 2011–12 and has built a credible track record of (a) turning around a loss-making Macmillan subsidiary into a debt-free, dividend-paying platform company, (b) successfully pivoting from BPO to platform/tech, and (c) maintaining stable operating margins through cycles [^vp-thread]. The promoter family's VLCC pedigree also lends reputational capital, though the two businesses are operationally independent [^vp-thread].

- **FCF deployment track record (3 years):** MPS has delivered **consistent positive FCF conversion (~85–90% of PAT)** over FY23–FY25, with capex requirements minimal (<3% of revenue) given the asset-light services model. The bulk of FCF has been returned to shareholders — the company has paid both regular and special dividends in each of the last 4 years, and at CMP the **3.14% trailing yield is understating true cash return** once specials are normalised [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread].

- **Debt management and credit profile:** MPS is **virtually net debt-free** with cash and equivalents comfortably exceeding any outstanding borrowings — the company has no term loans and operates with minimal working capital debt. No external credit rating is actively tracked given the negligible debt, which we view as a positive structural feature. The current period's temporarily distorted return ratios stem from the **pending Scheme of Arrangement** at NCLT, not from operating deterioration [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25].

- **Dividend policy and shareholder returns:** Dividend payout has trended up from ~40% (FY21) to ~75% (FY25), and the company has supplemented regular dividends with **special dividends on multiple occasions** in the last 5 years — average 5-year payout is ~65%. The current dividend yield of 3.14% is meaningful in absolute terms and compares favourably with most mid-cap platforms, which typically pay 0–1% [^vp-thread] [^ar-fy25].

- **Promoter pledging and governance flags:** **Promoter pledging is 0%** as of the latest shareholding pattern — a clean signal given the high 73.46% promoter holding. There are **no auditor changes** in recent history (statutory auditor has been a Big-4 firm continuously). **No qualified audit opinions** have been reported in the last 3 years. The **appointment of Mrs. Papinani Radha Rani (General Counsel) as Chief Risk Officer w.e.f. 21 July 2026** is a positive governance move, formalising a dedicated risk function ahead of the Scheme of Arrangement [^ar-fy25].

- **Corporate governance flags / RPTs:** Related-party transactions have been routine in nature (primarily group entity transactions on arm's length terms as disclosed in the Annual Report) and have not been flagged by the auditors. The most material governance item currently is the **Scheme of Arrangement itself**, with the **NCLT-convened shareholders' meeting on 22 August 2026** and a parallel creditors' meeting the same day — investors should track this binary event carefully [^ip-latest].

- **JVs, partnerships, and strategic rationale:** MPS has historically pursued **organic platform development (MagPlus, TOPS, MPS Interactive)** rather than large M&A. The current strategic focus — as articulated on the Q1 FY27 concall — is on (a) **consolidating platform subsidiaries** under the Scheme of Arrangement for cleaner structure, (b) **selective tuck-in acquisitions** in the eLearning and data-products space (deal sizes <$20 Mn), and (c) **deepening partnerships with global OA publishers** as open-access mandates scale [^cc-transcript] [^ip-latest]. The lack of large inorganic bets is consistent with the management's capital-light, cash-generative philosophy.
