### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Shanthi Gears is a precision gear and gearbox manufacturer that forms part of the well-known Murugappa Group (via parent Tube Investments of India) [^ar-fy25]. A "gear" is a toothed wheel that transfers rotation and force between machine parts—think of the chainring on a bicycle but built to micron-level accuracy for factories. The company’s moat (its durable competitive advantage, like a castle’s protective ditch) rests on high switching costs: once a customer such as a wind-turbine or railway brake maker qualifies Shanthi’s components, replacing them with another supplier requires re-certification and risk of machine failure [^cc-transcript]. Shanthi also holds niche global certifications—AS9100D (aerospace quality standard) and IRIS (railway standard)—which are like premium security clearances that most local competitors lack [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. This creates a protected franchise in mission-critical transmission parts.

- **Structural margin story:** Despite FY26 revenue declining 14% to ₹518.7 Cr, the company expanded EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation—a proxy for operating profit before non-cash and financing items) margin from 22.9% to 23.8% over three years [^ar-fy25]. This means for every ₹100 of sales, the core factory earned ₹23.8 before overheads, up from ₹22.9, achieved through "value engineering" (redesigning parts to use less costly material without losing strength) and lean manufacturing [^cc-transcript]. Such margin resilience in a downturn indicates pricing power and cost discipline, laying a base for margin accretion when volume recovers.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Management disclosed development of ₹120 Cr worth of new products targeting construction machinery, renewable power, process chemicals, machine tools, and railway segments [^cc-transcript]. "GGM" (Geared Motors & Gearboxes) and "GDS" (Gears & Drive Systems) currently split revenue 48%/52% [^ar-fy25]. The firm is also pushing into defence and aerospace import substitution—a government-led push to replace foreign buys with local ones—using its AS9100D certification [^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. This is like a specialist baker who traditionally sells bread now also baking premium cakes for hospitals and army canteens, opening new demand pools less tied to cyclical factory capex.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The order book (confirmed future sales not yet recognised) grew to ₹638 Cr, a ~24% YoY increase, with unexecuted portion around ₹378 Cr, an all-time high [^cc-transcript][^vp-thread]. The parent Tube Investments bought an extra 2.69% stake on 21 Aug 2026 for ₹77.5 Cr, taking holding to 73.16%, a strong related-party vote of confidence that triggered a 40% stock re-rating in three sessions [^vp-thread]. Planned capex of ₹150 Cr (vs ₹64 Cr spent in FY26) will lift capacity to make 80-ton gearboxes (up from 40 tons) and 3.2-metre diameter gears, enabling larger industrial jobs [^cc-transcript]. These are concrete triggers that can convert backlog to revenue and re-rate multiples.

- **Biggest structural risk:** The Indian gear industry is tied to capex cycles in steel, cement, mining, and wind—when these sectors delay projects, Shanthi’s utilization falls [^cc-transcript]. Chinese import competition and limited R&D breadth are explicit risks in the annual report [^ar-fy25]. Additionally, inventory (unsold finished/semi-finished gears) jumped 40% to ₹112.8 Cr, slowing turnover from 7.3x to 5.4x; if demand stays soft, this working-capital buildup could pressure free cash flow (FCF—the actual cash left after capital spending, like pocket money after buying school supplies) [^ar-fy25]. A prolonged margin squeeze from input-price inflation (seen in Q1 FY27 profit drop of 54%) is the key bear case [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Shanthi Gears operates a single reportable segment: the manufacture of gears, gearboxes, and geared motors [^ar-fy25]. To explain in simple terms, a gearbox is a enclosed unit containing multiple gears that changes speed and torque (twisting force) for machines; a geared motor integrates the electric motor with the gearbox. The company splits its product mix into GGM (Geared Motors & Gearboxes) at 48% of revenue and GDS (Gears & Drive Systems) at 52% [^ar-fy25]. Geographically, 86.6% of sales are domestic and 13.4% exported to America, Europe, and other regions [^ar-fy25].

Core customers span original equipment manufacturers (OEMs—companies that build finished machines) in wind energy (e.g., Winergy referenced in concall), cement, construction, railways, and process industries [^cc-transcript]. The firm is a subsidiary structure under Tube Investments of India Ltd (a Murugappa Group entity) with no major listed subsidiaries; it functions as a standalone precision-components unit [^ar-fy25]. Manufacturing footprint is concentrated in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, with the recent ₹64 Cr capex expanding existing lines rather than new states or plants [^cc-transcript][^ar-fy25]. The promoter group context is critical: Murugappa is a 120-year-old Indian conglomerate with expertise in engineering, finance, and agriculture; this lineage provides access to group procurement and governance standards [^ar-fy25].

The business model is B2B (business-to-business) custom manufacturing: it receives orders, engineers to spec, produces in batches, and earns margins on precision. Unlike consumer brands, its "moat" is technical know-how and certification rather than advertising. FY26 revenue of ₹519 Cr and PAT of ₹77 Cr show a small-but-profitable niche player [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian gear and gearbox industry is estimated to grow at >7% CAGR (compound annual growth rate—the smooth yearly growth rate over time) driven by wind, railway, steel, cement, mining, and extrusion capex waves [^ar-fy25]. Policy tailwinds include Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes for manufacturing, defence localisation mandates, and import-substitution thrusts that favour firms with aerospace certs [^ar-fy25]. Renewable energy and railway electrification are structural demand pools less correlated with traditional factory cycles.

Competitive moat analysis:
- **Switching costs:** Strong. Customised precision gears require customer qualification; replacing supplier risks machine downtime. Certifications (AS9100D, IRIS) raise barriers [^cc-transcript].
- **Scale:** Moderate. Shanthi’s ₹519 Cr revenue is modest vs global gear majors; however, within India’s precision segment its depth is meaningful [^ar-fy25].
- **Pricing power:** Moderate. Ability to pass input cost inflation is partial; Q1 FY27 margin compression shows limits [^cc-transcript].
