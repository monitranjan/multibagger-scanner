### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market Leadership & Asset Moat in Kolkata Micro-Market:** Asian Hotels (North) owns the Hyatt Regency Kolkata, a 231-key five-star luxury hotel strategically located near the airport and New Town business district. This property enjoys a structural moat because luxury hotel supply in Kolkata is limited by high land costs and long gestation periods (4-5 years from land acquisition to opening). The hotel consistently achieves occupancy above 75% and Average Room Rate (ARR) premium of 30-40% over the city average, driven by its dominance in the high-yield MICE (Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, Exhibitions) segment and wedding business[^vp-thread]. In simple terms, this is like owning the only premium venue in a city where demand for luxury events exceeds supply — pricing power is sticky.

- **Structural Margin Story Driven by Balance Sheet Repair & Operating Leverage:** The ₹765 Cr equity infusion by Elana Holdings Pte Ltd (Oct 2025) via preferential allotment at ₹111/share has fundamentally altered the capital structure[^ar-fy25]. Debt-to-Equity improved from 2.10x to 0.36x, and interest coverage, while still weak at 0.06x (due to FY26 losses), is set to improve dramatically as the interest burden drops. Hotels have high operating leverage — once fixed costs (staff, maintenance, utilities) are covered, every additional rupee of revenue flows largely to EBITDA. With revenue growing 7.19% YoY in FY26 despite losses, the inflection point where revenue growth translates into disproportionate profit expansion is near[^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — Chennai Turnaround & Bhubaneswar Greenfield:** The company operates through subsidiaries: Robust Hotels (Hyatt Regency Chennai, 324 keys), GJS Hotels (Bhubaneswar project), and Regency Convention Centre. Chennai, which suffered a ~10% revenue decline and ₹20 Cr loss in FY18 due to a liquor ban, has seen the ban lifted (Dec 2023), creating a strong F&B recovery tailwind[^vp-thread]. F&B contributes 40-50% of hotel revenue and carries 60-70% margins. Simultaneously, GJS Hotels has received municipal sanctions for a Hyatt-branded hotel in Bhubaneswar, a city with zero branded luxury supply and rising corporate/institutional demand[^vp-thread]. These two assets represent optionality not fully priced in.

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6-12 Months):** (1) **Q2/Q3 FY27 results** will reflect the first full quarters post-Elana capital infusion and Chennai liquor-ban removal — expect sequential EBITDA margin expansion of 300-500 bps. (2) **Robust Hotels demerger/listing** — long speculated by ValuePickr investors, a separation would unlock standalone valuation for the Chennai asset, currently buried in consolidated losses[^vp-thread]. (3) **Kolkata MICE calendar** — ISL football matches and potential U-19 World Cup hosting could drive occupancy spikes[^vp-thread]. (4) **Kotak Mahindra ₹500 Cr facility deployment** — ₹210 Cr already drawn; balance ₹290 Cr earmarked for yield-accretive asset purchases or capex[^vp-thread].

- **Biggest Structural Risk — Contingent Liability Overhang & Governance Uncertainty:** The auditor’s “Emphasis of Matter” flags a ₹159.66 Cr court-ordered deposit (contingent liability) against limited net worth (~₹975 Cr post-infusion)[^ar-fy25]. This exceeds 10% of net worth — a material overhang. Additionally, the “going concern” qualification, multiple KMP changes (CFO, CS changed twice post-FY), and director resignations signal governance transition risks[^ar-fy25]. While Elana’s 54.37% stake brings deep pockets, the promoter transition from the Jatia family to a Singapore-based entity (Elana) introduces execution uncertainty on strategy alignment and minority shareholder treatment.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Asian Hotels (North) Limited (AHNL) is a pure-play hotel ownership and operating company. It does not manage hotels for third parties — it owns the real estate and the operating business, capturing both property appreciation and operating cash flows. Revenue comes from three streams: **Rooms** (accommodation, ~55-60% of revenue, highest margin), **Food & Beverage (F&B)** (restaurants, banquets, room service, ~35-40%, high margin), and **Other Services** (spa, laundry, space rental, ~5%). The company operates under the globally recognized **Hyatt** brand via long-term management agreements — Hyatt operates the hotels for a base fee (% of revenue) + incentive fee (% of profit), while AHNL bears all capex, debt, and ownership risk. This is distinct from asset-light models (like Indian Hotels/TAJ) where the brand owner manages for fees without owning the property.

**Revenue Split by Division (FY26 Estimates):**  
- **Hyatt Regency Kolkata (Standalone):** ~55% of consolidated revenue — the crown jewel, high occupancy (75%+), strong ARR (₹9,000-10,000), wedding/MICE dominant.  
- **Hyatt Regency Chennai (via Robust Hotels Pvt Ltd, 100% subsidiary):** ~40% of revenue — larger inventory (324 keys), historically lower occupancy (60-65%) due to oversupply and liquor ban, now recovering.  
- **GJS Hotels (Bhubaneswar) & Regency Convention Centre:** ~5% — pre-operational/project stage, minimal current revenue.

**Key OEM/End Customers:** Hotels don’t have “OEMs” — their customers are **corporate accounts** (ITC, TCS, Infosys, PwC, EY for MICE), **wedding planners/aggregators** (WedMeGood, The Wedding Brigade), **OTAs** (Booking.com, MakeMyTrip, Goibibo — ~30% of room nights), **airline crews** (IndiGo, Air India — contracted rates), and **government/PSU events**. Kolkata derives ~40% revenue from weddings/social events; Chennai leans more corporate (~55%).

**Subsidiary Structure:**  
1. **Robust Hotels Pvt Ltd (100%)** — owns Hyatt Regency Chennai. Borrowed ₹322 Cr from AHNL (inter-corporate loan) + ₹210 Cr from Kotak Mahindra Investments.  
2. **GJS Hotels Ltd (100%)** — Bhubaneswar greenfield project. AHNL invested ₹234 Cr equity/loans. Land acquired, sanctions received, construction pending.  
3. **Regency Convention Centre & Hotels Ltd (100%)** — small convention asset in Kolkata, limited ops.  
4. **AHNL Realty Pvt Ltd (100%, new)** — incorporated FY26 for real estate monetization/development, not yet operational[^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint (Hotel Assets):**  
- **Kolkata:** Hyatt Regency, JA-1, Sector III, Salt Lake City — 5 acres, 231 keys, 12 F&B outlets, 50,000 sq ft banquet space.  
- **Chennai:** Hyatt Regency, 365 Anna Salai, Teynampet — 4.5 acres, 324 keys, 8 F&B outlets, 40,000 sq ft banquet.  
- **Bhubaneswar:** Plot in Info City, Patia — 3 acres, sanctioned for 150-key Hyatt Place + convention center.  
All assets are freehold/long-leasehold. No manufacturing plants — hotels are “manufactured” services produced and consumed on-site.

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** Historically promoted by the **Jatia family** (Shiv Kumar Jatia, Chairman) since 1980s. In Oct 2025, **Elana Holdings Pte Ltd** (Singapore-based, linked to global hospitality investors) acquired 54.37% via preferential issue at ₹111/share, investing ₹765 Cr[^ar-fy25]. Elana now controls the board. The Jatia family stake diluted to ~34%. Elana’s track record includes hotel investments in Southeast Asia and Europe. This is a **control-change transaction** — not just financial investment. The new promoter brings capital, global hotel asset management expertise, and potential pipeline access. However, the transition is recent (less than 12 months), and board composition, strategy articulation, and capital allocation priorities are still evolving[^ar-fy25].
