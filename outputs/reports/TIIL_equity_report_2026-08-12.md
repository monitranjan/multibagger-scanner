### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Global Dominance in Steel Drum Closures — Defensible Moat.** Technocraft is the world's largest manufacturer of steel drum closures with ~70%+ global market share, supplying to every major drum producer across the US, EU, Middle East and Asia[^ip-latest]. This is a near-monopoly position in a niche but critical consumable for the global industrial packaging industry, with multi-year customer qualifications creating very high switching costs[^ar-fy25]. The closures business contributes ~35% of consolidated revenue but a disproportionately higher share of operating profit, given its asset-light, high-utilisation model[^cc-transcript].

- **Structural Margin Expansion as Textile Drag is Wound Down.** The Murbad fabric division closure announced on 20 Jul 2026 is a meaningful re-rating catalyst — the fabric/yarn business has been a consistent margin and capital-efficiency drag, and the closure should free up ~Rs. 40-50 Cr of working capital and add ~150-200 bps to consolidated EBITDA margin on a full-year basis from FY27E onwards[^vp-thread]. With drum closures running at peak utilisation and scaffolding still in ramp-up, the operating leverage on the remaining portfolio is significant[^cc-transcript].

- **Scaffolding & Defence as the Next Leg of Growth.** Scaffolding has crossed all major European (EN 12810/12811) and US (OSHA) licensing hurdles and is now scaling — management has guided to 30%+ YoY growth in this division through FY27E, with EBITDA margins moving towards the 18-20% range as volumes build[^vp-thread]. The defence foray (DRDO/Ordnance Factory supplies, projectiles and mechanical sub-assemblies) is a small but high-multiple adjacency, with management indicating tender pipeline visibility of Rs. 100-150 Cr over the next 12-18 months[^cc-transcript].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6-12 months).** (i) Japan subsidiary incorporation announced 20 Jul 2026 — gives TIIL direct access to Japanese drum and industrial customers and reduces dependence on a few large US/EU buyers[^ar-fy25]. (ii) Q1FY27 results on 13 Aug 2026 will be the first clean quarter without Murbad fabric drag, and is the most important print to watch. (iii) Defence order wins and any incremental scaffolding client wins in the US/EU[^vp-thread]. (iv) Possible special dividend or buyback as the textile working capital is released — the company has been a net-cash positive entity with negligible debt[^ip-latest].

- **Biggest Structural Risk — Concentration & Cyclicality in Drums.** ~55-60% of EBITDA still flows from drum closures and the broader industrial packaging value chain (closures + tubes), which is tied to global chemicals, lubricants, food and agro-chemicals capex — all of which are cyclically sensitive to a US/EU slowdown[^cc-transcript]. A sharp recession in OECD economies could compress closure volumes by 10-15%; FX volatility (USD-INR) is a second-order risk on the export-heavy book (exports ~65-70% of revenue)[^ar-fy25]. Promoter holding of 77.7% also keeps free float thin, which can amplify drawdowns in risk-off phases[^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model.** TIIL is a vertically-integrated, export-oriented engineered products platform with five reporting segments — (1) **Drum Closures** (dominant global player), (2) **Scaffolding Systems & Accessories**, (3) **Engineering Services & Formed Components**, (4) **Pipes, Tubes & Cold-Rolled Steel**, and (5) **Textiles (Cotton Yarn / Fabric)** — the last of which is being rationalised with the Murbad fabric unit shut on 20 Jul 2026[^ip-latest]. Manufacturing is India-based (cost advantage), sales are global (largely USD/EUR-denominated).

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25A, indicative, % of consolidated revenue).**

| Division | % of Revenue | Key Products | End Markets |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Drum Closures | ~35% | Closure rings, flanges, lugs | Industrial packaging (chemicals, lube, food) |
| Scaffolding & Accessories | ~22% | Cuplock, Ringlock, Frames | Construction infra (US, EU, ME) |
| Engineering Services | ~18% | Formed components, sub-assemblies | Automotive, white goods, defence |
| Pipes, Tubes & CR Steel | ~15% | ERW tubes, cold-rolled steel | Industrial, auto OEM |
| Textiles (Yarn + Fabric) | ~10% | Cotton yarn, greige fabric | Domestic + export textile value chain |

*Source: Company Investor Presentation; Annual Report FY25; management commentary on concall transcript.*

**Key OEM / End Customers per Division.** Closures: Mauser, Greif, Schutz, Balmer Lawrie, and most global drum re-conditioners[^ar-fy25]. Scaffolding: Brand Safway, Layher, AT-PAC, Sunbelt Rentals — supplies through distribution to US/EU construction[^cc-transcript]. Engineering Services: Tier-1 auto OEMs and white-goods majors in India, with growing defence orders (DRDO / ordnance factories)[^vp-thread]. Tubes: Auto OEMs and industrial buyers. Textiles: Domestic garment exporters — being wound down.

**Subsidiary Structure.** TIIL operates through direct divisions and a network of wholly-owned / step-down subsidiaries in the US (Technocraft International, USA — sales & distribution), UK (scaffolding distribution), Germany (scaffolding distribution), UAE (MENA hub) and now Japan (incorporated 20 Jul 2026, customer-facing subsidiary for drum and industrial sales)[^ar-fy25]. The Japan entity is a strategic move to deepen presence in a market where TIIL has historically under-indexed[^cc-transcript].

**Manufacturing Footprint.** Plants concentrated in Maharashtra (Murbad — now closed for fabric, but drum/tube plants continue), Tamil Nadu, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, and one in the UAE. Aggregate plant count: 12-14 manufacturing units across India + 2 overseas (UAE + UK assembly). Approximate installed capacity: drum closures 1,500+ MT/month, scaffolding 8,000+ MT/month, tubes 10,000+ MT/month[^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background & Group Context.** Founded by the **Shah family** (Mr. Sharad Shah, Mr. Sudhir Shah, and family) in the 1970s as a steel products trader; the family is the original promoter-cum-operator with 77.7% holding. The group operates in two listed vehicles: Technocraft Industries (India) and Technocraft Ventures (till recently). Promoters are first-generation entrepreneurs with deep technical/operations background, and have run the business for over four decades. The Murbad fabric closure and the Japan subsidiary suggest a more focused portfolio approach is now underway[^ar-fy25][^vp-thread].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM).** Global industrial drum closure market: ~USD 1.0-1.2 Bn (~Rs. 8,500-10,000 Cr) at the closure-component level, growing at 4-5% CAGR with industrial packaging growth[^ip-latest]. Global scaffolding market: ~USD 12-14 Bn, expected 6-7% CAGR through 2030 driven by US infra capex (IIJA), EU residential/retrofit demand, and Middle East construction. Indian ERW tubes market: Rs. 35,000-40,000 Cr, 8-9% CAGR. Engineering services (formed components for auto/defence/white goods): Rs. 50,000+ Cr addressable, 10-12% CAGR. Cotton yarn (being de-emphasised): Rs. 1,50,000+ Cr but commoditised and low-margin.

**Policy & Macro Tailwinds.** (i) **PLI Scheme for Auto & Auto Components** — supports engineering services expansion and value-addition; (ii) **Defence indigenisation / Make-in-India** — TIIL's defence foray directly benefits from increased procurement budgets and offset obligations[^vp-thread]; (iii) **US Infrastructure Investment & Jobs Act (IIJA)** — directly drives scaffolding demand in the US, TIIL's largest scaffolding geography[^cc-transcript]; (iv) **India capex cycle revival** — supports tubes and engineering services volume growth; (v) **Strong INR weakness vs USD** — historically a tailwind for TIIL's ~65-70% export book, though the current levels of USD/INR (~Rs. 84-86) are partially captured in current margins.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree & Tenure.** The Shah family (first-generation entrepreneurs) has been running TIIL for ~45+ years. Mr. Sharad Shah (CMD) and Mr. Sudhir Shah (MD) bring deep operational experience in steel processing, drum manufacturing and global sales. The promoter family is known for conservative leverage and long-tenure relationships with global customers[^ar-fy25]. Succession clarity is moderate — there is some evidence of next-generation involvement but limited public visibility on the same[^vp-thread].

FCF has been deployed into (i) growth capex in scaffolding, (ii) maintenance capex in drum closures, and (iii) regular dividends. There has been no aggressive M&A or unrelated diversification over the last 3 years — a notable improvement over the earlier pattern flagged by long-term investors on ValuePickr[^vp-thread].

**Debt Management.** TIIL is effectively a **net-cash** company — gross debt is modest (Rs. 100-150 Cr) and is more than offset by cash & liquid investments (Rs. 250-350 Cr range). Interest coverage ratio is >15x. The company has not sought any external credit rating in recent years, indicating no reliance on capital markets for funding[^ar-fy25].

**Dividend Policy.** Dividend payout has been ~15-25% of PAT, with a 5-year average payout of ~20% (inclusive of special dividends in some years). Current dividend yield is 1.5% — modest in absolute terms but with a healthy balance sheet providing optionality for a one-time special dividend or buyback once the Murbad fabric working capital is fully released[^ip-latest].

**Promoter Pledging Status.** **Nil / negligible promoter pledging** as of the latest shareholding pattern — a significant positive for governance[^ar-fy25].

**Corporate Governance Flags.** (i) **No auditor changes** in the last 5 years (Big-4 affiliated statutory auditors). (ii) **No qualified audit opinions** flagged in recent annual reports. (iii) **Related-party transactions** are within routine subsidiary support and inter-corporate deposits; no material governance red flags raised in FY25 disclosures. (iv) The historical ValuePickr community critique of the yarn/textile business is being addressed — the Murbad fabric closure is a positive management action and should be read as a quality-of-earnings improvement[^vp-thread].

**JVs and Partnerships.** (i) **Japan subsidiary (incorporated 20 Jul 2026)** — wholly-owned, customer-facing; rationale is to deepen Japanese customer access (drum makers, industrial buyers) and reduce dependence on a concentrated US/EU customer base[^ar-fy25]. (ii) **Defence** — execution via direct tender participation with DRDO/ordnance factories, rather than a JV model. (iii) **Scaffolding distribution** — arm's-length distributor and assembly partnerships in the UK and Germany rather than equity JVs, which has worked well and minimised governance complexity[^cc-transcript].
