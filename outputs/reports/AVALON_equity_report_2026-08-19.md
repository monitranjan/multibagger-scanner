### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership in High-Mix, Low-Volume EMS Niche:** Avalon Technologies has carved out a defensible position in the complex, high-mix, low-volume segment of the Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) industry, distinct from mass-volume players like Dixon Technologies or Foxconn-style assemblers[^ip-latest]. The company's capabilities span 32-layer PCB assembly, HDI (High-Density Interconnect) boards, box-build solutions, precision sheet metal, injection moulded plastics, and magnetics, enabling it to serve mission-critical applications in aerospace, railways, semiconductor equipment, and clean energy[^ip-latest]. With box-build contributing 60% of Q1 FY27 revenue, Avalon has successfully migrated up the value chain from component-level PCBA to full system integration, a transition that commands structurally higher margins and stickier customer relationships[^ip-latest]. The dual-shore manufacturing model (72% India, 28% US) provides a unique "validate-in-US, produce-in-India" pathway that reduces customer switching costs and captures wallet share progressively[^cc-transcript].

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story:** Avalon has demonstrated a clear and sustained margin trajectory, with EBITDA margins expanding from 7.2% in FY24 to 10.5% in FY25 and reaching 12.0% in Q1 FY27, an improvement of approximately 480 basis points over eight consecutive quarters[^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. This expansion is driven by three structural levers: (a) operating leverage as fixed/semi-fixed costs (45-50% of gross margin) get absorbed over a scaling revenue base; (b) mix shift towards box-build and complex assemblies which carry higher value-add; and (c) India manufacturing EBITDA margins of 16.7% in Q1 FY27, significantly above the blended margin, indicating that as the US plant progresses towards break-even, consolidated margins have further runway[^ip-latest]. Gross margins have been consistently maintained in the 33-35% guided range despite tariff headwinds (~90 bps impact absorbed in Q1 FY27), underscoring pricing power in niche segments[^cc-transcript].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** The company has methodically diversified across four secular growth verticals: Clean Energy (29% of Q1 FY27 revenue, including energy storage systems with US subsidy support until 2032), Mobility/Transportation (32%, spanning aerospace cabin sub-assemblies, rail Kavach anti-collision systems, and automotive), Communication (10%, including HVDC power systems for data centres), and Industrials (28%)[^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. Recent strategic moves include entry into semiconductor equipment manufacturing (increased allocation with a leading wafer fabrication equipment supplier), partnership with Zepco Technologies for motors/drives in drones and EVs, and selection as a manufacturing partner for C-DAC's 'RUDRA' HPC programme[^ar-fy25]. The India EMS industry is growing at a ~27% CAGR, and Avalon's high-mix positioning allows it to capture disproportionate share of the value chain[^ip-latest].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** Several identifiable catalysts could drive re-rating in the next 6-12 months: (a) Commercial production commencement at the new Chennai brownfield facility in Q2 FY27, adding domestic capacity for the "Make in India for India" thesis[^cc-transcript]; (b) Ramp-up of Kavach anti-collision system production for Indian Railways, a multi-year opportunity given the mandatory rollout timeline; (c) Progressive ramp of semiconductor equipment manufacturing revenue, with management indicating production is scaling over coming quarters; (d) US plant progression towards EBITDA break-even by end of FY27 (currently narrowed losses to Rs. 4 Cr in Q1 FY27), which would consolidate margins; and (e) Potential ISM 2.0 scheme benefits, where Avalon is positioned as a likely beneficiary given its semiconductor equipment focus[^cc-transcript]. Management has revised FY27 revenue growth guidance upward to 26-30% (from 24-27%), with a long-term aspiration to double revenue from Rs. 1,603 Cr in FY26 to ~Rs. 3,200 Cr by FY29[^cc-transcript].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Customer Concentration & Working Capital Intensity:** The most significant structural risk is the combination of high customer concentration (US customers account for 59% of Q1 FY27 revenue) and elevated working capital intensity (Net Working Capital Days at 117, though improving from 142)[^ip-latest]. Trade receivables grew by ~Rs. 1,291 Cr in FY25, causing CFO/PAT conversion to drop to ~40%, a yellow flag that requires monitoring[^ar-fy25]. Additionally, related party transactions are material: sales to US subsidiaries (ABV/Sienna) stood at Rs. 2,429 Cr (22% of consolidated revenue), with corporate guarantees of Rs. 1,270 Cr extended to subsidiaries, creating contingent exposure[^ar-fy25]. Interest coverage is thin at ~1.1x due to finance costs on US subsidiary preference shares, and two key KMP resignations (CFO and Company Secretary) within FY25 warrant governance scrutiny[^ar-fy25]. A downturn in US industrial capex or a major customer loss could materially impact the growth trajectory.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Avalon Technologies operates as a full-service Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) provider offering end-to-end solutions from design and new product development (NPD) to final box-build assembly[^ip-latest]. The company's service portfolio encompasses seven integrated capabilities: (1) Printed Circuit Board Assembly (PCBA) including SMT, THT, and HDI; (2) Cable Assembly and Wire Harnesses; (3) Injection Moulded Plastics for aerospace, industrial, and power sectors; (4) Sheet Metal Fabrication and Machining focused on high-precision aerospace components; (5) Magnetics; (6) Design and NPD services including in-house PCB design, rapid prototyping, and DFM (Design for Manufacturing); and (7) End-to-end Box Build Solutions involving system integration, testing, and final assembly[^ip-latest]. This vertical integration strategy enables Avalon to capture value across the entire product lifecycle, from concept to shipment, and move up the value chain progressively.

**Revenue Split by Division (Q1 FY27):** Box-build solutions accounted for 60% of total revenue, representing the largest and fastest-growing capability[^ip-latest]. By end-industry vertical, the mix is: Mobility/Transportation at 32% (rail signalling, braking systems, aerospace cabin sub-assemblies, automotive), Clean Energy at 29% (solar, hydrogen, EV, energy storage systems), Industrials at 28% (power & automation), Communication at 10% (telecom, satellite, digital infrastructure including HVDC for data centres), and Medical & Others at 1%[^ip-latest]. By geography, US customers contribute 59% of revenue while India customers contribute 41%, though manufacturing footprint is 72% India and 28% US, reflecting the "validate-in-US, produce-in-India" hybrid model[^ip-latest].

**Key OEM/End Customers:** While specific customer names are not publicly disclosed due to confidentiality agreements, management has indicated marquee relationships across verticals: a leading aerospace company (cabin sub-assemblies and incremental box-build), a leading wafer fabrication equipment supplier (semiconductor equipment manufacturing), Indian Railways (Kavach anti-collision system and locomotive engine sub-systems), C-DAC (RUDRA HPC programme), and Zepco Technologies (motors/drives for drones, EVs, defence)[^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. The customer base is concentrated in the US (59% of revenue) with growing India contribution (41%), and management is actively pursuing geographic expansion into Europe and Southeast Asia[^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary Structure:** Avalon operates through a complex subsidiary structure with material inter-company flows. The US subsidiary (ABV/Sienna) serves as the customer-facing beachhead for North American clients, handling initial validation and low-volume production before transitioning to India for scale[^cc-transcript]. Indian subsidiary Avalon Technology & Services Pvt Ltd handles domestic manufacturing. Related party transactions are significant: sales to subsidiaries totalled Rs. 2,429 Cr (22% of consolidated revenue) and purchases from subsidiaries were Rs. 830 Cr in FY25, with loans of Rs. 800 Cr outstanding to the Indian subsidiary and corporate guarantees of Rs. 1,270 Cr extended to subsidiaries[^ar-fy25]. This structure, while common in EMS companies with dual-shore models, requires ongoing monitoring for arm's-length pricing and subsidiary financial health.

**Manufacturing Footprint:** Avalon operates manufacturing facilities across India and the US. In India, the company has facilities in Chennai (including the newly commissioned export-focused plant and the brownfield expansion for domestic demand operational from Q2 FY27), with additional capacity being added through land acquisition in Chennai to support the next decade of growth[^cc-transcript]. The US facility serves as a low-volume, high-mix validation centre for North American customers. Total capex in FY25 was Rs. 57.8 Cr (consolidated), with capex commitments on the balance sheet rising to Rs. 161.86 Cr (from Rs. 39.98 Cr), reflecting the brownfield expansion phase[^ar-fy25]. Asset turns remain strong at 9.9x (FY26), indicating a capital-light model relative to traditional manufacturing[^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background and Group Context:** Avalon Technologies was founded and is led by Kunhamed Bicha, who serves as Chairman and Managing Director with over 25 years of experience in the EMS industry[^cc-transcript]. The promoter group holds 44.39% of equity as of Jun 2026, down from 50.57% in Mar 2025, reflecting dilution through ESOP issuances and possibly secondary transactions[^ar-fy25]. There is no promoter share pledging, which is a positive governance signal. The management team includes Suresh Veerappan as CFO (appointed Nov 2024, replacing R.M. Subramanian who resigned Sep 2024) and Shriram Vijayaraghavan as COO[^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. The company is actively hiring 5-6 VP-level candidates for business units and capabilities to support scaling[^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) and Growth:** The India EMS industry is estimated at approximately USD 45-50 Bn (Rs. 3.75-4.15 Lakh Cr) as of 2026, with a projected CAGR of ~27% through 2030, driven by the China+1 supply chain diversification theme, domestic policy push (PLI schemes, ECMS), and rising electronics consumption[^ip-latest]. Globally, the EMS market is estimated at USD 750-800 Bn, growing at 6-8% CAGR, with high-mix, low-volume segments (Avalon's focus) growing faster at 10-12%[^ip-latest]. The semiconductor equipment manufacturing sub-segment, where Avalon has recently entered, is a USD 100+ Bn global market with India emerging as a key destination under the ISM (India Semiconductor Mission) and ISM 2.0 schemes[^cc-transcript].

**Policy Tailwinds:** Several government initiatives provide structural support: (1) PLI (Production Linked Incentive) schemes for electronics manufacturing, with specific allocations for components and sub-assemblies; (2) ECMS (Electronic Component Manufacturing Scheme) incentivising domestic component production; (3) ISM and ISM 2.0 for semiconductor equipment and fabrication, where Avalon is positioned as a potential beneficiary; (4) FAME-II and EV manufacturing policies driving clean energy demand; (5) Indian Railways' Kavach anti-collision system rollout, a multi-year mandatory programme; and (6) Defence indigenisation and Atmanirbhar Bharat push[^ar-fy25][^cc-transcript]. These policies collectively create a favourable regulatory environment for high-mix EMS players.
