## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Dominant BC network moat with structural lock-in:** BLS E-Services operates one of India's largest Business Correspondent (BC) footprints with ~20,000+ touchpoints across semi-urban and rural India, delivering banking, insurance, Aadhaar-enabled payment, and government e-services. The network is anchored by multi-year exclusive agreements with PSU banks (Bank of Baroda, Indian Bank, Central Bank of India, UCO Bank) and IRCTC, creating high switching costs given re-onboarding costs and customer habituation.

- **Margin expansion story driven by service-mix shift:** EBITDA margins have expanded from ~6% in FY22 to ~12–13% in FY25, a ~650–700 bps structural improvement, driven by (i) higher attach of value-added services (insurance, mutual fund distribution, PAN/TDS services), (ii) operating leverage on a largely fixed retail outlet cost base, and (iii) the IRCTC e-ticketing franchise which carries materially higher take-rates than vanilla BC transactions. We model further 150–200 bps expansion to ~14–15% by FY28E.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** The company is pivoting from a pure BC agent into a fintech distribution platform — (i) IRCTC rail ticketing (high-margin, sticky), (ii) insurance and mutual fund distribution under the BC-Insurance/BC-MF regulator framework, (iii) AePS-led cash withdrawal/deposit franchise riding Jan Dhan expansion, and (iv) emerging B2B SaaS-led merchant onboarding tools. Each adjacency is incremental to the core BC P&L and carries 2–3x the per-transaction revenue of vanilla banking correspondents.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (a) RBI's Financial Inclusion deepening agenda and continued Jan Dhan–MGNREGS–DBT disbursement flow-through, (b) IRCTC volume tailwind from peak festive and pilgrimage (Kumbh, Char Dham) travel season, (c) potential re-rating as institutional investors (currently 0% FII/DII) discover the stock post the lock-up expiry overhang, (d) any new PSU bank BC mandate win — historically each mandate adds Rs. 80–120 Cr of annualised revenue.

- **Biggest structural risk:** Concentration risk — top 3 bank partnerships contribute ~55–60% of revenue, and any unilateral renegotiation of commission rates (RBI has historically nudged banks to lower BC commissions) or in-house digitisation by PSU banks could compress unit economics by 200–400 bps. Secondary risk: regulatory ceiling on BC commissions under the Financial Inclusion framework remains a binary overhang.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** BLS E-Services is a tech-enabled Business Correspondent and e-Governance service delivery platform. It acts as the last-mile agent for banks, financial institutions, insurance companies, and government departments, delivering citizen and financial services through a distributed retail outlet network. Revenue is largely transaction-based (per-transaction commission) with a growing annuity component from IRCTC ticketing and insurance distribution.

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25 estimates):**

| Division | % of Revenue | Key Activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Banking Correspondent (BC) | ~55–60% | Cash deposit/withdrawal, Aadhaar-enabled payments, account opening, DBT disbursement |
| IRCTC E-Ticketing | ~20–25% | Railway ticket booking via authorised agent network |
| Insurance & MF Distribution | ~8–10% | LIC, health, life, mutual fund distribution |
| E-Governance / Other Services | ~7–10% | PAN, Aadhaar updates, passport, utility bill payments |

**Key OEM / End Customers per Division:**

| Division | Anchor Customers |
| --- | --- |
| Banking Correspondent | Bank of Baroda, Indian Bank, Central Bank of India, UCO Bank, Punjab & Sind Bank |
| IRCTC E-Ticketing | IRCTC (exclusive authorised agent in many districts) |
| Insurance | LIC, ICICI Lombard, HDFC Ergo, SBI General |
| E-Governance | UIDAI, Income Tax Dept, CSC e-Governance Services India Ltd |

**Subsidiary Structure:** Wholly-owned subsidiary of BLS International Services Ltd (BLS International — listed separately on NSE/BSE). BLS E-Services was demerged and listed independently in 2024 to unlock value of the fintech distribution business distinct from BLS International's visa processing core.

**Manufacturing / Operational Footprint:** Not applicable in the traditional sense — the company operates a distributed retail outlet model rather than centralised manufacturing. Operational presence spans ~20,000+ BC outlets across 25+ states and 500+ districts, with regional hubs in Delhi NCR, Lucknow, Patna, Jaipur, and Guwahati.

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** Promoted by Mr. Nikhil Gupta and the BLS Group. The parent BLS International (founded 2005) is a leading visa processing and consular services company with global operations across 50+ countries. The promoter group brings deep relationships with Indian government departments and PSU banks — a critical moat in the BC business where contracts are relationship-driven.
