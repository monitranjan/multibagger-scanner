### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**(1) Global Market Leadership with Defensible Moat:** Welspun Living is the **#1 towel and bath rug supplier to the US** and **Top 2 in sheets**, making it one of the very few Indian manufacturers with a decade-long, share-leading position inside the world's most demanding home-textile market.[^ip-latest] This is not just "scale" — it is the outcome of long-cycle vendor partnerships with Target, Walmart, Costco, and Bed Bath & Beyond (legacy accounts) where the switching cost is high because buyers must re-validate fabric, dye, audit, and ESG credentials before onboarding a new supplier.[^cc-transcript] The Economic moat is therefore classified as **Strong**, primarily driven by (a) customer stickiness, (b) ESG/quality certifications (S&P Global ESG score 90, Global #1 in Textile-Apparel-Luxury), and (c) innovation IP (50 patents, ~25% of revenue from innovation-led products).[^ip-latest]

**(2) Structural Margin Recovery Story — Trough Behind Us:** EBITDA margin collapsed from a cyclical peak of **19.2% in FY21 to a low of 6.8% in Q2 FY26** as a perfect storm hit the business — US tariffs (April 2025), cotton price inflation, and demand destocking. Q1 FY27 marks the **third consecutive quarter of margin expansion** (6.8% → 9.4% → 10.8% → 12.5%), with management explicitly stating that the recovery is **"structural, not circumstantial"** and is being driven by operating leverage, better business mix, and sustained cost optimisation.[^cc-transcript] PAT margin doubled from 3.8% in Q1 FY26 to **5.7% in Q1 FY27** (a +186 bps improvement).[^ip-latest] The 15% normalised EBITDA margin is a "gradual process" per management, but each quarter has been compounding on the prior one — a textbook operating leverage story as the largely-fixed Anjar/Vapi/Telangana manufacturing base absorbs higher volumes.[^cc-transcript]

**(3) Diversification into High-Growth, High-Margin Adjacencies:** Three new growth engines are now scaling and reducing dependence on the US towel/bed linen core: (a) **US Onshore Pillow Business** at **2.3x YoY growth**, on track to double to **USD 60 Mn in FY27** from a ~USD 30 Mn base, with Ohio at ~80% utilisation and Nevada operational from June 15, 2026; (b) **Advanced Textiles** (Spunlace, Needle Punch, Wet Wipes) — a value-add shift into technical/industrial textiles, though still small at 4% of mix; (c) **Branded Business** at **18% of revenue** (+25% YoY), targeting a double-digit growth trajectory with the Christy, Welspun, and WellHome brands, plus the newly incorporated **Christy Home Inc.** (Delaware, Sep 2024) for direct US luxury play.[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25] The mix-shift to branded and innovation-led products (now ~25% of revenue) is critical because these categories command **higher gross margins and customer stickiness** than commodity B2B.

**(4) Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** Multiple identifiable triggers should re-rate the stock from current depressed forward multiples: (a) **India-UK FTA live from 15 July 2026** puts India on equal tariff footing with Pakistan (which holds 50%+ of UK home textile imports) — management expects **"double-digit growth in UK businesses over the coming years"**; (b) **India-US BTA in advanced talks** could re-open tariff parity and unlock additional US share; (c) **Anjar debottlenecking project (₹121 Cr approved July 2026)** adding 10–20 tons/month of processing capacity; (d) **Tariff refund process initiated** — partial refunds already received, providing working capital relief; (e) **Domestic retail near break-even with 20% growth** trajectory toward the **₹1,000 Cr target**; (f) **Pillow business doubling to USD 60 Mn in FY27** is a clean, in-line execution milestone; (g) **Q1 FY27 numbers** with EBITDA margin at 12.5% (140 bps YoY) and PAT at +83.6% YoY provide the first hard evidence of cycle inflection.[^cc-transcript][^ip-latest]

**(5) Biggest Structural Risk — US Tariff & Demand Cyclicality:** Despite the 6.8% → 12.5% margin recovery, **~59% of revenue remains US-exposed**, and the 2025 tariff shock proved how quickly policy can destroy profitability. While management claims tariffs are "all behind us" and no market share was lost, the unresolved **ROStEL (export incentive) extension** (up for termination by September 2026) and the **delayed India-US BTA** leave a live policy overhang.[^cc-transcript] Cotton price volatility is the second risk — a 10% sustained move in cotton prices compresses gross margin by 80–120 bps before pass-through kicks in. The third structural concern is the **promoter stake reduction** from 70.5% (Sep 2023) to 66.4% (Jun 2026) via buybacks, which, while capital-return positive, reduces skin-in-the-game marginally.[^ar-fy25]

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Welspun Living Limited (WLL) is India's largest and the world's most vertically integrated home-textile manufacturer, operating across three synergistic platforms — **Home Textiles** (Bath Linen, Bed Linen, Rugs & Carpets), **Flooring Solutions** (a "disrupting the world of flooring" initiative spanning soft flooring, hard flooring, and area rugs), and **Advanced Textiles** (Spunlace non-wovens, Needle Punch, Wet Wipes).[^ip-latest] The company is essentially a **B2B-led, design-and-manufacturing powerhouse** that serves the world's largest retailers (Target, Walmart, Costco, IKEA, John Lewis, etc.) on a replenishment basis — meaning once Welspun wins a SKU (stock-keeping unit, i.e. a specific towel design and colour combination), it gets recurring repeat orders every 4–8 weeks as retailers restock shelves. This replenishment dynamic is what creates the deceptively high revenue visibility that institutional investors typically associate with consumer staples rather than textiles.[^cc-transcript]

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETRY LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM):** The global home-textile market is estimated at **USD 130–150 Bn (₹11–13 lakh Cr)** in 2026, growing at a **5–7% CAGR** through 2030 driven by housing completions, hospitality sector recovery, post-pandemic home-refresh cycles, and rising premiumisation. India is the **largest exporter of cotton terry towels and bed sheets to the US** — meaning Indian manufacturers are structurally positioned to capture a disproportionate share of the global supply chain as buyers diversify away from China.[^ip-latest] The branded home textile market (where Welspun is shifting focus) is smaller at **USD 25–30 Bn globally** but commands **higher margins and customer loyalty**, and is the natural next leg of growth for the company.

**Policy Tailwinds:**

| Policy | Status | Welspun Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **India-UK FTA** | Effective 15 July 2026 | Eliminates 12% tariff — India on equal footing with Pakistan which holds 50%+ of UK home textile imports. Management expects "double-digit UK growth over coming years."[^cc-transcript] |
| **India-EU FTA** | Concluded Jan 2026 | Reduces EU duties (~7–12% on textiles) — meaningful tailwind for the 20%+ UK/EU growth trajectory in Q1 FY27.[^ip-latest] |
| **India-US BTA** | Advanced talks | Could restore US tariff parity; critical for the ~59% US-exposed revenue base.[^cc-transcript] |
| **PLI Scheme for Textiles** | Approved but limited uptake | Less direct benefit for WLL (focused on MMF technical textiles); indirect boost via supply chain development.[^ip-latest] |

The combined addressable market across active FTA partners (UK + EU + US) exceeds **USD 85 Bn** — meaning Welspun's current revenue base (~USD 1.1–1.2 Bn) is less than 1.5% of the total opportunity, leaving **massive headroom for share gain**.[^ip-latest]

**Valuation Commentary — Discount/Premium vs Peers:**

Welspun Living trades at a **significant TTM P/E premium (~64x)** versus the peer median of ~25x, but this is **misleading** because the trailing twelve-month denominator is artificially depressed by the FY26 PAT collapse (PAT fell to ₹204 Cr from ₹639 Cr in FY25 due to the tariff + cotton + demand trifecta). On a **forward FY27E basis (assuming margins recover to ~13% mid-point)**, the P/E normalises to **~32x**, which is broadly in line with high-quality textile peers like Trident and Indo Count, and represents a **modest premium to Vardhman (~18.5x)**. The premium is justified by: (a) **#1 market position in US towels and bath rugs** — a durable share leadership that Vardhman and Indo Count do not enjoy; (b) **branded business at 18% of revenue and growing 25%+** — none of the peers have a comparable branded play at scale; (c) **ESG leadership (S&P #1 globally)** which is increasingly a buyer requirement, not just a marketing claim; (d) **diversified end-markets** (Pillows, Advanced Textiles, Flooring) reducing single-customer concentration risk.

Where the valuation is **less defensible** is the **8.7% ROCE** — well below peer leaders (KPR Mill 22%, Trident 12.4%, Vardhman 11.8%). The ROCE compression is the legacy of the FY25 capacity expansion (₹1,050 Cr Anjar capex is in CWIP — capital work-in-progress, i.e. money spent on assets not yet generating revenue) and the depressed FY26 earnings. **ROCE recovery to low teens (12–14%) is a key FY27 milestone** that management has explicitly committed to and that would justify the premium P/B of 3.83x vs Vardhman 1.95x.[^cc-transcript]

Goenka)**, a first-generation entrepreneur who built the group from a single textile mill in 1985 to a diversified conglomerate with combined revenue exceeding ₹25,000 Cr across pipes, textiles, infrastructure, and warehousing. He has been **Chairman since 1985** — over 40 years of leadership — providing remarkable continuity and long-cycle thinking. **Dipali Goenka, MD & CEO of WLL** since 2012, has been the architect of the "Welspun 2.0" brand-led transformation that has lifted branded share from ~8% to 18% in five years. **Rajesh Mandawewala (EVC, Executive Vice Chairman)** brings balance-sheet discipline and group-level capital allocation.
