### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS
- **Market leadership / moat:** Albert David Limited (ADL) owns the Placentrex franchise, a placental extract-based biologic with over 30 years of clinical heritage, contributing >30% of total revenue[^ar-fy25]. In plain terms, Placentrex is a unique wound-healing and tissue-repair injectable/liquid whose manufacturing know-how (called "science leadership") is difficult for competitors to copy because it requires specialized biomolecule extraction and long-standing physician trust. This brand recall among gynecologists and surgeons acts as a switching cost (doctors prefer a proven product), giving ADL a Moderate moat in the niche parenteral (injectable) segment[^ar-fy25]. The company also holds WHO Prequalification (WHO PQ – a global quality stamp that lets it supply to UN agencies) and has upgraded to Revised Schedule-M (India’s stricter Good Manufacturing Practice rules), which smaller unorganized players cannot easily meet[^ar-fy25].

- **Structural margin story:** FY26 EBITDA margin collapsed to 3.9% from ~29% in FY24 due to raw material inflation and the loss-making Mandideep unit[^ar-fy25]. The management has closed that unit and replaced unionised medical representatives (MRs – the salespeople who visit doctors) with a non-unionised, data-driven field force using AIOCD (retail audit) and SMSRC (prescription audit) data to push its top 5–6 brands that already form 50% of sales[^vp-thread]. This structural cleanup, together with inventory reduction of ₹7 Cr and receivables cut of ₹11 Cr, should expand EBITDA margin by 300–500 bps (1 bps = 0.01%, so 300 bps = 3 percentage points) over FY27E–FY28E as fixed costs get spread on a leaner base[^vp-thread][^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond legacy formulations, ADL is commercialising Miltefosine (an API – Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient, i.e., the pure chemical core – and capsule for Kala Azar/visceral leishmaniasis), a neglected-tropical-disease drug where it aims for global leadership via dossier filings[^ar-fy25][^vp-thread]. It is also expanding exports to South-East Asia, Africa and Latin America using existing WHO PQ and new regulatory registrations, tapping a pharma export TAM (Total Addressable Market – the full market size if everything were captured) of $20+ bn for Indian formulations[^ar-fy25]. This is a high-growth adjacency versus stagnant domestic generic competition.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1 FY27 already reported net profit of ₹31.99 Cr (₹3,199 lakh), swinging from FY26 full-year net loss of ₹1.49 Cr, indicating cost-action payoffs[^vp-thread]; (ii) Appointment of professional CEO Amit Mahla (ex-Jubilant) with top-tier pay ~₹1.5 Cr signals execution intent[^vp-thread]; (iii) AGM disclosures (Sep 2026) on gov scheme penetration (raising allopathic reach from 35% to 50%) and 25 depot/2000 stockist network could reveal untapped institutional supply[^vp-thread]; (iv) First export orders from Latin America/SEA registrations filed earlier[^vp-thread]; (v) Miltefosine commercial launch milestone[^ar-fy25].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The sudden resignation of Head–R&D Dr. Kuntal Ganguly in Aug 2026 creates pipeline and regulatory filing continuity risk, just as the Miltefosine dossier and Placentrex patent work with IIT-KGP are critical[^vp-thread]. Coupled with elevated "Other Expenses" at 5.3% of revenue (₹17.66 Cr) and negative CFO of ₹7.66 Cr in FY26 (meaning profits did not convert to bank cash due to working-capital absorption), the turnaround is unproven and could revert if raw-material costs (e.g., placental raw material, packaging) spike or if currency FX hits exports[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW
- **Core business model:** ADL is an 87-year-old pharmaceutical manufacturer operating as a single reportable segment "Pharmaceuticals"[^ar-fy25]. It makes sterile liquids (called parenterals) and oral dosage forms, selling to institutions (government schemes, hospitals) and through distributors/stockists to retail pharmacies. Think of it as a factory that buys chemical raw materials and biological extracts, processes them under strict hygiene (Schedule-M), and ships finished medicines to a network of 25 depots and ~2000 stockists[^vp-thread].

- **Revenue split by division (FY26, ₹ Cr):** Large Volume Parenterals (LVP – big sterile fluid bags like IV drips) ₹101.5; Small Volume Parenterals (SVP – small injectables) ₹72.3; Syrup ₹55.5; Tablet ₹35.4; Capsule ₹30.7; Ointment ₹31.1[^ar-fy25]. Placentrex franchise (part of SVP/biologicals) is >30% of total ₹333.6 Cr revenue[^ar-fy25]. No division is separately incorporated; all are inside the parent.

- **Key OEM/end customers per division:** For LVP/SVP, customers are government health schemes (e.g., state gov tenders), hospital groups, and retail via stockists; for Syrup/Tablet/Capsule/Ointment, primary care physicians and retail pharmacies served by the MR field force[^vp-thread]. There are no external "OEM" clients as in auto components; the "customer" is the patient via doctor prescription.

- **Subsidiary structure:** The company is a standalone listed entity under the Kothari promoter group; related-party transactions of ₹7.2 Cr occurred with Kothari-group entities (rent, professional fees, KMP pay, donation) stated at arm’s length[^ar-fy25].
