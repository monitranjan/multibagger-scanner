## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Sole-Source Defence Optics Franchise:** PARAS is the only Indian private-sector company with serialised production capability for submarine periscopes and optronic mast systems, a critical tech stack where in-country capability was non-existent prior to indigenisation drives[^ip-latest]. Periscope ASP has moved from Rs. 30 Cr/unit to Rs. 50 Cr/unit per management commentary, with two units awarded at Rs. 35.84 Cr/piece in Nov 2025[^vp-thread]. This is a near-monopolistic franchise — switching costs for the Indian Navy are prohibitive (re-certification, sensor integration, combat systems handshake).

- **Structural Margin Expansion (Mix-Driven):** EBITDA margin trajectory is moving from ~22% in FY24A toward 28–30% by FY28E as the mix tilts toward higher-margin Space, Diffractive Optics and Anti-Drone segments[^ip-latest]. Space and Drone verticals carry 300–600 bps higher gross margins than legacy defence electronics[^cc-transcript]. Operating leverage on the new Bangalore and Navi Mumbai facilities is expected to add another 100–150 bps as fixed-cost absorption improves through FY27E.

- **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies (Space + Drones):** Beyond core defence optics, PARAS is positioning across four secular verticals: (i) NavIC/space-grade atomic clocks and satellite subsystems, (ii) Anti-Drone systems (only 4 countries with battle-tested capability including India)[^vp-thread], (iii) Smart Warehousing (SAFEETY — SAMEER/MeitY ToT secured Feb 2024), and (iv) Agri-Drone spraying (Rs. 20 Cr IFFCO contract executed Sep 2024)[^vp-thread]. Each vertical carries Rs. 500–2,000 Cr TAM by FY30E.

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** (a) Sequential order inflows from DRDO/DPSU for optronic masts and periscopes; (b) Execution ramp on the Bangalore facility commissioned for space-grade optics; (c) Anti-Drone system export opportunities to Europe and Gulf (post Ukraine-Russia conflict demand validation)[^vp-thread]; (d) Possible PLI-2 allocations under the Defence Manufacturing PLI (Rs. 12,500 Cr corpus); (e) Q2 FY27 results (Oct 2026) — first full quarter reflecting post-indigenisation demand pull.

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** Customer concentration with MoD/DRDO/BEL/HAL (~80% of revenue) creates single-point procurement risk — any shift in capital procurement cycles or capex deferrals (as seen in FY22–FY23 with negative YoY growth in some quarters)[^vp-thread] can produce sharp order-book volatility. Working capital intensity is structurally elevated (defence receivables cycle 9–12 months), which strains FCF despite EBITDA expansion.
