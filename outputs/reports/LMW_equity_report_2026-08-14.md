### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market Leadership & Moat:** LMW is the single largest manufacturer of spinning machinery in India with an estimated ~60%+ domestic market share in the >50,000-spindle mills segment, competing against global majors Rieter (Switzerland) and Trutzschler (Germany).[^ip-latest] Installed base of 50,000+ ring frames and 1,200+ combers gives LMW a captive aftermarket stream — spares & services contribute ~20–25% of standalone textile machinery revenue at materially higher gross margins (~45% vs ~28% on new machines).[^cc-transcript] Switching costs are structurally High given long machine life (20–25 years), qualified tool approvals at every yarn-spinning customer, and embedded service contracts.

- **Structural Margin Story:** Consolidated EBITDA margin expanded by ~250–300 bps over FY22–FY25 driven by (a) richer mix from high-speed compact combers and auto-levellers, (b) backward integration into castings via the in-house Foundry division (60% internal value-add on machine tool castings), and (c) operating leverage as spinning mills capex cycles normalised post-pandemic.[^ar-fy25] Management guides for sustained 18–20% EBITDA margin band on standalone textile machinery in FY27E vs ~16% in FY24A.[^cc-transcript]

- **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** Three adjacencies are at inflection: (1) Composites — LMW supplies composite propeller blades and structural parts to HAL/DHAL for the Tejas Mk1A and LUH platforms, a Rs. 200–300 Cr TAM opportunity over FY26–FY28E.[^ip-latest] (2) Machine Tools — CNC vertical machining centres (VMC) and turnkey lines targeting aerospace and auto tier-1s; revenue scaled from ~Rs. 50 Cr in FY22 to ~Rs. 180 Cr in FY25A.[^ar-fy25] (3) Foundry — third-party castings for wind (Suzlon, Siemens Gamesa) and rail (BHEL) — incremental Rs. 150–200 Cr revenue potential in FY27E.[^cc-transcript]

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) PLI scheme for textiles (Rs. 10,683 Cr outlay) — disbursements accelerate in Q2FY27/Q3FY27, catalysing fresh spinning-mill capex; (ii) PM MITRA park rollout (7 parks sanctioned) drives cluster-based demand for ring frames; (iii) Order book of ~Rs. 3,800–4,000 Cr provides ~2.0x revenue cover for FY27E textile machinery; (iv) FY26 dividend at Rs. 350–400/share sustains ~2% yield on top of special dividends.[^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** Cyclicality of textile mill capex — Indian spinning capacity utilisation has hovered 75–85%, and any sharp downturn in cotton yarn realisations (as seen in H2FY23) can defer capex by 12–18 months.[^cc-transcript] Secondary risk: Chinese imports (e.g., Jingye) gaining share in the <30,000-spindle mill segment on 15–20% price undercutting — LMW has consciously avoided this tier, ceding ~Rs. 400–500 Cr addressable revenue.[^vp-thread]
