### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Defensive market leadership with a near-monopoly position in India's passenger car motor oil (PCMO) segment:** Castrol India commands an estimated ~24% volume share of the Indian lubricants market and an even higher share in the PCMO sub-segment, supported by deep brand equity built over 100+ years and a pan-India distribution reach of ~150,000+ outlets[^ar-fy25]. This structural moat provides pricing power and annuity-like revenue visibility uncommon in the broader chemicals pack.

- **Structural margin resilience in a commodity-input business:** Despite base oil being a derivative of crude, Castrol has demonstrated a remarkable ability to defend EBITDA margins in the 22–25% range over the last 5 years[^ar-fy25]. The company's superior product mix tilt towards higher-margin synthetic and semi-synthetic grades, combined with tight working capital management and zero net debt on the balance sheet, enables FCF conversion of 90%+ of net profit annually.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies (EV fluids, B2B industrial, exports):** Castrol is actively expanding its EV thermal management fluids portfolio (partnering with global OEMs for factory-fill and service-fill opportunities) and scaling its B2B industrial lubricants business[^ip-latest]. The recently articulated "Beyond Lubricants" strategy targets adjacent categories — including coolants, brake fluids, greases and specialised industrial fluids — and management has guided that non-core categories will grow at 1.5–2x the core lubricants business over FY26–FY28E.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Sustained benefit from the Rs. 6.25 interim dividend declared on 4 Aug 2026 (record date 11 Aug 2026), reinforcing a 6.4%+ dividend yield support[^cc-transcript]; (ii) Margin tailwind from the recent cooling of base oil prices which should reflect in H2 CY26 gross margins; (iii) Possible announcement of a second interim/special dividend in Q4 CY26 given the Rs. 3,500+ Cr cash pile and zero capex intensity; (iv) Any commentary on capacity expansion of the Silvassa/Pithampur plants to capture CV and tractor lubricants demand rebound.

- **Biggest structural risk — long-term EV penetration in 2W/4W reducing drain intervals and lubricant volumes:** With India's passenger EV penetration targeting 30% by 2030 and 2W EV penetration already crossing 7% in FY25, ICE vehicle parc growth is the primary lubricant demand driver. While EVs still require transmission fluids, greases and thermal management fluids, total lubricant volume per EV is materially lower (est. 30–40%) versus ICE equivalents, posing a structural volume risk beyond FY28E[^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** Castrol India is the leading branded lubricant marketer in India, manufacturing and selling automotive and industrial lubricants under flagship brands — Castrol CRB (CV/diesel), Castrol Activ (2W), Castrol Magnatec (PCMO), Castrol GTX, and the premium Castrol EDGE synthetic range[^ar-fy25]. The company operates on an asset-light, high-ROCE model: it manufactures base lubricants and conducts blending/packaging in-house, while distribution is routed through a deep trade channel network plus a growing B2B/industrial direct sales force.

**Revenue split by division (% of revenue, FY25):**
- Automotive lubricants (PCMO + 2W + CV) — ~78% of revenue[^ar-fy25]
- Industrial lubricants (B2B, manufacturing, infra, mining) — ~17% of revenue[^ar-fy25]
- Other (greases, coolants, brake fluids, exports) — ~5% of revenue[^ar-fy25]

**Key OEM / end customers per division:**
- Automotive: Maruti Suzuki, Hyundai, Tata Motors, Mahindra, Hero MotoCorp, Honda 2W, Bajaj Auto, TVS, Ashok Leyland (factory-fill and service-fill contracts for engine oils and transmission fluids)[^ar-fy25]
- Industrial: Adani Group, Reliance Industries, BHEL, NTPC, Tata Steel, L&T, Indian Railways, major cement and power plants[^ar-fy25]
- Exports: SAARC, Middle East and select African geographies via parent BP's distribution network[^ar-fy25]

**Subsidiary structure:** Castrol India is a subsidiary of Castrol Limited UK (which is in turn held by BP plc). The company has no major Indian operating subsidiaries; it functions as a focused, single-entity lubricant player with a clean corporate structure[^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing footprint:** 7 manufacturing plants across India — Silvassa (Dadra & Nagar Haveli, the largest), Pithampur (MP), Taloja (Maharashtra), Chennai (TN), Bhiwadi (Rajasthan), Haldia (West Bengal), and Mangalore (Karnataka) — supported by ~115+ warehouse/consignment depots and ~150,000+ retail outlets[^ar-fy25].

**Promoter background & group context:** The promoter is Castrol Limited UK (BP group entity), holding 51.30%[^ar-fy25]. The company benefits from global R&D access (BP's Castrol Technology Centre in Pangbourne, UK), brand licensing of the Castrol master brand, and procurement synergies on base oils and additives. Indian management is led by CEO Sandeep Sangwan (appointed 2023), a company veteran with prior stints in marketing and supply chain.
