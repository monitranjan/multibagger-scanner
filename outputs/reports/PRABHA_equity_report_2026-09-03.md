### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Prabha Energy Ltd (PEL) is building a focused position in Coal-Bed Methane (CBM) — which is natural gas trapped inside coal seams, extracted by de-pressurising the coal. The company holds participating interest (PI, i.e., ownership share of the block) in two Jharkhand CBM blocks: North Karanpura (NK) with 25% PI alongside ONGC (55%) and IOC (20%), and Jharia with 90% PI alongside BCCL (a Coal India subsidiary, 10%) [^ip-latest]. Combined gas initially in place (GIIP — the total estimated gas volume originally present underground) is 31 BCM (billion cubic metres), positioning PEL as one of the largest private-sector CBM players by reserve base [^ip-latest]. The moat is not brand-based but stems from (a) allocation of rare government-auctioned CBM blocks, (b) dedicated pipeline tie-ins to the national grid (≈68 km for NK, ≈8 km for Jharia) that create physical switching costs for offtakers who must connect to that infrastructure [^ip-latest]. For a 15-year-old: imagine owning the only pipe from a remote well to the city gas network — buyers cannot easily switch to another supplier without building a new pipe.

- **Structural margin story:** CBM economics over the block life show ~70% EBITDA margin (EBITDA = earnings before interest, tax, depreciation & amortisation — a proxy for core operating profitability before heavy non-cash and financing items) [^ip-latest]. This is because once wells are drilled and compression stations installed, the marginal cost of lifting gas is low. As NK scales to 68/74 wells (commercial since May 2025) and Jharia adds 8/55 wells (target Q4 FY27), fixed costs spread over more volume, potentially expanding consolidated margins by 300–500 bps (basis points; 1 bps = 0.01%, so 300 bps = 3%) [^ip-latest][^vp-thread]. The structural lever is operating leverage, not price hikes, because gas prices in India are largely administered.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond CBM, PEL holds Marginal Gas Fields in Rajasthan with GIIP of 1.42 BCM (≈4.4% of total reserve base), providing a secondary vertical less dependent on coal-seam geology [^ip-latest]. The government’s Special CBM Bid Rounds (2025 and 2026) and the NK Phase-II option for 50 further wells at lower incremental capex (because surface infrastructure already exists) are organic adjacencies that can multiply output without greenfield risk [^ip-latest]. This is akin to a farmer already owning a tractor and getting adjacent land on favourable terms.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The Jharia Block is targeted for commercial production in Q4 FY27 (i.e., by March 2027) with pipeline connectivity expected to complete within a 6–8 month buildout window per community trackers [^ip-latest][^vp-thread]. Additionally, the August 2026 conversion of 95.1 lakh partly-paid shares into fully-paid equity (face value Re 1) strengthens the equity base and may improve trading liquidity [^ar-fy25]. Any formal disclosure on NELP (New Exploration Licensing Policy) blocks could convert currently excluded assets into quantifiable re-rating catalysts [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Execution and timeline slippage on the Jharia pipeline (a community member flagged a missed March 2026 deadline, unverified in filings) is the key near-term execution risk [^vp-thread]. More structurally, asset-level opacity on marginal/NELP blocks limits valuation credibility, while extreme promoter concentration (94.73% holding per Screener data) and zero FII/DII ownership elevate governance and exit-liquidity risk [^ar-fy25][^vp-thread]. A 15-year-old would understand: if one family owns almost everything and few outsiders hold shares, any sudden promoter pledge or sale could swing price violently.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Prabha Energy operates an exploration & production (E&P) business model — it finds underground gas deposits, drills wells, and sells the raw gas to pipeline companies who then distribute to cities and industries. Currently the company is in an early-production transition: NK began commercial output in May 2025, while Jharia is pre-production [^ip-latest]. 

Revenue split by division is not explicitly disclosed because the firm is pre/early revenue phase, but the asset-base breakdown (a proxy for future revenue mix) is: Vertical 1 – CBM Assets (NK + Jharia) representing ~95.6% of GIIP (31 BCM of 32.42 BCM total), and Vertical 2 – Marginal Gas Fields in Rajasthan representing ~4.4% (1.42 BCM) [^ip-latest]. 

Key customers/partners per division: For CBM, the gas is fed into the national grid; the participating partners act as both co-developers and likely offtake coordinators (ONGC, IOC, BCCL) [^ip-latest]. End buyers are transmission utilities such as GAIL and state city-gas distributors, though PEL sells at the grid entry point. The subsidiary structure is not detailed in the annual report excerpt; the company appears to operate directly through project teams in Jharkhand and Rajasthan [^ar-fy25]. 

Manufacturing/extraction footprint: Operational blocks lie in Jharkhand (NK and Jharia) and Rajasthan (three marginal fields); corporate registered office is in Ahmedabad, with a Ranchi office for field coordination [^ar-fy25][^ip-latest]. 

Promoter background: Chairman Mr. Prem Singh Sawhney (long-tenured), Managing Director Mr. Shanil Paras Savla (appointed w.e.f. January 1, 2026), CFO Mr. Vishal G. Palkhiwala [^ar-fy25]. The group context is a focused micro-cap E&P entity without a large industrial parent, differentiating it from PSU giants like ONGC.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian gas demand trajectory shows consumption of 131 MMSCMD (million metric standard cubic metres per day) in 2015-16 rising to 188 in 2023-24 and projected at 297 (base case) to 365 (accelerated) by 2030 [^ip-latest]. Converting roughly: at ~$3.5/mmBtu and 1 MMSCMD ≈ 3.65 bcm/yr, the 2030 base demand implies a gas value pool of ~$30–40 bn annually; the stated ~$67 bn planned infrastructure investment by 2030 (pipelines 25,000 km in 2026 → 30,000+ km in 2027) underscores the TAM [^ip-latest]. In Rs Cr, the midstream+upstream opportunity is several lakh crore. CAGR of demand ~7–8% (2016–2030). Policy tailwinds: Government CBM target of 5.0 MMSCMD by 2027-28, Special CBM Bid Rounds 2025/26, and pipeline connectivity subsidies [^ip-latest].
