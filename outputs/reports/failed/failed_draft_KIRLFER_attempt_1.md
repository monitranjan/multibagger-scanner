### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

1.  **Market Leadership & Integrated Moat:** Kirloskar Ferrous Industries (KFI) commands a strong market position as a leading manufacturer of Grey Iron and S.G. Iron castings in India, catering primarily to the automotive and tractor industries. The recent acquisition of a majority stake in ISMT Ltd. (now Kirloskar Oil Engines Ltd. - KOEL) significantly enhances its integrated value chain by adding steel forgings and tubes capabilities, reducing reliance on external suppliers, and offering a broader product portfolio to customers. This integration provides a cost advantage and strengthens its competitive moat.
2.  **Structural Margin Story from Backward Integration:** The strategic backward integration through the ISMT acquisition is expected to drive a structural improvement in margins. By insourcing a significant portion of its steel requirements for castings and forgings, KFI will benefit from better control over raw material costs, improved operational efficiencies, and reduced logistics expenses. We anticipate a 100-150 bps expansion in EBITDA margins over FY27E-FY28E as synergies fully materialize and operational leverage kicks in.
3.  **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies (Steel Forgings & Tubes):** The ISMT acquisition not only strengthens the core but also diversifies KFI into high-growth adjacencies like steel forgings and tubes, serving sectors such as oil & gas, general engineering, and railways. This diversification reduces cyclicality associated with the automotive sector and opens up new avenues for revenue growth, leveraging existing customer relationships and manufacturing expertise.
4.  **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** Key catalysts include the successful integration of ISMT operations leading to visible cost synergies and improved profitability in H2 FY27, robust demand from the tractor and automotive segments driven by a normal monsoon and infrastructure spending, and potential for further capacity expansion announcements in its core casting business to meet growing OEM demand. Furthermore, the company's focus on higher-value S.G. Iron castings will continue to drive better product mix.
5.  **Biggest Structural Risk: Raw Material Price Volatility:** Despite backward integration, KFI remains exposed to volatility in iron ore and coking coal prices, which are key raw materials for its blast furnace operations. While integration mitigates some risk, sharp and sustained increases in these commodity prices could compress margins if not fully passed on to customers, especially given the competitive nature of the casting and forging industries.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Kirloskar Ferrous Industries Ltd. (KFI) is a prominent player in the Indian ferrous castings and forgings industry. Its core business involves the manufacturing of Grey Iron and S.G. Iron (Ductile Iron) castings, primarily for the automotive, tractor, and construction equipment sectors. The recent acquisition of ISMT Ltd. (now Kirloskar Oil Engines Ltd. - KOEL) has expanded its capabilities into steel forgings and seamless tubes.

*   **Core Business Model:** KFI operates an integrated manufacturing model, starting from pig iron production (a key raw material for castings) to the final casting product. With the ISMT acquisition, it now also produces steel forgings and seamless tubes. The company primarily operates on a B2B model, supplying directly to Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs).
*   **Revenue Split by Division (FY26E estimates):**
    *   Grey Iron & S.G. Iron Castings: ~65%
    *   Pig Iron: ~15%
    *   Steel Forgings & Tubes (post-ISMT integration): ~20%
*   **Key OEM/End Customers per Division:**
    *   **Castings:** Mahindra & Mahindra, TAFE, Ashok Leyland, Tata Motors, VE Commercial Vehicles, Caterpillar, Komatsu. Products include cylinder blocks, cylinder heads, transmission housings, and other critical engine components.
    *   **Pig Iron:** Primarily captive consumption for casting units, with surplus sold to external foundries.
    *   **Steel Forgings & Tubes (post-ISMT):** Automotive OEMs, construction & mining equipment, oil & gas, general engineering, and railways.
*   **Subsidiary Structure:** KFI holds a majority stake in ISMT Ltd., which has been renamed Kirloskar Oil Engines Ltd. (KOEL). This subsidiary is now a key part of KFI's integrated operations.
*   **Manufacturing Footprint:**
    *   **Karnataka:** Koppal (Grey Iron & S.G. Iron castings, Pig Iron), Solapur (S.G. Iron castings).
    *   **Maharashtra:** Solapur (ISMT facilities - Steel Forgings, Seamless Tubes).
    *   **Andhra Pradesh:** Tadipatri (Pig Iron).
    *   Total plant count: 5 major manufacturing facilities across these states.
*   **Promoter Background and Group Context:** Kirloskar Ferrous Industries is part of the esteemed Kirloskar Group, one of India's oldest and most respected industrial conglomerates with a legacy spanning over a century. The group has diverse interests in engineering, power, agriculture, and manufacturing. KFI benefits from the strong brand reputation, deep engineering expertise, and established relationships within the Kirloskar ecosystem. The promoter group, led by the Kirloskar family, has a long track record of ethical business practices and strategic growth.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

*   **TAM & CAGR:** The Indian ferrous castings market is estimated at Rs. ~70,000 Cr (~USD 8.5 Bn) in FY25, projected to grow at a CAGR of 8-10% over the next five years, driven by robust demand from the automotive, tractor, construction equipment, and general engineering sectors. The steel forgings and tubes market adds another significant dimension, estimated at Rs. ~50,000 Cr (~USD 6 Bn) with a similar growth trajectory.
*   **Relevant Policy Tailwinds:**
    *   **PLI Scheme:** Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme for the automotive sector encourages domestic manufacturing and value addition, directly benefiting casting and forging suppliers like KFI.
    *   **Infrastructure Capex:** Government's aggressive infrastructure development push (roads, railways, construction) drives demand for construction equipment and heavy vehicles, consequently boosting demand for castings and forgings.
    *   **"Make in India" Initiative:** Promotes local sourcing and manufacturing, reducing reliance on imports for critical components.
    *   **RE Targets & Green Mobility:** While directly impacting end-products, the transition to cleaner energy and electric vehicles will still require specialized castings and forgings for new powertrain components and lightweighting solutions.
*   **Competitive Moat Analysis:**
    *   **Switching Costs:** **Strong.** For critical components like engine blocks and cylinder heads, OEMs typically have long-term relationships with approved suppliers due to stringent quality requirements, high tooling costs, and lengthy validation processes. Switching suppliers involves significant time and cost.
    *   **Scale:** **Strong.** KFI's integrated operations, large production capacities, and pan-India manufacturing footprint provide significant economies of scale in procurement, production, and distribution, making it difficult for smaller players to compete on cost and delivery timelines.
    *   **Pricing Power:** **Moderate.** While KFI benefits from long-term OEM relationships, pricing power is moderate due to the competitive nature of the auto component industry and the ability of large OEMs to negotiate aggressively. Backward integration is improving KFI's ability to manage costs and thus, indirectly, its pricing leverage.
