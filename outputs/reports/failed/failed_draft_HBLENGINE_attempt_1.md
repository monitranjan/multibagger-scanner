### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Multi-vendor rail safety monopoly in Kavach (TCAS):** HBL is one of only three approved Indian Railways vendors for the indigenous Train Collision Avoidance System (Kavach), with the multi-year approval cycle (~2-3+ years) creating a near-impregnable entry barrier [^vp-thread]. Indian Railways has signalled rollout across ~35,000 Rkms, translating into a Rs. 15,000-20,000 Cr cumulative TAM over 4-5 years, of which HBL has already secured ~Rs. 600 Cr in FY26 awards (executable over 18-24 months) [^vp-thread]. The recently announced Rs. 24 Cr order from ICF Chennai for On-Board Kavach 4.0 equipment validates execution and demonstrates an accelerating order pipeline [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin re-rating from R&D normalisation:** Management has explicitly indicated that "much of the cost of product development has been expensed," and that "R&D expenditure would be lower" for the next few years while capex of FY24 will not recur [^vp-thread]. With standalone ROCE already at 48.3% and ROE at 36.0%, the mix shift toward higher-margin Kavach, TMS (Train Management System), and defence electronics should expand EBITDA margins by 250-400 bps over FY26-28E — a structural, not cyclical, story [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies (Defence + Data Centres):** Beyond rail, HBL is the only Indian player to have developed a PLT (Pure Lead Thin-plate) battery (critical for data centre UPS applications with only two global suppliers), the world's 2nd largest Ni-Cd battery manufacturer, and the only domestic producer of TMS [^vp-thread]. The Tonbo Imaging partnership for electro-optic defence products and ongoing fuzes/grenades work position HBL as a multi-vertical deep-tech defence electronics play, not a pure railway stock [^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months):** (i) Kavach tender awards — additional ~Rs. 2,000 Cr of tenders expected in FY27 with HBL targeting 25-30% share [^vp-thread]; (ii) PLT battery commercial scale-up at Vizag plant targeting data centre OEMs; (iii) Defence order book ramp via Tonbo Imaging tie-up; (iv) margin expansion as R&D peaks behind; (v) potential index inclusion flow benefit given MCap of Rs. 16,000 Cr and rising DII holding to 22.2% [^ar-fy25].

- **Biggest structural risk — execution + competitive intensity:** While Kavach is a 3-vendor market today, any aggressive pricing by the larger peer (BHEL-led consortium) or faster entry by global TCAS players (Hitachi/Mitsubishi) post-2027 could compress HBL's pricing power and execution spreads [^vp-thread]. Secondary risk is working capital intensity — management has flagged that "working capital would increase" as Kavach and defence order books scale, which could pressure FCF conversion [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model**

HBL Power Systems is a Hyderabad-based, technology-driven niche player operating across three verticals: (1) Batteries (industrial Ni-Cd, lead-acid, PLT, lithium); (2) Railway Electronics (Kavach/TCAS, TMS, axle counters, signalling); and (3) Defence Electronics (fuzes, grenades, electro-optic products via Tonbo Imaging JV, naval batteries) [^ar-fy25]. The company follows a "identify technology gap, develop in-house, become sole/limited supplier" model — visible in its unique positions in PLT batteries, indigenous TCAS, and TMS [^vp-thread].
