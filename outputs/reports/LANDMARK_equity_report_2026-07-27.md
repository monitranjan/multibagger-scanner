## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Sole listed proxy on India's premium/luxury car cycle.** Landmark is the only meaningfully liquid listed pure-play dealership for premium and luxury passenger vehicles in India, with exclusive/primary representation for Mercedes-Benz (contributing ~15% of Mercedes India retail sales), BMW, Honda, Jeep, Volkswagen, BYD, MG, Mahindra (newly onboarded) and Ashok Leyland (CV) across select geographies — a portfolio footprint that no listed peer can replicate at this scale [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread].

- **After-sales is the structural margin anchor.** Service business operated at ~18% EBITDA margin and ~25% ROCE in FY25, contributing ~60% of consolidated EBITDA; Landmark serviced ~3 lakh cars in FY25, and management indicates >50% of vehicles sold by the company continue to return to authorised service centres even 5 years post-sale, giving the division annuity-like characteristics that materially de-risk the consolidated P&L vs. peer dealers [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies is gaining traction.** Three layers of optionality are now visible: (1) the pre-owned car (used car) business is being scaled to a Rs. 100 Cr revenue run-rate in FY26 with minimal incremental fixed cost; (2) BYD EV dealerships in Delhi/Mumbai provide a front-row position in the NEV ramp; and (3) the Mahindra partnership (announced as Renault was exited) repositions the volume side toward the fastest-growing SUV franchise in India [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (next 6–12 months) are unusually clustered.** Q2FY27 onwards is expected to see: (a) launch deliveries of the Mercedes-Benz GLC with >1,500 confirmed bookings worth ~Rs. 1,200 Cr; (b) ramp of Honda Elevate; (c) commencement of MG Motor operations at Bhopal and Indore; (d) BYD vehicle arrivals that were stuck for ~2 months finally flowing through; and (e) launch of the Jeep 4x2 diesel — together likely to drive double-digit YoY growth in a Q1FY27 that was optically weak (sales Rs. 694 Cr vs Rs. 800 Cr YoY; EBITDA Rs. 44 Cr vs Rs. 51 Cr) [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk: OEM concentration and cyclicality.** Mercedes-Benz alone is the single largest profit contributor, and a strategic shift by Mercedes (e.g., direct-to-consumer, agency model, or downsizing dealer footprint — as globally discussed) is the principal long-term risk; near-term, new-car EBITDA is volume-cyclical and the TTM P/E of 40x already prices in a meaningful re-rating, leaving little margin for execution slippage or OEM contract renegotiations [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model.** Landmark Cars is an asset-light automotive retail platform that operates authorised sales, service, spares and allied businesses for premium and luxury passenger vehicles and select commercial vehicles. Revenue is generated from three streams: (i) new vehicle sales, (ii) after-sales service and accident repairs, and (iii) allied businesses including insurance, accessories, pre-owned vehicles, and finance products [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25].

- **Revenue mix by division (FY25/FY26 indicative).**
  - New car sales: ~80% of revenue / ~40% of EBITDA (low single-digit margin, high-velocity)
  - After-sales service: ~15% of revenue / ~60% of EBITDA (high-margin annuity, ~18% EBITDA margin)
  - Allied (insurance, pre-owned, accessories, finance): ~5% of revenue / balance of EBITDA, with pre-owned car business targeted to scale to Rs. 100 Cr in FY26 [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].

- **Key OEM / end-customer relationships by division.**
  - Luxury: Mercedes-Benz (flagship; ~15% of Mercedes India sales routed via Landmark) [^vp-thread]
  - Premium / mass-premium: Honda Cars, Jeep, Volkswagen, MG Motor (operations starting Q2FY27 at Bhopal/Indore) [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread]
  - New energy: BYD (Delhi and Mumbai showrooms) [^vp-thread]
  - New volume franchise: Mahindra & Mahindra (newly onboarded; replaces Renault in Landmark's portfolio) [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript]
  - Commercial vehicles: Ashok Leyland [^ip-latest]
  - Renault: being exited, as Renault's future portfolio moves to the affordable segment, which is outside Landmark's premium-focused strategy [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].

- **Subsidiary structure.** The company operates through subsidiary SPVs that are typical of dealership businesses — each dealership/workshop is housed under a separate entity for OEM-contract and licensing reasons. Group entities handle BYD, Mercedes-Benz, Honda and commercial vehicle operations in their respective territorial clusters [^ar-fy25].

- **Manufacturing/operational footprint (dealership outlets, not plants).** Showroom and workshop presence is concentrated in (i) the National Capital Region (Delhi), (ii) Mumbai Metropolitan Region, (iii) Madhya Pradesh (Bhopal, Indore — newly added for MG), and (iv) Punjab/Chandigarh (where Renault is being unwound). The company employs 4,000+ personnel across these touchpoints [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread].

- **Promoter background and group context.** The promoter group is the Thakker family, led by Sanjay Karshan Thakker; promoter holding stood at 55.25% as of 27 July 2026. The group has decades of automotive retail experience and Landmark's positioning around premium and luxury brands (vs. mass-market) is a deliberate strategic choice rather than a circumstantial one [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread].

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **Total Addressable Market (TAM).** India's passenger vehicle market is ~4 million units annually; within this, the luxury segment is currently only ~40,000 units, i.e. ~1% penetration vs. ~10% in most developed car markets — implying a 5–10x structural runway as incomes and wealth effect compound [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread]. In monetary terms, the addressable premium + luxury retail TAM in India is estimated at ~Rs. 1.0–1.2 lakh Cr at retail level, with the service TAM a long-duration annuity layered on top [^ip-latest].

- **Industry CAGR and growth drivers.** The Indian PV industry grew ~10% YoY in FY26 despite a high base, with SUV and premium segments growing materially faster than the industry average. The CV segment is recovering on the back of infra capex and replacement demand, while EV adoption is being catalysed by FAME-II and PLI-linked OEM investments [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].

- **Policy tailwinds relevant to Landmark.**
  - PLI scheme for automotive and auto components (incentivises local production by OEMs like Mercedes, Hyundai, Tata — supporting the long-term volume pipeline) [^ip-latest].
  - FAME-II and state EV subsidies (direct beneficiary via BYD dealerships) [^ip-latest].
  - Infrastructure capex cycle (supports Ashok Leyland CV volumes) [^ip-latest].
  - Progressive GST rationalisation and personal income tax relief improving discretionary spending power for premium purchases [^vp-thread].

- **Competitive moat analysis (rated Strong / Moderate / Weak).**
  - **Switching costs — Strong.** Customers are locked into authorised service networks for warranty, software updates, and parts; OEMs also have high contractual stickiness with top dealers due to real-estate and capex investments [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread].
  - **Scale — Strong.** Largest listed pure-play luxury/premium dealer; 4,000+ employees, multi-city presence, and exclusive BYD/strong Mercedes positioning create barriers to entry for any prospective listed peer [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread].
  - **Pricing power — Moderate.** Vehicle invoice prices are OEM-controlled; the dealer captures margin via discounts, finance/insurance commissions, accessories and service pricing — leaving the dealer moderately exposed to OEM discount aggression in soft months [^cc-transcript].
  - **OEM concentration risk — Weak (mitigant needed).** Concentration with Mercedes-Benz is the principal vulnerability; the Mahindra addition and MG/BYD expansion are partial offsets [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread].

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree and tenure.** The promoter group, led by Sanjay Karshan Thakker, has a multi-decade operating history in automotive retail. The decision to consciously exit the Renault (mass-market) franchise and onboard Mahindra, plus the deliberate long-cycle investment in BYD, reflects a strategy-led, not opportunistic, capital allocation approach. Promoter holding of 55.25% (with no reported pledging in the public data set) indicates skin-in-the-game alignment with minority shareholders [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread].

- **FCF deployment track record (3-year view).** The company's cash generation has been redeployed in three discernible buckets: (i) capex on new showrooms and workshops for BYD/Mahindra/MG additions; (ii) working capital build for inventory ahead of new launches (notably Mercedes GLC and Honda Elevate); and (iii) a return-of-capital programme that has delivered an unusually high ~10% trailing dividend yield (including a special dividend announced alongside FY26 results, as referenced in the 27 July 2026 press release) — a clear signal of management confidence in the cash-generative nature of the after-sales annuity [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].

- **Debt management and credit profile.** As a working-capital-intensive dealer, Landmark carries inventory and debtor financing typical of the sector; the company has not relied on long-term debt to fund capex, with internal accruals and supplier credit covering the bulk of the balance sheet needs. No public credit-rating downgrade or covenant breach has been flagged in recent filings, and the consolidated leverage remains comfortable relative to the EBITDA annuity from the after-sales stream [^ar-fy25].

- **Dividend policy.** The company has demonstrated a high payout orientation, with the FY26 results press release of 27 July 2026 specifically calling out a dividend alongside a bonus issue announcement. The ~10% trailing yield is therefore a blend of regular payout and a special one-time distribution; on a steady-state basis, the effective payout ratio is materially lower but still among the highest in the listed auto-dealer universe [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].

- **Promoter pledging and shareholding stability.** Promoter pledging is not flagged in the publicly available data, and the 55.25% promoter stake has been stable. FII holding at 12.24% and DII holding at 8.16% reflect reasonable institutional participation without crowding risk; the recent EGM notice (24 August 2026) is for the bonus issue and standard AGM-related items, with no negative implications indicated [^ar-fy25].

- **Corporate governance flags.** No auditor changes, qualified audit opinions, or material related-party transaction (RPT) concerns have been publicly flagged in recent filings. The Annual Report for FY26 (filed 27 July 2026) and the earnings call transcript of 22 July 2026 are the primary references, and disclosures are consistent with standard listed-entity practice. The board's decision to exit Renault (a non-performing brand in the Punjab catchment) and onboard Mahindra is itself a positive governance signal — capital being actively reallocated rather than defended [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript].

- **JVs and strategic partnerships.**
  - **Mercedes-Benz** — long-standing primary partner; the deepest economic relationship and the one most exposed to OEM-strategy risk [^ip-latest] [^vp-thread].
  - **BYD** — exclusive/showroom partner in Delhi and Mumbai, providing a structural EV optionality with minimal incremental capex required [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].
  - **Mahindra & Mahindra** — newly onboarded; rationale is to participate in India's fastest-growing SUV franchise, offsetting Renault exit [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].
  - **MG Motor** — partnership commencing Q2FY27 across Bhopal and Indore [^cc-transcript] [^vp-thread].
  - **Ashok Leyland** — CV partnership providing cyclicality hedge and infra-capex beta [^ip-latest].
