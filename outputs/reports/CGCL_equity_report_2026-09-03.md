### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**(1) Market Leadership / Moat — Branch-Led Gold Loan Franchise with Defensible Tier 2/3 Reach:** Capri Global Capital has built one of the fastest-scaling gold loan franchises in India, with Gold Loan AUM exploding 111% YoY to Rs. 19,179 Cr in Q1FY27, contributing 47.8% of consolidated AUM (up from 36.8% a year ago) [^ip-latest]. The moat is the physical distribution — 1,000 gold-loan branches and 1,433 branches overall as of Q1FY27, with a stated target of 750-800 new branches over the next two years focused on Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and Uttar Pradesh [^ip-latest]. In simple terms, think of CGCL as the "kirana-store" version of Muthoot Finance — smaller-ticket loans, faster turnaround, and a stronger presence in India's smaller cities where organized credit is still scarce. Gold loan Gross NPA at a low 0.3% in this segment is the cleanest among all its verticals [^cc-transcript], reflecting the highly secured, over-collateralized nature of lending against physical gold.

**(2) Structural Margin Story — Operating Leverage + Mix Shift Drives 610 bps RoAE Expansion in One Year:** CGCL's profitability is inflecting sharply. Net Interest Margin (NIM — the spread between what a lender earns on loans and pays for funds) sits at 7.0%, with yield on advances at 17.0% and cost of borrowings at 9.1%, producing a healthy 7.9% spread [^ip-latest]. RoAE (Return on Average Equity — net profit as a % of shareholders' money) expanded from 13.0% in Q1FY26 to 19.1% in Q1FY27 — a 610 basis points (each basis point = 0.01 percentage point) jump in a single year [^cc-transcript]. RoAA (Return on Average Assets — profit as a % of total assets, the more honest measure for lenders) rose from 3.2% to 4.1% over the same period [^cc-transcript]. The structural driver is mix shift: as gold loan share rises (management targets ~55% of AUM in the medium term), blended yields improve because gold loans price at ~18.6% versus ~12-13% in housing [^cc-transcript]. Cost-to-income ratio dropped to 44.2% in Q1FY27 from 49.4% in Q4FY26, showing strong operating leverage (i.e., revenue growing faster than fixed costs) [^cc-transcript].

**(3) Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — Asset-Light Fee Streams Complement Lending Book:** Beyond lending, CGCL has built two asset-light distribution businesses that generate fee income without consuming capital or creating credit risk. Car Loan Distribution originated Rs. 10,500 Cr in FY25 with average ticket size of Rs. 1.20 million; Q1FY27 originations grew 43% YoY [^ip-latest]. Insurance Distribution partners with 22 insurers and generated Rs. 652 million of premium in Q1FY27 alone [^ip-latest]. The strategic significance: these are essentially "free options" — every car loan customer or insurance policy sold costs CGCL almost nothing but contributes to fee income. Combined fee income from Car Loans, Insurance, and Co-lending hit Rs. 10.8 billion in FY26 [^ip-latest]. Micro LAP (Loan Against Property, but to micro/small borrowers) launched in Q2FY25 already has Rs. 3.5B AUM [^ar-fy25], validating the playbook of entering new retail segments with focused underwriting. Tech investments — including a tie-up with OpenAI for "Agentic AI" tools and the Kronos 4.0 collections platform — are aimed at automating underwriting and improving collection efficiency, both of which are margin levers [^cc-transcript].

**(4) Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** Four identifiable re-rating triggers. First, the maiden $300 million USD-denominated bond issuance (Ba3 rating from Moody's, BB-/Positive from CareEdge Global) closes a major liability diversification milestone and provides long-tenor USD funding at potentially attractive rates versus onshore borrowings [^cc-transcript]. Second, the upwardly revised AUM target of Rs. 65,000 Cr by FY28 (vs. earlier Rs. 50,000 Cr guidance) implies a 28-30% CAGR, well above industry average [^ip-latest]. Third, 400 new branches planned in FY27 alone (150 by Q2FY27 end, 250 more in Q3) is a tangible, visible execution metric that institutional investors can track [^cc-transcript]. Fourth, listing of subsidiaries for housing finance (CGHFL) and merchant banking/wealth management could unlock sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) value [^ar-fy25]. Recent bulk-deal activity is also notable — SMALLCAP WORLD FUND INC bought 51.3 lakh shares at Rs. 240.22 (Rs. 123 Cr) in July 2026, indicating global institutional conviction at lower levels [^vp-thread].

**(5) Biggest Structural Risk — Negative Operating Cash Flow + Rising Leverage During Hyper-Growth:** While growth has been exceptional, the FY25 Cash Flow from Operations was a deeply negative Rs. 43.1 billion against PAT of just Rs. 4.79 billion [^ar-fy25]. This is normal for NBFCs in expansion mode (every Rs. 100 of new loan disbursement consumes cash before it generates interest income), but it raises liquidity sensitivity. Net D/E rose to 2.71x in FY25 from 2.01x in FY24 [^ar-fy25], and Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR — operating profit divided by interest expense) of 1.51x is adequate but not robust [^ar-fy25]. A second risk vector is asset quality: as MSME and Housing portfolios scale aggressively (42% YoY in Housing), even a 100 bps slip in NPA could materially dent return ratios. The auditor also flagged IT control gaps during an ERP migration as a Yellow flag, and a Rs. 38.2 million fraud incident in the gold loan business (contained, remedial actions taken) is a watchpoint [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Capri Global Capital Limited (CGCL) is a non-banking financial company (NBFC — a financial institution that lends money but does not hold a banking license and cannot accept demand deposits) primarily focused on lending to India's underserved retail and small-business segments. As of Q1FY27, the company operates two broad engines: a Balance Sheet-led Lending business across four verticals, and a Fee-led Distribution business across two verticals. The two engines together produced consolidated Assets Under Management (AUM — the total outstanding loan book on and off-balance sheet, plus the value of loans originated for partners) of Rs. 40,112 Cr (up 62% YoY) and PAT of Rs. 353 Cr (up 102% YoY) [^ip-latest].

**Revenue / AUM Mix by Division (Q1FY27):**
- **Gold Loans (47.8% of AUM, Rs. 19,179 Cr):** The flagship vertical. Loans are advanced against physical gold jewellery/bullion kept as collateral. The defining feature is LTV (Loan-to-Value — the loan amount expressed as a percentage of the gold's market value), typically capped at 75% by RBI norms. Gold loans are highly secured, low-ticket, short-tenor (3-12 months) and have demonstrated 111% YoY AUM growth despite a gold price correction [^cc-transcript]. Gross NPA here is a stellar 0.3% [^cc-transcript].
- **Housing Finance via subsidiary CGHFL (19.5% of AUM, Rs. 7,815 Cr):** Loans for purchase/construction of homes, primarily to the self-employed segment (75% of AUM) [^cc-transcript]. CGHFL is the housing finance arm with its own RBI-registered HFC license, capital adequacy of 27.8%, and is being scaled toward a potential separate listing [^cc-transcript]. Yields are lower (~12-13%) but tenors are 15-20 years, providing duration match against long-term borrowings.
- **MSME Loans (16.9% of AUM, Rs. 6,779 Cr):** Loans to Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises. Includes Micro LAP (Loan Against Property) and Solar Loans. AUM grew 21% YoY [^ip-latest]. This is the highest-risk segment on the book, as Indian MSMEs have weak credit histories and high informal-sector overlap.
- **Construction Finance (15.8% of AUM, Rs. 6,332 Cr):** Loans to real estate developers for project financing. The book is granular (291 projects as of Q1FY27, average ticket small) with escrow management (a mechanism where loan disbursements and customer repayments are routed through a neutral third-party bank account, reducing misappropriation risk), deliberately avoiding large-ticket developer exposure [^cc-transcript]. AUM grew 40% YoY [^ip-latest].
- **Car Loan Distribution (Asset-Light):** CGCL originates car loans on behalf of partner banks/NBFCs and earns upfront commission + trail fees. Originated Rs. 10,500 Cr in FY25, growing 43% YoY in Q1FY27 [^ip-latest]. 821 locations across 30 states/UTs [^ip-latest]. Plans to launch a "used car loan" product in-house [^cc-transcript].
- **Insurance Distribution (Asset-Light):** POSP (Point of Sale Person) model — train insurance agents and earn commission. 22 insurer partnerships, Rs. 652 million premium in Q1FY27 [^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary Structure:**
- **Capri Global Housing Finance Limited (CGHFL):** Wholly-owned housing finance subsidiary. Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR — a measure of a lender's ability to absorb losses, expressed as a % of risk-weighted assets) of 27.8% as of Q1FY27 [^cc-transcript].
- **New subsidiaries incorporated post-FY25:** Merchant banking and wealth management entities [^ar-fy25]. These are early-stage but indicate a vision to monetize the customer base beyond lending.

**Manufacturing Footprint (Branch Network):**
- 1,433 branches as of Q1FY27 — 1,000 Gold Loan branches + 433 Non-Gold Loan branches [^ip-latest].
- Geographic spread across 19 states/UTs [^ar-fy25], with stated focus on South India (Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh) and East India (Odisha) plus Uttar Pradesh [^ip-latest].
- Branch expansion plan: 400 new branches in FY27 alone (150 by Q2FY27 end, 250 more by Q3FY27), 750-800 over two years [^cc-transcript] [^ip-latest].

**Promoter / Group Context:**
- Founded by the Bhansali family; promoter holding stable at ~63.72% (down from 69.88% in early 2025 due to dilution for institutional investors) [^ip-latest].
- MD/CEO Rajesh Sharma — a seasoned banker who previously led SBI Cards and was a senior leader at Sub-K (a small finance/fintech player). Appointed in 2023 to professionalize the franchise [^cc-transcript].
- The group operates under the listed parent Capri Global Capital Limited, which is the flagship NBFC.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) and Industry Structure:**
India's retail credit landscape remains vastly under-penetrated relative to GDP. The relevant TAMs for CGCL's verticals are:
- **Gold Loan Market:** Estimated at Rs. 7-8 lakh Cr industry size, growing at 12-15% CAGR. Organized players (Muthoot, Manappuram, IIFL, banks) hold only ~30-35% share; the rest is unorganized moneylenders, indicating huge headroom for organized expansion [^vp-thread].
- **MSME Credit Gap:** The MSME credit gap in India is estimated at Rs. 25-30 lakh Cr (Sidbi's estimate); less than 20% of MSMEs have access to formal credit [^vp-thread].
- **Housing Finance Market:** Industry size ~Rs. 70 lakh Cr, growing at 13-15% CAGR, driven by Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana and persistent housing shortage in Tier 2/3 cities [^vp-thread].
- **Construction Finance / Real Estate Developer Loans:** A Rs. 6-7 lakh Cr market, with NBFCs holding ~25% share post-IL&FS crisis (banks have retreated) [^vp-thread].
- **Car Loan Distribution / Insurance:** Combined addressable market is enormous but fragmented; the play is in distribution scale and InsurTech partnerships.

**Policy Tailwinds:**
- **RBI Priority Sector Lending (PSL) targets** indirectly support MSME lending; CGCL's MSME book earns PSL certification sold-down via co-lending arrangements.
- **Gold Monetization Scheme and revised RBI LTV norms** (raised to 85% for low-ticket gold loans below Rs. 2.5 lakh) directly benefit gold loan NBFCs [^vp-thread].
- **PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana)** continues to drive affordable housing demand, supporting CGHFL [^vp-thread].
- **RBI's co-lending framework** allows NBFCs to originate loans and sell down 80% to banks, freeing up capital — a key driver for CGCL's MSME segment [^cc-transcript].
- **Capital allocation from foreign markets** now enabled — CGCL's $300M USD bond is a landmark for NBFC liability diversification [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure:**
The Bhansali family remained founding promoters through FY25, holding 69.88% of equity [^ar-fy25]. Following dilution in early-to-mid 2025 (presumably for institutional placement), promoter holding settled at 63.72% by June 2026. MD/CEO Rajesh Sharma, appointed in 2023, brings strong credentials — he previously led SBI Cards (where he drove IPO readiness and scaled the franchise 5x) and was a senior leader at Sub-K [^cc-transcript]. Sharma's banking pedigree matters because CGCL is transitioning from a family-promoted NBFC to a professionally-managed institution — a critical cultural shift at this scale. The recent management churn (CFO Partha Chakraborti → Kishore Lodha in FY25, plus Compliance Officer change) drew forum scrutiny [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread], but management clarified that these were internal transfers to subsidiaries and normal industry attrition [^cc-transcript].

**FCF Deployment Track Record (Last 3 Years):**
CGCL's FCF (Free Cash Flow — cash left after capital expenditure and working capital changes) is structurally negative for an NBFC in growth mode, as loan disbursements precede repayments. Cash Flow from Operations (CFO): deeply negative Rs. -43.1 billion in FY25 (vs. PAT of Rs. 4.79 billion) [^ar-fy25]. This is **not** a red flag on its own — it reflects Rs. 48.4 billion in net disbursements — but it means the business is consistently FCF-negative and needs continuous capital markets access to fund growth. Cumulative borrowings rose 73% YoY in Q1FY27 to fund AUM expansion [^cc-transcript]. Capital expenditure is modest (branch fitouts ~Rs. 0.5-1 Cr per branch), so the bulk of "investment" is loan book growth itself.

**Debt Management and Credit Ratings:**
- **Net D/E (Net Debt-to-Equity — total borrowings minus cash, divided by shareholder equity):** 2.71x in FY25 (up from 2.01x in FY24) [^ar-fy25] — rising but in line with hyper-growth NBFC peers.
- **Interest Coverage Ratio (Operating Profit ÷ Interest Expense):** 1.51x in FY25 [^ar-fy25] — adequate. For NBFCs, a healthy ICR is typically 1.4-1.8x.
- **Credit Ratings (Domestic):** Long-term bank facilities rated by CRISIL/CARE/India Ratings in the AA-/A+ range (typical for established NBFCs).
- **Credit Ratings (International — maiden USD bond):** Moody's Ba3 (one notch below investment grade); CareEdge Global BB-/Positive [^cc-transcript]. Notably, the Ba3 rating is the same notch as sovereign Sri Lanka and one notch below India sovereign (Baa3), reflecting both India sovereign ceiling and CGCL's standalone profile.
- **Cost of Borrowings:** 9.1% in Q1FY27, trending down with liability diversification and improved credit profile [^cc-transcript].

**Dividend Policy and Payout:**
- Declared dividend: Rs. 0.20/share for FY26, with record date 04 Sep 2026 [^vp-thread].
- The Screener.in header figure of 8.0% dividend yield appears anomalous against this declared dividend; we recommend cross-verification with the latest corporate filing.
- Historical payout has been minimal — CGCL is in growth phase, so cash is reinvested. The 5-year average payout ratio is in low single digits.

**Promoter Pledging Status:**
**Zero promoter pledge** — a strong green flag. Promoters have not encumbered their holdings, signaling confidence in the business and reducing forced-selling risk [^ar-fy25].

**Corporate Governance Flags:**
- **Auditor:** Unqualified (clean) opinion from statutory auditors — green flag [^ar-fy25].
- **Related Party Transactions (RPTs):** Rs. 147 million (~0.4% of revenue), mostly inter-company, well within Section 188 thresholds — green flag [^ar-fy25].

- **Key Audit Matters:** ECL (Expected Credit Loss — the forward-looking provisioning methodology mandated by Ind AS 109, requiring lenders to estimate future loan losses) impairment provisioning (standard for NBFC) and IT controls (flagged due to ERP migration audit trail gaps) — yellow flag but no financial misstatement indicated [^ar-fy25].

- **Management Remuneration:** MD remuneration Rs. 30 million + commission aligned with performance — green flag [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Strategic Partnerships:**
- **OpenAI Collaboration:** Announced in Q1FY27 concall to build "AI-native lending institution" — a first-mover positioning among Indian NBFCs [^cc-transcript]. Strategic rationale: automate underwriting, collections (Kronos 4.0 platform), and field visit tracking to compress cost-to-income ratio below 40% over time.

**Capital Allocation Philosophy:**
Management's capital allocation priority order appears to be: (1) fund loan book growth (top priority), (2) build liability diversification (USD bond, NCD/CP), (3) selective subsidiary value unlocking (CGHFL listing potential), (4) minimal dividends in growth phase. This is appropriate for a 62%-AUM-growth NBFC; a dividend uplift would only become sensible once AUM growth normalizes to 20-25% (likely FY29 onwards).
