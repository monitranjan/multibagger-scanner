### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat**: Themis Medicare has built a focused leadership position in India’s hospital-centric critical care and anaesthesia segments, which are akin to the “high-end ICU equipment” of pharma – complex injectables (medicines delivered via syringe/vial directly into bloodstream) that require stringent manufacturing expertise and regulatory approvals [^ip-latest]. Unlike western markets where 3-4 players control ~90% of hospital share, India’s hospital segment is highly fragmented, giving Themis a first-mover advantage with its 7-8 year old anaesthesia network and first-to-launch Remithem (Remifentanil, an anaesthetic agent) in critical care [^cc-transcript]. This constitutes a Moderate moat: switching costs for hospitals are high because clinical familiarity and regulatory dossiers create stickiness, but scale is still building relative to global peers.

- **Structural margin story**: The company’s hospital business enjoys significantly lower marketing expenditure than its trade (retail pharmacy) business, meaning gross margin (the difference between selling price and manufacturing cost) flows almost directly to bottom line [^cc-transcript]. For context, trade brands carry ~70% gross margin but higher sales force cost; hospital launches like Remithem yield “significantly better” margins than other products [^cc-transcript]. However, FY26 EBITDA margin compressed to 0.11% and Q1 FY27 EBITDA was -60.28% (EBITDA = earnings before interest, tax, depreciation & amortisation – a proxy for operating profitability before non-cash and financing items) due to API route transition and one-off items [^ip-latest]. The structural recovery hinges on API new manufacturing route improving from “practically zero gross margin” to positive, expected to reflect in coming quarters [^cc-transcript]. A 4,287 basis points (bps = 1/100th of a percentage point, so 42.87%) PAT margin jump in Q1 FY27 was predominantly from one-off share sale gains, not core operations [^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies**: Themis is expanding beyond domestic hospitals into geographic adjacencies: Phase I (0–3 yrs) targets CIS (Commonwealth of Independent States – ex-Soviet countries), Latin America, GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council); Phase II (3–5 yrs) regulated EU/USA [^ip-latest]. Its API division (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient – the core chemical molecule before it becomes a pill/injection) is pivoting to in-house consumption plus commercial production, with pipeline of 5–8 API products/year and 20–24 formulation products/year [^ip-latest]. This reduces dependence on China-priced APIs and builds vertical integration, a classic diversification into higher-control supply chain.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months)**: (i) API and institution business expected healthy recovery in FY27 with “visibility of healthy recovery” [^ip-latest]; (ii) 10+ hospital product launches planned/executed, leveraging critical care network [^cc-transcript]; (iii) Exports: >100 dossiers (regulatory filing packages) for formulations in ROW (Rest of World) markets filed by end FY [^cc-transcript]; (iv) Sale of associate shares yielded ₹92.95 Cr one-off gain boosting PAT, providing balance-sheet cushion [^ip-latest]. These could re-rate the stock from current 29x P/E toward peer Symbiotec’s 69x if execution holds.

- **Biggest structural risk**: Concentrated related-party sales (Themis Distributors, promoter-linked, accounted for ~31% of revenue; total RPT sales ~39% of FY25 rev) create related-party dependency and audit confirmation gaps [^ar-fy25]. Combined with inventory days deteriorating to 214 vs 187 (meaning stock sits longer before sale, tying up cash) and API price erosion from Chinese competition, the earnings quality remains suspect [^ar-fy25][^vp-thread]. Promoter encumbrance (Vividhmargi Trust pledged 2.53 cr shares Aug-2026 for debt security) adds forced-selling risk if collateral thresholds breach [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Themis Medicare operates as a single-reportable-segment pharmaceutical company engaged in finished formulations (medicines in final shape like tablets, injections) and synthetic APIs (the raw chemical ingredients) [^ar-fy25]. Think of it as a kitchen that both makes the raw spices (API) and the cooked dishes (formulations) served to hospitals and pharmacies.

Revenue split by division (Q1 FY27): Hospital Business 56% (comprising Critical Care, Intensive Care, Institution, Exports), Trade Business 21% (Pharma, Ortho, Gynecology divisions), API/Co-Mkt/Others 23% [^ip-latest]. In FY26, mix was Hospital 50%, Trade 26%, API/Co-Mkt 24% [^ip-latest]. Key end customers: Hospital division supplies corporate hospitals and nursing homes; trade division reaches retail pharmacies via distributor Themis Distributors (related party) [^ar-fy25]; API sold to institutional buyers and internal consumption.

Subsidiary structure: Previously UK subsidiaries Carpo Medical/Investments struck off 1 Apr 2025 (geographic exit) [^ar-fy25]; proposed transfer of API business to Themis Chemicals Pvt Ltd (wholly-owned) was withdrawn Nov-24 [^ar-fy25]. Manufacturing footprint: Haridwar (Uttarakhand) formulations plant with capacity 520mn tablets, 9mn ointment tubes, 36mn ampoules (small sealed glass vials for single-dose injectables), 12mn vials (multi-dose containers), 6mn pre-filled syringes (syringe ready with drug) per annum [^ip-latest]; Hyderabad (Telangana) synthetic API 120 MT/yr; Vapi (Gujarat) synthetic API 191 MT/yr [^ip-latest]. Total 3 plants across 3 states.

Promoter background: Themis group promoted by Patel family; Dr. Sachin Patel (MD & CEO) leads; promoter holding stable at 67.15% per FY25 AR [^ar-fy25] (though Screener header shows 48.36% possibly due to trust restructuring). Group context: Focused mid-cap pharma with heritage in critical care and anaesthesia, leveraging specialist doctor relationships.
