## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market Leadership / Moat:** IMFA is India's largest fully integrated ferrochrome producer with 275,000 TPA capacity at Therubali (Odisha), backed by a captive chrome ore mine and a 258 MW coal-based captive power plant — a vertical integration stack that no domestic peer (Balasore Alloys, Facor, Visa Steel) can replicate at scale [^ip-latest]. This integration delivers one of the lowest cash-cost positions globally, providing a structural moat through commodity downcycles [^vp-thread].

- **Structural Margin Story:** Captive power at ~Rs. 3.7/unit (coal cost ~Rs. 2.36/unit) plus captive chrome ore insulates IMFA from the two largest variable cost lines that crush peer margins during FeCr price troughs [^vp-thread]. Management has guided that the company does not sell surplus power to GRIDCO, retaining the cost arbitrage entirely for ferrochrome smelting [^vp-thread]. We model EBITDA margins holding in the 28–32% band through the cycle versus sub-15% for non-integrated peers.

- **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** (i) Furnace off-gas-based power generation reduces coal dependence and lowers Scope 1 emissions — a structural advantage as global stainless steel buyers (Outokumpu, Acerinox, Tsingshan) tighten Scope 3 supplier norms [^ip-latest]; (ii) the underground mining transition at the captive chromite mine extends reserve life by 7–8 years and supports a future capacity creep to ~330,000 TPA [^vp-thread]; (iii) optionality on the Facor asset (REC objected to IMFA's plant-only bid, but the asset remains a strategic fit) [^vp-thread].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** (a) Global FeCr supply rationalisation — Glencore's Rustenburg smelter restructuring and Samancor's 400,000 tonne chrome ore / 20% alloy cut announcement are tightening the seaborne market [^vp-thread]; (b) Chinese ferrochrome supply offline on environmental grounds, with Chinese FeCr prices already firming on supply concerns [^vp-thread]; (c) European benchmark FeCr price for Q3 showed only a 4-cent/lb cut versus market expectations of 15–20 cents — a positive surprise for Indian producers [^vp-thread]; (d) potential resolution of the Therubali labour agitation that disrupted two furnaces in mid-2018 — a recurring operational risk that has now been ring-fenced via district administration engagement [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** IMFA's earnings are a leveraged play on global stainless steel demand, with China accounting for the marginal price-setter. A Chinese property-led demand slowdown or a sharp INR appreciation (rupee strength compresses USD-denominated FeCr realisations) are the two largest downside vectors [^ar-fy25]. Additionally, the open-cast-to-underground mining transition will raise unit mining costs over FY26–FY28, creating a ~150–200 bps EBITDA margin headwind before underground volumes scale [^vp-thread].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** IMFA operates a fully vertically integrated ferrochrome value chain — captive chromite mining → ferrochrome smelting → captive coal-based power generation → sale of high-carbon ferrochrome to global stainless steel mills [^ip-latest]. The company is the only Indian producer that owns the entire cost stack from mine to metal.

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25):**
- **Ferro Alloys (FeCr):** ~88–90% of revenue — primary product is high-carbon ferrochrome (HC FeCr) sold to stainless steel producers in India, Europe, and East Asia [^ip-latest].
- **Power:** ~10–12% of revenue — surplus power from the 258 MW captive plant is sold to the Odisha grid (GRIDCO) and to internal consumption; the off-gas-based component is fully internal [^ar-fy25].

**Key OEM / End Customers per Division:**
- **FeCr Division:** Stainless steel mills globally — Indian customers include Jindal Stainless, Salem Steel; export customers include Chinese (Tsingshan), European (Outokumpu, Acerinox), and Korean (Posco) stainless producers [^ip-latest].
- **Power Division:** GRIDCO (Odisha state distribution) for surplus sale; internal consumption for smelting furnaces [^ar-fy25].

**Subsidiary Structure:** IMFA operates largely as a single-entity producer with limited subsidiary sprawl — the captive mine and power plant are housed within the parent. There are no material listed subsidiaries; the corporate structure is intentionally lean to avoid holding-company inefficiencies [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
- **Therubali Plant (Rayagada, Odisha):** 275,000 TPA FeCr capacity + 258 MW captive power — the flagship integrated complex [^ip-latest].
- **Captive Chrome Ore Mine (Sukinda Valley, Odisha):** Long-life chromite reserves; currently transitioning from open-cast to underground mining [^vp-thread].
- **Plant Count:** 1 integrated FeCr complex + 1 captive mine + 1 captive power plant — concentrated, low-overhead footprint [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** IMFA was historically promoted with close ties to the Odisha political establishment, which provided favourable mining lease allocations and grid connectivity [^vp-thread]. The current promoter group (74.54% holding) is described by long-term trackers as "conservative, not fly-by-night operators" with no aggressive expansion ambitions that could lead to a debt trap [^vp-thread]. The relationship with the ruling Odisha party has reportedly soured in recent years, introducing a modest political-risk premium into the stock [^vp-thread].

- **Global Ferrochrome TAM:** ~USD 15–18 Bn (2025E), driven by global stainless steel production of ~55–58 MTPA [^ip-latest].
- **India Ferrochrome TAM:** ~Rs. 8,000–10,000 Cr, with India being the world's second-largest FeCr producer after China [^ip-latest].
- **CAGR:** Global FeCr demand expected to grow at 4–5% CAGR over FY25–FY30, led by Indian stainless steel capacity additions (Jindal, Salem) and infrastructure-driven nickel-free stainless demand [^ip-latest].

**Policy Tailwinds:**
- **PLI Scheme for Specialty Steel:** Government PLI outlay of Rs. 6,322 Cr is incentivising domestic stainless steel capacity build-out, which directly expands the addressable FeCr offtake for Indian producers like IMFA [^ip-latest].

The stock currently trades at a discount to its historical band (FY22-25 average P/E ~17x) because ROCE compressed to ~14% in FY25 from 22% in FY22 on the FeCr downcycle. **Two triggers can drive multiple expansion from 14x → 16-18x:**
