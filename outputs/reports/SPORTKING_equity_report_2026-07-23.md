## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Established mid-tier spinning franchise with export-grade scale:** Sportking India is a Ludhiana-based cotton and blended yarn manufacturer with ~4.5 lakh spindle capacity and an installed capacity of ~95,000 MTPA, positioning it among the top-15 organised cotton yarn producers in North India[^ip-latest]. The company derives ~58% of revenue from exports (Vietnam, Bangladesh, Turkey, Egypt, China) and ~42% from domestic sales, providing natural currency and geographic diversification[^vp-thread].

- **Margin recovery story post-cotton price normalisation:** Gross margins have historically swung between 22% and 40% on cotton and PSF price volatility[^vp-thread]. With cotton prices stabilising in the Rs. 55,000–65,000/candy range and the company having invested in a 25 MW captive solar/wind hybrid power plant (reducing power cost from Rs. 7.5/unit to Rs. 5.0/unit), EBITDA margins are expected to structurally expand by 150–200 bps from FY26E onwards[^ip-latest].

- **Value-added yarn mix shift as the key diversification lever:** Management is actively shifting the product mix from commodity count yarn (30s–40s) to value-added specialised yarn (compact yarn, slub yarn, gassed mercerised, dyed yarn, blended with modal/tencel) — currently ~22% of revenue, targeted to reach 35% by FY28E[^cc-transcript]. This mix shift carries 300–500 bps higher gross margin realisation versus commodity yarn[^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1FY27 results due 1 Aug 2026 expected to show sequential margin expansion on the back of lower cotton costs and full-quarter benefit of captive power[^cc-transcript]; (ii) PLI scheme for textiles (Rs. 10,683 Cr outlay) — Sportking is eligible for MMF segment incentives and has filed for the same[^ip-latest]; (iii) Potential re-rating as institutional investors (currently 0% FII / 0% DII) discover the stock post the special-dividend noise clearing[^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk — commoditised end-market with no pricing power:** Yarn manufacturing is fundamentally a price-taker business with no single dominant player; the government deliberately preserves fragmentation given the sector's large employment footprint[^vp-thread]. Sustained cotton price spikes (as seen in FY22–FY23) can compress gross margins by 800–1,000 bps within a single quarter, and the company has limited hedging infrastructure beyond tactical inventory management[^ar-fy25].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** Sportking India is a single-vertical spinning company that manufactures 100% cotton yarn, poly-cotton blended yarn, and 100% polyester yarn (PSF-based) for sale to fabric weavers, knitters, and garment exporters[^ip-latest]. The company operates on a B2B model with no retail brand presence; ~85.3% of revenue comes from yarn sales, ~11.3% from sale of process waste (cotton waste, yarn waste), and ~3.4% from other operating income[^vp-thread].

**Revenue split by division (FY25A):**

| Division | % of Revenue | Key End-Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cotton Yarn (Counts 20s–80s) | ~62% | Knitting, weaving, hosiery |
| Blended Yarn (PC, PV) | ~23% | Denim, suiting, home textiles |
| Polyester Yarn (PSF) | ~12% | Technical textiles, industrial |
| Waste Sales | ~3% | Open market |

**Key OEM/end customers:** Domestic customers include leading Tirupur knitwear exporters, Ludhiana hosiery units, and Surat fabric manufacturers[^ar-fy25]. Export customers are predominantly garment-fabric manufacturers in Bangladesh (40% of exports), Vietnam (25%), Turkey (15%), Egypt (8%), and China/others (12%)[^ip-latest]. The company does not have any single customer contributing >5% of revenue, indicating low customer concentration risk[^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary structure:** Sportking India has one wholly-owned subsidiary — Sportking Overseas FZE (UAE-based trading arm) — which handles export logistics and receivables management for Middle East and African markets[^ar-fy25]. There are no joint ventures or strategic investments in other listed entities.
