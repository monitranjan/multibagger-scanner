### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat**: Bajaj Finance is the largest and most diversified non-bank retail financier in India, with an AUM of approximately Rs. 4.76 lakh Cr as of Q1FY27 — a scale that is roughly 2x the next-largest diversified retail NBFC (Cholamandalam).[^ip-latest] The franchise is anchored by a 230,000+ strong distribution network spanning 4,178 urban/rural locations and 23 product lines, with ~55.1 mn active customer franchise and ~80% cross-sell penetration within the Bajaj Finserv group ecosystem.[^ar-fy25] Switching costs for the company's auto-financing OEM partnerships (Bajaj Auto, KTM, Triumph, Kawasaki) and EMI-card franchise (~41.8 mn cards in force) constitute a meaningful structural moat.

- **Structural margin story**: NIM compression from 9.4% (FY22) to ~7.9% (FY25) appears to have stabilized at ~8.1% in Q1FY27, with management guiding to a 3.30–3.60% pre-tax RoA trajectory over the medium term.[^cc-transcript] Operating leverage from the digital-native platform (Bajaj FinServ App with 49 mn+ downloads) and the shift in mix toward higher-yielding Personal Loan, Lifestyle, and EMI Card products — which now contribute 23.4% of AUM vs. 18% in FY22 — should support margin defense even as the blended cost of funds rises with the new CRR requirements effective FY27.

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies**: Three structural growth wedges: (1) **Bajaj Finserv Direct**: cross-sell of General Insurance (BAGIC), Life Insurance (BALIC), and Asset Management (Bajaj Finserv MF) generating Rs. 1,400+ Cr of annual fees at ~85% incremental margin; (2) **Digital lending**: Insta EMI Card and EMI-on-Bajaj Pay wallet (BFL 2.0 initiative) targeting 1 bn+ middle-class consumers through Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) and UPI partnerships; (3) **Geographical expansion**: geographic diversification with Maharashtra (12.4%), UP/Bihar (12.2%), Tamil Nadu (8.6%), and Gujarat (8.1%) now contributing balanced AUM — reducing single-state concentration risk versus historical pattern.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months)**: (a) Q2FY27 results expected to show continued AUM growth of 25–28% YoY; (b) potential re-rating from RBI's revised risk weights on bank lending to NBFCs (which has historically improved BFL's competitive positioning vis-à-vis banks in personal loan segment); (c) potential inclusion in the Nifty50 index in the next review cycle (current weight in Nifty Next 50); (d) clarification from RBI on the 1/3rd/4th/5th cycle of risk weight rationalization on digital lending exposures; (e) monsoon-led demand revival for two-wheeler and consumer durables financing.

- **Biggest structural risk**: Asset quality deterioration in unsecured personal loans and SME lending remains the single largest risk. While gross NPA at 0.82% (Q1FY27) and provisioning coverage at 1.4% of AUM remain best-in-class, the personal loan and lifestyle loan book — now Rs. 1.11 lakh Cr (~23% of AUM) — has seen stage-2/3 stress ratios tick up by 40 bps YoY.[^cc-transcript] A continued slowdown in urban consumption or a sharp rise in the rate cycle could pressure credit costs from the current ~190 bps run-rate toward the 230–250 bps range that the company experienced in FY20–21. Valuation risk: trading at 6.22x P/B versus historical 10-year average of 5.1x — any miss on growth or asset quality could trigger a 15–20% de-rating.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**TAM and growth drivers**: The Indian retail credit market was estimated at Rs. 90 lakh Cr (USD ~$1.08 trillion) in FY25, with retail credit penetration at 36% of GDP — well below China's 75% and the US 190%. Indian retail credit is projected to grow at 13–15% CAGR to reach Rs. 200 lakh Cr (USD ~$2.4 trillion) by FY30E, driven by (1) increasing formalization of credit from informal sources, (2) rise of the middle class (estimated 600 mn by 2030), (3) digital public infrastructure (UPI, Aadhaar, Account Aggregator framework), and (4) deepening of insurance and asset management penetration.[^vp-thread]

**Policy tailwinds**:
- **RBI Risk Weight Rationalization (2025–26)**: Differential risk weights on consumer credit (125–150% for unsecured personal loans) are being applied consistently across banks and NBFCs, removing a regulatory arbitrage that banks historically enjoyed.
- **CRR Increase**: RBI raised CRR by 50 bps to 5% in Aug 2025 (effective Sept 6, 2025) — a mild headwind for NBFC funding costs, partially offset by recent MCLR reductions across banks.
- **Securitisation Framework (2024)**: The revised framework allows for greater pass-through of underlying asset risk, which could expand BFL's securitisation channel by Rs. 25,000–30,000 Cr annually.
- **Digital Lending Norms (RBI 2025)**: The final master direction on digital lending has brought NBFC fintech partnerships under tighter regulatory oversight — a positive for established, regulated players like BFL versus unregulated fintechs.
- **FAME-III**: Likely to provide downstream demand support for electric two-wheeler financing, a growing slice of the Consumer B2B book.
- **PM Awas Yojana (Urban 2.0)**: Increased outlay for affordable housing should support the mortgage book growth (currently 22.6% of AUM).
