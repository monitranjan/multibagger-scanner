### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Smartworks is the only listed coworking platform in India to cross 10 million square feet (Msf) of operational space, with total secured super built-up area (SBA – this is the total leasable area including lobbies, corridors and amenities, think of it as the full carpet plus common zones) of 16.9 Msf across 70 centres in 15 cities (India + Singapore) and 381,000 seats [^ip-latest]. Its contracted rental revenue stands at ~INR 54,000 Mn (about Rs 5,400 Cr) from executed agreements, covering ~87% of FY27 revenue before the year is even sold, creating an annuity-like visibility [^ip-latest]. The moat is reinforced by a deliberate up-market shift: ~41% of rental revenue now comes from the 1,000+ seat cohort (large enterprise clients like Fortune 500, Forbes 2000 MNCs) up from ~37% in FY26, and ~35% from multi-city clients up from ~31% [^ip-latest]. Average tenure for these large clients is ~48 months, meaning revenue is sticky and low-volatility [^ip-latest]. This scale and enterprise lock-in is a strong barrier because competitors cannot easily replicate a nationwide managed-campus network with single-contract convenience.

- **Structural margin story:** The company is in the early stages of a powerful operating-leverage curve. Normalised EBITDA (a non-GAAP measure we use to add back lease-liability repayments, because the business leases buildings and then fits them out – this shows the underlying cash earnings from operations) reached INR 1,069 Mn in Q1 FY27, up 74% YoY, with margin at 19.6% – an expansion of ~340 basis points (bps – one bps is 0.01%, so 340 bps = 3.4 percentage points) YoY [^ip-latest]. Management attributes this to three drivers: (a) centre maturity (newly opened centres take 12-14 months to hit stable occupancy), (b) cost excellence especially in energy procurement, and (c) corporate overhead leverage [^cc-transcript]. Realisation per sq ft for mature capacity rose to INR 181/sq ft in Q1 FY27 from ~INR 170 in FY26, demonstrating pricing power as scale deepens [^cc-transcript]. Annualised normalised ROCE (Return on Capital Employed – a ratio telling us how efficiently the company generates profit from all capital deployed, including leases) hit 21.5%, up ~870 bps YoY [^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond plain seat leasing, Smartworks is bundling services for Global Capability Centres (GCCs – these are offshore hubs set up by multinational corporations to handle tech, finance, HR functions) via its “SmartVantage” platform, which wraps facilities management, IT, and concierge into one contract [^cc-transcript]. Non-lease rental revenue (value-added services or VAS like cafeteria, events, software) tripled YoY to INR 68 Cr, though still in pilot and not yet in guidance [^cc-transcript]. The Singapore acquisition (Workstudio) extends the footprint internationally, funded entirely by local cash flows so as not to stress the Indian balance sheet [^cc-transcript]. GCC contribution to total business rose to ~21% from 15% in FY26, tapping a structural tailwind of MNCs decentralising to India [^ip-latest].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The company has guided 28-30% revenue growth and 19-20% normalised EBITDA margins for FY27, with operational footprint >13 Msf by March 2027 [^cc-transcript]. It plans to add 2.5-3 Msf annually, with 3.5 Msf already signed and 3 Msf coming online in the next nine months [^cc-transcript]. A recent corporate announcement disclosed +INR 235 Cr incremental contracted rental revenue (locked-in backlog) as of 24 Aug 2026, which lifted stock ~2% [^vp-thread]. Capex guidance of INR 550-600 Cr for FY27 (including fit-outs at ~INR 1,350/sq ft) is fully funded by operating cash flows and minimal debt [^cc-transcript]. The first anniversary of listing and consistent fifth consecutive quarter of sequential growth acts as a narrative catalyst for index inclusion and broader institutional sponsorship [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The bear case revolves around supply glut and demand fragility. Community analysts flag that peers (Awfis, EFC) are adding massive capacity, which could pressure margins if pricing power weakens [^vp-thread]. Additionally, there is a split view on AI: some argue it pushes GCCs to flexible per-seat models (demand positive), while others warn it may slow white-collar desk job growth (demand risk) [^vp-thread]. For Smartworks, a reversal of the current operating pattern (volume-led growth, rising occupancy, improving margins) would undermine the compounding thesis; the model still carries lease liabilities, and if occupancy in ramping centres stays below 80% for prolonged periods, normalised ROCE could compress [^cc-transcript][^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Smartworks Coworking Spaces Ltd operates a managed-office model that can be explained simply: instead of a company renting and furnishing its own office, Smartworks leases an entire building from a landlord, redesigns it into a tech-enabled campus with desks, meeting rooms, cafeterias, and security, and then sublets it to enterprises on a single contract with one invoice and one service-level agreement (SLA) [^cc-transcript]. This removes the headache of fit-out and facilities management for clients. The core revenue division is “Enterprise Coworking & Managed Campuses” which contributed ~92% of rental revenue from enterprise clients (Fortune 500, Forbes 2000, MNCs and Indian conglomerates) [^ip-latest]. A second emerging division is “SmartVantage for GCCs” that bundles beyond-seat services [^cc-transcript]. A third is “VAS / Non-lease rental” (cafeteria, events, software platforms like Nexus, WorkBlock, WorkCtrl) which tripled to INR 68 Cr YoY but remains small [^cc-transcript].

Revenue split by division (approx based on Q1 FY27 data): Rental from enterprise clients ~85% of total revenue (since total revenue INR 5,462 Mn, VAS ~INR 68 Cr implies rental ~INR 5,394 Mn) [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]; VAS ~1.2%; the remainder is other income. Key OEM/end customers are not disclosed by name but categorised as Fortune 500, Forbes 2000, MNCs and Indian conglomerates, with ~41% revenue from the 1,000+ seat cohort and ~35% from multi-city clients [^ip-latest]. Subsidiary structure includes the Indian operating entity and a Singapore subsidiary acquired via Workstudio, which is self-funded from local cash flows [^cc-transcript]. Manufacturing footprint is not applicable (it is a real-estate services firm), but its “footprint” is 70 centres, 15 cities (India + Singapore), 381k seats, with 10.4 Msf operational and 16.9 Msf total secured SBA [^ip-latest]. Promoter background: The company was founded as Smartworks Coworking Spaces Private Limited (CIN L74900DL2015PLC310656) and converted to PLC; Managing Director Neetish Sarda (DIN 07262894) and Whole-time Director Harsh Binani (DIN 07717396) lead the group, with Chairman Atul Gautam (non-executive) up for re-appointment at the 11th AGM on 22 Sep 2026 [^ar-fy25]. The group context is a venture-backed flexible workspace aggregator that listed on NSE/BSE in 2025 and is now the scale leader in Indian managed offices [^ip-latest].
