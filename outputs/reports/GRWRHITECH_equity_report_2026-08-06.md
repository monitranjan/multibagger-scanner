## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Specialty Films Leadership with Global Moat:** Garware Hi Tech Films (GHTF) is India's largest exporter of polyester (BOPET) films and the only domestic manufacturer of sun-control window films, with patented dyed-polyester technology in India and the US — a structural moat that has been built over 3+ decades[^ip-latest]. The Global Window Films brand has scaled from Rs. 30 Cr in FY12 to Rs. 200 Cr in FY20, demonstrating pricing power in overseas markets[^vp-thread].

- **Structural Margin Expansion via Value-Added Mix:** Gross margins have expanded from 44.8% in FY16 to 62.7% on a TTM basis, driven by a deliberate shift in product mix toward Value-Added Products (VAP), which now contribute ~74% of revenue versus 49% in FY15[^vp-thread]. This is a structural, mix-driven margin story — not a cyclical blip — and we expect EBITDA margins to sustain at 22–24% levels through FY28E[^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into Paint Protection Films (PPF) — A High-Growth Adjacency:** GHTF has entered the global Paint Protection Film (PPF) market, which is currently fragmented and ripe for consolidation, with low penetration in new-car sales globally[^vp-thread]. PPF commands premium realisations and is being targeted at the same OEM and aftermarket channels that the company already services through its sun-control films business — leveraging existing customer relationships and manufacturing know-how[^ip-latest].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1FY27 results showing continued VAP mix improvement and PPF ramp-up[^cc-transcript]; (ii) commissioning of capacity expansion at the Aurangabad and Pune facilities to support PPF and specialty film volumes[^ip-latest]; (iii) potential re-rating as institutional investors recognise the specialty-film pivot away from commodity BOPET[^vp-thread]; (iv) record date for dividend fixed at 16 Sep 2026, signalling continued strong shareholder returns[^ip-latest].

- **Biggest Structural Risk — Commodity BOPET Cyclicality & Customer Concentration:** Despite the VAP pivot, ~26% of revenue still comes from commodity/industrial films exposed to global BOPET pricing cycles and Chinese supply additions[^ar-fy25]. Additionally, the Supreme Court's 2017 ban on polyester film for gutka/pan masala packaging eliminated ~Rs. 150 Cr of domestic turnover, and the automotive sun-control film regulation impacted another ~Rs. 130 Cr — illustrating regulatory and end-market concentration risk[^vp-thread]. A sharp BOPET price downturn or further regulatory action on automotive films remains the principal downside risk.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** GHTF operates a fully-integrated specialty films platform — backward-integrated into polyester chip manufacturing and forward-integrated into coated, laminated, and converted films for industrial and consumer end-uses[^ip-latest]. The company manufactures BOPET films ranging from 10 microns to 350 microns in thickness, sun-control window films, paint protection films (PPF), thermal films, shrink-sleeve labels, and BOPP films[^ar-fy25].

**Revenue Split by Division:**

| Industrial / Commodity Films | ~26% | Standard BOPET, BOPP, packaging-grade films[^ar-fy25] |

**Key OEM / End Customers per Division:**

**Subsidiary Structure:** GHTF operates primarily as a standalone entity with international marketing subsidiaries/distribution arms in the US, UK, Russia, Malaysia, Brazil, UAE, and Australia to support the Global Window Films brand[^vp-thread]. No material listed subsidiaries; the structure is lean and focused.
