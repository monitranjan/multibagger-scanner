## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Integrated weaver moat displacing unorganised powerlooms:** Borana Weaves operates water-jet looms that produce 7–8x more fabric per unit than conventional powerlooms, capturing share from fragmented unorganised players through superior delivery discipline, on-time supply, and scale capacity[^vp-thread]. The company has steadily increased grey fabric share to ~90% of revenue from a lower base, demonstrating successful value-chain integration from POY → PTY Yarn → Greige Fabric[^vp-thread].

- **Structural margin story with industry-leading returns:** ROCE of 23.24% and ROE of 22.95% place Borana in the top quartile of Indian textile manufacturers[^ip-latest]. Gross margins have remained resilient despite volatile POY prices (linked to crude oil), reflecting pricing power from integrated operations and scale-driven cost absorption[^ar-fy25]. The company has consistently delivered double-digit operating margins in a fragmented industry where most peers operate at single-digit margins[^vp-thread].

- **Diversification into high-growth technical textiles adjacencies:** Grey fabric output is increasingly being supplied into technical textiles — automotive airbags, seat covers, industrial canvas, agro-textiles, medical bandages, and protective clothing — providing a structural growth runway beyond conventional apparel/home textile cycles[^vp-thread]. The shift from PTY Yarn (commodity) to grey fabric (value-added) is the core margin expansion lever[^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Commissioning of 192 water-jet looms + 3 texturizing machines at Unit 4 by December 2026, approved at the 11 Aug 2026 board meeting; (ii) Units 6 & 7 expected operational by Oct/Nov 2026; (iii) Likely announcement of an additional 600–700 loom capex by March 2027, indicating phased expansion every quarter[^vp-thread]. Each phase adds ~15–20% to weaving capacity, supporting 30–35% revenue CAGR through FY27E[^ip-latest].

- **Biggest structural risk — promoter group governance overhang:** SEBI conducted a search and seizure operation at R&B Denims, a JV between the RawatKhedia/Dalmia family and the Borana family, raising corporate governance concerns that could spill over to Borana Weaves[^vp-thread]. Amit Dalmia (CMD of R&B Denims) is part of the broader promoter network, and the management's vague public explanation has created uncertainty around potential promoter-level implications[^vp-thread]. This is the single largest discount factor in our valuation framework.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:**
Borana Weaves Ltd is an integrated textile manufacturer producing polyester textured yarn (PTY) and grey (greige) fabric — unprocessed and unbleached woven fabric used as input for downstream dyeing, printing, and finishing operations[^ar-fy25]. The company sources POY (Partially Oriented Yarn) from Reliance Industries, Garden Silk Mills, Bhilosa, and select imported suppliers, then converts it in-house through texturizing and warping into PTY yarn, which feeds water-jet looms to produce greige fabric[^vp-thread].

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25A):**
- Grey Fabric (Greige): ~90% of revenue — value-added, higher-margin segment[^vp-thread]
- PTY Yarn: ~10% of revenue — commodity-grade, declining share as integration deepens[^vp-thread]

**Key OEM/End Customers:**
- Domestic apparel manufacturers (lining fabric, garments)[^vp-thread]
- Home textile processors (coating, tents, furnishings)[^vp-thread]
- Technical textile converters (automotive airbags/seat covers, industrial canvas, agro-textiles, medical bandages, protective clothing)[^vp-thread]
- Customer base is fragmented across hundreds of small/mid processors — no single customer concentration risk[^ar-fy25]

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
- Multiple manufacturing units in Gujarat (likely Silvassa and surrounding regions based on industry clustering)[^vp-thread]
- Unit 4: 192 water-jet looms + 3 texturizing machines under installation, commercial production targeted Dec 2026[^cc-transcript]
- Units 6 & 7: Expected operational by Oct/Nov 2026[^vp-thread]
- Additional 600–700 loom expansion likely announced by March 2027[^vp-thread]

**Subsidiary Structure:**
Borana Weaves operates as a standalone entity with no material subsidiaries disclosed in the FY25-26 annual report[^ar-fy25]. The promoter group has affiliated entities including R&B Denims (JV with RawatKhedia/Dalmia family), but these are not consolidated subsidiaries[^vp-thread].

**Promoter Background & Group Context:**
The Borana family controls 74.71% of equity, reflecting a closely-held promoter-driven structure typical of SME-origin textile manufacturers[^ar-fy25]. The group has expanded from a single-unit powerloom operation to a multi-unit integrated weaver over the past decade, with the IPO providing growth capital for the current expansion cycle[^vp-thread].
