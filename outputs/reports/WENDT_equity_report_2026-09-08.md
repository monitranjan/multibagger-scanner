### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

* **Niche Market Leadership with High Switching Costs:** Wendt India enjoys a quasi-monopoly in specific high-precision grinding solutions — particularly Thompson roll grinding machines for the steel industry where it holds ~68% market share — because its machines and super abrasive wheels (made of diamond or cubic boron nitride/CBN, which are synthetic super-hard materials used to grind hardened metals) are deeply embedded in customer production lines; once a steel plant or auto component maker qualifies a Wendt wheel or machine, switching to a competitor requires re-validation that can take 6–12 months, creating a powerful moat [^ip-latest][^vp-thread]. The recent exit of 3M (via Wendt GmbH stake sale) and consolidation under the Murugappa Group (CUMI) simplifies decision-making and aligns long-term strategy [^ar-fy25].

* **Structural Margin Recovery Post-3M Exit:** The FY26 margin collapse (OPM falling from ~22% to ~13% per community analysis) was driven by the loss of high-margin 3M volumes and one-time costs like the Rs 35 Cr brand buyback amortization and Rs 1.77 Cr tech-transfer fees [^vp-thread][^cc-transcript]. However, management asserts that at the gross margin level operations are stable, and the new Germany subsidiary (Wendt GmbH) plus ownership of the global "Wendt" trademark unlock export pricing power in Europe/US where 3M previously restricted them [^cc-transcript][^ip-latest]. We model a gradual OPM recovery to 18–20% by FY28E as the brand amortization normalizes and high-margin new products (semiconductor, solar glass) scale.

* **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** The company is deliberately moving beyond traditional auto/steel into semiconductor (Carbide Plate and LPDMS machines for chip manufacturing), solar glass (specialized grinding wheels for float glass lines), medical (implant grinding), aerospace/defence, and 3D printing (metal additive manufacturing post-processing) [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. The new Pune insert-grinding facility targets the fuel-injector and carbide-insert boom, while the Germany entity provides a direct channel to European semiconductor and medical OEMs [^ip-latest]. These adjacencies address a TAM growing at 18–30% CAGR vs. 8–10% for core auto [^ip-latest].

* **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** (1) Commissioning of the solar glass wheel capacity and first revenues from semiconductor-qualified products (H2 FY27) [^ip-latest]; (2) Wendt GmbH (Germany) reaching operational breakeven and booking first European peripheral grinding machine orders [^ar-fy25]; (3) Visibility on margin inflection as Q2/Q3 FY27 annualizes the brand amortization and benefits from dealer-channel growth (+32% YoY in FY25) [^cc-transcript]; (4) Potential capital return trigger (bonus/split) discussed at AGM, which could improve liquidity and retail participation [^cc-transcript].

* **Biggest Structural Risk — Customer Concentration & Technology Disruption:** The 3M exit exposed reliance on a single large account; replacing its volume/margin profile with fragmented new customers is uncertain [^vp-thread]. Simultaneously, the rise of laser machining, additive manufacturing (3D printing), and advanced coatings could reduce demand for traditional grinding over a 5–10 year horizon, though Wendt is hedging by entering 3D printing post-processing itself [^ip-latest][^vp-thread]. Raw material volatility (diamond/CBN grit prices) and environmental regulations on synthetic grit production add cost-push risks [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Wendt (India) Limited operates a **B2B manufacturing model** designing, producing, and servicing three core product lines: **Super Abrasives** (diamond/CBN grinding wheels, rotary dressers, fine-grinding wheels), **Machines & Accessories** (CNC cylindrical/creep-feed/rotary surface grinders, honing machines, profiling & dressing machines), and **Precision Components** (vanes, distance pieces, transfer blades, shoe cam rollers, fuel-injector components) [^ip-latest]. Think of super abrasives as "industrial diamond sandpaper" that shapes the hardest steel parts in car engines, bearings, and cutting tools; the machines are the "printers" that use these wheels; and precision components are the "finished parts" Wendt grinds for customers who outsource the grinding.

**Revenue Split (FY24 per Investor Presentation):** Super Abrasives 59% (Rs 133 Cr), Machines & Accessories 21% (Rs 47 Cr), Precision Products 12% (Rs 27 Cr), Others 8% [^ip-latest]. **Key End-Markets & Customers:** Automotive ~34% of sales (major OEMs/Tier-1s like Bosch, Cummins, bearing majors), Bearings 8–10%, Steel (roll grinding), Cutting Tools, Engineering, and emerging Electronics/Semiconductor [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. Sales channel: 118 direct OEM accounts (64% of revenue) and 78 dealers (16%) covering the long tail [^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary Structure:** Wholly-owned **Wendt GmbH (Germany)**, incorporated July 2025, acts as the European sales, service, and application-engineering hub for peripheral grinding machines and super abrasives, giving Wendt direct access to EU/US markets previously managed by 3M [^ar-fy25][^ip-latest]. No other material subsidiaries.

**Manufacturing Footprint:** Primary integrated campus at **Hosur, Tamil Nadu** (16.38 acres, 490+ employees) housing super abrasive synthesis, wheel manufacturing, machine assembly, and R&D [^ip-latest]. **Thailand** re-profiling unit (re-tipping used wheels). **Pune** insert-grinding facility (new, focused on fuel-injector/carbide inserts) [^ip-latest]. Capacity utilization varies by bond type (Resin/Metal/Vitrified/Electroplated) with room to add assembly bays via shop-floor relay-out [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background:** **Carborundum Universal (CUMI)**, flagship of the 124-year-old **Murugappa Group**, holds 37.5% (sole promoter post-3M exit). The Murugappa Group is renowned for conservative capital allocation, strong governance, and long-term industrial nurturing (e.g., CUMI, Tube Investments, Cholamandalam) [^ar-fy25]. 3M (via Wendt GmbH) exited completely in May 2025 via OFS, ending a 40-year JV [^ar-fy25]. Public holding now ~62.5%.
