## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Hidden asset value provides downside floor and rerating optionality.** Shalimar holds ~67 acres of freehold land across its three manufacturing locations (Howrah, Nashik, Sikandrabad) plus a high-value Gurugram parcel, with ~65% of total land currently unutilized and available for monetization or expansion [^vp-thread]. The 5 Aug 2026 announcement of a definitive sale agreement for the Gurugram property by wholly-owned subsidiary Shalimar Adhunik Nirman Ltd is a concrete first step in unlocking this latent value [^ar-fy25]. We estimate the Gurugram parcel alone is worth Rs. 150-200 Cr based on prevailing industrial/commercial land rates in Sector 32, Gurugram.

- **Promoter overhang is exiting, not entering.** The 03 Aug 2026 receipt of NOC from stock exchanges for re-classification of the promoter/promoter group from "Promoter" to "Public" category is a structurally positive event [^ar-fy25]. Once effective, free-float rises, governance optics improve, and the equity becomes eligible for institutional mandates that currently exclude promoter-heavy, loss-making names. Combined with the simultaneous asset sale, the original promoter group is clearly in a "harvest and exit" mode rather than fresh value destruction.

- **Capital allocation is finally shifting from bleeding operations to balance sheet cleanup.** Working capital has historically been a drag with extended receivable cycles in industrial paints and overcapacity at under-utilized plants; the asset monetization proceeds will materially de-risk the balance sheet and could fund either a) capex revival in the decorative segment, b) a strategic acquisition to gain distribution scale, or c) a buyback/special dividend [^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months):** (i) Closure and receipt of sale consideration for the Gurugram property — likely to be the most material single re-rating event; (ii) Further non-core asset divestments (any spare Nashik/Howrah/Sikandrabad land parcels); (iii) Promoter reclassification completion expanding institutional eligibility; (iv) Any inbound strategic interest from larger paint/coatings players looking to acquire a 5% market share foothold without greenfield capex of Rs. 800-1,000 Cr [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The decorative paints business has lost meaningful market share over the last decade and the brand competes against Asian Paints, Berger, Kansai Nerolac and Indigo Paints, which collectively command >80% of the organized market and outspend Shalimar on advertising/distribution by a factor of 8-10x [^ip-latest]. Without a clear strategic tie-up or operational overhaul, core EBITDA could remain negative, and even successful asset monetization may not be redeployed efficiently — a "value trap with a floor" rather than a compounding business.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Shalimar Paints is one of India's oldest branded paints companies (brand legacy dating to 1902), operating across two segments — Decorative Paints (retail-facing, distribution-led) and Industrial/Protective Coatings (B2B, project-based) [^ip-latest]. Manufacturing is anchored at three plants — Howrah (East), Nashik (West) and Sikandrabad (North) — with cumulative installed capacity significantly exceeding current utilization, providing optionality for both expansion and asset disposal.

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25 indicative):**
- Decorative Paints: ~45-50% of revenue (margin-accretive but market share has compressed)
- Industrial/Protective Coatings: ~45-50% of revenue (working-capital heavy, project cyclicality)
- Others/Trading: ~5% of revenue

**Key OEM/End Customers per Division:**
- Decorative: Distributor/dealer network of ~5,000 outlets, modern retail tie-ups (selective)
- Industrial: EPC contractors, infrastructure projects, oil & gas majors, automotive OEMs, marine clients, and original equipment in powder coatings [^cc-transcript]

**Subsidiary Structure:**
- Shalimar Adhunik Nirman Ltd (SANL) — wholly-owned subsidiary, holds the Gurugram real estate assets and is the entity executing the property sale announced on 05 Aug 2026 [^ar-fy25]
- Other step-down entities in paints distribution and coatings

**Manufacturing Footprint (states, plant count):**
- West Bengal (Howrah) — 1 plant, largest land bank (~145,000 sq.mtr, 72% spare)
- Maharashtra (Nashik) — 1 plant (~49,800 sq.mtr, 54% spare)
- Uttar Pradesh (Sikandrabad) — 1 plant (~41,242 sq.mtr, 61% spare)
- Tamil Nadu (Chinnapuliyur/Gummidipoondi) — South India greenfield, commissioned FY14 [^vp-thread]

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** The original promoter group traces its lineage to the founding of the Shalimar brand in 1902, with the company transitioning through multiple ownership eras. The current promoter cohort has been in a managed wind-down mode — reducing operational involvement, monetizing real estate, and now formally exiting the promoter category through the SEBI reclassification process filed under Reg 31A [^ar-fy25].
