### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership & Structural Moat in Phosphatic Fertilisers.** Krishana Phoschem is the **2nd largest player in India's Single Super Phosphate (SSP) market** with a 9% national share, and ranks as the **3rd largest private-sector producer of NPK/DAP** (complex fertilisers containing Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium) in the country [^ip-latest]. The company's competitive moat is reinforced by **backward integration into captive intermediates** — Beneficiated Rock Phosphate (BRP), Sulphuric Acid (SA), and Phosphoric Acid (PA) — which insulates it from raw material price volatility and secures supply chain control [^ip-latest]. Krishana is also **India's first private-sector player in low-grade rock phosphate beneficiation (BRP technology)**, a technical capability that allows it to process inferior phosphate ore that competitors typically reject [^ip-latest]. This combination of scale, vertical integration, and proprietary processing technology creates a defensible position in a commoditised industry.

**2. Structural Margin Expansion Story.** The company has demonstrated consistent margin improvement driven by a **favourable product mix shift toward higher-margin complex NPK grades** (12-32-16, 16-20-0-13, 15-15-15, 8-21-21, 9-24-24) [^cc-transcript]. EBITDA margins expanded from 14% in FY25 to **16.7% in Q1 FY27** (+270 bps YoY), while PAT margins improved from 7.7% to 8.8% (+113 bps YoY) [^ip-latest]. The structural drivers are: (a) **backward integration** reducing dependence on costly imported intermediates, (b) **operating leverage** as new NPK/DAP capacity (expanded from 330K to 495K MTPA) ramps up utilisation, and (c) **premium pricing on new complex grades** that command 8-12% higher realisations than traditional DAP [^cc-transcript]. Management has guided confidence in sustaining or improving these margins, citing the durability of the new product mix [^cc-transcript].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies.** Krishana has secured a **10-year Green Ammonia Purchase Agreement (GAPA) with SECI** for 70,000 MTPA under the National Green Hydrogen Mission, positioning it as **India's largest future consumer of green ammonia** [^ip-latest]. This delivers a **~108% structural cost advantage** — secured at ~$0.6/kg versus ~$1.1/kg global benchmark — and a **>30% competitive edge** over grey ammonia prices [^ip-latest]. Additionally, the company is targeting **import substitution**: ~42% of India's NPK+DAP demand remains import-dependent (up from 29% historically), and Krishana's expanded capacity directly addresses this gap [^ip-latest]. The **NPK consumption 3-year CAGR of 12%** (versus 3% for SSP) signals a structural demand shift toward complex fertilisers, aligning perfectly with Krishana's capacity expansion [^ip-latest].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months).** Three concrete catalysts could drive re-rating: (a) **NPK/DAP capacity utilisation ramp-up** from 49% in Q1 FY27 toward the 80%+ target, potentially adding ~₹1,000 Cr in annual revenue at full capacity [^ip-latest]; (b) **DAP subsidy increase to ₹32,786/MT for Kharif 2026-27** and total fertiliser subsidy outlay exceeding ₹2.4 lakh crore for FY27, improving working capital and demand visibility [^ip-latest]; (c) **monsoon recovery** — the all-India rainfall deficit narrowed from ~40% to ~24% by early July 2026, triggering a "massive acceleration" in Kharif sowing that should boost Q2 FY27 volumes [^cc-transcript]. Management has guided for **25%+ revenue growth** in FY27, with potential to reach 30-35% [^cc-transcript].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk.** The primary risk is **raw material price volatility and subsidy delays**. Sulphur and phosphate rock prices are globally determined and exposed to geopolitical disruptions (e.g., West Asia conflict impacting shipping lanes like the Strait of Hormuz) [^cc-transcript]. Subsidy receivables of **₹189.11 crore were pending as of March 2025**, creating working capital strain and negative free cash flow in some periods [^ar-fy25]. Additionally, the **government's price-cap formula for non-urea fertilisers (8-12% profit margin cap)** remains a regulatory overhang that could compress returns if raw material costs spike without corresponding subsidy revisions [^vp-thread]. The company's high financial leverage (D/E of 0.95 in FY25, though improving) amplifies these risks [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model.** Krishana Phoschem Limited is a **vertically integrated phosphatic fertiliser manufacturer** that produces SSP (Single Super Phosphate), NPK/DAP (complex fertilisers), and captive intermediates (BRP, Sulphuric Acid, Phosphoric Acid) [^ip-latest]. The company sells under two flagship brands: **"Annadata"** for SSP and **"Bharat"** for NPK/DAP [^ip-latest]. Its business model is built on **backward integration** — it crushes low-grade rock phosphate into BRP, uses BRP to produce Sulphuric Acid and Phosphoric Acid, and then converts these intermediates into finished fertilisers [^ip-latest]. This integration captures margin at multiple stages of the value chain and reduces dependence on costly imports.

**Revenue Split by Division.** Fertilisers contribute **96.35% of revenue**, while Chemicals (primarily Sulphuric Acid and Phosphoric Acid sold to third parties) account for **3.65%** [^ar-fy25]. Within fertilisers, **NPK/DAP is the dominant revenue driver at 71.58% of turnover**, while SSP contributes 12.98% [^ar-fy25]. The remaining ~15% comes from trading activities and other fertiliser variants. The **NPK/DAP segment is higher-value per ton** and has been the primary beneficiary of capacity expansion (330K → 495K MTPA), while SSP is a **volume-driven, lower-margin segment** that provides market reach and farmer loyalty [^ip-latest].

**Key OEM/End Customers.** Krishana's end customers are **farmers and agricultural retailers** across 11 states in India, not OEMs in the traditional industrial sense [^ip-latest]. The distribution network comprises **200+ marketing professionals, 40,000+ retailers, and 3,000+ wholesalers/dealers** [^ip-latest]. The company has strong market positions in **Chhattisgarh (16% SSP share) and Madhya Pradesh (6% SSP share)**, with growing presence in other states [^ip-latest]. Key institutional customers include government procurement agencies and cooperative fertiliser distributors, though the company does not disclose customer concentration metrics.

**Subsidiary Structure.** Krishana Phoschem is part of the **Ostwal Group**, a Rajasthan-based business house with diversified interests in fertilisers, chemicals, and agro-products [^ar-fy25]. The promoter holding company (Ostwal Phoschem India Limited / OPIL) increased its stake from 66.47% to 67.10% during FY25, and currently holds **72.93% as of June 2026** [^ar-fy25]. Related-party transactions with group companies (OPIL, Madhya Bharat Agro Products Ltd / MBAPL, and Shree Ganpati Fertilizers Ltd / SGFL) are material — proposed limits of **₹1,400 crore with OPIL and MBAPL (103% of turnover)** and ₹500 crore with SGFL have been flagged for governance monitoring [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint.** Krishana operates **multiple manufacturing facilities** primarily in **Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh**, with additional capacity in other states [^ip-latest]. The company has demonstrated a **turnaround track record**, transforming 4 loss-making units into fully profitable operations [^ip-latest]. Current installed capacity stands at: **SSP — 120K MTPA** (stable since 2013), **NPK/DAP — 495K MTPA** (expanded from 330K in March 2026), **BRP — 200K MTPA** (stable since 2007), **Sulphuric Acid — 363K MTPA** (expanded from 264K in March 2026), and **Phosphoric Acid — 99K MTPA** (stable since 2018) [^ip-latest]. The company has also received the **"Best Production Performance Award" in the SSP category from the Fertiliser Association of India (FAI)** [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background.** The company is led by **Managing Director Praveen Ostwal**, who has articulated a clear strategic vision focused on backward integration, capacity expansion, product diversification, disciplined capital allocation, and deep farmer connect [^ip-latest]. The Ostwal Group has a multi-decade presence in the fertiliser sector, and the promoter family has consistently increased its shareholding over time — from 66.22% in September 2023 to 72.93% in June 2026 — signalling confidence in the business [^ar-fy25]. No promoter pledging has been reported [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM).** India's phosphatic fertiliser market is substantial, with **NPK consumption growing at a 3-year CAGR of 12%** — from 4,353K MT (FY23) to 6,971K MT (FY26) [^ip-latest]. The **SSP consumption 3-year CAGR is 3%**, indicating a structural shift toward complex fertilisers [^ip-latest]. The total Indian fertiliser subsidy outlay for FY27 is budgeted at **over ₹2.4 lakh crore**, with DAP subsidy specifically increased to **₹32,786/MT for Kharif 2026-27** [^ip-latest]. In USD terms, assuming ₹83/USD, the subsidy pool alone exceeds **$29 billion**, representing the government's commitment to supporting farm inputs. The **NPK ratio normalisation target** — shifting from the current imbalanced 7.7:3.1:1 toward the ideal 4:2:1 — represents a multi-year structural demand driver for Phosphorus and Potassium nutrients [^ip-latest].

**Policy Tailwinds.** The fertiliser sector benefits from robust government support: (a) **Nutrient-Based Subsidy (NBS)** scheme provides per-tonne subsidies for P&K fertilisers, ensuring affordability for farmers; (b) **DAP subsidy increase to ₹32,786/MT** for Kharif 2026-27 improves working capital visibility; (c) **PM-KISAN disbursement of ₹18,640 crore (March 2026)** supports farm liquidity and input purchase capacity [^ip-latest]; (d) **National Green Hydrogen Mission** enables Krishana's green ammonia procurement at preferential rates; (e) **"Make in India" and import substitution** focus benefits domestic producers like Krishana as ~42% of NPK+DAP demand remains import-dependent [^ip-latest].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure.** Krishana Phoschem is part of the **Ostwal Group**, a Rajasthan-based business house with multi-decade presence in fertilisers and chemicals [^ar-fy25]. The company is led by **Managing Director Praveen Ostwal**, who has articulated a clear five-pillar strategy: backward integration, capacity expansion, product diversification, disciplined capital allocation, and deep farmer connect [^ip-latest]. The promoter family has demonstrated **long-term commitment through consistent stake increases** — from 66.22% in September 2023 to 72.93% in June 2026, a 6.71 percentage point increase over 33 months [^ar-fy25]. This rising promoter holding is a strong signal of management confidence in the business trajectory.

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years).** The company's free cash flow (FCF — the cash a company generates after accounting for capital expenditures needed to maintain or expand its asset base) has been volatile due to capacity expansion and working capital intensity. In FY24, operating cash flow was negative (-₹82 Cr) and FCF was -₹119.8 Cr due to high working capital requirements (subsidy receivables, inventory build-up) [^vp-thread]. However, by FY25, **CFO improved to ₹154.30 crore versus PAT of ₹86.54 crore**, indicating healthy cash conversion [^ar-fy25]. Capital expenditure has been directed toward **brownfield expansion** — NPK/DAP capacity to 500 TPD (tonnes per day) and Sulphuric Acid to 300 TPD, both expected by March 2026 [^ar-fy25]. CWIP (Capital Work-in-Progress — money already spent on assets not yet operational) increased from ₹43.01 crore to ₹77.63 crore, reflecting ongoing project execution [^ar-fy25].

**Debt Management.** Krishana has demonstrated **improving leverage and coverage metrics**. Net debt reduced from **₹435.15 crore (FY24) to ₹363.24 crore (FY25)** [^ar-fy25]. The **Debt-to-Equity ratio improved from 1.45 to 0.95**, and **interest coverage improved from 2.60x to 4.12x** [^ar-fy25]. The company carries a **credit rating of A+ from both CRISIL and ICRA**, indicating strong debt servicing capacity [^ip-latest]. However, the community has flagged that **debt levels increased nearly 3x historically**, and the EGM-approved borrowing limit of ₹1,500 crore suggests potential for further leverage if growth investments accelerate [^vp-thread]. Finance costs surged 118.9% YoY in Q1 FY27 (₹20.8 Cr vs ₹9.5 Cr), reflecting the impact of expanded capacity on interest burden [^ip-latest].

**Dividend Policy.** Krishana's dividend payout has been modest, consistent with its growth-phase capital requirements. The **dividend yield of approximately 0.20%** is well below mature fertiliser peers, reflecting management's preference for reinvesting cash into capacity expansion rather than distributing to shareholders. The 5-year average payout ratio is not explicitly disclosed, but the pattern suggests a **"reinvest-first" philosophy** aligned with the company's high-ROCE expansion strategy.

**Promoter Pledging Status.** **No promoter pledging has been reported** as of the latest disclosures [^ar-fy25]. This is a positive governance signal, indicating that promoters have not encumbered their shares for personal borrowings — a common red flag in Indian markets.

**Corporate Governance Flags.** The audit opinion is **unqualified**, and there are **no Key Audit Matters or CARO (Companies Auditor's Report Order) concerns** [^ar-fy25]. However, **material related-party transactions (RPTs)** warrant monitoring: proposed limits of **₹1,400 crore with OPIL and MBAPL (103% of turnover)** and ₹500 crore with SGFL involve loans, borrowings, and trade transactions [^ar-fy25]. While these are with group companies and may reflect operational synergies, the **scale relative to turnover (103%)** is unusually high and could indicate **potential conflict-of-interest risk** [^ar-fy25]. The community has flagged this as a governance concern requiring ongoing scrutiny [^vp-thread]. Management remuneration is reasonable — MD total compensation of ₹8.71 crore (0.64% of revenue) and CFO at ₹0.88 crore [^ar-fy25]. A Company Secretary transition occurred in December 2024 (Ankit Mundra resigned, Anil Sharma appointed), but no other KMP (Key Managerial Personnel) changes were reported [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Partnerships.** The most significant strategic partnership is the **10-year Green Ammonia Purchase Agreement (GAPA) with SECI** for 70,000 MTPA under the National Green Hydrogen Mission [^ip-latest]. This positions Krishana as **India's largest future consumer of green ammonia**, delivering a ~108% structural cost advantage versus global benchmarks [^ip-latest]. The strategic rationale is clear: (a) **cost security** — locked-in preferential pricing protects margin from grey ammonia volatility; (b) **ESG positioning** — decarbonisation alignment enhances long-term sustainability credentials; (c) **regulatory goodwill** — participation in a national mission may yield future policy benefits. The agreement commences in **FY29**, providing visibility on medium-term cost structure [^cc-transcript]. No other material JVs or acquisitions have been announced, though management has indicated openness to **inorganic growth through acquisition of sick units** [^ar-fy25].

**Analytical Commentary (Income Statement):**
Krishana Phoschem's revenue trajectory tells a compelling capacity-driven growth story. Revenue surged from Rs. 924 Cr in FY24 to Rs. 2,418 Cr in FY26 — a 2.6x jump in just two years, driven primarily by the NPK/DAP capacity expansion from 330K MTPA to 495K MTPA (a 50% capacity addition completed in March 2026). Think of it like a factory that suddenly got 50% bigger — once the new production lines started running, the company could sell much more fertilizer to farmers. EBITDA margins, however, compressed from 13.9% in FY24 to 12.3% in FY26 because the company ramped up trading activity (purchase of stock-in-trade surged 996.5% YoY in Q1 FY27) to fill distribution channels before the new captive capacity fully ramped up. Trading typically earns thinner margins than manufacturing, which is why the headline margin dipped despite strong volume growth. PAT margins, on the other hand, expanded impressively from 4.3% in FY24 to 7.4% in FY26 — this is because interest costs fell (net debt reduced from Rs. 435 Cr to Rs. 363 Cr between FY24 and FY25) and operating leverage kicked in. The FY26 effective tax rate of 17% (vs. the normal 25%) likely reflects deferred tax adjustments or one-time benefits; we have normalized this to 25% in our forward estimates.

**PROJECTION RATIONALE & ASSUMPTIONS (FY27E & FY28E):**

- **Revenue Growth (FY27E: +25%, FY28E: +20%):** Anchored to management's explicit guidance from the Q1 FY27 concall where MD Praveen Ostwal stated confidence in "achieving ~25% revenue growth from last year" with a "30-35% achievable" stretch target. The 165K MTPA NPK/DAP capacity addition (operational from March 2026) will contribute a full year of revenue in FY27 vs. only 3 months in FY26 — this alone provides ~Rs. 600-700 Cr of incremental revenue at ~70% utilization. The slight moderation to 20% in FY28E reflects base effect normalization and the fact that green ammonia benefits (70K MTPA SECI deal) only commence from FY29.
- **EBITDA Margin Expansion (FY27E: 14.0%, FY28E: 15.0%):** Q1 FY27 already demonstrated 16.7% EBITDA margins, validating the margin improvement thesis. The expansion is driven by (a) richer product mix from new complex NPK grades (12-32-16, 16-20-0-13, 15-15-15) which command higher realizations, (b) backward integration benefits from expanded Sulphuric Acid capacity (264K → 363K MTPA) reducing captive input costs, and (c) operating leverage as fixed costs get spread over higher volumes. We assume full-year FY27E margin at 14% (vs. Q1's 16.7%) because Q2-Q4 typically see some margin normalization as raw material costs catch up.
- **Interest Cost (FY27E: Rs. 80 Cr):** Elevated due to full-year impact of capex-related borrowings for the NPK expansion (D/E rose to 1.31 in FY26 from 0.98 in FY25). We expect interest to moderate in FY28E (Rs. 75 Cr) as cash flows improve and debt is paid down.
- **Tax Rate (25% normalized):** FY26's 17% effective rate is treated as a one-off; we revert to the statutory 25% for forward years to remain conservative.
- **Dividend Payout (8-10%):** Krishana is in a high-growth capex phase, so dividends remain modest. We expect gradual increases as the balance sheet strengthens.

**Analytical Commentary (Cash Flow & Key Ratios):**
The cash flow statement tells a story of "investment pain now, harvest later." CFO (Cash Flow from Operations) was deeply negative in FY24 (-Rs. 83 Cr) and FY26 (-Rs. 191 Cr) — this is because the company was building inventory and receivables to support its expanded operations. Think of it like a shopkeeper stocking up for the busy season: you spend cash first, then collect from customers later. The negative FY26 CFO is particularly notable because it coincided with the Rs. 359 Cr debt raise (CFF of +Rs. 550 Cr) to fund capex (CFI of -Rs. 350 Cr). This is a classic growth-phase pattern — the company is borrowing to build, and the cash drain will reverse once the new capacity generates steady profits. We expect CFO to swing positive to Rs. 200 Cr in FY27E and Rs. 350 Cr in FY28E as working capital normalizes.

**Working Capital Dynamics:** The Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC — the time between paying for raw materials and collecting cash from customers) improved from 154 days in FY24 to 95 days in FY25, but worsened to 126 days in FY26. The deterioration is primarily due to Debtor Days rising to 108 (subsidy receivables from the government are notoriously slow) and inventory build-up. We expect CCC to compress to 85 days by FY28E as the new capacity stabilizes and collections improve.

**Return Ratios:** ROCE improved dramatically from 13.0% in FY24 to 21.6% in FY26 — this means for every Rs. 100 of capital employed, the company now generates Rs. 21.6 of operating profit, up from Rs. 13. ROE surged even more impressively from 13.5% to 32.1%, reflecting the impact of financial leverage (debt amplifies equity returns). We expect ROCE to expand further to 24% by FY28E as the new capacity operates at full utilization, while ROE moderates slightly to 30% as the balance sheet deleverages.
