### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Duopoly market structure with S&P Global backing**: CRISIL operates in a clear three-player domestic credit-rating market (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE) with CRISIL holding the No. 1 position by revenue and the largest share of incremental bank loan ratings (BLR) mandates, anchored by S&P Global's 71.89% parentage [^ar-fy25]. The S&P brand and methodology overlay allow CRISIL to charge a 5-10% premium over ICRA/CARE on surveillance fees, particularly for FPI-eligible bond issues where S&P linkage is a marketing plus [^ip-latest]. Switching costs are structurally high because rating agency choice is largely an issuer reputational decision, and the regulator-licensed status is a legal barrier to entry.

- **Structural margin story with operating leverage in ratings + mix upside in research**: Consolidated EBITDA margin has sustained in the 30-32% band over FY22-FY25 even as ratings pricing came under pressure, driven by (i) operating leverage on BLR volumes growing at 8-10% CAGR, and (ii) higher-margin Coalition Greenwich revenue now contributing ~30% of consolidated mix vs. sub-10% pre-2017 acquisition [^ar-fy25]. Concall commentary indicates the rating business margin has stabilised post the IL&FS-induced repricing; incremental rating mandates (SME, BLR, structured finance) carry lower incremental cost since analyst capacity is largely fixed [^cc-transcript]. We model EBITDA margin expansion of 50-80 bps over FY26E-FY28E.

- **Diversification into global financial benchmarking via Coalition Greenwich**: The 2017 acquisition of Greenwich Associates (now Coalition Greenwich) and the earlier Irevna buyout have transformed CRISIL from a domestic ratings franchise into a global buy-side benchmarking and analytics platform with 60%+ of revenue in non-rating verticals [^ar-fy25]. Coalition Greenwich revenue is USD-linked, providing a natural USD-earning currency hedge against INR weakness; it serves the top-25 global bulge-bracket investment banks, asset managers and hedge funds, with renewal rates above 90% [^ip-latest]. The strategic rationale is to derate the consolidated multiple from a pure-play rating-agency discount to an information-services premium.

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months)**: (a) RBI tightening of the FLDG framework and renewed focus on lender transparency is expected to drive incremental BLR mandates through 2HFY27 [^cc-transcript]; (b) SEBI's review of minimum promoter holding for rating agencies could unlock further parent-led capital actions; (c) India sovereign rating upgrade cycle with any positive S&P global action on India would create disproportionate flow-through to CRISIL ratings volumes; (d) SME rating volumes recovered to >12,000 active clients in FY25 and continue to scale as TReDS / MUDRA / CGTMSE adoption widens [^ar-fy25]; (e) Coalition Greenwich cross-sell into CRISIL's existing 4,000+ domestic issuer client base is in early stages and represents an underappreciated TAM.

- **Biggest structural risk: pricing pressure in core ratings and regulatory cap on fees**: Indian credit rating agency fees remain regulator-influenced (no free-pricing on small-ticket BLR), and the entry of India Ratings (a Fitch group entity) plus aggressive India market push by Brickwork has led to fee compression of 8-12% on incremental BLR mandates between FY22-FY25 [^cc-transcript]. Adjacent risk is the rising share of bank loan ratings vs. bond ratings in the mix, which structurally carries lower fee per mandate. Margin protection will depend on sustained SME rating volume growth and Coalition Greenwich scaling; failure on either lever will compress consolidated EBITDA margin back to the high-20s.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model**: CRISIL is an information services and analytics company operating across three reportable segments: (1) Ratings (domestic credit ratings, including BLR, corporate bond, SME, structured finance, sovereign); (2) Research (equity and credit research, custom research, Irevna offshore research outsourcing); and (3) Advisory & Data Analytics (Coalition Greenwich global benchmarking, infrastructure advisory through CRISIL Infrastructure, risk solutions, and the recently expanded sustainable-finance analytics) [^ar-fy25].

- **Revenue split by division (FY25 estimate)** [^ip-latest]:
  - Ratings: ~45% of consolidated revenue (~Rs. 1,500 Cr)
  - Research: ~22% (~Rs. 720 Cr)
  - Advisory & Data Analytics (incl. Coalition Greenwich): ~30% (~Rs. 990 Cr)
  - Other / unallocated: ~3%

- **Key end customers per division**:
  - **Ratings**: 500+ corporate issuers, 100+ banks and NBFCs, multilateral agencies (World Bank, ADB); major BLR clients include the top-10 Indian banks and the top-20 NBFCs [^ar-fy25].
  - **Research**: Domestic and global institutional investors (mutual funds, FIIs, PMS, prop desks); equity research coverage across ~600+ listed Indian companies.
  - **Coalition Greenwich**: Top-tier global investment banks (Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPM, Citi, BofA), large asset managers (BlackRock, Fidelity, PIMCO), insurance balance-sheet managers.
  - **Infrastructure Advisory**: Government of India, NHAI, MoHUA, multilateral lenders, state infrastructure agencies.

  - Pragati (sustainability advisory)
  - Multiple international step-downs in US, UK, Singapore, Argentina, Poland for global delivery

- **Manufacturing / operational footprint**: As a services and information company, CRISIL does not run factories; the footprint is talent-and-office based with primary hubs in Mumbai (HQ), with technology, research and analytics centres in Chennai, Pune, Bengaluru and Gurgaon for domestic operations and New York, London, Singapore, Buenos Aires, Warsaw and Hong Kong for international operations [^ip-latest]. Total employee count is in the 3,500-4,000 range with ~55% in India and the balance distributed globally.

S&P Global's strategic intent, as disclosed in successive annual reports, is to retain CRISIL as its India and emerging-markets services hub, with capital allocation decisions subject to S&P Global board oversight.

The adjacent global financial benchmarking, analytics and research outsourcing TAM is materially larger at ~USD 8-10 Bn, growing at 5-7% CAGR, of which CRISIL addresses a sub-segment of ~USD 1.0-1.2 Bn via Coalition Greenwich and Irevna [^ip-latest]. Combined addressable TAM for CRISIL's three segments is therefore ~Rs. 12,000-14,000 Cr (~USD 1.4-1.7 Bn).

- **Policy and regulatory tailwinds**: (a) SEBI's tightening of rating action timelines and the requirement for one-time review of BLR ratings have structurally expanded BLR volume; (b) RBI's emphasis on retail-loan transparency and FLDG framework has triggered a wave of bank-driven rating mandates for NBFC counter-parties; (c) corporate bond market deepening under the RBI's 'V-shaped recovery' of the bond market target of Rs. 47 lakh Cr bond stock by 2030 implies doubling of debt capital-market rating volumes [^vp-thread]; (d) PLI / infra capex cycle is driving a large pipeline of long-tenure debt and project-finance ratings; (e) ESG and sustainable-finance rating mandates are creating a new fee pool (CRISIL acquired Pragati in 2023 to address this) [^cc-transcript].

- **Competitive moat analysis** [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]:
  - **Switching costs: Strong**. Rating agency choice is reputational and a regulatory precondition for issuance. An issuer switching rating agencies mid-cycle faces coupon re-pricing and FPI eligibility questions, making churn structurally low.

  - **Pricing power: Moderate**. While CRISIL commands a brand-led premium, ratings are regulator-supervised and incremental mandates are seeing 8-12% fee compression. Coalition Greenwich and Research retain genuine pricing power due to differentiated offerings and high renewal rates.
