### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS (5 bullet points)

*   **Market Leadership & Moat:** Prataap Snacks holds a strong position in the value-for-money segment of the Indian organized snacks market, particularly in Tier 2/3 cities and rural areas, with its "Yellow Diamond" brand. Its extensive distribution network, tailored product portfolio (e.g., various sizes, regional flavors), and cost-efficient manufacturing base provide a significant competitive moat against both unorganized players and larger FMCG incumbents. The company's focus on affordable price points (Rs. 5, Rs. 10 packs) ensures high volume velocity and penetration.
*   **Structural Margin Story:** The company is strategically shifting its product mix towards higher-margin categories like western snacks (pellets, extruded snacks) and sweet snacks (wafers, cakes) which currently contribute ~55-60% of revenue, up from ~50% three years ago. Furthermore, initiatives such as backward integration into potato flakes, optimization of logistics, and increased in-house manufacturing (reducing reliance on contract manufacturing) are expected to drive a sustainable 150-200 bps expansion in EBITDA margins over the next 2-3 years, targeting ~9-10% by FY28E.
*   **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** Prataap Snacks is actively diversifying beyond its core savory snacks. The "Avadh" brand for namkeen and the foray into sweet snacks (wafers, cakes) under "Rich Feast" are key growth engines. These segments offer higher per-unit realizations and cater to evolving consumer preferences, expanding the company's addressable market. The recent acquisition of Avadh Snacks further strengthens its regional leadership in the namkeen segment, a large and growing market.
*   **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** Key catalysts include the ongoing expansion of distribution reach, particularly in underpenetrated regions, which is expected to drive volume growth. The stabilization of key raw material prices (palm oil, potatoes) after a period of volatility should provide tailwinds for margin improvement in the coming quarters. Furthermore, increased ad-spend and brand-building activities for newer categories like wafers and cakes are anticipated to boost consumer pull and market share.
*   **Biggest Structural Risk:** The most significant structural risk is intense competition from both large, well-funded national FMCG players (e.g., PepsiCo, Haldiram's, Balaji Wafers) and numerous regional unorganized players. This competition can lead to pricing pressures, increased marketing expenses, and potential erosion of market share, especially if Prataap Snacks is unable to innovate rapidly or maintain its cost advantage in the value segment. Volatility in raw material prices (potatoes, edible oils) also remains a persistent risk to profitability.
