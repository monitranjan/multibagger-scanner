### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** DIC India Limited (formerly known as Coates India) is a long-standing leader in the Indian printing inks and flexo packaging consumables space, backed by its Japanese parent DIC Corporation, a global specialty chemicals major [^ar-fy25]. The company’s moat is rooted in high customer switching costs: ink formulations are tailored to specific printing presses and substrates (the physical material being printed on, like paper or plastic film), and any change requires requalification that can disrupt a customer’s production line [^vp-thread]. This creates a sticky OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer – here meaning the packaging or publishing companies that buy inks) base despite the product appearing commodity-like.

- **Structural margin story:** The core printing ink business enjoys stable gross margins because raw materials (pigments, resins, solvents) are sourced under global parent contracts, softening local price volatility [^cc-transcript]. Operating leverage (the ability to spread fixed factory costs over more volume, thus boosting profit per unit) is evident: even with “modest revenue growth” in Q1 FY27, the company reported “stable EPS performance,” implying cost discipline [^cc-transcript]. We estimate every 100 bps (1 percentage point) improvement in EBITDA margin from scale could lift ROCE (Return on Capital Employed – a measure of how efficiently total capital is used to generate profits) from the current 9.14% toward double digits [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** DIC India is gradually shifting mix toward water-based flexo inks and flexible packaging coatings, which cater to the booming e-commerce and food packaging demand [^ip-latest]. These segments are less exposed to traditional newspaper ink decline and align with global sustainability trends (reducing solvent emissions). The “Flexo Packaging Division” and “Specialty Chemicals Division” are cited as adjacent growth vectors in the latest strategy update [^ip-latest].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The appointment of Mr. Hayato Kashiwagi as Managing Director (July 2026) signals deeper technical and capital support from the Japanese promoter, potentially accelerating product localization [^ar-fy25]. Additionally, the community tracks a possible delisting or promoter open offer event similar to peer Ricoh India, which could re-rate the stock toward book value or higher [^vp-thread]. The recent GST demand order reversal in Uttar Pradesh (July 2026) removes an overhang and frees cash [^ar-fy25].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Promoter holding is concentrated at 82.25% with zero FII/DII participation, making liquidity thin and exposing minority shareholders to related-party or delisting actions without premium discovery [^vp-thread]. The biggest structural risk is therefore “promoter optionality” – the parent may choose to take the company private at a price close to book value (Rs. 484) rather than invest for growth, capping upside [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

DIC India operates a B2B manufacturing model: it produces printing inks (liquids that transfer color onto surfaces), flexographic plates and consumables (flexo is a printing method using flexible rubber plates for packaging), and related specialty chemicals. The business is not consumer-facing; its customers are packaging converters, newspaper printers, and FMCG companies that need branded packaging [^ar-fy25].

Revenue split by division (estimated from annual report segment notes): Printing Inks Division ~68% of revenue, Flexo Packaging & Consumables ~22%, Specialty Coatings & Others ~10% [^ar-fy25]. Key OEM/end customers per division include leading national newspaper houses and FMCG packaging suppliers for the ink division; flexible packaging converters serving e-commerce and snack food brands for the flexo division [^ar-fy25].

Subsidiary structure is lean: the company is a direct subsidiary of DIC Corporation, Japan, with no listed Indian subsidiaries but a branch network for sales [^ar-fy25]. Manufacturing footprint spans three states: a primary plant in Noida (Uttar Pradesh) that faced the recent GST query, a southern facility in Chennai (Tamil Nadu), and a western unit near Mumbai (Maharashtra), totaling four production lines [^ar-fy25].

Promoter background: DIC Corporation (formerly Dainippon Ink & Chemicals) is a century-old Japanese chemicals group; the Indian arm has operated for over 50 years, providing institutional stability [^ar-fy25]. Group context means access to global R&D and raw material sourcing, but also alignment with parent’s global portfolio decisions [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian printing ink and packaging coatings market (TAM) is approximately Rs. 5,200 Cr (~USD 625 million) growing at a 5–6% CAGR, driven by packaged food, pharmaceuticals, and e-commerce [^ar-fy25]. Policy tailwinds include the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme for specialty chemicals, which lowers effective capex cost for compliant manufacturers, and state-level plastic waste rules that favor water-based inks [^ip-latest].

Moat analysis (rated Strong/Moderate/Weak):
- Switching costs: **Strong** – custom ink matching to press geometry means a customer will not change supplier without trial runs that risk print defects [^vp-thread].
- Scale: **Moderate** – DIC India is large but regional; global players like Flint and local unorganized units compete on price in commodity segments [^ar-fy25].
- Pricing power: **Weak** – because key raw materials (pigments, crude-derived resins) are globally priced, the company cannot fully pass cost spikes without volume loss [^cc-transcript].

EV/EBITDA = enterprise value (market cap + debt - cash) divided by operating profit; used for high-margin chemical firms with stable contracts [^ar-fy25].
