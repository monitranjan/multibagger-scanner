### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership & Moat in Niche Instrumentation.** Rishabh Instruments is a 35+ year-old, family-promoted engineering company with deep-rooted expertise in electrical and electronic test & measurement instruments — a category that demands UL/IEC certifications, long OEM qualification cycles, and high design-in stickiness [^ip-latest]. The company counts Siemens, ABB, Hitachi, Schneider Electric, and Havells among its marquee customer list [^ar-fy25], and its metering, control and protection segment alone contributed ₹2,578 Mn (36% of FY25 consolidated revenue), reflecting entrenched design wins across industrial OEMs [^ar-fy25]. The EEI segment — encompassing current transformers, energy meters, panel meters, automation products, and SCADA software (via the Microsys Czech Republic acquisition) — has emerged as the primary growth engine of the consolidated entity [^ip-latest].

**2. Structural Margin Story in EEI with 520 bps YoY Expansion in Q1 FY27.** The Electrical & Electronic Instruments (EEI) segment posted an adjusted EBITDA margin of 24.8% in Q1 FY27, a sharp 520 bps YoY expansion from 14.4% in Q1 FY26 [^ip-latest]. Adjusted EBITDA in the EEI segment grew 69.1% YoY in the quarter, validating that the margin uplift is not a one-off but is being driven by (a) richer product mix skewed toward current transformers for data centres and solar applications, (b) operating leverage on the new Nashik SMT lines, and (c) better absorption of fixed costs [^cc-transcript]. Management has guided for ~20% top-line growth and ~22% EBITDA margin in EEI for FY27, with Q1 already exceeding this trajectory [^cc-transcript]. Consolidated EBITDA margin expanded 190 bps YoY to 16.8% in Q1 FY27 (from 14.9% in Q1 FY26), even as the consolidated PAT margin dipped marginally to 9.8% due to a ₹20 Mn increase in depreciation and a ₹20 Mn ESOP charge [^cc-transcript].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — Data Centres, Solar, US, and SCADA.** Rishabh is repositioning itself from a traditional panel-meter supplier to a "globally recognized engineering and technology solutions company" with strategic forays into four high-growth adjacencies [^cc-transcript]: (a) **AI/Data Centre** — current transformers (CTs) and energy meters for hyperscaler-led power distribution, where the company stated domestic order bookings in Q1 FY27 were 20% higher YoY; (b) **Solar** — operational profitability achieved on the inverter business (IONO single-phase and INEO three-phase), with next-gen 50 kW models and hybrid inverters under development; (c) **US market** — actively growing from ~₹30 Cr (FY25) to a target of ₹100 Cr in 2-3 years, leveraging UL-certified products and localised sales teams in Mexico, Canada and Latin America; and (d) **SCADA/Industrial Software** — Microsys (Czech Republic) acquisition provides software-side moat in industrial automation [^cc-transcript] [^ar-fy25]. Geographically, Q1 FY27 saw Europe rise to 28.0% of revenue (from 22.3% YoY) and Others (Africa, SEA, US) to 9.4% (from 5.1%), reducing dependence on Asia from 77.7% to 62.5% [^ip-latest].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts over 6-12 Months.** (i) **Nashik Facility I & II commissioning** — expected to be fully operational within 1-2 months of Q1 FY27, with management stating this will increase production capacity by **>2.5x over 4-5 years**, providing a multi-year operating leverage runway [^cc-transcript] [^ar-fy25]. (ii) **ALUCAST segment** (formerly a drag) is targeting **adjusted EBITDA breakeven for FY27** with a path to double-digit margins over 24 months as legacy auto contracts are exited and replaced with industrial/non-auto business — management explicitly called ALUCAST the "planned decline" segment in Q1 FY27 [^cc-transcript]. (iii) **Solar inverter scale-up** with 15+ products in the certification pipeline and a dedicated manufacturing floor, expected to drive a "very strong growth pattern" as Indian OEMs prefer local suppliers over Chinese imports [^cc-transcript]. (iv) **Potential bolt-on M&A** — management disclosed evaluating 4-5 opportunities in the ₹50-200 Cr ticket size across the US, Europe, and India, with strict strategic-fit and ethical-screen criteria [^cc-transcript].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Top-Line Growth Lagging Internal Targets + ALUCAST Drag.** Despite the strong Q1 FY27 margin print, the elephant in the room remains **sustained underperformance on revenue growth**. Management has guided for a 20-22% consolidated revenue CAGR, but actual quarterly YoY growth has tracked at just 12.4% (Q1), 7.7% (Q2 FY25), and 1.3% (Q3 FY25) [^vp-thread]. FY25 consolidated revenue grew only 4.4% to ₹7,203 Mn (from ₹6,897 Mn in FY24), and consolidated PAT **collapsed 47%** to ₹212 Mn (from ₹399 Mn in FY24) — driven primarily by die-casting segment losses and product-mix dilution [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]. The ALUCAST (HPDC) segment posted a -6.4% adjusted EBITDA margin in Q1 FY27 (vs. +10.2% YoY), with revenue contracting 41.2% YoY to ₹443 Mn as the company deliberately exits low-margin legacy contracts [^ip-latest]. While management's "value over volume" stance is strategically correct, it creates a **lumpy earnings profile** and **execution risk** in the form of slower-than-anticipated conversion of the order pipeline — management itself stated this is "a slow process" taking "about 2 years" [^cc-transcript]. Until ALUCAST returns to double-digit margins and consolidated revenue growth visibly re-rates above mid-teens, the stock will trade at a discount to its mid-cycle P/E multiple.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Rishabh Instruments Limited (RISHABH) is a Nashik-headquartered, 35+ year-old, family-promoted manufacturer of electrical and electronic test & measurement instruments, with a secondary business in high-pressure aluminium die casting (HPDC) [^ip-latest] [^ar-fy25]. The company operates across two consolidated reporting segments:

**1. Electrical & Electronic Instruments (EEI) — 77.7% of Q1 FY27 segment revenue (₹1,540 Mn):** This is the "primary growth engine" per management [^ip-latest]. The segment comprises five sub-divisions:

| Sub-Segment | FY25 Revenue (₹ Mn) | % of FY25 Revenue | Key Products |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Metering, Control & Protection | 2,578 | 36% | Analog/digital panel meters, current transformers, energy meters |
| Electrical Automation | 791 | 11% | SCADA, PLC, power quality instruments, control relays |
| Portable Test & Measurement | 358 | 5% | Clamp meters, multimeters, insulation testers |
| Solar String Inverters | 209 | 3% | IONO (1-phase), INEO (3-phase), upcoming 50 kW and hybrid inverters |
| **EEI Total (incl. inter-segm. adj.)** | **~4,200** | **~58%** | |
| Aluminium Die-Casting (ALUCAST) | 2,574 | 36% | High-pressure die-cast components (auto + industrial mix) |

*Source: FY25 Annual Report, consolidated segment disclosures [^ar-fy25]. Q1 FY27 segment mix from Investor Presentation [^ip-latest].*

**Key OEM/End Customers by Division:** The Metering, Control & Protection division supplies to global industrial OEMs including **Siemens, ABB, Hitachi, Schneider Electric, and Havells** [^ar-fy25] [^ip-latest]. Electrical Automation targets industrial automation end-users and OEMs with the Microsys (Czech Republic) subsidiary providing SCADA software solutions [^ar-fy25]. Solar String Inverters sells to Indian C&I and utility customers, with management highlighting a deliberate shift toward Indian OEM customers to displace Chinese imports — leveraging local service, after-sales support, and IEC certifications as competitive moats [^cc-transcript]. Portable Test & Measurement supplies to both Indian and export B2B customers, with a recent push into UL-certified clamp meters for the US market [^ar-fy25]. ALUCAST's customer base has historically been European automotive Tier-1s, but the company is now actively shifting toward industrial, energy, and railway customers — explicitly targeting non-auto to reach ~75% of segment revenue [^vp-thread].

**Subsidiary Structure:** The group has a meaningful international footprint, including (i) **Microsys** (Czech Republic) — acquired for SCADA software capabilities; (ii) **European foundry operations** (likely Poland based on SMT line commissioning language) for both die-casting and EEI assembly; and (iii) sales/distribution subsidiaries enabling direct-to-customer engagement in the US, UK, Middle East, Mexico, and Canada [^cc-transcript] [^ar-fy25]. Consolidated financials include subsidiary-level goodwill of ₹304.13 Mn (sensitive to impairment assumptions — flagged in the auditor's Key Audit Matters) and a ₹2.96 Mn impairment taken on Energy Solutions Labs in FY25 [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint:** Domestic operations are anchored at Nashik, Maharashtra — where the company is commissioning two new facilities (Nashik Facility I & II) that are expected to more than 2.5x instrumentation production capacity over 4-5 years [^cc-transcript] [^ar-fy25]. New SMT (surface-mount technology) lines have been commissioned in both India and Poland in FY25 [^ar-fy25]. A 1.5 MW captive solar plant has been set up at the European foundry, reflecting operational ESG commitments [^ar-fy25]. The Nashik expansion is the single largest capex in the company's history, with consolidated capex of ₹762 Mn in FY25 vs. an even larger multi-year envelope [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** The company remains firmly under **founder-family promoter control** with promoter holding at 69.40% as of Jun 2026 (down marginally from 70.68% in Sep 2023 — reflecting ESOP-based dilution rather than stake sale) [^ar-fy25]. Promoter pledging is **nil** as per the Jun 2026 shareholding pattern, with FII holding at just 1.17% and DII holding at 11.21% [^ar-fy25]. The MD & Group CEO position transitioned to **Mr. Dineshkumar Musalekar** in Aug 2024, with Mr. Vishal Kulkarni continuing as CFO [^ar-fy25]. The board has 4 independent directors (including 1 woman director), compliant with SEBI LODR norms [^ar-fy25]. The business has historically been a profitable, dividend-paying franchise (FY25 dividend yield of 31% reflects special distributions; underlying dividend policy remains consistent with surplus cash deployment) [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Industry TAM and Growth Vectors:** Rishabh Instruments operates across multiple, distinct industry verticals that share secular tailwinds but have different addressable market sizes:

- **Indian Electrical & Electronic Test & Measurement (T&M) Market:** Estimated at ~₹8,000-10,000 Cr (USD 1.0-1.2 Bn), growing at ~10-12% CAGR, driven by industrial automation, smart metering, and power quality compliance [^cc-transcript].
- **Global Current Transformer / Energy Meter Market:** Estimated at USD 3-4 Bn, growing at ~7-8% CAGR, with the AI/data centre sub-segment growing at >20% CAGR as hyperscalers densify power-distribution intelligence per rack [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].
- **Indian Solar Inverter Market:** Estimated at ~₹15,000-20,000 Cr (~USD 2 Bn), growing at 18-22% CAGR through FY28, supported by India's 500 GW non-fossil capacity target by 2030 [^cc-transcript].
- **Indian SCADA/Industrial Automation Market:** Estimated at ~₹25,000-30,000 Cr (~USD 3-3.5 Bn), growing at ~12-14% CAGR with PLI tailwinds for manufacturing automation [^ar-fy25].
- **Aluminium Die-Casting for Auto + Industrial:** Global market estimated at USD 60-70 Bn, growing at 6-8% CAGR, but with structural softness in auto-exposed sub-segments [^ar-fy25].

**Policy Tailwinds:** Rishabh is a direct beneficiary of multiple Indian policy frameworks: (i) **Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS)** for smart metering rollout targeting 250 Mn prepaid smart meters by 2027; (ii) **Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme** for Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) and grid-scale battery storage; (iii) **FAME-II / PM E-Drive** for EV charging infrastructure, indirectly boosting energy-metering demand; (iv) **500 GW non-fossil target by 2030**, creating direct demand for solar string inverters and grid-side instrumentation; and (v) **Data Centre infrastructure push** under India's National Data Centre Policy, with multiple hyperscalers announcing >USD 20 Bn cumulative investment through 2030 [^cc-transcript]. Management explicitly listed **electrification, grid modernisation, renewable energy, industrial automation, and AI/data centres** as the five secular drivers [^ip-latest].

The leadership transition was formalised in Aug 2024 with the appointment of **Mr. Dineshkumar Musalekar** as Whole-Time Director, now serving as **MD & Group CEO** [^ar-fy25]. **Debt Management:** The consolidated balance sheet is **net debt-free** with cash and equivalents of ₹1,606 Mn as of Q1 FY27 vs. borrowings of ~₹958.7 Mn (concentrated at the subsidiary level) [^ar-fy25] [^cc-transcript]. Debt-service coverage is not a meaningful concern given the net cash position. No credit ratings actions or downgrades have been disclosed in the recent filings reviewed.

**Dividend Policy:** The dividend yield of 31% reflects **special/distribution payouts during FY25-FY26** rather than a sustainable run-rate. The underlying dividend policy has historically been to distribute surplus cash while preserving capital for growth capex. The company has used a combination of regular dividends, special dividends, and ESOPs to reward shareholders — this year's bumper payout is consistent with the cash-rich, capex-flexible profile [^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Pledging Status:** **Zero pledged shares** as of Jun 2026 — a significant positive for governance and shareholder confidence [^ar-fy25].

**Corporate Governance Flags:** (a) **Related Party Transactions** — ₹257.85 Mn in sales to related parties (primarily subsidiaries) is elevated but fully disclosed in the FY25 Annual Report; (b) **Loans/receivables from subsidiaries** of ₹14.62 Mn and corporate guarantees of ₹86.25 Mn are tracked transparently; (c) **Contingent Liabilities** of ₹157.9 Mn (~2.7% of net worth) — well within the 10% safety threshold, comprising primarily legal claims (₹65.8 Mn) and guarantees; (d) **Miscellaneous Expenses** at 1.29% of revenue — well within the <3% safe limit; (e) **CARO Report** notes procedural lapses (audit committee quorum, delayed filings) but **no qualifications or adverse remarks**; (f) **Key Audit Matters** flagged goodwill impairment (₹304.13 Mn) as sensitive to assumptions, with a ₹2.96 Mn impairment taken on Energy Solutions Labs in FY25 — this is a watch-item but not yet a red flag given the cash-generative profile of the underlying businesses [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Strategic Partnerships:** No material JVs were disclosed in the recent filings reviewed. However, management has indicated **active evaluation of 4-5 bolt-on acquisition opportunities** with a **₹50-200 Cr ticket size**, focused on the US, Europe, and India — with strict criteria of strategic fit, ethical alignment, and synergistic value creation ("A + B should be more than A + B") [^cc-transcript]. The Microsys (Czech Republic) acquisition — providing SCADA software capabilities — remains the most recent inorganic move and is the foundation of the company's positioning in industrial automation software [^ar-fy25]. The strategic rationale for future M&A is to (a) deepen US distribution, (b) acquire complementary software/automation capabilities, and (c) potentially acquire solar/renewables-focused entities to accelerate that segment's scale-up. Management was notably non-committal on near-term deal closure timing, stating "deals take time" — a measured approach consistent with their disciplined capital allocation track record [^cc-transcript].
