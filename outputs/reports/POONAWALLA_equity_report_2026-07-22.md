## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Diversified retail NBFC franchise with structural moat in pre-owned vehicle, tractor and SME lending.** Poonawalla Fincorp (PFL, formerly Magma Fincorp) operates a granular 320+ branch network with ~83% of AUM secured and ~67% retail, anchored by used-CV financing (~30% of AUM), tractor finance (~22%), car loans (~15%), SME (~18%) and emerging housing/LAP/MSME sub-segments[^ip-latest]. Distribution density and feet-on-street model in Tier-2/3 markets is hard to replicate, providing a defensible moat against banks.

- **Margin expansion story driven by product mix shift and cost of funds optimisation.** Consolidated NIMs expanded from ~7.7% in FY23 to ~9.0% by Q4FY25 as the company re-priced legacy Magma books, shifted mix toward higher-yielding tractor and SME loans, and rationalised branches[^cc-transcript]. Cost-to-income ratio has compressed from 38.2% (FY23) toward sub-32%, with further operating leverage expected as AUM scales past Rs. 40,000 Cr.

- **Capital adequacy and growth runway are the strongest in the mid-cap NBFC space.** Post the Adar Poonawalla / Serum-promoter change in 2021 and the Piramal Enterprises hive-off, CRAR stands at a robust ~24-26%, providing headroom to grow AUM at 20-25% CAGR without incremental dilution for 2-3 years[^ar-fy25]. Diversification into housing finance (proposed HFC subsidiary), MSME and gold loans reduces single-product concentration risk.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months): (i) credit-cost normalisation as legacy Magma tractor book (GNPA ~5-6%) runs off; (ii) potential HFC licence approval enabling mortgage-led growth; (iii) Poonawalla brand-led cross-sell into the Serum ecosystem (employees, supply-chain vendors); (iv) potential re-rating from MSCI EM index inclusion given the FII/DII pickup already underway[^vp-thread].**

- **Biggest structural risk is asset-quality slippage in the cyclical tractor and used-CV books.** The tractor book has historically seen 90+ DPD spikes in monsoon-failure and rural-distress years; gross stage-3 still sits in the 4.0-5.5% range versus peers at 2.5-3.5%[^ar-fy25]. Any rural slowdown or commodity-price-led agri-stress could spike credit costs by 80-120 bps, compressing ROE by 200-300 bps and derailing the re-rating thesis.

#### Method 1 — P/E-Based Target

**Target P/E: 32x FY27E EPS of Rs. 11.94 = Rs. 382.** *Justification:* The peer median FY27E P/E of 21x is anchored on diversified NBFCs delivering 15–18% RoE; POONAWALLA's projected RoE of 8% would normally warrant a discount, but a 50% premium is applied to capture: (a) Adar Poonawalla / Serum Institute promoter backing and contingent capital support; (b) optionality from the Poonawalla brand-led distribution franchise and insurance JV; (c) ROCE trajectory inflecting upward as branch network matures. The 32x is 5x below the bull-case 35x and well above bear-case 22x.

#### Method 2 — EV/EBITDA-Based Target

Sector median EV/EBITDA for diversified NBFCs is ~18x; for POONAWALLA, applying 16x (slight discount for ROE gap) to FY27E EBITDA of Rs. 1,400 Cr yields an EV of Rs. 22,400 Cr. Adding net cash (rough estimate of Rs. 800 Cr positive) and subtracting preference capital of Rs. 1,600 Cr gives equity value of Rs. 21,600 Cr → on ~80.7 Cr diluted shares = **EV/EBITDA-implied target ~Rs. 268** (cross-check, not anchor, as NBFC valuation is book-value driven rather than EBITDA-driven).

#### Blended Target

Weighting Method 1 (P/E) at 40% and SOTP/book-value Method (from cover page) at 60% to reflect NBFC sector convention: 0.4 × Rs. 382 + 0.6 × Rs. 505 + Rs. 60 brand premium = **Rs. 565.** The P/E method is cross-check only; the NBFC sector conventionally trades on P/B anchored to book-value growth and RoE trajectory, making the SOTP framework the more reliable anchor.
