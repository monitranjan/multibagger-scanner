### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **BFSI-led moat with sticky direct-mortgage platform:** Mphasis derives ~58% of revenue from Banking, Financial Services & Insurance (BFSI), with deep entrenchment in top-tier US banks and capital-markets clients[^ip-latest]. The proprietary Mphasis Deimos mortgage-servicing platform creates meaningful switching costs (multi-year platform integrations, regulatory compliance lock-ins), giving the franchise a defensible moat versus mid-tier IT peers[^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin expansion story:** EBIT margin has expanded from ~15% in FY22 to ~17.5% in FY25, driven by pyramid rationalisation, sub-contractor cost optimisation, and a higher mix of fixed-price cloud transformation deals[^ar-fy25]. Management has guided to sustained 17–18% EBIT margin band with focus on operational leverage from the integrated onshore-offshore model[^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Strategic build-out in Generative AI, cloud-native modernisation, and core modernisation (mainframe-to-cloud) is opening new wallet share with existing BFSI clients[^ip-latest]. The company has also scaled its Healthcare and Logistics verticals (~22% combined revenue contribution) and is investing in industry-specific platforms (e.g., Mphasis Stelligent for cloud, Mphasis Wyde for insurance)[^ip-latest].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1FY27 results (already announced 06-Aug-2026) showing sequential TCV growth of USD 700+ Mn, (ii) ramp-up of large deal wins in US mortgage modernisation, (iii) potential re-rating from Crisil ESG rating upgrade and EcoVadis sustainability scorecard improvement[^ip-latest], (iv) Blackstone strategic optionality given 31.20% promoter holding and history of value-creation in portfolio companies[^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** Concentration risk — top 10 clients contribute ~38% of revenue with disproportionate dependence on a handful of US banking relationships[^ar-fy25]. Any macro-driven capex cut by top US banks (especially in mortgage origination volumes, which are rate-sensitive) could materially impact growth, as seen in FY24 when BFSI vertical growth decelerated to 4.2% YoY[^cc-transcript].
