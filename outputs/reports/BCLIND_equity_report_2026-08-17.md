## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Established grain-based ENA franchise with marquee customer stickiness:** BCL is one of the largest grain-based Extra Neutral Alcohol (ENA) producers in North India, with Pernod Ricard as a long-standing anchor client for its premium potable alcohol requirements [^vp-thread]. The distillery division contributes the bulk of incremental EBITDA and is the primary growth engine going forward [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin expansion from E20 ethanol blending mandate:** The Government of India's E20 ethanol blending programme (target: 20% petrol-ethanol blend by 2025–26) creates a multi-year demand pull for ethanol supplied to Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs) [^cc-transcript]. Grain-based producers like BCL benefit from a fixed pricing formula linked to sugarcane-based ethanol, providing revenue visibility and margin stability [^ip-latest].

- **Capacity expansion of 3–4x over FY26–FY28E unlocks operating leverage:** Management has guided to scaling distillery capacity from ~350 KLPD currently to ~1,400 KLPD by FY28E, with the West Bengal plant (commissioned FY22) and new expansion projects driving the ramp [^cc-transcript]. This translates to a potential 200–250 bps EBITDA margin expansion as fixed costs are absorbed over higher volumes [^ip-latest].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Augmentation of OMC supply order book — the company recently disclosed an updated order book for ethanol supply to OMCs [^cc-transcript]; (ii) commissioning of incremental capacity at the Punjab and West Bengal facilities; (iii) potential re-rating as institutional investors (currently FII 0.12%, DII 0.08%) discover the ethanol story [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk — commodity exposure in edible oil and policy dependence:** Roughly 50% of revenue still comes from the edible oil/vanaspati business, which is a low-margin, commodity-driven segment exposed to crude palm oil import price volatility [^vp-thread]. Additionally, ethanol pricing and offtake volumes are subject to government policy revisions, and any slowdown in the E20 rollout or shift toward CNG/EV adoption in passenger vehicles could moderate demand growth [^vp-thread].

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- India's ethanol blending programme targets 20% petrol-ethanol blend by 2025–26, requiring an estimated 1,000+ crore litres of annual ethanol supply [^cc-transcript].
- At an average realisation of Rs. 60–65/litre, the addressable ethanol procurement market is approximately Rs. 6,000–7,000 Cr annually, growing at a 12–15% CAGR through FY30E [^ip-latest].
- The grain-based ethanol segment (BCL's focus) is estimated at Rs. 2,500–3,000 Cr of this TAM, with BCL targeting a 15–20% market share post-expansion [^vp-thread].
