## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Undisputed Category Leadership With Margin Moat:** Castrol India commands ~21% market share in the branded finished lubricants (BFL) segment, making it the #1 player in two-wheeler and passenger car motor oil (PCMO) categories[^ip-latest]. Strong brand equity built over 100+ years, exclusive tie-ups with OEMs like Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, Hyundai, and Royal Enfield, and ~5 lakh active retail touchpoints create a near-impregnable distribution moat[^ar-fy25].

- **Structural Margin Expansion Story:** EBITDA margins have structurally expanded to ~25% in 1H FY26 vs. sub-15% levels a decade back, driven by premiumisation of the product mix (CRB Turbomax, Castrol Activ, Power 1), sharper B2C bias (>60% of revenue), and benign base oil costs[^cc-transcript]. We see further 100-150 bps margin upside as the high-margin personal mobility sub-segment gains mix.

- **Diversification into EV Fluids & Industrial Adjacencies:** Castrol is actively building the next S-curve via EV-specific thermal management fluids, advanced driveline e-fluids, and industrial specialty lubricants (hydraulic, gear, turbine oils) — the company has signed technology tie-ups with BP's global R&D centres for localisation[^ar-fy25]. The high-margin industrial segment (~18% of revenue, growing 10%+ vs. mid-single-digit for auto lubes) is the under-appreciated compounder[^ip-latest].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12M):** (i) Sustained gross margin recovery tracking crude base oil normalisation, (ii) Interim dividend of Rs. 6.25/share (record date 11 Aug 2026) signals robust cash generation and high payout intent, (iii) Festive-season retail pull expected to drive 7–9% volume growth in 2H FY27, and (iv) possible re-rating once EV-fluids revenue stream becomes meaningful and disclosed separately[^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** Slowing ICE vehicle volumes in passenger cars (post BS-VII transition and faster-than-expected EV adoption in two-wheelers) could compress the addressable automotive lubricants TAM by 200–300 bps annually beyond FY28 — though partly offset by rising lubricant intensity per remaining ICE vehicle (BS-VII spec) and EV fluid adjacencies[^vp-thread].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Business Model:** Castrol India is the country's largest branded finished lubricants (BFL) manufacturer and marketer with a vertically integrated operating model spanning import/blending of base oils, additives, packaging, and distribution to B2C retail and B2B industrial/OEM customers[^ar-fy25]. The company operates a B2C-led model (65–70% of revenue from retail/institutional sales through distributors) and a B2B/OEM segment (~30–35%) supplying factory-fill and service-fill lubricants to automotive and industrial OEMs.

**Revenue Split by Division:**
- **Personal Mobility (Two-wheeler, PCMO, diesel):** ~58% of revenue — Castrol Activ (2W), Castrol CRB Turbomax (HD diesel), Castrol Power 1[^ip-latest].
- **Commercial Vehicle & Industrial:** ~22% — CRB, Castrol Vecton long-drain, industrial gear oils.
- **B2C/Specialty (Power 1, GTX Magnatec, bike point):** ~14% — premium synthetic/semi-synthetic SKUs.
- **Exports & Other (Marine oils, etc.):** ~6% — small but high-margin[^cc-transcript].

**Key OEM/End Customers:**
- Auto OEM: Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, Hyundai, M&M, Royal Enfield, Bajaj, TVS, Hero, Ashok Leyland, Eicher[^ar-fy25].
- Industrial: BHEL, NTPC, Indian Railways, Tata Steel, cement majors — supplied via institutional sales channel[^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary/JV Structure:** Wholly owned subsidiary — Castrol India has no active operating subsidiaries; historically it operated Castrol BP Petco JV (JVs with BP's global arm) which has been fully absorbed. The parent BP plc (UK) holds 51.30% and provides technology, additive access, and global best practices[^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint:** 5 blending plants — Silvassa (Dadra & Nagar Haveli), Patalganga (Maharashtra), Kolkata, Chennai, and a JV in UAE for re-export. Total installed capacity of ~500 million litres; capacity utilisation ~70–75%[^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background:** Promoted by the **Castrol Group (UK)**; majority owned by **BP plc** (post the 2000 BP-Castrol merger). BP is a global energy major — this provides superior technology, additive sourcing (from BP's Infinium/Asia additive supply chain), and governance standards[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 8 — VALUATION

**Valuation philosophy:** Castrol India is a mature, cash-rich, high-ROCE franchise trading at a premium to consumer-facing peers but a discount to branded consumer staples given commodity input volatility in base oils. We anchor on P/E given the clean capital structure (net cash) and use EV/EBITDA as a cross-check.

- **Justification vs peers:** Castrol trades at a structural premium to oil-marketing peers (BPCL ~10x, IOCL ~9x) but a discount to FMCG staples (HUL ~55x, Marico ~40x).
