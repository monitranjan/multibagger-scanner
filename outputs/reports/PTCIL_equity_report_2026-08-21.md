### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership / Moat — A Vertically Integrated, "China+1" Aerospace & Defence Champion.**
PTC Industries is one of the very few private-sector Indian manufacturers that operates a fully vertically integrated titanium and superalloy value chain — from critical-materials processing (VAR — Vacuum Arc Remelting, EBCHR — Electron Beam Cold Hearth Refining, VIM — Vacuum Induction Melting furnaces), through casting, forging, rolling, and finally precision machining of ready-to-fit components[^ip-latest]. This stack is rare even globally; in India, the only meaningful comparable is the state-owned Mishra Dhatu Nigam (Midhani), which has not added meaningful titanium capacity in nearly seven decades[^cc-transcript]. PTC's landmark long-term agreement with Airbus for titanium castings on the A320neo, A330neo, and A350 platforms — combined with active programmes for BrahMos sub-systems, the 105mm Indian Light Weight Tank (for DRDO's ARDE lab), and artillery gun components for Gun Factory Kanpur — establishes a defensible, certified moat that is virtually impossible to replicate in the next 5–7 years[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25].

**2. Structural Margin Story — Mix Shift to Aerospace Drives Multi-Year Margin Expansion.**
Consolidated EBITDA margin has expanded from 18% in FY19 to 32.0% in FY25, and Q1FY27 printed an exceptional 27.5% consolidated EBITDA margin (+954 bps YoY) with Aerolloy Technologies (ATL) alone delivering a 45.0% EBITDA margin[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. The structural driver is mix: industrial castings (commoditised, low-margin) are being progressively replaced by aerospace-grade titanium and superalloy castings, machined assemblies, and now systems/sub-systems integration (BrahMos) — each rung of the value ladder carries materially higher revenue per kg and gross margin[^cc-transcript]. We expect blended EBITDA margin to sustain in the 28–32% band as the Strategic Materials Technology Complex (SMTC) ramps up and Trac Precision Solutions (UK) — acquired Dec 2024 — moves from integration to full utilisation[^ar-fy25].

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — From Components to Systems & Sub-Systems.**
PTC has consciously moved up the "technology pyramid" — from being a casting supplier to becoming a design-led, systems-integration partner[^cc-transcript]. Three concrete examples illustrate this transition: (a) the BrahMos sub-systems contract, where PTC now integrates multiple components into a missile sub-system rather than just supplying castings; (b) the ARDE/DRDO Titanium Cradle for the 105mm Indian Light Weight Tank, where PTC participated from the design stage; and (c) the BAE Systems M777 Ultra-Light Howitzer structure assembly & welding partnership, which extends beyond castings into fabrication[^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. The Dec 2024 acquisition of Trac Precision Solutions (UK) added a new geography (UK), new customers (Rolls-Royce, GKN Aerospace ecosystem), and crucially, a precision-machining capability that converts PTC's castings into ready-to-fit, higher-realisation aerospace components[^ar-fy25].

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months) — QIP Closure, SMTC Ramp, Trac Synergies.**
Four specific catalysts should drive the next 12 months: (i) closure and deployment of the recently approved ₹1,800 Cr QIP — proceeds will fund SMTC Phase-2, Trac capex, and working capital for the Airbus ramp; (ii) commissioning and ramp-up of the titanium ingot/billet line at SMTC Lucknow (inaugurated May 2025 by the Raksha Mantri), which converts PTC from a casting-only player into a fully integrated materials-to-components supplier; (iii) first full year of Trac Precision Solutions consolidation (acquired Dec 2024), which contributed ₹71.4 Cr to Q1FY27 revenue at an 8.5% EBITDA margin — significant scope for margin expansion as Trac's machining volumes scale; and (iv) potential new order announcements from Safran, BAE Systems, and IAI (Israel Aerospace Industries), where PTC has secured its first export order[^ar-fy25][^ip-latest].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk — Working Capital Intensity & Execution of Multi-Year Capacity Build-Out.**
The single biggest structural risk is execution of the simultaneous ramp-up of SMTC Lucknow, Trac UK integration, and the Airbus industrialisation curve — all while managing a working capital cycle that has materially deteriorated. Inventory days have ballooned from ~123 days in FY24 to ~262 days in FY25, and CFO/PAT conversion has collapsed (CFO of ₹1,359 Cr vs PAT of ₹6,102 Cr on a consolidated basis in FY25)[^ar-fy25]. This is a deliberate, strategic build-up of WIP (Work-in-Progress) inventory and trade payables to support the Trac integration and project ramp-up — but it leaves the balance sheet exposed if Airbus or BrahMos order flow disappoints, or if SMTC commissioning slips. A secondary risk is the equity dilution overhang from the ₹1,800 Cr QIP, which triggered an immediate ~3% stock price decline on announcement (29 Jun 2026)[^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model — A "Mine-to-Machined-Component" Titanium & Superalloy Platform.**
PTC Industries is a Lucknow-headquartered, vertically integrated advanced materials and precision engineering company that manufactures ready-to-fit metallic components and sub-systems for the global aerospace, defence, and industrial sectors[^ar-fy25]. The company's defining characteristic is its refusal to operate as a pure-play foundry — instead, it owns the entire value chain: critical raw material processing (titanium scrap recycling via EBCHR, primary melting via VAR and VIM), casting (investment casting of titanium and superalloys), forging, rolling mill, and finally precision machining of finished aerospace components[^ip-latest]. This "mine-to-machine" model is what management calls its "Parity Principle" — the company only enters business areas where there is a technology gap in India, ensuring sustainable, comfortable margins by avoiding commoditised competition[^cc-transcript].

**Revenue Split by Division (Q1FY27 Consolidated).**
For Q1FY27, total consolidated income was ₹197.1 Cr (+83.0% YoY), split across three reporting buckets[^ip-latest]:
- **Aerolloy Technologies Ltd (ATL) — Aerospace & Defence Subsidiary:** ₹74.3 Cr (~37.7% of total income), EBITDA margin of 45.0%, PAT of ₹22.1 Cr. ATL is the high-margin aerospace engine of the group.
- **TRAC Precision Solutions (UK) — Acquired Dec 2024:** ₹71.4 Cr (~36.2% of total income), EBITDA margin of ~8.5%. Trac is in early integration; margin expansion is a key 12-24 month catalyst.
- **PTC Industries Standalone (Castings, Forgings, Industrial):** ~₹51.4 Cr (~26.1% of total income), which includes the legacy industrial castings business, titanium/superalloy materials, and the newly commissioned SMTC Lucknow complex.

**Key OEM / End Customers per Division.**
- **Aerospace Castings & Machined Components:** Airbus (A320neo, A330neo, A350 titanium castings — landmark long-term agreement), Safran (long-term contract), BAE Systems (M777 Howitzer structure assembly + machined components), IAI (first export order), GKN Aerospace ecosystem via Trac, and Rolls-Royce (via Trac UK)[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25].
- **Defence & Strategic Systems:** BrahMos Aerospace (sub-systems integration), DRDO's ARDE lab (Titanium Cradle for 105mm Indian Light Weight Tank), Gun Factory Kanpur (two major artillery gun components), and Indian Army / Indian Air Force via various DPSU (Defence Public Sector Undertaking) channels[^ip-latest].
- **Industrial Castings & Materials:** Domestic and export industrial customers in pumps, valves, marine, and general engineering — these are the legacy, lower-margin businesses being progressively de-emphasised[^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary Structure.**
The group has three principal operating entities: (1) **PTC Industries Ltd (Standalone)** — the listed parent, housing the legacy castings business, the SMTC Lucknow complex, and the titanium/superalloy materials mill; (2) **Aerolloy Technologies Ltd (ATL)** — a wholly-owned subsidiary focused on aerospace castings and the highest-margin business in the group (45% EBITDA margin in Q1FY27); and (3) **Trac Precision Solutions Ltd (UK)** — acquired in December 2024, providing precision machining capability and UK-based customer access (Rolls-Royce, GKN)[^ar-fy25][^ip-latest].

**Manufacturing Footprint.**
PTC operates from a 50-acre integrated campus at Lucknow (Uttar Pradesh), which houses the Strategic Materials Technology Complex (SMTC) — including the VAR, EBCHR, PAM (Plasma Arc Melting), and VIM furnaces, the rolling mill, forging facilities, casting facilities, and a large HIP (Hot Isostatic Pressing) unit that is rare in India[^cc-transcript]. The SMTC was inaugurated in May 2025 by the Raksha Mantri (Defence Minister) and the Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh, marking it as a strategic national asset[^ar-fy25]. Trac Precision Solutions adds a UK manufacturing footprint (likely in the Midlands aerospace cluster), giving PTC its first overseas production base[^ar-fy25].

**Promoter Background & Group Context.**
PTC Industries is a promoter-driven, family-controlled company. The promoter group holds 66.87% of equity (per the latest disclosed shareholding pattern), with zero pledging — a strong governance signal[^ar-fy25]. The company is led by **Mr. Sachin Agarwal, Chairman & Managing Director (CMD)**, who has been the public face and strategic architect of the company's "Parity Principle" and technology-led positioning[^cc-transcript]. **Ms. Smita Agarwal** serves as CFO (and is the CMD's spouse), and the board has been recently strengthened with the induction of independent directors Mr. Kamesh Gupta and Mr. Rakesh Shukla, adding relevant domain expertise[^ar-fy25]. The Agarwal family has been associated with PTC for over two decades, and insider trading data shows a pattern of small, periodic open-market purchases by the CMD (e.g., 815 shares at Rs. 4,510 in Mar 2022, 1,329 shares at Rs. 2,983 in Sep 2021) — a positive signal of promoter skin-in-the-game, though absolute quantities are modest given the high share price[^vp-thread].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) — A Multi-Billion-Dollar Aerospace & Defence Opportunity.**
The global titanium market is estimated at ~USD 35–40 Bn, with aerospace-grade titanium (the segment PTC targets) accounting for roughly 50–55% of this — i.e., a USD 18–22 Bn TAM[^cc-transcript]. The global aerospace castings market is estimated at USD 8–10 Bn, growing at a 6–8% CAGR, while the Indian defence capital procurement budget has grown from ~₹1.0 lakh Cr in FY15 to a planned ~₹1.6 lakh Cr in FY25, with a stated indigenisation target of 70% by 2030[^ar-fy25]. PTC's served addressable market — combining aerospace titanium castings, machined components, and defence sub-systems — is conservatively USD 2–3 Bn today, expanding to USD 4–5 Bn by 2030 as Airbus, BrahMos, and DRDO programmes ramp.

**Policy Tailwinds — A Convergence of Five Favourable Policies.**
Five specific policy tailwinds underpin PTC's growth: (1) **Aatmanirbhar Bharat (Self-Reliant India)** — the Indian government's push for defence indigenisation, with positive indigenisation lists restricting imports of components that PTC can now supply domestically[^ar-fy25]; (2) **"China+1" Global Supply Chain Diversification** — post-COVID and post-Russia-Ukraine war, Western OEMs (Airbus, Boeing, Safran) are actively seeking non-Russian, non-Chinese titanium sources, and PTC is one of the very few qualified alternatives globally[^cc-transcript]; (3) **PLI (Production-Linked Incentive) Scheme for Aerospace & Defence** — though PTC's primary products are not directly PLI-listed, the broader scheme is catalysing domestic OEM demand that flows through to PTC's order book; (4) **FAME and Aviation Infrastructure Capex** — the UDAN regional connectivity scheme and airport modernisation are driving demand for commercial aerospace components; and (5) **DRDO's Design-Led Development Push** — programmes like the 105mm Indian Light Weight Tank and advanced artillery systems are explicitly seeking private-sector design partners, of which PTC is one[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25].

**Valuation Discount / Premium vs Peers — A Justified Premium for Asymmetric Growth Optionality.**
PTC trades at a P/E of 244.5x and P/B of 20.5x — a significant premium to all five peers. This premium is **mathematically justified but optically uncomfortable** for traditional value investors. The justification: PTC is in a hyper-growth, capacity-ramp phase where the FY25 EPS of Rs. 83.78 is artificially depressed by (a) the QIP equity infusion of ~Rs. 700 Cr in FY25, (b) Trac integration costs, and (c) SMTC commissioning expenses ahead of revenue recognition. On a **FY27E forward basis**, applying a normalised 150–180x P/E (still a premium to peers, but justified by 50%+ revenue CAGR and 60%+ PAT CAGR visibility from Q1FY27's +466% YoY PAT print) to a FY27E EPS of Rs. 130–140 yields a fair value of Rs. 22,000–25,000 — broadly aligned with our Rs. 24,500 target[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. The key risk to this premium is execution slippage at SMTC or Trac, which would compress the multiple sharply.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree & Tenure — A First-Generation, Technology-Led Founder.**
PTC Industries is led by **Mr. Sachin Agarwal (CMD)**, a first-generation entrepreneur who has built the company over more than two decades from a domestic industrial castings player into a vertically integrated aerospace & defence platform[^cc-transcript]. His public commentary consistently emphasises the "Parity Principle" — the philosophy that PTC should only operate in technology domains where India has a gap, ensuring sustainable margins and avoiding commoditised competition[^cc-transcript]. This is not a promoter who chases revenue for its own sake; he has explicitly declined to provide short-term revenue guidance, preferring to guide on capacity potential and market size — a hallmark of long-term, capital-allocator thinking[^cc-transcript]. The CFO, **Ms. Smita Agarwal** (CMD's spouse), has been with the company for over a decade and provides continuity in financial discipline. The recent induction of independent directors Mr. Kamesh Gupta and Mr. Rakesh Shukla adds relevant domain expertise to the board[^ar-fy25].

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years) — From Internal Accruals to QIP-Funded Mega Capex.**
PTC's free cash flow (FCF) deployment over the last three years tells a clear story of a company transitioning from a "capability-building" phase to a "capacity-expansion" phase[^cc-transcript]. In FY23 and FY24, FCF was modest and largely reinvested into the SMTC Lucknow complex (₹330 Cr total planned capex, of which ~₹70–80 Cr was spent by mid-2023)[^cc-transcript]. In FY25, the company raised ~₹700 Cr via a QIP (Qualified Institutional Placement — a method of issuing shares directly to institutional investors) to fund the Trac acquisition (Dec 2024) and accelerate SMTC Phase-2 capex[^ar-fy25]. The CFO/PAT divergence in FY25 (CFO of ₹1,359 Cr vs PAT of ₹6,102 Cr on a consolidated basis) is not a sign of poor cash conversion — it reflects a deliberate, strategic build-up of inventory (+₹1,337 Cr) and trade payables (+₹3,332 Cr) to support the Trac integration and project ramp-up[^ar-fy25]. This is "growth capex working capital" — uncomfortable in the short term but value-accretive if the Airbus and BrahMos order flow materialises on schedule.

**Debt Management — Net Cash Balance Sheet with Excellent Coverage.**
PTC's balance sheet has strengthened dramatically over the last 12 months. As of FY25, the company is in a **net cash position of ₹1,288 Cr** (vs net debt of ₹475 Cr in FY24), driven by the QIP proceeds[^ar-fy25]. The **debt-to-equity ratio is 0.04** (vs 0.28 in FY24) — exceptionally low for a capital-intensive manufacturing business. **Interest coverage stands at 12.2x** (vs 5.6x in FY24), indicating ample cushion to service any incremental debt the company may take for the ₹1,800 Cr QIP-funded capex[^ar-fy25]. The company has not disclosed formal credit ratings from CRISIL or India Ratings in the documents reviewed, but the net cash position and 12x interest coverage imply a strong investment-grade profile.

**Dividend Policy — Zero Payout, Full Reinvestment.**
PTC currently pays **no dividend** (dividend yield 0.0%), and the 5-year average payout ratio is effectively 0%[^ar-fy25]. This is a deliberate, value-accretive choice: the company is in a multi-year capex super-cycle (SMTC, Trac, QIP deployment), and every rupee of FCF is better reinvested at ROCE that — once the new capacity ramps — should comfortably exceed the cost of capital. We expect dividend initiation only after FY28–29, once SMTC and Trac reach steady-state utilisation and ROCE normalises above 15%.

**Promoter Pledging Status — Zero Pledging, Strong Skin-in-the-Game.**
**Zero promoter shares are pledged** as of the latest disclosure — a critical governance positive, especially for a company that has just raised ₹1,800 Cr via QIP[^ar-fy25]. Promoter holding stands at 66.87% (per the latest disclosed pattern), down from 67.03% in Sep 2023 — the dilution is entirely attributable to the QIP, not to promoter selling[^ar-fy25]. Insider trading data shows a healthy pattern of small, periodic open-market purchases by the CMD (e.g., 815 shares at Rs. 4,510 in Mar 2022, 1,329 shares at Rs. 2,983 in Sep 2021) and intra-family transfers (gifts to next generation, off-market transfers between HUF and individual accounts) — no concerning insider sales[^vp-thread].

**Corporate Governance Flags — Clean Audit, Negligible RPTs, No Contingent Liabilities.**
The FY25 auditor opinion is **unqualified (clean)** for both standalone and consolidated financials, with the only Key Audit Matter (KAM) being inventory valuation (overhead allocation and NRV — Net Realisable Value — assessment) — a procedural, not substantive, concern[^ar-fy25]. **Related Party Transactions (RPTs)** are not material — limited to investment in the Aerolloy subsidiary and arm's-length purchase/sale of goods, with no suspicious flows identified[^ar-fy25]. **Contingent liabilities** stand at a negligible ₹16.59 lakh (a GST dispute) vs net worth of ₹1,38,666 Cr — effectively 0.001%[^ar-fy25]. **Miscellaneous expenses** are 0.03% of revenue — no flag[^ar-fy25]. **Management remuneration** as a % of revenue is exceptionally low at 0.018% (₹5.58 Cr total KMP — Key Managerial Personnel — remuneration vs ₹30,807.40 Cr revenue)[^ar-fy25]. **CARO (Companies (Auditor's Report) Order)** has no qualifications or adverse remarks[^ar-fy25].

**JVs & Partnerships — Strategic, Customer-Aligned, Not Financial.**
PTC has not entered into financial JVs, but has built a network of strategic, customer-aligned partnerships that are central to its moat: (1) **Airbus** — long-term titanium castings supply agreement covering A320neo, A330neo, A350; (2) **Safran** — long-term contract (specifics not disclosed); (3) **BAE Systems** — M777 Ultra-Light Howitzer structure assembly & welding partnership, plus machined components; (4) **IAI (Israel Aerospace Industries)** — first export order secured; and (5) **DRDO / ARDE / Gun Factory Kanpur** — design-led development partnerships for the 105mm Indian Light Weight Tank and artillery gun components[^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. The Trac Precision Solutions (UK) acquisition (Dec 2024) is the most significant inorganic move — it adds precision machining capability, UK-based customer access (Rolls-Royce, GKN Aerospace), and a foothold in the European aerospace supply chain[^ar-fy25]. The strategic rationale is clear: Trac converts PTC's castings into ready-to-fit, higher-realisation machined components, completing the vertical integration story and enabling PTC to bid for higher-value work packages on Airbus and other OEM platforms.

**Commentary on Income Statement:**

The income statement tells a story of three distinct phases at PTCIL. From FY24 to FY25, growth was modest (~20% YoY) as the company was in capability-building mode, with EBITDA margins actually contracting from 28% to 24% due to high pre-operative costs at the new Strategic Materials Technology Complex (SMTC). The big jump in FY26 (revenue +96% YoY to Rs 603 Cr) is primarily driven by the December 2024 acquisition of Trac Precision Solutions (UK), which contributed its first full quarter in Q4FY25 and was consolidated for ~9 months in FY26. EBITDA margins remained compressed in FY26 (22%) because of acquisition integration costs, underutilized new capacity, and inventory build-up.

The projections show a sharp acceleration. FY27E revenue is forecast at Rs 1,000 Cr (+66% YoY) anchored on the Q1FY27 print of Rs 197 Cr (which alone was +83% YoY), the first full year of Trac consolidation, and the initial ramp of the titanium/superalloy material lines at SMTC. The Q1FY27 EBITDA margin of 27.5% validates our FY27E assumption of 28% — the operating leverage is finally kicking in as fixed costs get spread over a higher revenue base. FY28E revenue of Rs 1,525 Cr (+53%) reflects the second-year ramp of the Airbus titanium castings program, the BrahMos sub-systems deliveries, and capacity utilization climbing toward 60-70% at SMTC. EBITDA margin expansion to 30% in FY28E is driven by mix shift toward higher-margin aerospace/defence products (realizations of Rs 5-25 lakh/kg for finished titanium castings vs. Rs 1,000-1,500/kg for industrial castings).

**PROJECTION RATIONALE & ASSUMPTIONS (FY27E & FY28E):**

- **FY27E Revenue (+65.8% YoY to Rs 1,000 Cr):** The growth assumption is anchored to (a) Q1FY27 actual of Rs 197 Cr (annualized run-rate of ~Rs 790 Cr), (b) management's commentary in the August 2026 investor presentation highlighting ramp-up of Airbus titanium castings and the BrahMos sub-systems order, (c) full-year consolidation of Trac Precision (only ~9 months in FY26), and (d) the commissioning of the titanium and superalloy material lines at SMTC (Lucknow) in May 2025 now contributing a full year. A 66% growth rate is conservative versus the Q1FY27 YoY print of 83% and accounts for typical H2-weighting in defence order deliveries.
- **FY28E Revenue (+52.5% YoY to Rs 1,525 Cr):** Modest deceleration vs. FY27E (from 66% to 53%) reflects the law of large numbers, but absolute incremental revenue of Rs 525 Cr is justified by (a) the second-year ramp at SMTC (management has guided to material revenue potential of "several hundred crores" at full utilization), (b) progressive industrialisation of the Airbus A320neo/A350 titanium castings programs, (c) new missile sub-system deliveries for BrahMos follow-on orders, and (d) initial export revenue from BAE Systems and IAI.
- **EBITDA Margin (FY27E: 28%, FY28E: 30%):** The Q1FY27 print of 27.5% margin (up 954 bps YoY) provides strong validation for our 28% FY27E assumption. Margin expansion drivers include (i) operating leverage as fixed manufacturing overhead gets absorbed over higher volumes, (ii) mix shift toward high-value aerospace castings and machined components (Aerolloy Technologies alone delivered 45% EBITDA margin in Q1FY27), and (iii) full-year benefit of Trac synergies. The expansion to 30% in FY28E reflects further mix improvement and the absorption of one-time integration costs.
- **Other Income (Rs 45-50 Cr):** The QIP proceeds of ~Rs 1,800 Cr parked in liquid/mutual fund investments will generate Rs 35-45 Cr of treasury income annually, supplemented by interest on operational cash balances.
- **Interest Expense (Rs 12-15 Cr):** Marginal increase reflecting incremental working capital borrowings to fund the receivables and inventory build-up, partially offset by QIP proceeds reducing reliance on debt.

**Commentary on Cash Flow & Ratios:**

The cash flow statement is the most contentious part of the PTCIL story right now, and it deserves careful explanation. From FY24A to FY26A, the company has generated a cumulative operating cash flow of just Rs -35 Cr against a cumulative PAT of Rs 205 Cr. In simple English: the company has reported Rs 205 Cr in profits on paper but has actually had to put cash into the business rather than take cash out. This is not fraud — it's a working capital story.

The CFI (Cash Flow from Investing) line tells you where the cash has gone. In FY26 alone, Rs 502 Cr of cash was deployed into capex and acquisitions (primarily the SMTC build-out and Trac integration). The CFF line of Rs 544 Cr in FY26 includes the proceeds from the FY25 QIP that were being deployed in tranches. The Free Cash Flow (CFO minus CFI) is deeply negative at -Rs 488 Cr in FY26 — this is the "cash burn" that concerns conservative investors.

The working capital cycle (the time it takes to convert raw materials into cash from customers) has deteriorated catastrophically: from 490 days in FY24 to 1,261 days in FY26. To put this in simple terms, it now takes PTCIL over 3 years to get its money back from the moment it buys raw materials! This is driven by:
1. **Inventory Days of 1,423** — massive stockpile of work-in-progress (WIP) inventory for the SMTC ramp-up and Trac integration. Defence and aerospace qualification cycles are long (12-18 months), so inventory sits on the shelf before it can be billed.
2. **Debtor Days of 170** — extended credit terms offered to large defence PSUs and global OEMs (Safran, BAE) which is normal in this industry but still heavy.
3. **Days Payable of 333** — partially offsetting, PTCIL has been able to extract extended credit from suppliers, which is a sign of its negotiating power.

For FY27E and FY28E, we project a dramatic normalization: inventory days falling to 600 then 350, the CCC compressing to 550 then 330, and CFO/EBITDA recovering to ~54%. This is the central operational bet of our investment thesis — the working capital cycle MUST normalize, or the equity story falls apart.

**ROCE/ROE Analysis:** ROCE fell from 12% (FY24) to 8% (FY26) — a significant decline. This is NOT because the business got less profitable, but because the denominator (capital employed) grew from Rs 827 Cr to Rs 920 Cr while the numerator (EBIT) grew slower. In simple terms: the company built a Rs 500 Cr factory and that factory is not yet running at full capacity, so the return on the total capital base is diluted. Our FY28E ROCE of 18% assumes the new capacity is at 60-70% utilization by then. If this assumption is wrong, the multiple will re-rate sharply lower.

**Net D/E:** The company is in a net cash position throughout the forecast period, even after the Rs 1,800 Cr QIP. The negative net D/E in FY27E and FY28E reflects QIP proceeds sitting as cash/investments, gradually being deployed into capex.

**DPS:** Zero. PTCIL has not paid a dividend in the recent past, and given the capex requirements, this is unlikely to change in our forecast period. Dividend yield investors should look elsewhere.
