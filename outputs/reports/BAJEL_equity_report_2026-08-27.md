### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat in core transmission EPC:** Bajel Projects is a specialised EPC player (EPC stands for Engineering, Procurement, and Construction — meaning the company designs the project, buys all materials, and physically builds it). Its core strength is in Power Transmission lines at voltages of 132kV, 220kV, 400kV and 765kV [^ip-latest]. To explain in simple terms: kV means kilo-volts, which is a measure of electrical pressure; 765kV is among the highest voltage transmission lines in India, used to move massive amounts of power across states with minimal loss. The company holds an 82% share of its own Unexecuted Order Book (UEOB — this is the total rupee value of contracts it has won but not yet built and billed; think of it as confirmed future work waiting in the pipeline) in this division, and it has proven execution for central utility PGCIL (Power Grid Corporation of India, the government-owned backbone transmission company) [^ip-latest][^vp-thread]. The moat is technical qualification to handle 765kV AIS (Air Insulated Substation — where electrical equipment is insulated by open air, requiring vast expertise to engineer safely) and a long-standing relationship with central-government off-takers, which smaller unproven players cannot easily replicate [^vp-thread].

- **Structural margin story driven by operating leverage:** The company’s EBITDA margin (EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation — a proxy for core operating profitability before financing and accounting costs) improved from 3.4% in FY25 to 4.4% in FY26, a clean 100 bps (basis points; 1 bps = 0.01%, so 100 bps = 1 full percentage point) expansion [^ip-latest]. More importantly, ROACE (Return on Average Capital Employed — a metric showing how much profit the company generates for every rupee of total capital invested in the business) rose to 16.3% in FY26 from 13.2% in FY25 [^ip-latest]. As revenue scales from ₹2,792 Cr (FY26) toward our FY27E ₹3,200 Cr and FY28E ₹3,800 Cr, the fixed cost base (management, admin, digital monitoring systems) gets spread over a larger top-line, mathematically lifting margins without needing price hikes [^ip-latest][^vp-thread].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond plain transmission, Bajel is entering New Energies (formerly Power Distribution) and International EPC [^ip-latest]. It secured a ₹300+ Cr GIS (Gas Insulated Substation — a compact, metal-enclosed switchgear where gas replaces air to insulate live conductors; vital for space-starved data centers in metros) data-center substation order in Q1FY27 [^ip-latest]. On the global front, it won a ₹400+ Cr 500kV Egypt (EETC) ultra-mega transmission order, and structured a 50:50 JV named “ASC Bajel” with Al-Sharif Group for the MENA region plus a 100% subsidiary “Bajel T&D Projects” in Abu Dhabi for GCC coverage [^ip-latest]. These adjacencies reduce single-market dependence and open USD-denominated revenue pools.

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The UEOB hit an all-time high of ₹4,055 Cr as of 30 Jun’26, up 12% YoY from ₹3,442 Cr, signalling visible execution runway [^ip-latest]. In Aug 2026, the company received another domestic transmission order from PGCIL for the WR-ER (Western Region–Eastern Region) interstate scheme, confirming repeat central-government engagement [^vp-thread]. The Ranjangaon manufacturing unit is being expanded from 56,514 MT (FY26) to 110,000 MT across three phases (galvanizing bath, tower, monopole) with a committed ₹170 Cr capex, which de-risks raw-material supply and improves margin capture [^ip-latest]. We also note the formalisation of the Egypt Transmission Pact (announced 22-Jul-2026) as a sentiment catalyst [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The absolute operating margin remains thin — Q1FY27 EBITDA margin was only 4.1% [^ip-latest], far below peer Skipper’s ~10% [^vp-thread]. Retail and analyst community correctly flag that if “economies of scale” do not translate into high single-digit PAT (Profit After Tax) margins as management scales, the ROE (Return on Equity — profit generated per rupee of shareholders’ funds) will stay sub-par despite top-line growth [^vp-thread]. Additionally, the lack of earnings calls (concalls) by management limits transparency, a governance-adjacent red flag [^vp-thread]. International ventures add forex (currency exchange) and geopolitical execution risk.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model:** Bajel Projects operates as an EPC (Engineering, Procurement, Construction) contractor in power infrastructure. In plain language, if a utility wants a new high-voltage power line or substation, Bajel designs the engineering drawings, procures the steel towers and cables, builds the structures on-site, and hands over a working asset. The company makes money via a contract margin between the bid price and its executed cost. It is not a manufacturer-first business, though it owns downstream manufacturing for self-consumption and third-party sale [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25].

- **Revenue / order-book split by division:** Explicit quarterly revenue split by division is not disclosed; the sole granular breakdown is the UEOB mix as of 30 Jun’26 (₹4,055 Cr ex-GST) [^ip-latest]. Power Transmission dominates at 82% (~₹3,325 Cr), New Energies (ex-distribution, now focused on data-center GIS and 33/11kV substations) is 9% (~₹365 Cr), and International EPC is 9% (~₹365 Cr) [^ip-latest]. Historical UEOB trajectory shows disciplined book-building: Mar’23 ₹1,638 Cr → Mar’24 ₹3,598 Cr → Mar’25 ₹2,984 Cr → Mar’26 ₹3,442 Cr → Jun’26 ₹4,055 Cr [^ip-latest].

- **Key customers per division:** Power Transmission serves PGCIL and state transmission utilities (e.g., the 765kV AIS Pune and 400kV Bellary-Davanagere orders won in Q1) [^ip-latest][^vp-thread]. New Energies serves data-center developers requiring compact GIS substations (₹300+ Cr order cited) [^ip-latest]. International serves EETC (Egypt Electricity Transmission Company) for the 500kV line and MENA/GCC clients via JV/subsidiary [^ip-latest].

- **Subsidiary structure:** The company has a 100% subsidiary “Bajel T&D Projects” in Abu Dhabi to capture GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council) EPC demand, and a 50:50 JV “ASC Bajel” with Al-Sharif Group for broader MENA coverage [^ip-latest]. This legal structuring localises bidding and reduces foreign-ownership barriers in those geographies.

- **Manufacturing footprint:** The core manufacturing unit is at Ranjangaon (Maharashtra) producing monopoles (single steel pole structures used instead of lattice towers in constrained spaces), lattice structures (the classic criss-cross steel towers), lighting poles & masts, and offering galvanizing services (galvanizing means dipping steel in molten zinc to prevent rust — critical for 30-year transmission asset life) [^ip-latest]. Current capacity is 56,514 MT (FY26), expanding to 110,000 MT via ₹170 Cr capex [^ip-latest].

- **Promoter background:** Bajel Projects belongs to the Bajaj Group, a legacy Indian industrial house; the annual report evidences Bajaj family trusts and entities as promoter group with pedigree in engineering and infrastructure [^ar-fy25]. The group context provides balance-sheet credibility and relationship access with central utilities, though the company is separately listed (BSE 544042, NSE BAJEL) [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **Market size and growth:** India’s power transmission infrastructure is entering a long-cycle expansion phase driven by renewable evacuation (moving solar/wind power from remote farms to cities), grid modernisation, and energy security. The government’s transmission plan envisages capex of roughly ₹9–10 lakh crore (₹9,000,000–10,000,000 Cr; approx USD 110–120 bn at ₹83/USD) through 2032, implying a TAM (Total Addressable Market) for EPC players well above ₹2 lakh Cr annually in coming years. CAGR of transmission capex is estimated in the low-to-mid teens [^ip-latest].

- **Policy tailwinds:** Key drivers include the 500 GW renewable energy target by 2030 (requiring massive new transmission corridors), the Green Energy Corridor schemes, and PLI (Production Linked Incentive) indirectly supporting domestic steel/equipment. While FAME (scheme for electric vehicles) and RE targets are more demand-side, the core transmission push is a direct supply-side tailwind for Bajel’s order book [^ip-latest].

- **Competitive moat analysis:**
  - **Switching costs:** Moderate. Utilities (like PGCIL) prefer proven EPC players for critical 765kV work because a failure causes grid collapse; re-qualifying a new vendor is costly and slow. Rating: Moderate [^vp-thread].
  - **Scale:** Moderate. Bajel’s ₹2,792 Cr revenue and ₹4,055 Cr order book are meaningful but smaller than KEC or Kalpataru; however, its dedicated manufacturing gives partial vertical integration. Rating: Moderate [^ip-latest].
  - **Pricing power:** Weak. EPC bids are competitive and tariff-driven; the company cannot unilaterally raise prices without losing bids. Rating: Weak [^ip-latest][^vp-thread].

This is a disciplined use of capital given the ₹4,055 Cr UEOB requiring steel structure supply [^ip-latest]. This is a board enabling resolution, not current debt level; it provides headroom for large EPC working-capital cycles (transmission projects require upfront mobilisation). Explicit credit ratings and coverage ratios (interest coverage — a ratio of operating profit to interest expense, showing ability to service debt) are not disclosed in the provided materials, but the absence of promoter pledging (no pledge data in insider transactions) suggests conservative equity-backed structuring [^ar-fy25][^vp-thread].

Related Party Transactions (RPTs) are not flagged as adverse in provided extracts.

- **JVs and strategic rationale:** The 50:50 JV “ASC Bajel” with Al-Sharif Group and 100% subsidiary “Bajel T&D Projects” (Abu Dhabi) are structured to localise Gulf/MENA bidding, mitigate forex risk via on-ground presence, and leverage RAASTA2030 Phase 2–4 strategy of entering adjacent high-voltage niches including HVDC (High Voltage Direct Current — a technology for ultra-long-distance efficient power transfer) [^ip-latest]. This is a calculated, low-equity-intensity method to scale international EPC without organic country setup costs.
