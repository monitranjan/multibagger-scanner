## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Single-site scale moat with global relevance:** HEG operates one of the world's largest single-location graphite electrode plants at Mandideep (Madhya Pradesh) with ~80,000 MTPA nameplate capacity. The integrated facility — captive power, in-house machining, and dedicated R&D — delivers a structural cost advantage of ~8-10% versus multi-site global peers such as GrafTech and Showa Denko. This is the single biggest reason HEG sustains positive EBITDA through downcycles when smaller competitors bleed cash.

- **Margin recovery story is intact, not derailed:** FY25 EBITDA margin compressed to ~12-14% (vs. mid-cycle 25-30%) on the back of Chinese export dumping and weak EAF steel demand. We see 350-450 bps of margin expansion over FY26-28E as (a) Chinese producers face anti-dumping duties in EU/India, (b) needle coke prices normalize, and (c) HEG's UHP-grade mix rises to ~75% of volumes (from ~65% currently). Each 100 bps of margin recovery translates to ~Rs. 90-100 Cr of incremental EBITDA at current revenue base.

- **Diversification into battery-grade graphite is the optionality:** HEG has begun commercial trials for battery-grade synthetic graphite for lithium-ion anodes, leveraging its existing needle coke and graphitization expertise. While this is <5% of revenue today, the addressable market is Rs. 15,000-20,000 Cr by FY30 in India alone (PLI-linked ACC PLI targets). We do not assign explicit value to this in our base case, but it provides a free-call option worth Rs. 50-70/share in a bull scenario.

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months):** (1) Anti-dumping duty imposition by India's DGTR on Chinese graphite electrode imports — final hearing expected Q3FY26; (2) Commissioning of 3-4 large Indian EAF steel projects (JSW, Tata Steel Kalinganagar expansion) which will lift domestic offtake by ~15,000 MT; (3) EU CBAM transition phase ending 2026, which structurally favours low-emission Indian electrode producers over Chinese peers; (4) Potential dividend reinstatement at FY26 AGM after FY25 payout was skipped to preserve cash.

- **Biggest structural risk:** Cyclicality of global EAF steel production, particularly Chinese crude steel output which directly drives electrode demand. A sustained Chinese property downturn could keep electrode prices 15-20% below mid-cycle for 2-3 more years, deferring our margin recovery thesis. Secondary risks include needle coke price spikes (HEG imports ~40% of needle coke requirement) and any unexpected capacity additions by Chinese exporters to non-tariff geographies.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** HEG manufactures ultra-high power (UHP), high power (HP), and regular power (RP) graphite electrodes used primarily in electric arc furnace (EAF) steelmaking. The company also operates a 77 MW captive power plant (coal-based) that supplies ~70% of its electricity needs, providing a critical cost hedge.

**Revenue split by division (FY25):**
- Graphite Electrodes: ~93% of revenue (~Rs. 2,150 Cr)
- Captive Power (merchant sale of surplus): ~5% of revenue (~Rs. 115 Cr)
- Others (machining, by-products): ~2% of revenue (~Rs. 45 Cr)

**Key OEM/end customers:**
- Domestic: Tata Steel, JSW Steel, Jindal Stainless, SAIL
- International: Nucor (USA), Gerdau (Brazil), ArcelorMittal (Europe), various Turkish and Middle Eastern EAF operators
- Top 10 customers contribute ~45% of revenue; no single customer >12%

**Subsidiary structure:**
- HEG Limited (parent) — manufacturing entity
- HEG Graphite Products Limited (wholly-owned subsidiary) — exploring battery-grade graphite
- No material JVs; operations are largely standalone

**Manufacturing footprint:**
- Mandideep, Madhya Pradesh — single integrated plant (~80,000 MTPA electrode capacity + 77 MW power)
- Total plant area: ~200 acres; one of the largest single-site electrode facilities globally
- Workforce: ~1,400 permanent employees

**Promoter background and group context:**
- Promoted by the LNJ Bhilwara Group, founded by the late Lakshmi Niwas Jhunjhunwala
- Current Group Chairman: Ravi Jhunjhunwala (also HEG Chairman)
- Group interests span textiles (RSWM), power (Bhilwara Energy), and graphite (HEG)
- HEG is the group's flagship listed entity by market cap; promoter holding stable at ~59% for over a decade
