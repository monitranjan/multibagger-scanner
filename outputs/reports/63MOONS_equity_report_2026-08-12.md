### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Unique Capital-Markets Tech Franchise with Asset-Light Economics:** 63 Moons (formerly Financial Technologies / FTIL) is one of the few listed Indian pure-play exchanges-and-trading-technology platforms, with its Ticker Limited subsidiary providing mission-critical trading, clearing, and surveillance software to exchanges globally — a structurally high-margin, recurring-revenue franchise that operates with negligible capex intensity [^ip-latest].

- **Structural Margin Re-rating Optionality from Ticker Subsidiary:** Ticker Limited's recent capital raise (announced 12 Aug 2026) and step-down subsidiary acquisition signal an aggressive build-out of fintech, blockchain, and digital-asset infrastructure; if Ticker scales to even a fraction of global peers like Nasdaq (operating margins ~45%) or BSE Tech, consolidated EBITDA margins could expand 400–600 bps over FY26–FY28E [^ar-fy25].

- **NSEL Overhang Resolution Unlocks Hidden Value:** The MPID Court Order dated 29.07.2026 allowing the Scheme of Arrangement between NSEL and specified creditors for a one-time settlement is a decisive milestone — full NSEL resolution removes the single largest discount factor on the stock and could re-rate the holding company discount from current ~50% to book to a more typical 10–20% [^cc-transcript].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1FY27 results (announced 12 Aug 2026) showing Ticker revenue ramp; (ii) further NSEL settlement tranches unlocking trapped cash; (iii) potential dividend continuity at the current ~14% yield acts as a hard floor; (iv) any strategic stake sale or JV in Ticker Limited at a premium to book [^ip-latest].

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** Persistent negative consolidated EPS (Rs. -14.15 in FY25A) and negative ROCE (-5.19%) reflect ongoing provisioning for NSEL legacy liabilities and subdued core revenue; until Ticker achieves scale and NSEL provisioning normalises, headline P&L will remain optically weak and could deter institutional accumulation [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** 63 Moons Technologies is a holding-cum-technology company that designs, develops, and deploys trading and exchange technology platforms for capital markets, commodities, and emerging digital-asset ecosystems. The company operates an asset-light IP-licensing model monetised via licensing fees, transaction royalties, and managed-services contracts [^ip-latest].

**Revenue Split by Division (Indicative, FY25A):**
- **Ticker Limited (Trading Tech / Exchange Solutions):** ~55–60% of consolidated revenue — provides exchange matching engines, clearing & settlement systems, and surveillance tech to domestic and international exchanges [^ar-fy25].
- **NSEL / Spot Markets (Legacy):** ~10–15% — currently in run-down mode pending settlement scheme execution [^cc-transcript].
- **Other IT Services & IP Licensing:** ~25–30% — including blockchain pilots, KYC/AML utilities, and white-label fintech platforms [^ip-latest].

**Key OEM / End Customers per Division:**
- **Ticker:** BSE, MCX, NSE (legacy contracts), Dubai Gold & Commodities Exchange, and several African/ASEAN exchanges [^ar-fy25].
- **NSEL:** Historically served commodity spot market participants; operations suspended since 2013, currently in settlement mode [^cc-transcript].

**Subsidiary Structure:**
- **Ticker Limited** (wholly-owned subsidiary, step-down entities being added per 12 Aug 2026 acquisition announcement) — the growth engine [^ip-latest].
- **National Spot Exchange Ltd (NSEL)** — under settlement scheme per MPID Court Order dated 29.07.2026 [^ar-fy25].
- **Indian Commodity Exchange (ICEX)** and other legacy exchange stakes — non-core, potential divestment candidates [^cc-transcript].

**Manufacturing / Operational Footprint:** Mumbai (HQ and primary tech centre), with smaller development centres in Bengaluru and Gandhinagar; total employee base of ~500–700, predominantly engineers [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** The promoter group, historically led by Jignesh Shah, retains 58.13% stake; the company is the listed survivor of the erstwhile FTIL group whose NSEL subsidiary triggered the 2013 payment crisis. Post-crisis, the company has pivoted decisively toward technology licensing under the "63 Moons" rebrand [^ar-fy25].
