### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Dominant Market Leadership in Core Therapeutic Franchises with Defensible Moat:** GSK Pharma is the market leader in dermatology, holds the #2 position in the anti-infectives segment, and ranks #3 in vaccines[^ip-latest]. Switching costs in physician-prescribed pharmaceuticals remain high given entrenched brand equity (Augmentin, Ceftum, Synflorix, Twinrix), entrenched physician relationships built over 3+ decades, and a regulatory barrier from the Schedule H/H1 framework[^ar-fy25]. We rate the moat as **Strong**.

- **Structural Margin Expansion Story:** Gross margins expanded to ~64% in FY25 from ~57% in FY22 on the back of a richer product mix towards dermatology and vaccines, both of which command pricing power[^ar-fy25]. EBITDA margins have structurally shifted up to ~25-26% (FY25) from ~17-18% pre-FY22[^cc-transcript]. Management has guided to sustained 200-300 bps margin expansion through FY28E as field-force productivity rises and the chronic/dermatology share of mix continues to grow[^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies — Vaccines and Specialty:** The company has stepped up capex in Nashik and built a dedicated vaccines manufacturing facility targeting the NIP (National Immunisation Programme) opportunity[^ip-latest]. Specialty focus on Adult Immunisation, Pain Management (Tapentadol) and Paediatric Vaccines (Menveo, Bexsero) addresses Rs. 30,000+ Cr adjacencies growing at 12-14%[^cc-transcript]. The Ankleshwar site is being repurposed for high-margin dermatology topicals, a Rs. 12,000 Cr segment growing at 14% CAGR[^ar-fy25].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** (1) Twinrix adult hepatitis A+B combo relaunch in the private market post-supply normalisation; (2) Price hikes in DPCO non-controlled portfolio (~70% of domestic revenue is outside NLEM); (3) Continued momentum in dermatology where growth has outpaced the industry by 800-1,000 bps; (4) Margin tailwind from softening API costs in cephalosporins; (5) Possible launch of new global GSK pipeline assets in India via in-licensing route[^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest Structural Risk — Regulatory and Pricing Headwinds on NLEM Portfolio:** ~30% of domestic revenue is under NLEM/DPCO price control with periodic compulsory price cuts (last cut October 2025, next expected October 2026)[^cc-transcript]. Further, any delisting of cough/cold FDCs following the 2023 ban precedent poses an estimated 3-5% revenue-at-risk[^vp-thread]. The biggest risk, however, is concentration of ~75.6% promoter holding with GSK Plc — any stake sale overhang (similar to HUL/parent recasts) could trigger technical pressure on the stock despite no change in underlying fundamentals[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

GSK Pharmaceuticals India is a subsidiary of GlaxoSmithKline plc (UK) and operates a pure-play prescription pharmaceuticals model across two broad verticals: **Pharmaceuticals (Rx)** and **Vaccines**[^ip-latest].

- **Revenue split by division (FY25A):** Anti-Infectives ~28%, Dermatology ~22%, Respiratory/Anti-Allergy ~12%, Vaccines ~15%, Gastrointestinal/Nutritionals ~8%, Pain/Analgesics ~7%, Others ~8%[^ar-fy25]. Dermatology has grown from ~12% of mix in FY20 to ~22% in FY25 — the most significant mix shift in the portfolio.

- **Core business model:** The company sells primarily through a 4,000+ strong field force targeting GPs, paediatricians, dermatologists, chest physicians and consulting specialists[^ip-latest]. Distribution is via ~3,200 stockists and a national CFA network. There is negligible exposure to government/Institutional business and the company is not a participant in the bulk generic APIs market.

- **Key OEM/end customers per division:** (1) **Dermatology** — Derm-EMR, 8X (Tretinoin) and Cosvate franchise prescribed by ~70,000 dermatologists; (2) **Anti-Infectives** — Augmentin (Amoxycillin+Clavulanate) is the largest brand at >Rs. 700 Cr annual sales; (3) **Vaccines** — Synflorix (pneumococcal), Twinrix, Havrix, Engerix, Bexsero, Menveo, Boostrix, Shingrix[^ar-fy25]; (4) **Pain/Analgesics** — Tapentadol (acute pain) and Nucynta franchise.

- **Subsidiary/JV structure:** Wholly-owned subsidiary model. Holds 100% in 'Biddle Sawyer Limited' (legacy distribution arm) and ' GSK Consumer Healthcare India' (OTC was demerged/divested). Current subsidiary count is minimal — the company is largely a single operating entity with manufacturing at 2 sites[^ar-fy25].

- **Manufacturing footprint:** Two key plants — (1) **Nashik, Maharashtra** — large integrated manufacturing hub (tablets, injectables, vaccines including new line); (2) **Ankleshwar, Gujarat** — being re-purposed for dermatology topicals and newer specialty formats[^ip-latest]. Total plant count is 2 vs. 6-7 in 2010 — a deliberate rationalisation under the global 'supply chain of the future' programme.

- **Promoter background and group context:** GlaxoSmithKline plc (UK) holds 75.63% via Glaxo Group Limited[^ar-fy25]. GSK Plc is one of the world's largest vaccine manufacturers (Shingrix, Bexsero globally) and this provides India access to global pipeline assets on a priority basis — a critical structural advantage not available to Indian peers[^ip-latest]. The Indian listed entity is professionally managed; Sridhar Venkatesh (MD) has been with GSK for 20+ years and was earlier in charge of the Middle East/Africa region[^cc-transcript].

- **Policy tailwinds:** (1) **Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme** for bulk drugs — limited benefit as GSK does not participate in APIs but benefits from cheaper local sourcing; (2) **National Immunisation Programme (NIP)** expansion — Union Budget 2026-27 increased immunisation outlay by 18% YoY, providing upside for vaccine players with WHO-prequalified facilities like GSK Nashik[^ip-latest]; (3) **PMJAY extension** to senior citizens — drives chronic prescription growth; (4) **Faster Formulary inclusion** of newer drugs in NLEM, though a double-edged sword.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

The current Managing Director, Sridhar Venkatesh, has 20+ years GSK tenure and the CFO/Whole-time Director has been in seat since FY22. This continuity of leadership is rare in Indian pharma where promoter churn is high.

- **FCF deployment track record (3 years FY23-FY25):**

  - Of this, ~70% is returned to shareholders via dividends (Rs. 4,000+ Cr in cumulative dividends over FY23-25), ~20% retained for capacity expansion, ~10% for working capital[^cc-transcript].
  - No large debt-funded acquisitions in the last 5 years — a clean, disciplined capital allocator profile.

- **Debt management:** Net cash positive. The company has had negative net debt (cash + investments > total debt) consistently over the last decade[^ar-fy25]. Cash and equivalents stand at ~Rs. 3,000-3,500 Cr. There is no long-term rupee debt on the books; only working capital lines. No external credit rating required given the net cash position.

- **Dividend policy:** Payout ratio averaged ~70% over FY21-25 (Rs. 32 in FY21 to Rs. 42 in FY25 per share) — consistent with the 'mature pharma, no major capex ahead' profile[^ar-fy25]. 5-year average dividend yield is ~1.8%; current yield 2.18%[^ip-latest].

- **Promoter pledging status:** Zero pledging. Promoter holding has been static at 75.63% for several years — a strong signal of promoter stability and confidence[^ar-fy25].

- **Corporate governance flags:**
  - **Auditor changes:** Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP has been statutory auditor for 5+ years (rotating within the Deloitte network as per RBI guidelines) — no adverse history[^ar-fy25].
  - **RPTs:** Related-party transactions are limited to the brand/technology license fees paid to GSK Plc (~3-4% of revenue) and a few shared services — all board-approved and disclosed[^ar-fy25].
  - **Qualified opinions:** None. Audit reports are clean and unqualified for the last 10+ years[^ar-fy25].

- **JVs and strategic partnerships:** No active JVs. Historical JV with SmithKline Beecham merged into the global GSK Plc structure in 2001. The relationship with the global parent functions as a de-facto in-licensing partnership — GSK India gets first-call on global pipeline assets (Shingrix was launched in India 2 years post-global launch)[^ip-latest]. The trade-off is a brand/technology license fee of ~3-4% of sales paid to the parent — a reasonable cost given pipeline access.
