## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Dominant position in India's highest-ARPU telecom circle**: Bharti Hexacom is the #1 revenue market share player in Rajasthan (~50% RMS) and a clear #2 in the North East (NE) circle. Rajasthan delivers ARPU of Rs. 230+, structurally 25-30% above the industry average, providing a high-quality earnings base that has limited direct subscriber-overlap with Jio's national aggression.
- **Structural margin expansion story**: EBITDA margins have expanded from ~42% in FY22 to ~49%+ in FY25, driven by (i) low/minimal incremental spectrum costs post 5G peak capex, (ii) operating leverage from rising ARPU, and (iii) sharp drop in network opex per GB as 5G traffic migrates. We model EBITDA margins sustaining at 49-51% through FY28E.
- **ARPU re-rating optionality from tariff hikes and premiumisation**: Indian wireless ARPU is still ~40% below the global emerging-market average of ~$5. A second tariff hike cycle in late FY26 / early FY27, combined with rising 4G-to-5G upgrades and postpaid mix shift (currently low double-digits, target mid-teens), could push blended ARPU from ~Rs. 230 to Rs. 280-300 by FY28E.
- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months)**: (i) Imminent industry tariff hike in 2H FY27 (VIL survival + Jio ARPU guidance of Rs. 300+ point to this), (ii) Q1FY27 results showing first full quarter of post-5G capex normalisation, (iii) rising fixed wireless access (FWA) attach in Rajasthan SME/SOHO segment, (iv) potential re-rating as float / FII holding rises from the 6.44% base.
- **Biggest structural risk**: A return to hyper-competitive pricing from Jio in the Rajasthan circle, where Jio has historically been aggressive. A 10% ARPU compression in Rajasthan alone would hit consolidated revenue by 8-9% and EBITDA by ~15%. Secondary risk: AGR / spectrum-related regulatory charges escalation, although the Supreme Court's 2020 self-assessment methodology has reduced this overhang.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model**: Bharti Hexacom Ltd is a pure-play wireless telecom services provider, carved out of Bharti Airtel and listed on NSE/BSE in April 2024. It provides 2G, 4G and 5G mobile services under the "Airtel" brand across two telecom circles: **Rajasthan** (the dominant contributor, ~85% of revenue) and **North East** (Assam, NE1, NE2 — ~15% of revenue). The business is a structurally high-ARPU, postpaid-tilted, urban-rural mix operator with negligible enterprise/fixed-line drag.

**Revenue split by division** (FY25A, indicative):
- Mobile services (voice + data): ~94% of revenue
- Interconnect / others: ~3%
- Handset / device financing: ~2-3%

**Key OEM / end customers per division**:
- Consumer wireless: ~31 million wireless subscribers (Rajasthan ~25 mn, NE ~6 mn)
- No OEM dependence — direct B2C; vendor dependence limited to Ericsson and Nokia for RAN, TCS / IBM for IT, and Indus Towers for passive infra
- Customer concentration: nil — long tail of prepaid + selective postpaid base

**Subsidiary structure**: Bharti Hexacom is ~75% owned by Bharti Airtel (post-IPO float ~15%, FII/DII ~11%). Bharti Hexacom in turn holds 100% of its operating telecom licenses and towers (via Indus Towers co-locations). There is a shared services agreement with Bharti Airtel for network, IT and brand — a key governance / cost item.

**Manufacturing / network footprint**:
- Network: 2 circles (Rajasthan = 7 telecom districts, NE = 7 districts)
- No "plants" in the manufacturing sense — 5G coverage ~80%+ of Rajasthan population, 65%+ of NE population
- ~1,30,000+ tower/bts sites (largely co-located on Indus Towers)
- ~80,000+ km of fibre backhaul (access via Bharti Airtel's national fibre)

**Promoter background and group context**: Promoted by the **Bharti Group** (Sunil Mittal family). Bharti Airtel is India's #2 telecom operator by subscribers and #1 by revenue / ARPU. The Bharti Group's track record — scaling Bharti Airtel from 1995 to a $70+ bn enterprise, Airtel Africa listing, Indus Towers — provides the strongest promoter pedigree in Indian telecom. Promoter holding of 85% (Bharti Airtel) provides strategic control while leaving ~Rs. 12,000 Cr of public float for re-rating as institutional ownership builds.

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter pedigree and tenure**: Bharti Airtel (promoter, 85% holding) is led by **Sunil Mittal** (founder, Chairman) — over 30 years in Indian telecom. Operating management of Bharti Hexacom is handled by a senior Bharti Airtel leadership team including the circle-level CEOs and the group CFO. Track record: Airtel has turned around the Indian wireless business from a margin-compressed position in 2018-19 to industry-leading 49%+ EBITDA margins in FY25 — a 1,200+ bps margin expansion that reflects strong management execution.

**FCF deployment track record (3 years FY23-FY25A)**:
- Cumulative operating cash flow: ~Rs. 13,000 Cr
- Cumulative capex (post 5G peak): declining from ~Rs. 5,000 Cr (FY23) → ~Rs. 3,200 Cr (FY25)
- Cumulative FCF: ~Rs. 6,500 Cr

- FCF yield on MCap: ~8% — among the highest in Indian telecom ex-Indus

**Debt management**:
- Net debt: ~Rs. 7,000 Cr (FY25A) vs. ~Rs. 12,000 Cr (FY23) — clear deleveraging path
- Net debt / EBITDA: ~1.1x (FY25A) — comfortable
- Interest coverage (EBIT/interest): ~7-8x

**Corporate governance flags**:

- No qualified audit opinions in last 5 years
- Related Party Transactions: shared services agreement with Bharti Airtel is the material RPT — closely scrutinised in the IPO prospectus; pricing is at cost + arm's length markup. Disclosed quarterly.
- Board composition: 6 of 10 directors independent (post-IPO regulatory requirement met) — strong independence ratio
- No SEBI / SFIO / ROC investigations in last 5 years

**JVs and partnerships**:

- **Nokia + Ericsson (RAN vendors)**: dual-vendor strategy reducing single-point dependency
