## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

IFCI Limited is a "special situations" PSU NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company) play. The stock has re-rated from a 52W low of Rs. 46.23 to a 52W high of Rs. 99.49 — a 115% surge — without any corresponding improvement in operating profitability. Our BUY rating is anchored on Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) optionality and group restructuring arbitrage, NOT on traditional earnings or asset quality metrics. Below are the five pillars of our investment view.

**1. Hidden Value in Unlisted Equity Portfolio (SOTP Story).** IFCI holds strategic stakes in unlisted/delistable entities — most notably a legacy equity position in NSE (National Stock Exchange), which has been a controversial overhang for over a decade. The Delhi High Court recently dismissed a PIL challenging IFCI's NSE share sale, removing a legal overhang[^ar-fy25]. If NSE's much-awaited IPO materialises (or if IFCI monetises its stake via block sale), the unlocking value could materially exceed the company's current market capitalisation. This is the single largest driver of the recent re-rating. Think of it like owning a small slice of a private company that everyone believes is worth a lot — until the company sells or lists, you don't get the cash, but the market gives you credit for the "potential" payout.

**2. Pivot to a Fee-Based, Quasi-Government Advisory Franchise.** The most underappreciated structural shift is IFCI's transition from a balance-sheet-heavy lender to a fee-based advisory entity. IFCI is now the Project Management Advisor (PMA) for **10 out of 14 PLI (Production Linked Incentive) schemes** under the government's AatmaNirbhar Bharat programme[^ar-fy25]. Advisory income of Rs. 69 crore in FY25 (8% of revenue) is a low-risk, government-counterparty annuity stream with high incremental margins — a critical cushion as the legacy loan book decays. The PLI schemes under IFCI's PMA umbrella include Electric Mobility (EMPS), SMEC, PM E-Drive, and IT Hardware 2.0 — all fast-growing, capex-heavy sectors where the government wants execution support.

**3. Group Consolidation Catalyst Pending.** The board has approved (in-principle) the merger of IFCI Factors, SHCIL (Stock Holding Corporation of India), IIDL (IFCI Infrastructure Development Ltd.), and other subsidiaries into the parent[^ar-fy25]. This restructuring could unlock synergies (cost rationalisation, common treasury, unified brand) and potentially attract a strategic investor. While the timeline is uncertain (likely 12-24 months), even partial completion would meaningfully re-rate the consolidated entity. Imagine four small companies coming together as one bigger, cleaner balance sheet — that's the consolidation story.

**4. Near-Term Catalyst Path (6-12 Months).** Three identifiable triggers could sustain the rally: (a) NSE IPO or stake monetisation announcement, (b) Cabinet approval of the group consolidation scheme, and (c) the AGM on 25 September 2026 where management may provide forward guidance on the advisory vertical and recovery targets. The recent reaffirmation of credit rating by CARE Edge (18 Aug 2026) is a small positive — it removes one element of uncertainty around refinancing[^ar-fy25]. Additionally, the BRSR filing (Sep 2026) signals IFCI is aligning with ESG disclosure norms, which could attract sovereign ESG funds.

**5. Biggest Structural Risk: Solvency & Asset Quality Deterioration.** The single biggest risk is the company's **negative CRAR of -23.04%** (Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio — a key solvency metric for lenders; positive CRAR means the company has enough capital to absorb losses, and 15% is the RBI norm) and Gross NPAs (Non-Performing Assets — loans where the borrower has stopped paying for 90+ days) of ~50% of gross advances (Rs. 3,694 crore in bad loans)[^ar-fy25]. This means IFCI is technically under-capitalised — it cannot raise fresh borrowings without regulatory consequences. The business is surviving on GoI (Government of India) equity infusions (Rs. 1,000 crore in two tranches) and the gradual runoff of the legacy loan book. Without a credible path to NPA resolution or fresh capital, the SOTP thesis is essentially a bet on government willingness to keep funding this entity — a real but not guaranteed tail-risk. **Caveat emptor**: this is a restructuring play, not a quality compounder.

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model.** IFCI Limited is India's oldest Development Financial Institution (DFI), originally established in 1948 as a Statutory Corporation to provide long-term finance to industry. It was later converted into a public limited company in 1993 and is now classified as an NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company) under the RBI's regulatory framework, with the Government of India as the dominant shareholder (73.14%)[^ar-fy25]. Think of IFCI as a "government-owned bank that doesn't take deposits" — it lends, invests, and earns fees, but funds itself by issuing bonds and equity rather than accepting savings from the public.

The company operates a **single business segment** as per Ind AS 108 disclosure requirements: "Financing Activities." However, within this segment, IFCI earns from multiple income streams — interest on loans, dividends from equity investments, advisory fees, rental income, and treasury gains. The strategic shift over the last 18 months has been from being a "balance-sheet lender" (where you make money on the spread between what you lend at and what you borrow at) to becoming a "fee-based advisor" (where you make money on services without putting your own capital at risk).

**Revenue Split by Division (Standalone, FY25).** The Total Income of Rs. 842 crore breaks down as follows[^ar-fy25]:

| Revenue Stream | Rs. Crore | % of Total |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Interest Income (loans) | 351 | 42% |
| Dividend Income (equity portfolio) | 111 | 13% |
| Net Gain on Fair Value Changes | 105 | 13% |
| Other Income (incl. treasury, recoveries) | 161 | 19% |
| Advisory Income (PLI/ESG/BSE) | 69 | 8% |
| Rental Income (investment property) | 44 | 5% |
| **Total** | **842** | **100%** |

A critical observation: **interest income (42%) has shrunk materially** from prior years as the loan book runs off, while fair value gains and other income now contribute 32% combined — these are inherently lumpy and less predictable. Advisory income at 8% is the only "growth" line item and management's strategic focus.

**Key End Customers / Counterparties.** The customer base is bifurcated:

- **Legacy Lending Book**: Predominantly corporates across infrastructure, manufacturing, and services. Notable exposures (as per annual disclosures) include companies in steel, textiles, real estate, and aviation — many of which are now in the NPA list. The annual report does not name specific borrowers due to confidentiality, but the Rs. 3,694 crore NPA book (representing ~50% of gross advances) is concentrated in 20-30 large stressed accounts[^ar-fy25].
- **Advisory Counterparties**: Government of India ministries (Department of Pharmaceuticals, Ministry of Heavy Industries, Ministry of Electronics & IT) for PLI scheme management. IFCI also has an MoU with BSE for SME listing support. These are quasi-sovereign counterparties — payment risk is virtually zero, but pricing power is also limited (rates are set administratively).

**Subsidiary Structure.** IFCI has a sprawling group structure (the "IFCI Group"), which is at the heart of the consolidation thesis[^ar-fy25]:

| Subsidiary / Associate | Nature of Business | IFCI Stake |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| IFCI Factors Ltd. | Factoring / receivable finance | ~100% |
| Stock Holding Corporation of India (SHCIL) | Custodial services for FIIs / PMS | ~52% |
| IFCI Infrastructure Development Ltd. (IIDL) | Infrastructure advisory | ~100% |
| IIDL Sikkim (formerly TFCI) | Tourism / infra finance | Stake |
| IVCF (IFCI Venture Capital Funds) | PE / VC fund management | Majority |
| MPCON (MPCON Ltd.) | Consultancy | Associate |
| IFL (IFCI Financial Services) | Stressed asset resolution | Subsidiary |

The complexity of this group structure is a key reason for the consolidation move — overlapping boards, shared services, and inter-company exposures make consolidated governance difficult. The board has approved (in-principle) merging SHCIL, IFCI Factors, and IIDL into the parent[^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing / Operational Footprint.** IFCI is **not a manufacturing company** — it has no plants, production lines, or factory footprint. Its physical presence is limited to:
- **Registered Office**: New Delhi
- **Branch Offices**: Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata (legacy lending branches — many have been scaled down)
- **Investment Property**: A significant office building (likely IFCI Tower in Delhi) earning rental income of Rs. 44 crore

Capex (capital expenditure) is minimal: only Rs. 0.15 crore on PPE (Property, Plant & Equipment) in FY25 vs Rs. 0.32 crore in FY24[^ar-fy25]. There is **no capital work-in-progress**, no expansion plans, and no new branches announced. This is a deliberately winding-down operational footprint — the company is in run-off mode on the lending side.

**Promoter Background & Group Context.** The promoter is the **Government of India (GoI)**, holding 72.57% as of June 2026 (up from 70.32% in March 2024)[^ar-fy25]. Over the past five years, GoI has repeatedly infused capital via preferential allotments to keep IFCI solvent: Rs. 200 crore (2020), Rs. 200 crore (2021), Rs. 100 crore (2022), Rs. 100 crore (2022), Rs. 400 crore (2023), and Rs. 500 crore each in 2024 and 2025 — totalling **Rs. 2,000+ crore in seven years**[^ar-fy25]. This is not "promoter support" in the conventional sense — it is a sovereign backstop, and the cumulative dilution for minority shareholders has been substantial.

The current leadership team includes:
- **MD & CEO**: Rahul Bhave (appointed 21 March 2025; previously Deputy MD)
- **Former MD**: Manoj Mittal (moved to SIDBI as CMD in July 2024)
- Board has multiple government-nominated directors and only **one Independent Director** (Umesh Kumar Garg), against the SEBI LODR requirement — a flagged governance issue[^ar-fy25].

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Industry Context: PSU NBFCs & Development Finance.** IFCI sits at the intersection of two industry buckets: (a) NBFC / HFC universe (NBFC = Non-Banking Financial Company; HFC = Housing Finance Company), and (b) PSU government-owned financial institutions. The broader NBFC industry in India has been under structural pressure since 2018 (IL&FS crisis), with regulatory tightening, asset quality concerns, and liquidity stress. However, PSU-owned NBFCs (REC, PFC, IRFC, IREDA, IFCI) have been implicitly backstopped by the sovereign, allowing them to borrow at near-sovereign rates.

**Total Addressable Market (TAM).** The Indian financial services TAM is massive (>$5 trillion in total credit), but IFCI's addressable slice is shrinking. With a loan book of ~Rs. 7,000-7,500 crore (estimated from gross advances minus NPAs) and no fresh sanctions, IFCI's "lending TAM" is essentially zero going forward[^ar-fy25]. The relevant TAM for the next 3-5 years is:
- **PLI Scheme Advisory Market**: 14 PLI schemes with total outlay of ~Rs. 2 lakh crore over 5-7 years; if IFCI is PMA for 10 of these, the cumulative advisory fee pool could be Rs. 500-1,000 crore over the scheme life (at typical 0.5-1% PMA fee on outlay).
- **Group Consolidation / SOTP Value**: The combined asset value of IFCI + SHCIL + IIDL + IFCI Factors could be 1.5-2x the standalone book value, IF synergies are realised.

**CAGR.** The traditional lending industry (NBFC + HFC) is expected to grow at 12-15% CAGR through FY28E (FY28E = our estimate for financial year ending March 2028) on a credit-penetration story. However, **IFCI itself is a negative-CAGR business on the lending side** — disbursements are zero, and the loan book is shrinking via repayments and write-offs. The "growth" thesis is entirely on the advisory and SOTP side.

**Policy Tailwinds (PLI / FAME / Infra).** Several government policy frameworks indirectly support IFCI's pivot[^ar-fy25]:
- **PLI Schemes** (14 schemes, Rs. 2 lakh crore outlay): IFCI is PMA for 10 — direct beneficiary of scheme execution.
- **FAME-II / PM E-Drive** (EV subsidy schemes): IFCI manages disbursements, creating fee income.
- **AatmaNirbhar Bharat**: Nationalist policy stance supports domestic DFI (Development Financial Institution) role.
- **ESG / BRSR Mandate**: SEBI's Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting (BRSR) requirements create advisory opportunities — IFCI has launched an ESG advisory vertical[^ar-fy25].

**Competitive Moat Analysis.** Let us rate IFCI's competitive moat (i.e., the structural advantages that protect it from competitors) on the standard three dimensions:

| Moat Dimension | Rating | Reasoning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Switching Costs** (how hard is it for a customer to switch to a competitor?) | Moderate | For PLI advisory, switching cost is high once IFCI is embedded as PMA — government would need to retender, and IFCI has institutional knowledge of scheme design. But this moat is policy-dependent and could disappear if the government decides to retender. |
| **Scale / Network** (does being big give IFCI an advantage?) | Weak | IFCI has shrunk to a sub-scale operation. With 0 fresh sanctions and minimal branch network, it has lost the scale advantage it once had as India's premier DFI. Comparable PSU NBFCs (REC, PFC) have Rs. 4-5 lakh crore+ loan books. |
| **Pricing Power** (can IFCI charge premium prices?) | Weak | As a government-mandated PMA, advisory fees are administratively set. As a lender, IFCI has zero pricing power in the current market — it cannot even raise fresh debt. |
| **Regulatory / Sovereign Moat** (does government backing create a barrier?) | Strong | The GoI's 73% ownership is a strong implicit guarantee — competitors like private NBFCs cannot match IFCI's "government counterparty" status. This is the strongest moat and the reason PSU NBFCs command premium valuations. |
| **Asset Quality Moat** (is the loan book healthy?) | None / Negative | With ~50% GNPA, IFCI has the worst asset quality in its peer set. This is not a moat — it is a structural liability. |

**Overall Moat Verdict: WEAK on operational moats, but STRONG on sovereign / regulatory moat.** The investment case hinges on whether the sovereign backstop translates into value-unlocking actions (group consolidation, NSE monetisation).

## SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure.** The promoter is the **Government of India** — a sovereign, not an individual. The "promoter pedigree" is therefore the strength of the Indian government's commitment to keeping IFCI solvent. This is mixed:
- **Positive**: GoI has infused Rs. 2,000+ crore in 7 years, demonstrating commitment to avoid systemic embarrassment.
- **Negative**: The repeated capital infusion has been **reactive (bailing out a failing entity) rather than proactive (funding growth)**. Each Rs. 500 crore infusion dilutes minority shareholders by ~2-3%.

**Management Team (Key Managerial Personnel).** The leadership has seen significant churn in the last 24 months[^ar-fy25]:
- **Manoj Mittal** (former MD & CEO): Resigned July 2024, moved to SIDBI as CMD. Served ~3 years.
- **Rahul Bhave** (current MD & CEO): Promoted from Deputy MD to MD on 21 March 2025 — has been with IFCI for several years, providing continuity.
- **Surjith Karthikeyan** (Government Director): Ceased July 2025.
- **Surendra Behera** (Government Director): Resigned August 2025.

The churn reflects typical PSU "revolving door" management — government-appointed directors typically rotate every 2-3 years. This is not unusual for PSU NBFCs but does limit long-term strategic continuity. Rahul Bhave's elevation provides some stability.

**FCF (Free Cash Flow) Deployment Track Record (3 Years).** Let me explain FCF in plain terms: Free Cash Flow is the cash a company actually generates after paying for its day-to-day operations and capital investments. A positive FCF means the company is self-funding; a negative FCF means it is burning cash.

| Year | PAT (Rs. Cr) | Net CFO (Rs. Cr) | FCF (Rs. Cr, est.) | Deployment |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | :--- |
| FY25 | 44 | -1,175 | ~-1,175 | Loan repayments, debt reduction |
| FY24 | 128 | ~-200 (est.) | ~-200 | Debt servicing |
| FY23 (Restated) | -288 | ~+50 (est.) | ~+50 | Used to absorb losses |

The standout fact: **CFO of -Rs. 1,175 crore in FY25 vs PAT of Rs. 44 crore** — IFCI's "profit" is entirely non-cash, driven by impairment reversals and fair value gains. In simple terms, the company is reporting it earned Rs. 44 crore in profit, but actually had Rs. 1,175 crore of cash leave the building (mainly to repay borrowings). This is a critical quality-of-earnings red flag[^ar-fy25].

**Capital allocation over 3 years**:
- ~Rs. 1,000 crore to GoI (preferential equity infusion, 2024-25)
- ~Rs. 1,300+ crore to debt repayment (debt-equity improved from 4.49 to 2.18)
- ~Rs. 580 crore recovered from NPAs and Security Receipts (SRs are tradable instruments issued by ARCs — Asset Reconstruction Companies — when banks sell bad loans)
- **Zero capex**, zero new lending, zero acquisitions

This is **defensive capital allocation** (paying down debt, shrinking balance sheet) — not value-creating. The only "growth" deployment is advisory vertical hiring, which is immaterial in rupee terms.

**Debt Management.** Let me explain a few key terms first:
- **Debt-Equity Ratio**: A company's total debt divided by shareholders' equity. Lower is generally safer; >2 is considered high for an NBFC.
- **Interest Coverage Ratio**: How easily a company can pay interest on its outstanding debt. Calculated as EBIT (Earnings Before Interest and Tax) divided by interest expense. Above 1.5x is considered adequate.

**Credit Ratings**: CARE Edge reaffirmed the rating on 18 August 2026 — the rating itself is low (likely 'A-' or equivalent, indicating "adequate safety" but vulnerable to economic conditions) due to negative CRAR, but the reaffirmation signals ratings stability. The low rating is a key reason IFCI cannot raise fresh market borrowings at viable rates[^ar-fy25].

**Dividend Policy.** IFCI has **not declared any dividend** in recent years (dividend yield 0.00%). With retained losses and negative CRAR, dividend payments are not feasible. There is no stated payout policy — typical for a PSU NBFC in survival mode.

**Promoter Pledging Status.** **Zero pledging** — GoI's 72.57% stake is unencumbered. There are no pledged shares, no encumbrances on any of IFCI's own shares (confirmed in June 2026 disclosure). This is a positive governance signal[^ar-fy25].

**Corporate Governance Flags.** This is where IFCI scores poorly:
- **Only 1 Independent Director** (Umesh Kumar Garg) vs SEBI LODR requirement of at least 2 (or 1/3 of board, whichever is higher). Persistent non-compliance. **Fines imposed by NSE/BSE of ~Rs. 47 crore cumulative**[^ar-fy25].
- **Audit Committee not properly constituted** (insufficient independent directors).
- **Nomination & Remuneration Committee** similarly non-compliant.
- **CAG (Comptroller and Auditor General of India) Observations**: Multiple recurring valuation errors over 4 years without corrective action — a yellow flag for accounting hygiene.
- **Auditor Opinion**: Unqualified with **7 Emphasis of Matter paragraphs** (standalone), including negative CRAR, Stage 3 (NPA) interest write-off, ECL (Expected Credit Loss — a forward-looking provisioning methodology) methodology change, and conflict-of-interest in SDF (Sustainable Development Fund) scheme. An "unqualified" opinion is the cleanest, but "Emphasis of Matter" paragraphs are like footnotes saying "things look fine, BUT pay attention to these specific concerns."
- **ECL Methodology Change**: Switched from portfolio-level to account-level provisioning in FY25, adding Rs. 291 crore of provisioning. This is technically a change in accounting estimate (per Ind AS 8), but the discretionary nature is a yellow flag.
- **Related Party Transactions (RPTs)**: Extensive — advisory fees from government ministries (~Rs. 70+ crore), interest paid to subsidiaries (Rs. 14+ crore), bond holdings in subsidiaries (Rs. 75+ crore to IIDL, etc.). All claimed "arm's length" but the concentration of government-related counterparties creates circular-flow risk[^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Partnerships.** Strategic partnerships include:
- **MoU with BSE** for SME listing support — a low-cost growth lever in the advisory vertical[^ar-fy25].
- **PLI Scheme PMAs** (10 of 14 schemes) — the most important "partnership" is effectively with the Government of India, acting as PMA.
- **Group Consolidation** (in-principle approval) — internal reorganisation, not a JV, but a strategic restructuring.

**Capital Allocation Verdict: DEFENSIVE / WIND-DOWN.** Management is not creating incremental shareholder value through traditional capital allocation (capex, acquisitions, R&D). The strategy is survival: reduce debt, recover NPAs, extract advisory fees, and await group consolidation / NSE monetisation. This is acceptable for a "special situations" play but would be a negative for a "compounder" thesis. As a minority shareholder, you are betting on the government to act — and that bet has historically paid off slowly.

**PROJECTION RATIONALE & ASSUMPTIONS (FY27E and FY28E):**
- **Revenue (-10.8% FY27E, -9.5% FY28E):** The company is in "transition mode" with no new loan sanctions since FY25 (per Board's Report). Interest income is projected to fall ~16% YoY in both years as the legacy loan book amortises and is replaced by recoveries rather than fresh disbursements. Other Income is assumed to recover modestly (Rs. 70 Cr → Rs. 85 Cr) on the back of growing PLI-scheme advisory mandates (now PMA for 10 of 14 PLI schemes) and the newly launched ESG advisory vertical, but the volatile "net gain on fair value changes" line is normalised at lower run-rate levels.
- **EBITDA Margin (101% → 88%):** The optically absurd >100% margins in FY24A-FY26A reflect the fact that PBT is propped up by non-operating items — ECL write-backs (Rs. 291 Cr methodology change in FY25), fair value gains on investments, and recovery income from security receipts — that are not captured in the "Total Income" line. As these one-offs exhaust, the apparent margin compresses sharply, even though the underlying franchise is broadly stable.
- **PBT (Rs. 337 Cr → Rs. 245 Cr):** We model a 36% / 27% YoY decline as the one-time gains (Stage-3 reversals, fair value uplifts on subsidiary investments) that boosted FY24A-FY26A roll off. Lower interest expense (debt paydown) provides partial offset.
- **Tax Rate (normalised at 25%):** The historical 17%-68% volatility reflects deferred tax asset adjustments and one-time items. We standardise to 25% (effective corporate rate) for forecasting.
- **PAT (Rs. 253 Cr → Rs. 184 Cr):** Decline mirrors PBT compression.
- **EPS (Rs. 0.36 → Rs. 0.25):** Assumes further equity infusion of ~Rs. 350-500 Cr from GoI (consistent with the Rs. 500 Cr + Rs. 500 Cr pattern seen in FY24 and FY25), expanding the diluted share count from ~649 Cr to ~700 Cr (FY27E) and ~750 Cr (FY28E).
- **Dividend Payout (0%):** Maintained at zero; CRAR remains negative and dividend distribution is incompatible with capital conservation needs.

**Commentary on Income Statement:**
- **Revenue trajectory:** Revenue actually *grew* 2.3% in FY25A (Rs. 701 Cr → Rs. 717 Cr) on the back of higher Other Income (advisory ramp-up, dividend receipts from subsidiaries). However, FY26A saw a sharp 34% collapse to Rs. 471 Cr, driven by the wind-down of interest-earning assets as legacy loans are repaid or resolved, and a dramatic drop in Other Income (Rs. 182 Cr → Rs. 55 Cr) as the prior year's one-time fair value gains did not repeat.
- **Margin optics vs reality:** EBITDA margin appears to *expand* in FY26A (116% → 130%), but this is illusory. EBITDA is computed here as PBT + Depreciation (the most defensible bottom-up build from the available data), and the resulting >100% margin simply tells us PBT > Total Income — a mathematical impossibility for a normal operating company. For IFCI, this gap is filled by (i) write-backs of excess provisions, (ii) fair value gains on equity investments in subsidiaries, and (iii) gains on sale of security receipts. The "quality" of this profit is poor (see Section 7).
- **Profit growth is engineered, not earned:** PAT grew 45% in FY25A and 25% in FY26A, even as revenue collapsed. This is a classic sign of "release of reserves" earnings — provisions made in earlier years (when the ECL was calculated on a portfolio basis) are now being reversed (after the methodology shifted to account-level basis, which released Rs. 291 Cr). Such profits do not recur.
- **Forward earnings trajectory:** FY27E and FY28E mark the start of the "normalised" earnings regime where PAT declines to Rs. 184-253 Cr as one-time gains exhaust and the smaller loan book generates less interest income. EPS of Rs. 0.25-0.36 puts the stock at a forward P/E of 270-390x at CMP — clearly, valuation cannot be on earnings. The P/B of 2.91x (per Part 1) is the only meaningful multiple, which is why the Rs. 110 target is anchored on book value, not earnings.
- **No dividend:** The company has not paid dividends (Div Yield 0%) and is unlikely to in the forecast period given the negative CRAR and ongoing capital requirements.

### SECTION 8 — VALUATION

**Valuation Philosophy:** IFCI is a stressed PSU NBFC in transition — conventional P/E is distorted (TTM 148.97x is mathematically meaningless on Rs. 0.66 TTM EPS), and earnings visibility is poor. We anchor valuation on **P/B (primary)** because book value captures residual claim on the loan book, investment portfolio, and advisory intangibles, and is the metric the market is implicitly trading on (current P/B 2.91x). **P/E (secondary)** captures any inflection in profitability. **EV/EBITDA** is used as a cross-check using pre-provision operating profit (PPI proxy) since conventional EBITDA is distorted by ECL provisions [^ar-fy25].

**Sector Methodology Justification:** Finance/PSU-Lending peers (PFC, REC, IRFC, IREDA, PFC) trade on P/B because the balance sheet IS the business — leverage and asset quality determine returns. P/E is secondary because earnings are lumpy (ECL swings). For IFCI specifically, P/B is doubly important because future earnings depend on (a) NPA recovery flows and (b) advisory scaling, both of which eventually accrete to book value [^ar-fy25].
