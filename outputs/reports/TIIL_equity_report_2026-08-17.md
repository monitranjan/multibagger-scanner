### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Dominant Global Franchise in Drum Closures:** TIIL is one of the world's largest manufacturers of steel drum closures (flanges, rings, caps) with an estimated 30–35% global market share, supplying to all top-5 global industrial packaging majors [^ip-latest]. The closure business alone contributes ~45% of consolidated revenue and operates at 25%+ EBITDA margins — significantly above the group blended margin of ~18% — making it the cash engine funding diversification [^ar-fy25].

- **Structural Margin Story Through Product Mix Shift:** Management has guided to a 200–250 bps EBITDA margin expansion over FY26–FY28E driven by (i) ramp-up of higher-margin scaffolding systems (post-EU/US approvals), (ii) share gain in higher-value engineering services for overseas EPC clients, and (iii) declining share of low-margin cotton yarn [^cc-transcript]. Consolidated EBITDA margin expanded from 16.4% in FY23 to 18.7% in FY25 and we model 21.0% by FY28E [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification Into High-Growth Adjacencies — Defense & Scaffolding:** The Board has approved a foray into defense manufacturing (components for ordnance factories and DPSUs), leveraging existing CNC/machining capabilities [^ip-latest]. Simultaneously, the scaffolding division (now ~12% of revenue) has secured EN 12810/12811 European certification and OSHA US approvals, unlocking addressable markets estimated at $8–10 Bn globally [^vp-thread]. The Q1 FY27 concall indicated the order book in scaffolding is at an all-time high [^cc-transcript].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** (i) Defense order win announcement expected in H2 FY27 (mgmt indicated 3-4 active proposals with MoD/DPSUs) [^cc-transcript]; (ii) Scaffolding revenue doubling from Rs. 175 Cr in FY25 to Rs. 350–400 Cr in FY27E; (iii) Potential value-unlocking event via demerger/spin-off of drum closures or yarn businesses — a long-standing ValuePickr thesis that has gained traction as the business mix matures [^vp-thread]; (iv) Q1 FY27 results showed 22% YoY revenue growth, indicating demand momentum has carried into FY27 [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest Structural Risk — Cotton Yarn Drag & FX Volatility:** The legacy cotton yarn division (5–7% of revenue) remains structurally low-margin (3–5% EBITDA) and absorbs working capital [^vp-thread]. Any delay in its divestment (a multi-year community ask) will continue to dilute consolidated ROCE. Additionally, ~65% of revenue is USD/EUR-denominated exports, exposing earnings to rupee appreciation — a 5% INR appreciation can compress EBITDA by ~150 bps based on sensitivity disclosed in the Annual Report [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Technocraft Industries operates a four-pillar diversified engineering business built around (a) industrial packaging components, (b) engineered scaffolding, (c) cotton yarn/textiles, and (d) engineering services for global EPC clients. The company follows a B2B contract-manufacturing model with 65%+ revenue from exports to 60+ countries [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue Mix by Division (FY25 Consolidated, Estimated):**

| Division | Est. % of Revenue | Key End-Products | Key Customers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Drum Closures & Packaging | ~45% | Steel drum flanges, rings, caps, gaskets | Greif, Mauser, Schutz, Balmer Lawrie |
| Scaffolding Systems | ~12% | Cuplock, Ringlock, Frames, Props | Layher (EU), BrandSafway, AT-PAC |
| Engineering Services & Pipes | ~28% | Precision tubes, fabricated structures | EPC majors in EU, US, Middle East |
| Cotton Yarn | ~10% | Combed cotton yarn, fabric | Domestic textile players, exporters |
| Defense (Emerging) | <2% | Components for ordnance | DPSUs / MoD (in qualification) |

*Source: Investor Presentation Q1 FY27 [^ip-latest], Annual Report FY25 [^ar-fy25]*

**Manufacturing Footprint:** Operates 9 manufacturing facilities — concentrated in Maharashtra (Murbad, Tarapur, Ambernath — 6 plants) and Dadra & Nagar Haveli (2 plants), with 1 recently commissioned unit for scaffolding expansion [^ip-latest]. Overseas trading/service subsidiaries exist in Germany, USA, UK, and Australia for last-mile customer engagement [^ar-fy25].

**Subsidiary Structure:** TIIL consolidates 12 subsidiaries, including wholly-owned overseas marketing arms (Technocraft International, UK; Technocraft GmbH, Germany; Technocraft Inc, USA) and step-downs in Australia and Canada [^ar-fy25]. The Indian subsidiaries include Technocraft Tubular Solutions (engineering) and Bright International (scaffolding marketing). This structure enables direct customer access in regulated export markets.

**Promoter Background & Group:** Promoted by the **Saraf family**, led by **Sharad M. Saraf** (Chairman & MD) and **Ashish M. Saraf** (Jt. MD), the company traces its origins to 1972 as a textile trader before pivoting to industrial packaging in the 1980s and engineered products from 2003 onwards [^vp-thread]. The promoter family has held a controlling stake of 77.65% for over two decades and has a track record of growing consolidated revenue from Rs. 800 Cr in FY13 to ~Rs. 2,600 Cr in FY25 — a 3.25x expansion in 12 years [^ar-fy25]. The group also operates a separate listed entity historically (now largely absorbed) and has an active JV in defense (announced FY26) [^cc-transcript].

- **Global Scaffolding Market:** TAM of $55–60 Bn (2026), CAGR 5.8% led by infrastructure capex in Asia, Middle East, and US energy/commercial construction [^vp-thread]. India is the fastest-growing geography at 9–10% CAGR.
