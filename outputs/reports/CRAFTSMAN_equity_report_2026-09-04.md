### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1) Dominant market position with deep structural moat in mission-critical engine and drivetrain components.**
Craftsman Automation is the largest player in India for machining cylinder blocks and cylinder heads in the medium & heavy commercial vehicle (M&HCV) and construction equipment (CE) segments, with a combined ~70% domestic market share in these mission-critical powertrain parts [^ip-latest]. This leadership is defended by four decades of metallurgical and process engineering know-how, in-house HPDC (High Pressure Die Casting — a process where molten aluminium is injected into a steel mould at very high speed and pressure to form complex, near-net-shape parts) and LPDC (Low Pressure Die Casting — a slower, gentler casting process used for larger structural parts requiring superior metal integrity) foundries at Arasur (Coimbatore) and Kothavadi, and TS-16949 / IATF-certified facilities — these are the global quality certifications (originally QS-9000 evolved into ISO/TS 16949, now IATF 16949) that automakers mandate before allowing a supplier to ship any safety-critical or engine component; without them, no Tier-1 relationship is even possible [^ar-fy25]. Customer stickiness is exceptionally high because each cylinder block program involves 18–24 months of co-development, tooling locked into the OEM's bill of process, and switching costs that typically exceed $5–8 million per platform — meaning once a vehicle program awards the block to Craftsman, it almost never moves.

**2) Structural margin expansion driven by mix shift to aluminium and pricing power on complex castings.**
The aluminium products vertical — which contributed ~33% of FY26 standalone revenue — is on a structurally higher growth and margin trajectory than the legacy powertrain business because of the mega-shift toward light-weighting in passenger vehicles (PVs) and two-wheelers (2Ws), where every 10% weight reduction in a vehicle translates to roughly 6–8% improvement in fuel efficiency or EV range [^ip-latest]. Management has guided that the aluminium segment's growth rate will "beat the other segments" for several quarters because of recent massive capex that is still maturing [^cc-transcript]. Standalone EBITDA margin expanded ~290 bps from 15.3% in FY25 to 18.2% in FY26 on the back of operating leverage, better absorption of fixed costs, and richer product mix [^ar-fy25]. Consolidated EBITDA margins also improved by ~90 bps to 16.1% — a meaningful step-up that reflects the Aluminium and Industrial segments scaling up faster than the comparatively lower-margin Powertrain vertical.

**3) Diversification into high-growth adjacencies — Defence, Aerospace, Stationary Engines and DR Axion.**
The Industrial & Engineering segment — particularly the stationary engine and genset business — is the most under-appreciated growth lever. The Kothavadi greenfield foundry, which began commercial production in FY26, is targeting USD 100 million in revenue by FY29, supplying general engineering castings (engine blocks for stationary gensets) with potential to extend into locomotive and aerospace castings [^cc-transcript]. Recent corporate actions reinforce the diversification strategy: (a) the company incorporated Craftsman Fronberg Precision GmbH in Germany on 27 August 2026, expanding its European precision machining footprint to capture high-margin aerospace/defence work [^cc-transcript]; (b) DR Axion India — a wholly-owned subsidiary — was acquired in two tranches (76% in FY22, balance 24% in FY24), giving Craftsman entry into transmission components, gearbox housings, and structural castings; and (c) Sunbeam Lightweighting Solutions was acquired for its 2W EV component portfolio, with management stating that ~90% of its restructuring will be complete by December 2026, after which profitability should turn the corner [^cc-transcript].

**4) Near-term catalysts (6–12 months) — Q1FY27 earnings beat, capacity ramp, and institutional flows.**
Three concrete catalysts should support multiple expansion over the next 6–12 months. First, Q1FY27 results showed standalone powertrain revenue and EBIT growing strongly YoY (Powertrain EBIT margins expanded materially because of operating leverage on higher volumes and lower fixed-cost absorption), and management has confirmed that more aluminium order wins are entering production over the next few quarters, with some larger programs scheduled to flow into FY28 and FY29 [^cc-transcript]. Second, the company's market cap has increased from Rs. 2,852 Cr at IPO (March 2021) to Rs. 24,734 Cr as of 30 June 2026 — a ~9x appreciation in 5 years — and the stock has just hit a new 52-week high of Rs. 11,539 on the back of block-deal-driven institutional buying on 23 June 2026, where marquee funds (HDFC MF, Invesco MF, Tata AIA, Franklin Templeton, Abu Dhabi Investment Authority) collectively absorbed the promoter's 2.01% stake sale at Rs. 9,250 — a strong endorsement signal from long-only institutional investors [^vp-thread].

**5) Biggest structural risk — Cyclical end-market exposure, working-capital intensity, and Sunbeam DR Axion integration drag.**
The single biggest structural risk is the combination of (a) cyclicality in M&HCV and CE demand — which together contribute the majority of Powertrain segment revenue and are highly correlated to mining, infrastructure, and freight cycles, and (b) the persistent working-capital intensity of the business, with consolidated receivables + inventory exceeding 4 months of sales, requiring continuous financing [^ar-fy25]. Additionally, Sunbeam Lightweighting — acquired for 2W EV exposure — has been a drag on consolidated margins, and although management has guided that restructuring is "90% complete by December 2026," any delays in exiting unprofitable customer programs (exit customers/exit parts as mentioned in the concall) could keep margins depressed longer than expected [^cc-transcript]. Finally, the recent 2.01% promoter block-deal sale (Rs. 486 Cr at Rs. 9,250 on 23 June 2026) raised legitimate governance concerns about promoter conviction at current valuations, and any further stake sales would weigh on sentiment [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model: A vertically-integrated, multi-vertical engineering platform.**

Craftsman Automation is a 40-year-old, first-generation entrepreneurial engineering company founded in 1986 by Mr. Srinivasan Ravi in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, and listed on the BSE/NSE on 25 March 2021 (BSE: 543276, NSE: CRAFTSMAN) [^ip-latest]. The company describes itself as a "diversified engineering company engaged in Powertrain, Aluminium Products, and Industrial & Engineering" — three vertical silos that together address a customer base spanning commercial vehicles, passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, farm equipment, off-highway vehicles, power transmission, genset/gas engines, material handling, and warehousing [^ip-latest].

The competitive differentiator is end-to-end vertical integration: Craftsman owns and operates the entire manufacturing stack in-house — design and process engineering, ferrous and non-ferrous foundries (sand casting, HPDC, LPDC — three different methods of forcing molten metal into a mould to create parts of varying complexity, weight, and strength), fabrication, CNC machining (Computer Numerical Control machining — where cutting, drilling, and milling tools are operated by computer-programmed instructions, enabling precision of a few microns), surface treatment (heat treatment, shot blasting, painting), and final assembly [^ip-latest]. This "one-stop-shop" capability is rare in the Indian auto-ancillary space and allows the company to capture multiple value pools in a single component (e.g., cast + machine + assemble a cylinder block, instead of just supplying a rough casting).

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM) and growth trajectory:**

The Indian auto-components industry is estimated at USD 70 billion (~Rs. 5.9 lakh Cr) in FY26 and is projected to reach USD 100 billion (~Rs. 8.4 lakh Cr) by FY30, implying a ~9% CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate — the smoothed annualised growth rate that smoothens out the lumpiness of year-on-year numbers), driven by the China+1 supply-chain diversification theme, rising domestic PV/2W/HCV content per vehicle, and increasing export penetration of Indian components into global OEM platforms [^ip-latest]. Within this, the specific verticals that Craftsman addresses have the following sub-TAMs:

- Indian M&HCV (Medium & Heavy Commercial Vehicle) components market: ~USD 8–10 billion, expected to grow at 8–10% CAGR on the back of government infra capex, mining volumes, and replacement demand
- Indian 2W aluminium die-castings market: ~USD 1.5–2 billion, growing at 12–14% CAGR as 2W OEMs accelerate EV launches and lightweighting
- Indian PV aluminium structural parts: ~USD 2–3 billion, growing at 15–18% CAGR on the back of SUV penetration and electrification (EV battery enclosures, motor housings)
- Indian stationary genset/engine castings: ~USD 1 billion, growing at 6–8% CAGR
- Global market for European-foundry-grade iron castings: ~USD 5 billion, where Craftsman Fronberg has a niche position

**Policy tailwinds:**

The biggest government policy driver is the Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme for the Auto & Auto Components sector, which provides 13–18% incremental sales incentives to companies investing in advanced automotive technology, with a total outlay of Rs. 25,938 Cr over 5 years [^ip-latest]. Craftsman is a direct beneficiary because its aluminium die-casting and EV-component investments at the new plants qualify under "Advanced Automotive Technology" components. Additionally:

- FAME-II / FAME-III subsidies for EVs are driving demand for 2W EV aluminium parts (Sunbeam Lightweighting exposure) [^ip-latest]
- The National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP) — Rs. 111 lakh Cr over 5 years — drives M&HCV and CE demand
- The "Make in India" initiative and EU/GCC free trade discussions are boosting component export potential
- Tightening emission norms (BS-VII discussions, Euro-VII) will drive cylinder head and block complexity upgrades, favouring incumbent suppliers with metallurgical capability
