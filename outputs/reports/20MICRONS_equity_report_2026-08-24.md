### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** 20 Microns is India’s largest producer of micronized minerals (meaning naturally occurring rocks such as limestone, kaolin clay, silica that are ground to micron-scale fine powder used as fillers or performance enhancers in paints, plastics, rubber etc.) [^ip-latest]. The company estimates its share at 10–30% depending on specific product category, with a broad portfolio spanning Ground Calcium Carbonate, Kaolin, Talc, and Functional Additives [^cc-transcript]. The moat rests on scale of multi-state manufacturing and backward integration into Malaysian mines; however switching costs for customers are moderate because while formulations are tailored, alternative suppliers exist [^vp-thread]. This leadership gives volume pricing leverage on raw stone but not absolute monopoly.

- **Structural margin story:** The firm reported EBITDA margin (EBITDA = earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, amortisation – a proxy for operating profitability before financing and accounting charges) of 12.9% in FY26 which expanded to 13.2% in Q1 FY27 (a 107 bps quarter-on-quarter and 40 bps year-on-year improvement, where 1 bps = 0.01 percentage point) [^ip-latest]. Management guides 200–250 bps of EBITDA margin expansion over the medium term driven by favourable product mix (higher specialty functional additives like sub-micron CaCO₃, fumed silica), better raw material utilisation, and scale efficiencies from the ₹100 Cr capex [^cc-transcript]. In plain terms, as the share of high-value engineered powders rises, each rupee of sales yields more profit because these are differentiated and less commodity-like [^ip-latest].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Historically dependent on paints (46% of FY26 revenue), the company is deliberately expanding into polymers (26%), rubber (10%), adhesives, inks, and construction chemicals via JVs with Sievert (tile adhesives under 20MCC brand) and Dorfner (coloured quartz) [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. It also entered mineral-based agri inputs through Minfert (Humicrons, Geo Care) and acquired Malaysian limestone entities GTLQ and IQ Marbles to secure raw material [^ip-latest]. This reduces concentration risk and taps end-markets growing faster than paints, creating cross-cyclical stability [^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Commissioning of the new Malaysian processing plant within ~12 months of mine start, adding 1.08 lakh MT capacity by mid-FY28 [^cc-transcript]; (ii) Phase 2 of Sievert JV going live in coming months, contributing to B2B/B2C construction chemical revenues [^cc-transcript]; (iii) Final dividend of ₹1.25/share for FY26 (face value ₹5, i.e., 25% payout) providing shareholder return signal [^ar-fy25]; (iv) Recent acquisition of partnership interest in Shree Shrest Minerals announced Aug 2026 furthering backward integration [^ip-latest]. These should drive earnings visibility and re-rating potential.

- **Biggest structural risk:** Customer concentration and commodity pricing. Community-sourced data suggests top 4 clients (e.g., Asian Paints, Nerolac, Pidilite, ITC) may constitute ~60% of revenue, and key products like CaCO₃, dolomite, silica are non-rare commodities with limited pricing power [^vp-thread]. Additionally, geopolitical freight/fuel spikes can raise costs that are only partially passed through with lag, compressing margins if demand weakens [^cc-transcript]. This structural reliance on cyclical paint demand (flat in FY26) is the key downside [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

20 Microns operates a business model of extracting and processing industrial minerals into fine powders and specialty additives. To explain simply: they take bulky raw stones (like limestone, kaolin clay, talc) and grind them in mills to micro-sized particles (micronized means reduced to microns, i.e., thousandth of a millimetre). These powders are then sold as fillers or performance enhancers to factories that make paints, plastics, rubber, paper, ceramics. The value addition comes from consistent particle size, purity, and custom surface treatments [^ip-latest].

Revenue split by division/application for FY26 is as follows: Paints 46% (≈₹438.8 Cr), Polymers 26% (≈₹248.0 Cr), Rubber 10% (≈₹95.4 Cr), Ceramics 5% (≈₹47.7 Cr), Paper 4% (≈₹38.2 Cr), Others 9% (≈₹85.8 Cr) [^ip-latest]. Geographically, domestic India contributes 86% and exports 14% [^ip-latest]. Key OEM/end customers per division are not officially disclosed in filings, but investor community indicates major paint companies like Asian Paints, Nerolac, and adhesive/materials firms like Pidilite and ITC are significant buyers [^vp-thread]. The company structure includes subsidiaries: 20 Microns Nano (functional additives), Malaysia/FZE/Sharjah trading entities, MCC (construction chemicals), Minfert (agri), IQ Marble/GTLQ (limestone in Malaysia), Vietnam CaCO3 subsidiary, and JVs with Sievert and Dorfner [^ip-latest].

Manufacturing footprint: New plants added at Vadadala (18k TPA – tonnes per annum), Hosur (15k TPA kaolin), Alwar (6k TPA), Tirunelveli (6k TPA) complement existing facilities across Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Rajasthan etc [^ip-latest]. The promoter group is led by Atil Parikh (CEO & MD) with decades in mineral processing; the group has transitioned from a pure industrial mineral player to a specialty materials platform [^cc-transcript]. The recent acquisition of partnership in Shree Shrest Minerals (Aug 2026) extends quarrying access [^ip-latest].
