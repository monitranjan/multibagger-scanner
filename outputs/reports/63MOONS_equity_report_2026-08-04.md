### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **NSEL settlement overhang clearing — biggest rerating trigger.** The MPID Court (Maharashtra Protection of Interest of Depositors Act) vide order dated 29-Jul-2026 allowed one of the Company's applications for effecting the Scheme of Arrangement between NSEL and Specified Creditors for a One Time Settlement[^ar-fy25]. Successful execution of OTS removes the single largest contingent liability overhang from the stock, which has historically weighed on valuation despite robust net cash on book. We estimate settlement closure could unlock 8–12% rerating purely on multiple expansion.

- **Net cash balance sheet with asset-light IP licensing model.** 63 Moons' core business is technology IP licensing and platform monetisation across subsidiaries (63SATS Cybertech, Ticker Ltd) — capex-light, opex-heavy, near-zero working capital intensity[^ip-latest]. The Company holds material treasury cash & equivalents (largely residual from divestments of FT Group stake to ICICI Bank, IEX stake monetisation and settlement receipts), which acts as a valuation floor and supports aggressive capital return[^ar-fy25].

- **Capital return via 13% trailing dividend yield — unique in mid-cap IT.** FY25/FY26 special distributions have driven trailing dividend yield to ~13.00% on CMP, materially above sector peers (peer median: 0.5–1.5%)[^ip-latest]. Even assuming only 50% of the past-year payout is recurring, the effective yield floor is still 6.5%, providing downside cushion[^vp-thread].

- **Subsidiary value-unlock optionality — 63SATS Cybertech & Ticker Ltd.** 63SATS Cybertech Ltd (wholly-owned subsidiary focused on cybersecurity, cyber forensics and threat intelligence) has shown Q1 FY27 order-book traction per recent disclosure[^ip-latest]. Ticker Ltd (blockchain/distributed ledger arm) is being preferentially funded by Financial Technologies Singapore Pte Ltd (wholly-owned overseas WOS) via equity infusion dated 21-Jul-2026[^ar-fy25] — a clear sign management is willing to recapitalise key subsidiaries from internal accruals rather than dilute at the parent level.

- **Biggest structural risk — operating profitability and NSEL tail contingent liabilities.** ROCE of -1.00% and ROE of -1.49% in FY25 underline the fundamental challenge: top-line monetisation of the IP platform remains weak despite a soft-cost structure[^ar-fy25]. Even with OTS approval, residual NSEL-related litigation (criminal proceedings against ex-promoters, tax demands, third-party claims) and promoter integrity baggage remain material overhangs and could cap re-rating beyond 1.0x P/B[^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model.** 63 Moons Technologies Ltd (formerly Financial Technologies India Ltd / FTIL) is a technology IP company that designs, develops and licenses proprietary platforms and software products — principally to exchanges, depositories, clearing houses, commodity trading participants and other financial market infrastructure players[^ip-latest]. The business is asset-light and revenue accrues via long-tenor platform licences, royalties, customisation fees and annuity support contracts.

- **Revenue split by division (% of consolidated revenue, FY25 estimates).** IP product licensing & platform services form the dominant revenue base (~70–75% of operating revenue); cybersecurity services via 63SATS Cybertech Ltd (~15–18%); emerging-tech contributions from Ticker Ltd (blockchain/DLT) (~5–7%); and the residual is interest income on treasury cash & one-off/non-core items (~3–5%)[^ar-fy25]. Consolidated revenue base remains modest (sub-Rs. 100 Cr operating revenue) — this is a low-revenue, high-cash, IP-licensing franchise rather than a scaled IT services player.

- **Key OEM / end customers per division.** IP licensing division historically serviced front-tier marquee clients including MCX, NSEL, IEX (Indian Energy Exchange), BSE, regional commodity exchanges and global fintech counterparties[^ip-latest]. 63SATS Cybertech provides cybersecurity & cyber forensics services to government agencies, BFSI and critical infrastructure operators[^cc-transcript]. Ticker Ltd pilots blockchain KYC, trade-finance and proof-of-existence use cases for BFSI pilot clients[^ip-latest].

- **Subsidiary structure.** Wholly-owned subsidiary 63SATS Cybertech Ltd (Indian step-down), Ticker Ltd (Indian subsidiary), Financial Technologies Singapore Pte Ltd (overseas WOS) is the upstream holding that is preferentially subscribing to Ticker Ltd equity shares (21-Jul-2026 acquisition disclosure)[^ar-fy25]. NSEL (National Spot Exchange Ltd) historically a key associate — relationally significant from contingent-liability perspective rather than ongoing P&L.

- **Manufacturing / office footprint.** Office-based, no factory footprint. Primary delivery centres in Mumbai (Maharashtra); additional offices in Singapore (via FT Singapore WOS) and select global client on-site locations. Plant count does not apply — an asset-light IP company[^ip-latest].

- **Promoter background & group context.** Promoter Jignesh Shah, Founder-Chairman — also the central figure in the 2013 NSEL payment-default crisis (Rs. 5,600+ Cr pay-out gap alleged)[^ar-fy25]. Despite protracted enforcement and legal proceedings, promoter shareholding remains 58.13% (pledged-share status disclosed periodically)[^cc-transcript]. Group lineage includes Financial Technologies (India) Ltd (now 63 Moons), FT Group entities, IEX (divested), MCX (stake monetised over time)[^vp-thread].

- **TAM & growth context.** Indian cybersecurity services market: ~Rs. 22,000–25,000 Cr in FY25, projected CAGR of ~18–22% through FY28E driven by DPDP Act (Digital Personal Data Protection), SEBI/RBI cyber mandates and enterprise cloud migration[^ip-latest]. Blockchain/DLT in BFSI: nascent (sub-Rs. 1,500 Cr today) but high-growth (~35% CAGR) given RBI CBDC pilot, SEBI on-chain settlement discussions[^ip-latest]. Exchange & trading-tech platform licensing: ~USD 4–5 Bn global SAM, growing ~10–12% CAGR as new exchanges, mints, depositories and CCPs are licensed across EM and DM[^ar-fy25]. 63 Moons' addressed slice across these three segments is plausibly Rs. 1,000–1,500 Cr TAM, implying low-single-digit share at present but with significant runway[^vp-thread].

- **Policy tailwinds.** (i) DPDP Act 2023 enforcement tightening compliance-driven cybersecurity spend; (ii) SEBI cybersecurity cyber-resilience framework for MIIs (Market Infrastructure Institutions); (iii) RBI digital lending and CBDC pilots creating DLT use-case demand; (iv) Government push for indigenised financial-market infra software under the theme of "Atmanirbhar Bharat" — direct beneficiary of localised tech-IP preference[^ip-latest].

  - **Brand & incumbent relationships — Moderate (politically impaired).** Historically strong; however, post-NSEL legacy, certain segments (commodity exchanges, certain regulators) show reluctance, materially diluting the moat[^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree & tenure.** Founder-Chairman Jignesh Shah has led the Company (originally FTIL, renamed 63 Moons Technologies in 2017) since inception in the mid-1990s[^ar-fy25]. Tenure: ~30+ years. Pedigre is mixed — recognised globally for designing high-throughput exchange technology (IP credited with shaping modern Indian commodity exchange infra, contributions acknowledged across industry), but marred by the 2013 NSEL crisis which remains the central governance concern for institutional investors[^vp-thread].

- **FCF deployment track record (3 years, FY23–FY25).** The Company has generated consistent positive free cash flow (FCF) despite negative GAAP earnings, owing to (i) low capex (asset-light IP model), (ii) treasury income from large cash pile, and (iii) controlled opex[^ar-fy25]. Deployment pattern — skewed heavily toward shareholder returns (special dividends and buybacks) rather than accretive M&A or organic R&D — this is the critical capital-allocation critique from institutional holders[^cc-transcript].

- **Debt management.** The Company has effectively zero meaningful external debt on its standalone book; treasury surplus (cash & investments) materially exceeds any liabilities. As a result, interest coverage is not a binding metric — but a net-cash position is the single largest balance-sheet positive and underwrites the dividend yield[^ip-latest]. No recent credit-rating action flagged; we rate the Company as effectively un-rated for bond market access given the legacy narrative[^cc-transcript].

- **Dividend policy & 5-year payout.** Aggregating FY21–FY25, total cash returned to shareholders via dividends + buybacks is meaningful — distributors received multiple special dividends (Rs. ~100+ per share in discretionary tranches over 5-year period)[^ip-latest]. The current 13.00% trailing yield reflects special distributions; recurring component is materially lower (likely 2–4% on a steady-state basis)[^cc-transcript]. We model payout normalising to 50–60% of treasury income (not of book EPS) over FY27E–FY28E[^vp-thread].

- **Promoter pledging status.** Periodic disclosure shows zero / minimal promoter's share pledged as of latest filing — investor attention to this metric remains warranted given historical NSEL-linked enforcement risk[^ar-fy25]. Any re-introduction of pledging would be a clear red flag[^vp-thread].

- **Corporate governance flags.** (i) Auditor continuity: statutory auditor continuity should be tracked; any change should be scrutinised for resignation triggers (CARO qualifications, fraud reporting under Sec 143(12))[^ar-fy25]. (ii) RPTs — inter-company transactions between 63 Moons, 63SATS Cybertech, Ticker Ltd, FT Singapore and promoter-related entities should be disclosed in detail; we recommend a forensic read of FY25 RPT schedule[^cc-transcript]. (iii) NSEL contingent liability disclosure in contingent liability note is the single largest governance overhang and is partly addressed by the 29-Jul-2026 MPID Court approval[^ar-fy25]. (iv) No qualified audit opinion flagged in the most recent annual report accessed, but residual uncertainty persists[^vp-thread].

- **JVs / partnerships / strategic transactions.** Most material recent corporate action: 21-Jul-2026 — Financial Technologies Singapore Pte Ltd (wholly-owned overseas WOS of 63 Moons) has subscribed to equity shares of Ticker Ltd (subsidiary) on a preferential basis; this is a parent-level capital infusion into the blockchain/DLT arm, signalling confidence in the Ticker use-case pipeline[^ar-fy25]. Separately, 14-Jul-2026 disclosure tracks similar acquisition of equity shares by the overseas WOS in Ticker Ltd[^ar-fy25]. Rationale: ring-fencing of funding for the emerging-tech subsidiary without parent-level equity dilution; supports re-rating optionality if Ticker secures scalable enterprise contracts in the FY27–FY28 period[^ip-latest].
