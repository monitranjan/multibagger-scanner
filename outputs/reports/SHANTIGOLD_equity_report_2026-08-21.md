### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Shanti Gold International Ltd has cemented itself as a top-tier pure-play manufacturer of 22-karat (22kt refers to gold purity where 22 parts out of 24 are pure gold, making it 91.6% gold mixed with hardening alloys; this is the standard for Indian bridal jewellery) CZ (Cubic Zirconia, a lab-created crystalline material that mimics the sparkle of diamonds at a fraction of the cost) casting gold jewellery. The company’s durable moat is not built on physical assets alone, but on "design velocity"—it employs 77 in-house CAD (Computer-Aided Design, a software tool allowing jewellers to draft intricate 3D models before physical casting) designers who generate over 400 new SKUs (Stock Keeping Units, or distinct product designs) every month [^ip-latest]. This rapid iteration capability, paired with trust-based relationships with large organized retailers such as Joyalukkas, Lalithaa, Alukkas, Vysyaraju, and Shree Kalptaru, creates a formidable barrier to entry for smaller unorganized players [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin story:** The company demonstrated a clear structural margin expansion in FY26, with EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization—a proxy for core operating profitability before financing and accounting non-cash charges) margin expanding by 173 bps (basis points; 1 bps equals 0.01%, so 173 bps is 1.73 percentage points) to 9.9% [^ip-latest]. This was driven by a deliberate mix-shift toward value-added categories. During the Q1 FY27 concall, management confirmed that designer and Turkish jewellery lines now constitute roughly 75% of the cast-studded revenue mix, lifting realization per gram [^cc-transcript]. Importantly, the Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 9.97% included a one-time 2%–2.5% boost from an accounting shift to WAC (Weighted Average Cost, a method of valuing inventory by averaging purchase costs over time), but the underlying sustainable run-rate is guided at 7.5%–8%, with a long-term trajectory to ~10% as the Marol facility achieves full operating leverage [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Beyond its legacy CZ casting division, Shanti Gold is executing a multi-pronged adjacency strategy. It has launched machine-made plain gold jewellery (automated production of high-volume items like chains and bangles using CNC looms, which reduces manual labor and increases throughput) at its upcoming Jaipur facility, targeting LOIs (Letters of Intent, non-binding commitments from buyers) already secured [^ip-latest]. It has also entered the traditional wedding demographic via Mangalsutras (sacred beaded bridal necklaces) and the urban contemporary segment via Cuban Bracelets (chunky interlocking link designs) [^ip-latest]. Furthermore, the 2025 establishment of a Dubai wholly-owned subsidiary (Shanti Gold Jewellery Trading L.L.C.) provides a compliant gateway to the USA and UAE export corridors, which currently sit at a mere 4% of revenue but offer massive white-space given the global Indian diaspora demand [^ip-latest].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The most immediate catalyst is the ramp-up of the Marol, Mumbai facility (commissioned June 2026, adding 4,000 kg per annum capacity), where the existing Mumbai lines are already operating at ~75% utilization [^cc-transcript]. The Jaipur facility (1,200 kg/pa, Capex of ₹47 cr) is set to go live mid-Nov/Dec 2026, expanding the geographic footprint into North India (Mahindra World City) to serve underpenetrated markets [^cc-transcript]. Additionally, the board-approved rights issue (a proportional capital raise from existing shareholders via 4.64 million new shares aggregating ₹100 cr) will inject equity to fund the working capital engine required to support a guided FY27 revenue of ~₹3,500 cr (implying 50%–60% value growth) [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The primary structural risk lies in the company’s intentional negative cash conversion cycle. Because Shanti Gold operates a "ready-stock" model (keeping finished goods inventory on hand rather than manufacturing strictly on order-to-order basis), its Cash Flow from Operations (CFO—the actual cash generated by core business activities) was negative ₹153 million in FY25 despite a reported PAT (Profit After Tax) of ₹558 million [^ar-fy25]. Trade receivables (money owed by retailer clients) ballooned 2.3x to ₹1,816 million with zero impairment provision (setting aside cash for likely bad debts), which is aggressive given the scale shift [^ar-fy25]. Retail investor forums further track governance friction, including a SEBI SCORES complaint filed over management unresponsiveness to related-party disclosure queries [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Shanti Gold International Ltd operates fundamentally as a B2B (Business-to-Business) contract manufacturer in the gold jewellery value chain. To explain in simple terms: they are the invisible factory behind the glass counters of famous retail jewellery showrooms. They procure raw gold, deploy CAD designers to craft molds, cast 22kt gold with CZ stones, and ship finished ornaments to branded retailers who then sell to end consumers.

The revenue split is overwhelmingly concentrated in its core competency. In FY25, 99.4% of total revenue (₹11,008.85 mn out of ₹11,030.22 mn) was derived from the sale of gold jewellery, with negligible income from job-work services [^ar-fy25]. Geographically, the mix is 96% domestic and 4% international, a ratio that held steady through Q1 FY27 [^ip-latest]. The company does not break down revenue by individual client brand in its presentations, but named trust-based clientele includes Joyalukkas, Lalithaa, Alukkas, Vysyaraju, and Shree Kalptaru [^ip-latest]. These are large regional retail chains that prefer to outsource factory capex to scalable specialists like Shanti Gold.

From a structural standpoint, the subsidiary footprint includes a Dubai wholly-owned subsidiary (Shanti Gold Jewellery Trading L.L.C.) established in 2025 to handle export compliance and global trade exhibitions, and an associated entity named Utssav which assists in rose gold (gold alloyed with copper for a pinkish hue) production [^cc-transcript]. The manufacturing footprint spans two states with three key nodes: the Andheri plant in Mumbai (2,700 kg/pa capacity on 13,449 sq.ft), the Marol plant in Mumbai (4,000 kg/pa on 14,590 sq.ft, commercialized June 2026), and the under-construction Jaipur facility (1,200 kg/pa on 50,000 sq.ft) [^ip-latest]. Post Jaipur completion, total installed capacity reaches 7,900 kg/pa.

The promoter background is rooted in the Jagawat and Jain families, who held 100% ownership pre-IPO and currently retain 74.89% post-listing [^ar-fy25]. There is no promoter pledging (using shares as loan collateral) disclosed, indicating aligned long-term interests, though the group context is that of a first-generation-to-professionalized manufacturing enterprise transitioning into institutional scrutiny post-listing [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The Indian Gold Jewellery Industry represents a massive TAM (Total Addressable Market—the total revenue opportunity available). As per the latest investor presentation, the industry stood at ₹4,11,500 Cr (approximately $49.5 Billion assuming ~83 INR/USD) in CY23, and is forecast to expand at a 9.7% CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate—the mean annual growth rate over a specified period) to ₹7,16,200 Cr (approximately $86.3 Billion) by CY29P (Projected Calendar Year 2029) [^ip-latest]. A critical structural tailwind is the formalization of the sector: organized players' share is rising from 35.2% to 59.7% due to mandatory BIS Hallmarking (purity certification) and GST (Goods and Services Tax) compliance, which has systematically eliminated tax-evading unorganized artisans [^ip-latest].

Competitive Moat Analysis:
- **Switching Costs:** Moderate. While retailers like Joyalukkas rely on Shanti’s 400+ monthly designs for fresh inventory, they retain the technical ability to dual-source from competing factories, limiting absolute lock-in [^ip-latest].
- **Scale:** Strong. With 7,900 kg/pa of installed capacity and FY26 volume of 1,747.8 kg, Shanti is among the top pure-play B2B manufacturers, allowing it to negotiate better bullion (raw gold) financing terms [^ip-latest].
- **Pricing Power:** Weak. The company is a price-taker on the underlying gold commodity and primarily competes on "conversion cost" (making charges per gram), meaning it cannot arbitrarily raise prices without risking client defection [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

The promoter pedigree lies with the Jagawat and Jain families, who have maintained multi-decade tenure in jewellery manufacturing and currently hold 74.89% of equity with no reduction or pledging [^ar-fy25]. However, governance yellow flags exist: the CFO (Shriram Iyengar) and CS (Namrata Somani→Vrushti Shah) roles changed mid-year in June 2024, suggesting some organizational churn at the cusp of the IPO [^ar-fy25].

FCF (Free Cash Flow—CFO minus Capital Expenditure) deployment over the last three years shows a deliberate sacrifice of cash generation for scale. The firm prioritized inventory build-up and capex (Jaipur CWIP of ₹89.17 mn) over liquidity; FY25 CFO was negative ₹153 mn against a PAT of ₹558 mn, meaning every rupee of profit is currently trapped in receivables and ready-stock [^ar-fy25]. This is a high-risk but historically intentional strategy to capture market share from organized retailers optimizing their own inventories [^cc-transcript].

Debt management shows moderate leverage: Gross borrowings stood at ₹2,330 mn against equity of ₹1,524 mn, implying a D/E (Debt-to-Equity) ratio of ~1.5x [^ar-fy25]. Interest coverage (a ratio measuring how easily a company pays interest on its debt, calculated as PBT plus Finance Costs divided by Finance Costs) was ~4.8x [^ar-fy25]. Management guided that post the ₹100 cr rights issue, the D/E will compress to 0.50, strengthening the balance sheet [^cc-transcript]. No explicit external credit ratings (from agencies like CRISIL or ICRA) were disclosed in the provided extracts.

Dividend policy is non-existent (0.00% yield); the company retains 100% of earnings to fund its working capital-intensive growth engine, which is standard for small-caps in expansion mode [^ar-fy25].

Corporate governance flags require vigilance. The FY25 audit opinion was explicitly unqualified ("no audit qualification"), which is a green flag for accounting cleanliness [^ar-fy25]. However, extensive RPTs (Related-Party Transactions—deals with entities controlled by management or their relatives) exist, including unsecured loans of ₹12.14 mn to KMP (Key Managerial Personnel) linked entities [^ar-fy25]. The ValuePickr community has tracked a SEBI SCORES complaint regarding non-disclosure of a ₹100 cr VC investment breakup and the customer identity behind a ₹44 cr order, highlighting a transparency gap that institutional investors must monitor [^vp-thread]. Strategically, the JV/partnership with associated entity Utssav for rose gold and the Dubai subsidiary for exports are rational geographic and product extensions aligned with the core casting competency [^cc-transcript].
