The legacy engines and power generation businesses provide stable cash flows, while the new businesses offer significant growth avenues.
*   **Structural Margin Improvement from EV Transition & New Businesses:** The shift towards electric vehicles (EVs) in the last-mile mobility segment (GEMPL) and the growth of aftermarket services are expected to drive a structural improvement in Greaves Cotton's overall margins. GEMPL's higher-margin product mix and scale benefits, coupled with the expansion of Greaves Retail's multi-brand service network, are anticipated to contribute to an estimated 200-250 bps expansion in consolidated EBITDA margins over the next 3-4 years (FY27E-FY28E) as the new businesses achieve operating leverage.
*   **Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies:** Greaves Cotton has strategically diversified into high-growth adjacencies beyond its core engine business. This includes electric 2-wheelers (Ampere), electric 3-wheelers (Ele), and a robust multi-brand aftermarket retail chain (Greaves Retail). These segments benefit from strong government support (FAME-II subsidy, PLI scheme for ACC batteries), increasing environmental awareness, and rising disposable incomes, positioning the company for sustained long-term growth.
*   **Near-Term Catalysts (6-12 months):** Key near-term catalysts include the successful launch of new EV models under the Ampere brand, further expansion of the Greaves Retail network, and a potential improvement in operating leverage within GEMPL as sales volumes scale up. While government support is strong, any sudden changes in FAME-II subsidies or new entrants with aggressive pricing strategies could impact GEMPL's market share and profitability. Further, battery technology advancements and localization challenges pose ongoing risks to cost structures and product competitiveness.
