## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Asset-light tier-2 expansion model drives asymmetric bed-growth:** Artemis is rolling out a network of partner-driven 100–150 bed secondary-care hospitals across Raipur, Patna, Lucknow, Indore and other Tier-2/3 catchments, where the new Raipur (Shanti) facility commenced operations in July 2026[^ip-latest]. Capex per bed is materially lower (sub-Rs. 35–40 lakh per operational bed) than greenfield Tier-1 builds, which historically cost ~Rs. 80–100 lakh per bed — this underwrites our 14–15% CAGR in operational beds over FY26–FY28E without proportionate balance-sheet leverage.

- **Margin re-rating from specialty mix shift:** Mature Gurugram hospital ARPOB stands at ~Rs. 2.75–2.85 Cr/bed (vs. sector median of Rs. 1.9–2.1 Cr/bed), anchored by high-acuity mix in oncology, cardiac sciences, orthopaedics, and transplants[^cc-transcript]. As the new Tier-2 facilities mature through Year-2/Year-3 (typically hospital EBITDAR margin lifts from sub-15% to ~24–26% by Year 3), consolidated EBITDA margins should expand from current low-20s to ~25% by FY28E — a structural 250–300 bps tailwind.

- **International patient revenue moat is widening:** Foreign-patient revenue share has expanded to ~36% of sales in FY26 vs. 35.6% in FY18 and 31.8% in FY17[^vp-thread], and Gurugram's catchment captures a disproportionate share of medical value travel (MVT) into North India given proximity to IGI Airport. With ~USD 7–9 bn MVT TAM growing at 12–14% CAGR, even a flat market share translates into 15%+ growth in the highest-margin revenue line[^ip-latest].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1 FY27 results on 03-Aug-2026 — first full quarter consolidating Raipur operations and the AGM/Postal Ballot clearances received in Jul-2026[^ar-fy25]; (ii) commissioning of 2–3 additional Tier-2 facilities by FY27-end taking total bed count from ~700 (current) to ~900+; (iii) potential re-rating as public shareholding normalises post-demerger lock-in (currently 100% public holding), enabling FII/DII flow-through — institutional float uplift is a non-fundamental but real technical catalyst.

- **Biggest structural risk:** Execution risk on the partner-hospital roll-out. Each Tier-2 unit needs to ramp occupancies from 30–40% in Year 1 to break-even 60%+ by Year 2–3, and any single-project delay (regulatory, clinical talent, equipment commissioning) materially shifts the FY27–FY28E ramp curve. Stretched working capital (operating cycle of ~80–95 days) and FY26 ROCE of just 13.95% also suggest returns on the new capex will take time to feed through — we model 18–24 months before consolidated ROCE crosses 16%.[^cc-transcript]

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model:** Artemis Medicare Services Ltd (AMSL) operates a multi-speciality tertiary and secondary-care hospital platform headquartered in Gurugram, Haryana. The company runs the flagship 400+ bed Artemis Hospital (Sector 51, Gurugram — established 2007, JCI + NABH accredited) and a growing network of secondary-care "Artemis Lite" / partner hospitals in Tier-2/3 cities[^ar-fy25]. Revenue model is a blend of (a) IPD (In-Patient Department) high-acuity surgical/medical admissions, (b) OPD (Out-Patient Department) consultation and diagnostics, and (c) International Patient Services (medical value travel) — the latter contributing ~36% of revenue at the Gurugram unit.

  | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | In-Patient Department (IPD) | ~58–62% | High-acuity surgical, cardiac, oncology, ortho, transplants |
  | Out-Patient Department (OPD) | ~14–18% | Consultations, diagnostics, day-care |
  | International Patient Revenue | ~16–18% (consol.) / ~36% (Gurugram) | MVT — Africa, CIS, MENA, SAARC |
  | Pharmacy & Other Ancillary | ~6–8% | In-house pharmacy, F&B, parking |

- **Key OEM/end customers per division:** As a healthcare services provider, "customers" are patients and corporate/payer partners. Top payer relationships include CGHS, ECHS, major TPAs (Medi Assist, Paramount, MD India), PSU insurers and corporate tie-ups; key clinical specialities driving revenue include Cardiac Sciences (CABG, angioplasty), Oncology (medical + surgical), Orthopaedics & Joint Replacement, Neuro Sciences, Organ Transplant (kidney + heart transplant launched), Gastroenterology and Critical Care[^ar-fy25].

- **Subsidiary structure:** Post the FY20 reverse merger of Artemis Global Life Sciences (AGLSL) into AMSL, the structure has been significantly simplified — Artemis Medicare Services is the operating parent[^vp-thread]. Operational subsidiaries/JVs include entities housing Tier-2 hospital SPVs (Raipur Shanti, upcoming Patna/Lucknow/Indore units), and a diagnostic vertical.
