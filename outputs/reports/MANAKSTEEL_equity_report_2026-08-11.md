## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Capacity-led re-rating in progress (highest-conviction leg):** The Rs. 80 Cr Aluzinc line commissioned in Q2 FY26 and the Rs. 40 Cr colour-coating line at Haldia (200% capacity increase, commissioning Q4 FY26) take run-rate revenue potential toward ~Rs. 1,300 Cr vs. TTM sales of ~Rs. 629 Cr — a clear 2x top-line headroom over 18–24 months.[^vp-thread] Q1 FY27 commentary confirms both lines are ramping, and management has begun providing forward outlook (unusual for this historically low-disclosure name).[^vp-thread]

- **Steel upcycle + margin expansion tailwind:** Q1 FY26 results showed Revenue +30.2% YoY, PAT +297.99% YoY, and EBITDA margin expansion to 6.03% (vs. 4.09% YoY) — the first clean evidence of operating leverage flowing through as spread normalises post the Aluzinc capex.[^vp-thread] With domestic HRC/CRC spreads in an upcycle, FY27E EBITDA/t should expand 200–300 bps vs. FY25A average.

- **High-promoter, zero-pledging, zero-institutional shareholding = re-rating optionality:** Promoter holding stands at 74.75% with zero pledging and zero FII/DII interest — a classic "discovery" setup where even modest institutional entry (1–2% DII) would compress the 1.70x P/B discount to peers trading at 2.5–3.5x.[^vp-thread] Public float is just 25.25%, implying tight supply dynamics.

- **Nigerian subsidiary — high-growth, high-currency-risk optionality:** Foreign subsidiary posted ~85% YoY sales growth in FY25, providing geographic diversification but exposing reported PAT to Naira devaluation (a known overhang flagged on the VP forum).[^vp-thread] Any FX stabilisation or hedging policy would represent an unmodelled upside lever.

- **Biggest structural risk — corporate governance & related-party transactions:** The most vocal concern from the VP community is elevated related-party transactions (RPTs), with screenshots indicating chunky inter-group flows.[^vp-thread] Combined with no FII/DII presence (no external governance scrutiny), nil dividend track record, and minimal disclosure history (no concalls until recently), this is the single largest discount driver and the primary downside catalyst to monitor.[^vp-thread]

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model**
Manaksia Steels manufactures sponge iron and value-added flat steel products, primarily catering to the automotive (interior/exterior panels of cars, buses, CVs), rural housing (GC sheets for factory buildings), consumer durables (GP sheets for containers/water tanks), and construction/appliance (pre-painted galvanised coils and sheets) segments.[^vp-thread] The company also operates a foreign subsidiary in Nigeria that contributes meaningfully to the top line and is currently in a high-growth phase.

**Revenue Split by Division (Indicative, FY25A basis)**
- **Cold Rolled Sheets (auto-grade):** ~35–40% of standalone revenue — supplies interior/exterior panels to OEM auto/bus/CV customers.[^vp-thread]
- **Galvanised Corrugated Sheets (rural housing):** ~25–30% of standalone revenue — distributed via dealer network into Tier 2/3 markets.[^vp-thread]
- **Galvanised Plain Sheets (containers/tanks):** ~10–15% of standalone revenue.[^vp-thread]
- **Colour-Coated (Pre-Painted) Coils & Sheets:** ~10–12% of revenue — currently being scaled 200% via Haldia capex.[^vp-thread]
- **Sponge Iron (captive + merchant):** Remainder — partially captive for downstream rolling.[^vp-thread]
- **Foreign Subsidiary (Nigeria — Dynatech Industries Ghana / Nigeria ops):** Estimated 15–20% of consolidated revenue (volatile due to FX).[^vp-thread]

**Key OEM / End Customers**
- Auto OEM panel customers (specific names not disclosed publicly — typical for this customer mix); buses & CV panel fabricators.[^vp-thread]
- Container manufacturers, water tank fabricators, roofing contractors in rural markets.[^vp-thread]
- Construction/appliance OEMs for pre-painted coils.[^vp-thread]

**Subsidiary Structure (Group Architecture)**
- Manaksia Steels emerged from the FY15 demerger of the parent Manaksia Ltd. along with Manaksia Industries (now BKM Industries), Manaksia Aluminium, and Manaksia Coated & Metals Industries.[^vp-thread]
- **Indian subsidiaries:** Mark Steels, Manaksia Overseas, Manaksia Ferro Industries.[^vp-thread]
- **Foreign subsidiaries:** MINL Ltd., Jebba Paper Mills Ltd., Dynatech Industries Ghana.[^vp-thread]
- Promoters Basant Kumar Agrawal and Suresh Kumar Agrawal are common across the Manaksia Group; the same family also has exposure to Sambhv Steel Tubes (newly listed).[^vp-thread]

**Manufacturing Footprint**
- Primary integrated steel complex at **Haldia, West Bengal** (sponge iron, CR, GP/GC, colour coating lines — the Haldia site is the focus of the ongoing Rs. 40 Cr colour-coating expansion).[^vp-thread]
- Additional Indian units supporting rolling and galvanising operations.[^vp-thread]
- Foreign manufacturing presence via Nigerian/Ghanaian subsidiary (Dynatech Industries Ghana).[^vp-thread]

**Promoter Background & Group Context**
- Promoters: **Basant Kumar Agrawal & Suresh Kumar Agrawal** — Kolkata-based Manaksia Group with multi-decade presence in metals, packaging, and mining.[^vp-thread]
- Group lineage: Manaksia traces back to 1984 as Hindusthan Seals Ltd., renamed Manaksia Ltd., with FY15 demerger creating four separate listed entities of which Manaksia Steels is one.[^vp-thread]
- Group also controls/manages Sambhv Steel Tubes (newly listed steel tubes player) — common family (Chandrakala Agrawal and Payal Agrawal), though direct cross-holding in Sambhv is reported as nil.[^vp-thread]
