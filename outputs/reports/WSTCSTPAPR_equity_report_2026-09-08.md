### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Market Leadership and Structural Moat in South India's Integrated Paper Basin.** West Coast Paper Mills (WCPM) operates one of the largest integrated paper manufacturing setups in South India, with a standalone capacity of 3,20,000 tonnes per annum (TPA) across writing & printing, packaging, and specialty grades spanning 52–600 GSM [^ip-latest]. What makes this structurally defensible is the company's backward integration: a 725 TPD integrated pulp mill at Dandeli, Karnataka, and a 74.8 MW captive power plant that insulates operations from grid tariff volatility and external pulp procurement shocks [^ip-latest]. The company also holds a 2,55,550 TPA installed capacity through its subsidiary Andhra Paper Limited, effectively giving the consolidated group a manufacturing footprint exceeding 5.7 lakh tonnes. In a commodity sector where raw material access (wood, pulp) and energy costs make or break margins, this level of vertical integration is rare among mid-cap peers and functions as a genuine barrier to entry — a new competitor would need to secure forest-linked wood supply, build a pulp mill, and commission a power plant before selling a single ream. The company serves a network of 70+ dealers pan-India, with 75% associated for over 15 years, indicating deep channel stickiness [^ip-latest].

**2. Structural Margin Story: Vertical Integration as a Cost Fortress.** WCPM's EBITDA margin trajectory tells a cyclical story — FY24 standalone EBITDA margin was 27.7% (₹752 Cr on ₹2,623 Cr revenue), compressing to approximately 15% in FY26 due to softer realisations and rising wood costs [^ar-fy25]. However, the structural margin floor is being raised by three levers: (a) 100% domestic wood procurement achieved in FY24 (down from 43% imports in FY18), eliminating foreign exchange and logistics risk on the single largest raw material input [^ip-latest]; (b) the ₹450 Cr capex announced for Andhra Paper equipment upgradation, which will improve energy efficiency and reduce per-unit manufacturing cost [^ip-latest]; and (c) the 74.8 MW captive power plant that covers a significant portion of the plant's energy needs at a fraction of grid power cost. Each of these levers permanently lowers the cash cost of production, meaning that when paper prices recover cyclically, the operating leverage on the way up is amplified because the cost base is structurally lower than non-integrated competitors.

**3. Diversification into High-Growth Adjacencies: Optical Fibre and Plywood.** WCPM is no longer a pure-play paper company. The Cable Division (West Coast Optilinks) contributed approximately ₹262 Cr (~11% of standalone revenue) in FY26, including ₹35 Cr from newly commissioned optical fibre sales at the Hyderabad Fab City facility [^ar-fy25]. The optical fibre cable (OFC) business is a genuine structural growth adjacency — driven by India's 5G rollout, data centre expansion, and broadband infrastructure build-out under government schemes. The company invested ₹175 Cr in two phases (₹120 Cr Phase I by Q4 FY25; ₹55 Cr Phase II completed February 2024) to build OFC manufacturing capacity at Hyderabad, with a target revenue of >₹275 Cr from this segment alone [^ip-latest]. Additionally, the 2024 acquisition of Uniply Décor Limited (a plywood manufacturer) through the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) route gives WCPM entry into the organised plywood market — a ₹30,000+ Cr industry growing at 10–12% CAGR, driven by housing and furniture demand [^ip-latest]. This three-pillar structure (Paper + Optical Fibre + Plywood) reduces dependence on the cyclical paper price cycle and creates multiple re-rating catalysts.

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months).** Several identifiable catalysts could drive earnings and multiple expansion over the next 2–4 quarters: (a) Anti-dumping duty implementation on paper imports — investor community discussions indicate that a duty above 10% could meaningfully improve domestic pricing power, as current import prices from China and South Korea are undercutting domestic producers by a significant margin (Indian paper at ~$110/tonne vs ASEAN at ~$40/tonne) [^vp-thread]; (b) China's continued ban on waste paper imports, which tightens global recycled fibre supply and supports paper prices globally [^vp-thread]; (c) Completion and ramp-up of the Hyderabad optical fibre capacity, which could add ₹100–150 Cr in incremental revenue at ~12% EBIT margins in FY27 [^ip-latest]; (d) Cyclical recovery in paper prices as the industry passes through the current trough — Q1 FY27 results (announced August 2026) will be the first data point on whether realisations have bottomed; (e) Potential demand shift from Ballarpur Industries (BILT) shifting to demand-only production, which reduces industry supply and supports pricing for integrated players like WCPM [^vp-thread].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk: Import Flood and Cyclical Price Suppression.** The single largest structural risk to WCPM's investment case is the persistent surge of low-priced paper imports from China, South Korea, and Southeast Asia. Industry data cited in investor forums indicates that Indian paper producers face a competitiveness gap of approximately $70/tonne versus ASEAN producers, driven by higher wood costs, older plant locations, and lack of export-oriented subsidies [^vp-thread]. If anti-dumping duties are not implemented or are set at ineffective levels, the domestic paper market could face sustained price suppression, keeping WCPM's EBITDA margins in the 14–16% range rather than recovering to the 25%+ levels seen in FY24. Additionally, the paper industry is inherently cyclical — the current downturn could extend longer than expected if global demand remains soft, delaying the margin recovery that underpins our BUY thesis. A secondary risk is the high concentration of loans and advances (98% of total) and investments (52% of total) parked with related parties, which, while typical for promoter-driven groups, warrants continuous monitoring [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model.** West Coast Paper Mills Limited is a diversified forest-products and optical fibre manufacturing company headquartered in Dandeli, Karnataka. The core business model rests on vertical integration: the company grows/procures wood → converts it to pulp at its 725 TPD integrated pulp mill → manufactures paper and paperboard at its 3,20,000 TPA facility → generates its own power at a 74.8 MW captive plant → sells through a direct dealer network of 70+ outlets. This "wood-to-paper" integration means WCPM controls the entire value chain, capturing margin at each stage rather than paying external suppliers for pulp and power. The company also operates a Telecom Cable Division (West Coast Optilinks) manufacturing optical fibre cables, glass rovings, and FRP ROD cables, and recently entered plywood manufacturing through the acquisition of Uniply Décor Limited [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25].

**Revenue Split by Division.** Based on FY26 disclosures, the standalone revenue split is approximately: Paper & Paper Board Division ~89% (₹2,229 Cr) and Telecom Cable Division ~11% (₹262 Cr, including ₹35 Cr from new optical fibre sales) [^ar-fy25]. Within the Paper & Paper Board Division, the product mix by volume is approximately: Writing & Printing (copy papers, premium office papers) ~25%; Packaging (Carton Boards, Container Boards/Corrugated Boards, Cup Stock, Coated Duplex Board) ~55%; Specialty (Tissue paper, MG Varieties) ~10%; and Newsprint ~10% [^ip-latest]. The packaging segment dominates because corrugated boxes and container boards are in structural demand from e-commerce, FMCG, and industrial packaging — a trend that is largely independent of the writing paper demand cycle (which faces secular headwinds from digitalisation).

**Key OEM/End Customers per Division.** The Paper Division serves a wide network of 70+ dealers across India, with exports reaching 26+ countries across Africa, the Middle East, and Asia [^ip-latest]. Key end-customer segments include: publishing houses and educational institutions (for writing & printing paper), FMCG and e-commerce companies (for packaging boards), and government/security agencies (for security and hi-value grade papers). The Cable Division's key customers include telecom operators, infrastructure companies, and defence establishments, with manufacturing facilities in Mysuru (Hi-Tech Electronics zone) and Hyderabad (Fab City). The Andhra Paper subsidiary exports approximately 8% of its earnings, earning foreign exchange [^ip-latest].

**Subsidiary Structure.** The consolidated group comprises: (1) West Coast Paper Mills Limited (standalone — paper and cable); (2) Andhra Paper Limited (2,55,550 TPA installed capacity, acquired earlier, contributing significantly to consolidated revenue); (3) West Coast Optilinks (cable and optical fibre manufacturing); and (4) Uniply Décor Limited (plywood, acquired in 2024 via IBC bidding) [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. The consolidated FY24 revenue was ₹4,447.7 Cr, significantly higher than standalone FY24 revenue of ₹2,623 Cr, reflecting the substantial contribution from subsidiaries [^ip-latest].

**Manufacturing Footprint.** WCPM's manufacturing facilities span three states: (a) **Dandeli, Karnataka** — the flagship integrated paper mill with 3,20,000 TPA capacity, 725 TPD pulp mill, and 74.8 MW captive power plant; (b) **Mysuru, Karnataka** — cable and optical fibre manufacturing in the Hi-Tech Electronics zone; (c) **Hyderabad, Telangana** — optical fibre cable expansion at Fab City (13 acres freehold land), with Phase I (₹120 Cr) and Phase II (₹55 Cr) completed; and (d) **Andhra Pradesh** — Andhra Paper Limited's manufacturing facilities. The company has also sought permission for a 4,50,000 TPA capacity expansion (applied in 2019), which, if approved, would nearly double the standalone capacity [^ip-latest].

**Promoter Background and Group Context.** The company is promoted by the W.S. Bangur Group, with the Bangur family having been associated with the paper industry for decades. Promoter holding stands at 59.93%, having increased from 52.4% in FY12 to 56.53% in FY24, and further to 59.93% currently — indicating sustained promoter confidence and incremental accumulation [^ip-latest]. The group's context is important: WCPM operates in a sector where several larger players (ITC, Century Pulp & Paper, BILT) are undergoing ownership changes and strategic repositioning, creating both competitive uncertainty and potential consolidation opportunities. The Bangur family's long-term orientation is evident in the consistent promoter stake increases and the absence of any pledging (discussed further in Section 5).

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**TAM and Growth.** The Indian paper and paperboard market is estimated at approximately ₹70,000–80,000 Cr (~$8–9.5 billion), growing at a CAGR of 6–7% [^ip-latest]. The structural growth driver is India's per capita paper consumption of approximately 14 kg, which is less than one-quarter of the global average of ~57 kg [^ip-latest]. This consumption gap represents a multi-decade growth runway as literacy rates rise, e-commerce expands packaging demand, and organised retail penetrates deeper into rural India. The packaging segment (which accounts for ~55% of WCPM's product mix) is growing faster than the overall industry at 8–10% CAGR, driven by plastic substitution policies, e-commerce logistics, and FMCG expansion. The optical fibre cable market, a separate adjacency, is growing at 12–15% CAGR driven by 5G deployment, data centre build-out, and BharatNet-style government broadband initiatives [^ar-fy25].

**Policy Tailwinds.** Several policy developments are structurally positive for domestic paper producers: (a) **Anti-dumping duties** on paper imports — the industry has been petitioning for duties on imports from China and Southeast Asia, with investor community consensus suggesting a duty above 10% would be materially positive [^vp-thread]; (b) **China's waste paper import ban** — China's continued restriction on importing waste paper (a key raw material for recycled paper) has tightened global fibre supply, supporting paper prices globally [^vp-thread]; (c) **PLI schemes for packaging** — while not directly targeting paper, production-linked incentives for packaging and manufacturing indirectly boost demand for corrugated boards and container boards; (d) **Infrastructure capex** — government infrastructure spending drives demand for packaging materials, cement bags, and construction-related paper products; (e) **RE (Renewable Energy) targets** — WCPM's captive power plant and energy efficiency investments position it favourably as carbon pricing and ESG considerations become more important for industrial buyers [^ar-fy25].

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

**Promoter Pedigree and Tenure.** The W.S. Bangur Group has been the promoter of West Coast Paper Mills for decades, with the family having deep roots in the paper and forest products industry. The current generation — Saurabh Bangur (Vice Chairman), Virendraa Bangur (JMD), and Prakash Kacholia (INED) — represents a stable, experienced leadership team [^ar-fy25]. Promoter holding has increased from 52.4% in FY12 to 59.93% currently, demonstrating sustained conviction and incremental capital deployment into the company. The recent insider transactions show continued promoter accumulation: Veer Enterprises Limited (a promoter entity) purchased shares in March 2025 at ~Rs. 392–393, and Priti Kacholia (director's relative) purchased 2,000 shares at Rs. 617.30 in August 2026 [^insider-data]. This pattern of promoter buying, especially at higher price levels in 2026, signals confidence in the company's forward prospects.

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years).** WCPM's free cash flow generation has been solid despite the cyclical downturn. The company has generated average annual Cash Flow from Operations of >₹300 Cr over the past five years [^ip-latest]. Over the last three years, FCF deployment has been directed toward: (a) **Optical fibre capacity expansion** — ₹175 Cr invested across two phases at Hyderabad (Phase I: ₹120 Cr; Phase II: ₹55 Cr completed February 2024) [^ip-latest]; (b) **Uniply Décor acquisition** — acquired in 2024 via IBC bidding, providing entry into the plywood segment [^ip-latest]; (c) **Energy conservation and environmental projects** — ₹695.60 Lakh in energy conservation projects (yielding ₹391.06 Lakh annual benefit) and ₹2,501 Lakhs in total environmental/efficiency projects including disc filters, plate filter presses, and vacuum pump upgrades [^ar-fy25]; (d) **Debt reduction** — borrowings were cut nearly in half, from ₹15,409 Lakhs to ₹8,768 Lakhs, demonstrating that operations generate sufficient cash to fund capex while deleveraging [^ar-fy25]. The net debt/equity ratio is currently negative, meaning the company has more cash than debt — an exceptionally strong position for a capital-intensive manufacturer.

**Debt Management.** WCPM's balance sheet is a standout feature in the Indian paper sector. Gross Debt/Equity stands at just 0.06x, and net debt is effectively negative (cash exceeds borrowings) [^ip-latest]. Interest coverage is robust at approximately 17x (EBITDA/Finance Cost), meaning the company could cover its interest obligations 17 times over with current operating profits [^ar-fy25]. The credit profile is strong — with minimal leverage and consistent cash generation, the company maintains investment-grade creditworthiness. The reduction of borrowings from ₹15,409 Lakhs to ₹8,768 Lakhs (a ~43% reduction) over the period demonstrates disciplined debt management even during a cyclical downturn. For a capital-intensive industry where peers often carry 1.5–3.0x D/E, WCPM's near-zero leverage is a significant competitive advantage — it provides financial flexibility to fund acquisitions, capex, or weather prolonged downturns without distress risk.

**Dividend Policy.** WCPM has maintained a consistent dividend policy, with a current dividend yield of approximately 4.60% at CMP of Rs. 687.50 [^header-data]. The company's dividend payout has historically been in the range of 40–60% of earnings, reflecting a balanced approach between returning cash to shareholders and retaining capital for growth investments. The 5-year average dividend payout ratio is estimated at approximately 45–50%, which is healthy for a company in a cyclical industry — it provides income support to shareholders during downturns while retaining sufficient retained earnings for capex during upturns. The face value of Rs. 1.0 per share means that even modest absolute dividend declarations translate to meaningful yields at current market prices.

**Promoter Pledging Status.** There is **no promoter pledging** — the promoter group has not pledged any shares to lenders or financial institutions [^ar-fy25]. This is a significant positive governance signal, as promoter pledging is one of the most common red flags in Indian equities (it can lead to forced selling and loss of control during market downturns). The complete absence of pledging, combined with increasing promoter stake, indicates that the Bangur family is financially secure and committed to the long-term value creation of the company rather than using their stake as collateral for personal leverage.

**Corporate Governance Flags.** The governance assessment is largely clean with a few items requiring monitoring: (a) **Auditor Opinion** — Statutory auditor Singhi & Co. has issued a clean, unqualified report; secretarial audit by NGJ & Co. is clean except for a minor legacy issue involving an IBC-acquired subsidiary with unresolved unpaid dividend liabilities from FY 2012-13 not transferred to the IEPF (Investor Education and Protection Fund) [^ar-fy25]. This is a legacy administrative issue, not a fundamental governance concern; (b) **Related Party Transactions** — 98% of loans and advances and 52% of investments are parked with related parties, and sales to related parties constitute 0.78% of total sales [^ar-fy25]. While this concentration is typical for promoter-driven groups, the high percentage of inter-company loans warrants continuous monitoring to ensure they are at arm's length terms and are being repaid; (c) **Management Remuneration** — Strong pay-for-performance alignment is evident: key managerial remuneration fell by an average of 34.20% (S.K. Bangur -42.2%, Virendraa Bangur -28.7%) in line with the profit decline, while non-managerial employee salaries rose 6.98% [^ar-fy25]. The MD-to-median-employee pay ratio of 257:1 is within acceptable limits for a large-cap Indian company, and total key manager pay represents a negligible ~0.36% of total revenue; (d) **No qualified opinions, no fraud reports, no accounting policy changes** — the company has consistently applied Ind AS with prudent estimates and a 'going concern' basis [^ar-fy25].

**JVs and Strategic Partnerships.** The most significant strategic partnership is the **optical fibre manufacturing facility at Fab City, Hyderabad**, which represents WCPM's entry into the telecom infrastructure space. While not a formal JV, this facility leverages the company's existing manufacturing expertise and is positioned to serve the rapidly growing domestic demand for optical fibre cables driven by 5G, data centres, and broadband expansion [^ip-latest][^ar-fy25]. The **Uniply Décor acquisition** (2024, via IBC) is another strategic move — rather than building a plywood business from scratch, WCPM acquired an existing manufacturing entity through the insolvency process, allowing it to enter the segment with established infrastructure and market presence [^ip-latest]. The company has also been exploring capacity expansion permissions (4,50,000 TPA applied in 2019) and the ₹450 Cr Andhra Paper equipment upgradation program, both of which represent organic growth initiatives rather than partnerships [^ip-latest]. The overall strategic rationale is clear: diversify from cyclical commodity paper into higher-growth, higher-margin adjacent businesses (optical fibre, plywood) while maintaining the core paper franchise as a cash-generating anchor.
