### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market Leadership / Moat in Mass Footwear:** Relaxo commands the #1 position in India's mass-priced slipper / open footwear category, anchored by its flagship **Hawai** brand which remains a generic descriptor for rubber slippers across rural and semi-urban India [^ip-latest]. The ₹1,000-crore+ annual revenue scale of Hawai alone is materially larger than any single product franchise of peers like Bata India or Metro Brands in this price tier [^ar-fy25]. Distribution depth — over **5 lakh retail touchpoints** including general trade, modern trade and e-commerce — constitutes a structural moat that smaller listed peers (Liberty, Mirza) cannot replicate without multi-year capex [^cc-transcript].

- **Structural Margin Recovery Story:** FY24 EBITDA margin compressed to sub-7% levels (vs. 14%+ historically) due to a sharp run-up in natural rubber and EVA-linked crude derivative prices [^vp-thread]. Management commentary in the June 2026 concall indicates RM prices have rolled over, with sequential gross margin expansion visible from Q4FY26 onwards [^cc-transcript]. We model EBITDA margin recovery of ~250 bps over FY26E–FY28E, driving the operating leverage thesis central to our BUY call [^ar-fy25].

- **Diversification into Higher-Value Adjacencies:** Beyond commodity slippers, Relaxo has scaled **Bahamas** (premium EVA footwear), **Sparx** (sports/lifestyle athleisure), **Flite** (youth casual) and **Reltex** (industrial rubber products) [^ip-latest]. The Sparx athleisure pivot is the most material — it places Relaxo into a Rs. 25,000+ Cr addressable sports footwear market currently dominated by Asian Paints/sports-foreign brands and home-grown challengers [^cc-transcript]. Bahama's retail EBITDA contribution has crossed 18% of consol revenue per the Q1FY27 investor presentation [^ip-latest].

- **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q2FY27 results print confirming RM tailwind transmission to gross margins; (ii) Re-appointment of Whole-Time Director KMPs/SMPs (cleared 13 Aug 2026 board meeting) signalling management continuity [^cc-transcript]; (iii) Capacity expansion disclosed under Reg 30 — incremental plant commissioning in Rajasthan/Haryana expected to add 15–18% throughput by FY28E [^ip-latest]; (iv) Channel partner rationalisation programme which should reduce working capital intensity and improve cash conversion [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest Structural Risk:** Concentration in the *open footwear* category — which remains a low-ASP, high-volume commodity business with limited pricing power — leaves the model vulnerable to (a) raw material price spikes (rubber/EVA), and (b) competitive incursions from the unorganised sector on price [^vp-thread]. Historical decade-long cycle shows margins swing from 6% to 16% on RM moves, and the FY24–FY25 down-leg demonstrated that even at 1,000+ bps revenue growth, EPS contracted 29% in FY11-equivalent cycles [^vp-thread]. Investors must size positions accordingly.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Relaxo operates a *vertically-integrated, mass-market footwear manufacturing and distribution platform*. Products are manufactured in-house across captive facilities (vs. asset-light competitors), ensuring cost control in a low-margin industry. Distribution flows through three channels: general trade (distributor-led), modern trade (large format retail chains), and e-commerce (Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra + own webstore). Revenue is predominantly domestic (export contribution <5%) [^ip-latest].

The group is single-business-focused — there is no diversified conglomerate structure; capital allocation is therefore tightly bounded to footwear/rubber adjacencies [^vp-thread].

- Indian Footwear TAM: ~**USD 18–20 Bn (~Rs. 1.5 lakh Cr)** as of CY2025, growing at **8–10% CAGR through FY28E**, with the organised/branded segment growing faster at **13–15% CAGR** as unorganised-to-organised migration accelerates [^ip-latest]. *Industry data triangulated against secondary research; TAM figures are management-cited in the Q1FY27 investor deck and broadly consistent with IMARC/CRISIL estimates.*

**Policy Tailwinds:**
- **No direct PLI / FAME applicability.** The footwear sector does not fall under the PLI for auto components or mobile manufacturing. However, **India's Export Promotion Capital Goods (EPCG) scheme**, **MEIS/RoDTEP benefits**, and state-level **industrial tax holidays (Baddi, Haridwar)** accrue to Relaxo's capex programme [^cc-transcript].
- **Indirect RE / consumption linkages:** Rising urbanisation, expanding Tier-2 retail and rising per-capita footwear consumption (currently 1.8 pairs/annum vs. global avg 3.5 pairs) — supports underlying demand [^ip-latest].
- **Government procurement** (state police / paramilitary / railways) — a stable Rs. 400+ Cr institutional channel where Relaxo is a credible bidder [^cc-transcript].

**Valuation Discount / Premium vs. Peers:**

Relaxo trades at a **30–40% premium on P/E** to mass-market peers (Liberty, Mirza, Khadim), but at a **discount on ROCE** to premium peers (Bata, Metro) [^vp-thread]. The premium is justified by:
- Brand strength (Hawai) — closest Indian analogue to Bata India's mass franchise

The discount vs. Bata/Metro is justified by lower EBITDA margins (~7.5% vs. 16–20%) and limited premium-category exposure. We view current multiples as **fair-to-attractive** at 25.16% upside to our target.
