### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

**1. Dominant Niche Leadership in Structured Copper LAN Cables:** Birla Cable has built a formidable position in the Structured Copper LAN Cables segment, which now accounts for a commanding 86% of product revenue at Rs. 663.13 Cr in FY26 [^ar-fy25]. This category—think of the ethernet cables that run through office buildings, data centers, and IT infrastructure—has lower cyclicality than optical fibre and benefits from secular tailwinds like data center build-outs, AI infrastructure expansion, and enterprise digitization. The company's ability to scale LAN cable revenue while peers struggled with OFC (Optical Fibre Cable) downturns demonstrates a genuine product-level moat [^ar-fy25]. The LAN cable business effectively insulated the company during the severe OFC cyclical downturn that has plagued the industry since FY24.

**2. Structural Margin Recovery Story Underway, Though from a Low Base:** EBITDA margins expanded from ~2.3% in FY25 to ~3.6% in FY26—a ~130 bps improvement that reflects operating leverage on higher LAN cable volumes [^ar-fy25]. While absolute margin levels remain thin compared to peers like Polycab (10–12% EBITDA) or KEI Industries (11–13% EBITDA), the trajectory is positive. The key driver is mix improvement: as LAN cables (which carry better unit economics than commodity OFC) grow as a share of revenue, blended margins should structurally improve. Management's focus on higher-value LAN variants for data center customers should support 50–80 bps of incremental margin expansion over FY27–FY28E, assuming stable copper prices and no major OFC pricing pressure.

**3. Diversification via Landmark Amalgamation with Vindhya Telelinks:** The proposed Scheme of Amalgamation between Birla Cable (Transferor) and Vindhya Telelinks (Transferee) at a swap ratio of 10:115 is the single most important strategic catalyst [^ar-fy25]. The National Stock Exchange has issued a no-objection letter and BSE has no adverse observations as of August 14, 2026 [^vp-thread]. Post-merger, the combined entity will have significantly larger scale, a more diversified product portfolio spanning both copper and fibre cables, and improved bargaining power with customers and raw material suppliers. This is not merely financial engineering—Vindhya Telelinks brings complementary OFC capabilities and telecom customer relationships that Birla Cable lacks. The combined entity should trade at a re-rating premium to standalone Birla Cable, which currently suffers from a small-cap discount.

**4. Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months):** Three significant catalysts could drive re-rating: (a) **Global OFC Recovery**—management has highlighted improving demand signals from North America and Europe, which could reverse the 14%-of-revenue OFC segment's drag [^ar-fy25]; (b) **India 5G & BharatNet Phase 3**—the government's rural broadband push and telecom operator capex on 5G backhaul should drive incremental OFC demand; (c) **NCLT Approval of Amalgamation**—once the Scheme of Arrangement is approved by the National Company Law Tribunal, the swap ratio becomes effective and shareholders of Birla Cable receive Vindhya Telelinks shares, unlocking immediate value [^vp-thread]. Additionally, the FY26 dividend record date has been fixed (Aug 30, 2026), signaling management confidence in cash generation [^vp-thread].

**5. Biggest Structural Risk—Customer Concentration & OFC Cyclicality:** The company faces two intertwined risks. First, **high customer concentration** in a few large telecom and data center clients, which gives buyers significant pricing leverage [^ar-fy25]. Second, and more critically, the OFC business remains in a severe cyclical downturn, and there is no guarantee that global recovery will materialize on the timeline management assumes. If OFC demand stays weak through FY27, the diversification benefit of the LAN cable business is partially offset, and the pending amalgamation's strategic logic weakens. Additionally, **working capital deterioration** is a serious concern: Cash Flow from Operations was negative Rs. 20.91 Cr in FY26 against a PAT of Rs. 16.87 Cr, indicating that reported profits are not converting to cash due to ballooning inventory and receivables [^ar-fy25]. This is a classic red flag for cyclical businesses at the bottom of their cycle—if the recovery is delayed, liquidity could tighten.

##### Commentary — Income Statement (Layman-Friendly)

Think of this like a small-town shopkeeper who sells two things: copper LAN cables (the wires that connect your computer to the internet at office/home) and optical fibre cables (super-fast glass wires used by telecom companies). In FY24 and FY25, the optical fibre business was in a bad phase globally — think of it as a drought year for farmers — and revenue actually shrank. But FY26 was a "monsoon comeback" year, especially because demand for LAN cables exploded (think: every office and data centre upgrading their networks). Revenue jumped 16.5%.

The "EBITDA margin" is basically how much profit the shopkeeper makes on each Rs. 100 of sales after paying for raw materials and direct costs, but before interest, taxes, and depreciation (the cost of his shop and equipment wearing out). This margin expanded from 4.9% to 6.1% in FY26 — meaning for every Rs. 100 sold, the company kept Rs. 6.10 as gross operating profit vs. Rs. 4.90 the year before. That's a big jump. Why? Because when you sell more without proportionally increasing rent and salaries, each extra sale drops more money to the bottom. This is called "operating leverage."

Looking ahead, we expect the LAN cable momentum to continue, and the optical fibre business to gradually recover as 5G (the latest fast mobile internet technology) towers get built and BharatNet (India's rural internet project) Phase 3 kicks in. We model 14% revenue growth in FY27 and 12.5% in FY28, with margins expanding to 7.5% and 8.5% respectively. The bottom line (Profit After Tax, or PAT — what shareholders actually keep) is forecast to grow from Rs. 17 Cr in FY26 to Rs. 45 Cr in FY28 — nearly 2.6x in two years. EPS (Earnings Per Share — total profit divided by number of shares, so what each share "earns") rises from Rs. 5.63 to Rs. 15.00, which is the engine driving the target price of Rs. 465.

We normalise this to Rs. 55-65 Cr in FY27E-FY28E, which still represents strong ~80% conversion of EBITDA to operating cash flow. This is healthy — a company that shows profits but can't collect cash is in trouble.
- **FCF (Free Cash Flow) — what's left after buying/replacing equipment:** Turns positive at Rs. 30-35 Cr in FY27E-FY28E vs. negative in FY24A-FY25A. This means the business can self-fund growth and dividends without raising new debt.
- **CFF (Cash Flow from Financing) negative Rs. 20-25 Cr:** The company is using cash to pay down debt and reward shareholders — a sign of financial discipline.
- **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) — the time gap between paying suppliers and getting paid by customers:** Improved dramatically from 148 days (FY25) to 107 days (FY26), and we model further improvement to 100 days. Lower is better — it means the company's money isn't stuck in the system for long. The FY25 spike was a working capital nightmare; FY26 shows management fixed it.
- **Debtor Days (92 → 80):** Customers are paying faster. Good.
- **Inventory Days (35 → 50):** We model a normalisation — FY26's 35 days was unusually low (possibly a one-off drawdown of stockpiled raw materials). The company will need to rebuild some buffer stock for the growing business.
- **ROCE (Return on Capital Employed — how efficiently the company turns its total capital into profit):** Improves from 8.4% to 13.5% as profitability rises and capital base stays lean.
- **ROE (Return on Equity — how much profit the company generates for every rupee shareholders have invested):** Triples from FY25A's 2.3% to 13.0% in FY28E. The current ROE is below the cost of equity (the minimum return shareholders expect — typically 13-15% in India), but our forecasts show it crossing that threshold in FY28E.
- **Net D/E (0.47x → 0.34x):** Declining leverage (less debt relative to equity) is a positive — the company is becoming financially stronger.
- **DPS (Dividend Per Share — total dividend paid divided by number of shares):** Expected to grow from Rs. 1.63 (FY26) to Rs. 3.75 (FY28E), implying a ~2.3x increase over two years.
