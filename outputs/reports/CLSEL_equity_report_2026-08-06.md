### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Asset-light, debt-free business model with structurally high ROCE.** CLSEL runs an outsourced milling/procurement model — minimal capex on owned rice mills, virtually zero debt on the balance sheet, and FY25 ROCE of 17.68% demonstrates capital efficiency rare in agro-processing [^ar-fy25]. Asset turnover is supported by an export-led model (predominantly basmati shipments to Middle East, Europe, and select African markets) that has consistently delivered double-digit operating margins [^ip-latest].

- **Promoter holding of 75.94% — exceptional skin-in-the-game.** Founder family retains over three-quarters of equity, with negligible pledging and only ~2.72% FII / 1.81% DII float — implying price discovery is still emerging and any institutional accumulation could rerate the stock materially [^ar-fy25].

- **Margin tailwind from low paddy inventory carry + branded push.** Management commentary indicates current-year paddy procurement is at multi-year lows (~Rs. 1,600/quintal), and the company holds thin inventory carryover from the prior expensive season — setting up a favourable cost-realisation spread for FY27E [^cc-transcript]. The "Maharani" brand push into domestic modern trade, while early, is incrementally margin-accretive given branded basmati commands 25–40% pricing premium over loose grain [^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) Q1FY27 results (declared 06 Aug 2026) likely to show continued margin expansion, (ii) resumption of Iran/ME export volumes as geopolitical freight normalises, (iii) potential first institutional BUY initiation as float expands, (iv) branded domestic revenue crossing 10% of total sales — a key sentiment trigger for re-rating to branded-FMCG multiples [^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk: paddy price volatility + Iran exposure.** Basmati realisations are hostage to two factors — (a) mandi paddy prices (which swung from ~Rs. 2,500/quintal at peak to ~Rs. 1,600 currently) and (b) Iran-related receivables/geopolitical disruption, given Iran is historically the single-largest basmati destination. A 10% adverse forex or paddy move can compress EBITDA margins by 200–300 bps in any given quarter [^vp-thread].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Chamanlal Setia Exports Ltd (CLSEL) is a Ludhiana-headquartered, single-product focused basmati rice exporter-cum-processor operating an asset-light model — the company procures paddy and processed rice from third-party domestic millers and traders, sorts, processes, grades and packs at its own facilities, and dispatches largely to export markets under the "Maharani" brand and private labels [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue Split by Division (FY25A, estimated):**
- Basmati Rice Exports: ~88–90% of revenue
- Domestic Branded Sales (Maharani): ~6–8% of revenue (rising)
- Other by-products / Rice Bran: ~2–4% of revenue
*Source: Investor Presentation FY25* [^ip-latest]

**Key Customers / End Markets:**
- Export: Middle East (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Iran, Iraq), Europe (UK, Germany), select African and Far East markets
- Domestic: Modern trade, e-commerce platforms, general trade via distributors
- Customer concentration: Top 10 customers estimated at ~45–55% of export revenue, but no single customer >15% — providing reasonable diversification at the customer level [^vp-thread] [^ar-fy25]

**Subsidiary Structure:** The group has historically operated with minimal subsidiary complexity — primarily a standalone entity with one or two step-downs for specific export geographies, all consolidated. No listed subsidiaries [^ar-fy25].

**Manufacturing Footprint:**
- Punjab: Main milling, sorting and packaging facility (Ludhiana)
- Haryana: Secondary processing unit
- Total plants: ~3–4 owned facilities plus extensive outsourced milling tie-ups (the "asset-light" model)
*Source: Annual Report FY25* [^ar-fy25]

**Promoter Background & Group Context:**
- Promoted by the Setia family (Chamanlal Setia, founder; subsequent generation includes Mr. Rajeev Setia and Mr. Anil Setia, both currently on the Board)
- The group has remained focused exclusively on basmati since inception — no diversification into unrelated FMCG categories
- Promoter pedigree: 40+ years in basmati trading, well-networked with Punjab/Haryana mandis and Middle East buyers
- Promoter holding: 75.94% — among the highest in the listed basmati peer set (vs. LT Foods ~30–35% and KRBL ~40%) [^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Total Addressable Market (TAM):**
- Global basmati rice market: ~USD 12–14 Bn (CY25 estimates)
- Indian basmati exports: ~USD 5 Bn+ annually (FY25); India supplies ~80% of world basmati
- Domestic branded basmati: ~Rs. 18,000–20,000 Cr and growing
- Implied CAGR: 8–10% over CY25–CY30E, driven by (a) Indian diaspora premiumisation globally, (b) per-capita rice consumption in Africa and ME, (c) shift from loose to branded in domestic India [^ip-latest]

**Policy Tailwinds:**
- **APEDA / GI Tag for "Indian Basmati"** — protects origin premium, restricts Pakistani competition in EU/US markets
- **Minimum Export Price (MEP)** — periodically tweaked by Govt; currently relaxed, supportive of export volumes
- **FMCG / PLI for food processing** — not direct beneficiary but overall sector capex incentive
- **E-commerce / ONDC push** — aids branded basmati distribution to Tier-2/3 cities [^ip-latest]
