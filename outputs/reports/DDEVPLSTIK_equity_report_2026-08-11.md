## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Capacity-led market leadership in polymer compounding:** DDev Plastiks operates the largest domestic polymer compounding capacity at 239,000 MTPA (expanding to 524,000 MTPA post-Dhule Phase II commissioning), giving it ~25-30% market share in organized PE/PVC compounding and clear scale economies over fragmented peers [^ip-latest].

- **Structural margin re-rating from XLPE/HFFR mix shift:** Operating margins expanded from sub-7% historically to 11-12% in FY25-26, driven by a richer mix in XLPE (cross-linked polyethylene) and HFFR (halogen-free flame-retardant) compounds used in wire & cable insulation; management commentary suggests sustainability of 10%+ EBITDA margins through FY27E [^cc-transcript].

- **Power cable capex + HVDC export tailwind:** Marquee customers include Havells India, KEI Industries, Polycab, and European majors (Nexans, Prysmian) [^vp-thread]; HVDC submarine cable demand from UK/France interconnectors and India's Reforms-linked transmission capex (Rs. 9-10 lakh Cr over FY25-FY30) provide a multi-year volume runway for XLPE/HFFR compounds [^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months):** (i) Phase II capacity ramp-up at Dhule (Maharashtra) adding 250,000+ MTPA by Q3FY27; (ii) Saudi Arabia wholly-owned subsidiary commercial production (target FY27) opening MENAT export market; (iii) CRISIL upgrade to A/Positive from Stable supporting working-capital lines [^vp-thread].

- **Biggest structural risk — raw material volatility and promoter holding overhang:** Polymer prices (PE/PP/PVC) track crude/naphtha and are pass-through but with 60-90 day lag, exposing Q-o-Q EBITDA to ±200-300 bps swings [^vp-thread]; promoter & related-party holding of ~85% (74.86% promoter + 8.23% Almond Polytraders + 2.81% Liable Textiles) creates persistent float scarcity and deters institutional accumulation [^vp-thread].
