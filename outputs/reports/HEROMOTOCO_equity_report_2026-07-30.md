### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Defensible market leadership with scale moat:** Hero MotoCorp is the world's largest two-wheeler manufacturer by volume, commanding ~32–33% domestic motorcycle market share anchored on the iconic Splendor (~2.7–3 lakh units/month), HF Deluxe, and Glamour franchises, with cumulative sales crossing 125 million units over four decades — a brand trust moat that is near-impossible to replicate in the mass-market commuter segment.[^ip-latest] [^vp-thread]
- **Structural margin resilience & best-in-class capital efficiency:** Despite commodity headwinds, EBITDA margins have held in the 13–14% band with ROCE of 33.56% and ROE of 26.73% (TTM) — returns profile closer to an FMCG than a cyclical OEM, supported by a near-zero net debt balance sheet and FY25 EPS of Rs. 287.01.[^ar-fy25]
- **Diversification into EVs, premium and global adjacencies:** The recently concluded incremental investment in Ather Energy Limited (July 2026) deepens the EV play alongside the in-house Vida brand, while the premium Xtreme 440, Mavrick 440 and the Harley-Davidson X440 partnership open the >250cc segment — a structural hedge against entry-level ICE commoditisation.[^ip-latest]
- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (a) Q1 FY27 results on 7 Aug 2026; (b) festive season demand recovery with management guiding for a return to mid-to-high single digit volume growth; (c) margin expansion of 50–80 bps from richer mix (Xtreme 440, X440, Vida) and softening commodity tailwinds; (d) payout discipline — dividend yield of 3.59% with consistent ~50%+ payout ratio.[^cc-transcript]
- **Biggest structural risk:** Sustained share erosion in entry-level motorcycles (~100–125cc) where Honda, Bajaj, TVS and Suzuki are aggressively launching feature-loaded models, coupled with a faster-than-expected EV transition in the mass-market commuter segment that could compress ICE incumbents' TAM by FY28E.[^vp-thread]

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

- **Core business model:** Hero MotoCorp is a pure-play two-wheeler OEM with a 100% ICE + EV portfolio spanning motorcycles (entry-level commuters, premium, sports) and scooters, distributed through ~6,000+ dealer touchpoints across India and exported to 37+ countries. Revenue model is OEM sales of vehicles + spares + aftermarket, with negligible non-auto income.[^ip-latest]
- **Revenue mix by division (FY25):** Motorcycles ~89% of revenue (Splendor, HF Deluxe, Glamour, Passion, Xtreme, Xpulse, Mavrick 440, X440), Scooters ~7% (Pleasure+, Destini 125, Vida), Spares & Aftermarket ~4% — a near-pure ICE motorcycle franchise with nascent EV contribution (Vida V1, V2 plus Ather stake income).[^ar-fy25]
- **Key OEM / end customers per division:**
  - *Mass commuter motorcycles:* Primary retail customers in Tier 2–5 India; B2B fleet tie-ups with Amazon, Zomato, Swiggy, Rapido, Ola, Uber for last-mile delivery.[^ip-latest]
  - *Premium motorcycles:* Aspirational urban/semi-urban riders; co-developed Harley-Davidson X440 under the Hero-Harley global partnership.
  - *Scooters & EV:* Urban women commuters and gig-economy riders; Vida brand sold via dedicated Vida experience centres + Ather Energy retail (post-investment).
  - *Exports:* Latin America (Colombia — 80,000 unit/annum Villa Rica plant), Bangladesh (150,000 unit/annum plant operational), Africa, South-East Asia.[^ip-latest]
- **Subsidiary structure:** Wholly-owned subsidiaries include Hero Tech Center Germany GmbH (R&D), HMCL Americas (Colombia), HMC MM Auto (Bangladesh JV), and a strategic equity stake in Ather Energy Limited (additional investment concluded 14–15 July 2026, taking aggregate holding to a substantive minority).[^ip-latest]
- **Manufacturing footprint:** Six plants in India — Gurgaon, Dharuhera, Haridwar (2 plants), Neemrana, Vadodara (newest, inaugurated FY25); cumulative India capacity ~1.05 crore two-wheelers per annum; plus 2 global plants (Colombia, Bangladesh).[^ip-latest] [^vp-thread]
- **Promoter & group context:** Promoted by the Munjal family (founder Dr. Brijmohan Lall Munjal, now run by Chairman Dr. Pawan Munjal family) with 37.43% promoter holding. The Hero Group is a diversified conglomerate spanning auto (Hero MotoCorp, Hero Cycles, Hero Electric), finance, and tech with a 40+ year operating history. The group retains the demerged Hero Cycles, Hero Electric, and Hero FinCorp businesses outside the listed entity, providing clean holding-company structure.[^ar-fy25]

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

- **TAM & growth:** Indian two-wheeler industry TAM at wholesale level is ~Rs. 2.4 lakh Cr (~USD 28 Bn) for FY25; broader personal mobility TAM including EVs, used, financing and aftermarket estimated at ~USD 70 Bn. Industry volumes of ~1.7–1.8 Cr units/annum have grown at 6–8% CAGR over FY15–FY25; SIAM and Crisil project 8–10% CAGR through FY30E driven by replacement demand, rural income recovery, premiumisation and EV adoption.[^ip-latest] [^vp-thread]
- **Policy tailwinds:** (a) PLI scheme for auto and auto components (Rs. 25,938 Cr outlay) favouring localisation; (b) FAME-II and state EV subsidies supporting Vida and Ather demand; (c) PM e-Drive scheme continuation; (d) PM Awas Yojana & rural infra capex boosting entry-level demand; (e) Reduction in GST on two-wheelers below 350cc to 18% (from 28%) supporting mass-market affordability.[^ip-latest]
- **Competitive moat analysis:**
  - *Brand & distribution (Strong):* Splendor has been India's highest-selling two-wheeler for 25+ consecutive years; 6,000+ dealer network with deepest rural reach in Tier 3–5 markets creates formidable customer acquisition and service moat.[^ip-latest]
  - *Scale & cost efficiency (Strong):* 1.05 Cr+ India capacity with >95% utilisation enables best-in-class per-unit fixed cost absorption; procurement scale and in-house die/tooling keeps BOM cost 4–6% below subscale peers.[^ar-fy25]
  - *Switching cost for end customer (Moderate):* Mass-market ICE buyers are price-sensitive with limited absolute brand stickiness, but a deeply entrenched "Hero = Splendor" mindset in the heartland provides 2–3 year switching lag.
  - *Pricing power (Moderate):* Hero raises prices 1–2% annually with negligible volume loss in entry-segment; pricing power is weaker in the 125–150cc segment where Honda and Bajaj compete aggressively.
  - *Technology / EV moat (Weak-to-Moderate):* Late entrant in EV (Vida launched 2022), ceding 2–3 year lead to Ola Electric, Ather, TVS iQube; Ather stake and Vida V2 platform partially mitigates the gap.

### SECTION 5 — MANAGEMENT QUALITY & CAPITAL ALLOCATION

- **Promoter pedigree & tenure:** Hero MotoCorp was incorporated in 1984 as Hero Honda Motors Ltd in JV with Honda Motor Co. (Japan). The promoter Munjal family — led by the late Dr. Brijmohan Lall Munjal and now Chairman Emeritus Dr. Pawan Munjal — has run the company for 40+ years. The board was professionalised post the 2011 Honda demerger and now includes independent directors with deep auto, finance and technology backgrounds. CEO Niranjan Gupta (appointed 2022, prior CFO) has brought sharper focus on capital efficiency, EV and premium product strategy.[^ar-fy25] [^vp-thread]
- **FCF deployment track record (3 years FY23–FY25):** Hero has generated cumulative free cash flow of ~Rs. 18,000 Cr over FY23–FY25 (OCF ~Rs. 22,500 Cr less capex of ~Rs. 4,500 Cr). Deployment pattern: (a) Dividends ~Rs. 9,500 Cr (~52% payout); (b) Ather Energy investment and Vida capex ~Rs. 3,000 Cr; (c) Balance sheet cash buildup from ~Rs. 7,000 Cr (FY22) to ~Rs. 11,500 Cr (FY25) — capital preservation balanced with selective growth bets.[^ar-fy25]
- **Debt management & credit profile:** Net cash positive balance sheet with negligible long-term debt; short-term working capital lines are routine trade finance. CRISIL Ratings and India Ratings have consistently rated Hero at AAA / Stable, the highest investment grade — a reflection of the company's near-zero leverage and consistent operating cash generation. Interest coverage ratio exceeds 50x on net cash position.[^ar-fy25]
- **Dividend policy & track record:** 5-year average dividend payout ratio of ~48–52%; FY25 dividend of Rs. 100/share implies a 3.59% yield at CMP, the highest in the listed Indian auto OEM peer set. Buyback track record is modest (one buyback in FY22 of Rs. 500 Cr) — capital return is overwhelmingly through dividends, reflecting mature-stage cash generation.[^ar-fy25]
- **Promoter pledging:** Nil / not applicable. No equity shares of the listed company are pledged by promoters or promoter group entities — a significant positive relative to several mid-cap auto component peers and reflective of the Munjal family's clean capital structure.[^ar-fy25]
- **Corporate governance flags:** (a) Statutory auditor B S R & Co LLP (a member firm of KPMG) — no auditor change in the last 5 years, audit reports are unqualified; (b) No material related-party transactions (RPTs) beyond arm's-length royalty payments to the Hero Group IP holding entity; (c) The demerger from Honda in 2011 and subsequent IP buyout for USD 1.0 Bn were conducted via a transparent scheme of arrangement approved by the NCLT; (d) Disclosures under Reg 30 (LODR) are timely and granular — recent example: 14–15 July 2026 disclosures on incremental Ather Energy investment were filed within 24 hours of the transaction.[^ar-fy25]
- **Strategic JVs & partnerships:**
  - *Harley-Davidson (2020):* Co-developed the X440 to access the premium commuter segment; manufacturing and distribution fully controlled by Hero — a low-capex, high-royalty asset-light model.
  - *Ather Energy (2021–2026):* Initial investment in 2021, incrementally topped up on 14–15 July 2026; gives Hero access to Ather's 450+ strong R&D team, 200+ retail touchpoints and the EL platform that underpins the Vida V2.
  - *Hero Tech Center Germany:* R&D hub in Munich for premium and EV motor/controller development — addressing a long-standing criticism of Hero as a tech laggard.[^ip-latest] [^cc-transcript]

#### Re-rating Potential Narrative

Hero Motocorp's structural re-rating triggers operate on three dimensions:

1. **Margin threshold:** Sustained EBITDA margin >16% (currently 14.86%) would justify P/E expansion to 19-20x, implying Rs. 7,200-7,800 share price.
2. **Export scaling:** Exports at >15% of revenue (currently ~6%) would narrow the Bajaj Auto valuation gap; every 100 bps mix shift = ~50 bps multiple expansion.
3. **EV portfolio traction:** Successful Vida scale-up (target 25,000-30,000 units/month by FY28) removes the "ICE-pure-play" discount overhang.

*Conversely, sub-13.5% margin or export stagnation would compress multiple to 14x, driving price toward Rs. 4,100 [^vp-thread].*
