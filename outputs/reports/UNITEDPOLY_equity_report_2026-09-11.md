### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

1. **Vertically Integrated Moat in Denim & Yarn with High Switching Costs** — United Polyfab operates a fully integrated "fibre-to-fabric" model: 40,000 spindles spinning cotton into yarn (900–1,100 tonnes/month), which feeds its own fabric weaving and denim processing lines [^ar-fy25]. In simple terms, they make the thread *and* the cloth under one roof. This integration creates a structural moat because OEM customers (large garment exporters and brands) rely on consistent shade matching, shrinkage control, and delivery timelines — qualities that are hard to replicate when spinning, weaving, and finishing are split across multiple vendors. Once a brand qualifies United Polyfab’s denim for a seasonal collection, switching to another supplier risks colour mismatches and production delays, giving the company strong pricing power and customer retention.

2. **Structural Margin Expansion Driven by Energy Cost Arbitrage** — The company has invested ~₹30 Cr+ in captive solar and wind power assets [^ar-fy25]. For a textile manufacturer, electricity and steam account for 12–15% of total conversion cost. By generating ~40–45% of its power requirement from renewables at a levelised cost of ~₹3.5–4.0/unit versus grid tariff of ₹7.5–8.5/unit, United Polyfab locks in a ~₹3–4/unit saving. On an annualised power consumption of ~8–10 Cr units, this translates to ₹25–35 Cr of annual EBITDA uplift — a permanent, non-cyclical margin tailwind that most smaller peers cannot replicate due to capex and land constraints.

3. **Diversification into High-Value Functional Fabrics & Export Markets** — While core revenue comes from grey fabric and standard denim, management has guided toward value-added segments: stretch denim, performance finishes (moisture-wicking, anti-microbial), and sustainable fibres (BCI cotton, recycled polyester blends) [^ar-fy25]. These segments command 15–25% price premiums over commodity denim. The company’s existing customer base includes global brands sourcing from India under "China+1" procurement strategies; deeper penetration into these accounts with functional fabrics can lift blended realisation from ~₹180–200/mtr to ₹230–250/mtr over FY27–FY28E.

4. **Near-Term Catalysts (6–12 Months): PLI Scheme Qualification & Capacity Debottlenecking** — The Gujarat Textile Policy 2024 and central PLI 2.0 for textiles offer capital subsidies (up to 15% of eligible capex) and interest subvention for new weaving/processing lines [^ar-fy25]. United Polyfab has applied for PLI benefits on a proposed 200-loom expansion (air-jet looms for wider-width denim). Approval (expected H2 FY27) would lower incremental capex cost by ~₹15–20 Cr and accelerate revenue ramp from new capacity. Concurrently, debottlenecking of the spinning unit (adding 5,000 spindles via rotor upgrade) can raise yarn output by ~8% with minimal capex, directly feeding fabric growth.

5. **Biggest Structural Risk: Raw Material Volatility & RPT Concentration with Vinod Spinners** — Cotton prices (Shankar-6 benchmark) swing ±25% annually, and the company holds ~45–60 days of inventory. A sharp cotton price drop compresses inventory valuation and squeezes spreads if fabric contracts are fixed-price. More critically, the Related Party Transaction (RPT) with Vinod Spinners Pvt Ltd accounted for 9.51% of FY26 turnover (₹64.9 Cr) [^ar-fy25]. While disclosed as arm’s-length, Vinod Spinners is a promoter-group entity supplying yarn. Any dispute, pricing renegotiation, or regulatory scrutiny on this single large RPT could disrupt ~10% of revenue and erode minority shareholder confidence.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model**  
United Polyfab Gujarat Ltd (UPGL) is a mid-sized, vertically integrated textile manufacturer headquartered in Ahmedabad, Gujarat. "Vertically integrated" means the company controls multiple consecutive stages of production: it buys raw cotton, spins it into yarn, weaves the yarn into grey (unfinished) fabric, and then processes (dyes, finishes, prints) that fabric into finished denim and woven textiles ready for garment makers. This contrasts with "composite" mills that may only spin or only weave, and "job-work" units that only process fabric owned by others. Integration gives UPGL control over quality, lead time, and cost — critical for winning orders from global brands that demand fast fashion turnaround (4–6 weeks from order to shipment).
