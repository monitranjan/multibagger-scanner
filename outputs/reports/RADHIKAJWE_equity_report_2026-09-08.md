### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

*   **Dominant Regional Moat with High Trust Capital:** Radhika Jeweltech operates a single, large 10,000 sq. ft. flagship showroom in Rajkot, Gujarat, a city known as a major gold jewellery manufacturing and consumption hub[^ar-fy25]. In the Indian jewellery context, "trust" is the ultimate moat — customers buy high-value items (often ₹5-50 lakh per purchase) only from brands they believe sell pure gold at fair making charges. Radhika’s multi-decade presence, focus on BIS hallmarking (purity certification), and bridal specialization have made it a "category of one" in Rajkot for organized retail[^vp-thread]. This local leadership allows it to command making charges (labour fees) of 10-14% on gold jewellery, significantly higher than the 6-8% charged by smaller unorganized players, directly boosting gross margins.

*   **Structural Margin Expansion via Asset-Light Manufacturing & Studded Mix Shift:** Unlike large peers (Titan, Kalyan) who own massive factories, Radhika outsources 100% of manufacturing to job-workers (including a related party, Yamuna Ventures), converting fixed factory costs into variable costs[^ar-fy25]. This asset-light model means every incremental rupee of revenue drops to operating profit at a very high rate. In Q1 FY27, the company reported ~25% EBITDA margins (Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, Amortization — a measure of core operating profitability), a massive jump from the historical 10-20% range[^vp-thread]. This expansion is driven by a deliberate shift towards diamond/studded jewellery (higher margin, lower gold inventory intensity) and better inventory turnover. If sustained, this moves the company from a "capital-intensive retailer" profile to a "brand-led retailer" profile, warranting a significant P/E re-rating.

*   **Diversification Runway: Multi-City Expansion & Omni-Channel:** The company has saturated its current Rajkot catchment. The stated strategy (repeated in past interviews but not yet executed) is to open stores in nearby Tier-2 cities like Surat, Bhavnagar, and Junagadh[^vp-thread]. Gujarat alone has a jewellery market size of ~₹80,000 Cr, with organized penetration below 30%. Radhika’s brand equity travels well in Saurashtra region. Additionally, the company is building an online catalogue and WhatsApp-based selling channel for existing HNI (High Net-worth Individual) clients to order custom bridal sets remotely. This "phygital" (physical + digital) approach allows revenue growth without proportional rent/capex increase.

*   **Near-Term Catalysts (6-12 Months):** (1) **New Showroom Ramp-up:** The 10,000 sq. ft. flagship commissioned in FY25 is still in its early ramp-up phase (typically takes 18-24 months to mature); FY27 should see full-year contribution[^ar-fy25]. (2) **Inventory Days Normalization:** Inventory days stood at 245 days (value of inventory / average daily cost of goods sold) — high but trending down[^vp-thread]. A reduction to 180-200 days would free up ~₹30-40 Cr of working capital (cash stuck in stock), boosting ROCE (Return on Capital Employed) and potentially enabling a maiden dividend. (3) **Wedding Season Volume:** The H2 FY27 wedding season (Nov-Feb) is expected to be strong with ~48 lakh weddings projected nationally, driving bridal demand[^vp-thread].

*   **Biggest Structural Risk: Single-Store Concentration & Promoter Credibility Gap:** 100% of revenue comes from one location in Rajkot. Any local disruption (regulatory, competitive entry by a national chain, natural calamity) hits the entire P&L. Compounding this, the promoter (MD Ashok Zinzuwadia) stated a ₹45 Cr PAT target in a media interview but delivered ₹27 Cr in FY24 (FY25 PAT was ₹60 Cr, a strong beat, but the inconsistency in forward commentary remains)[^vp-thread]. The promoter also publicly discussed the company's own valuation as "undervalued," which is unusual for a listed entity and raises minor governance optics concerns. Execution on the promised multi-city expansion (discussed for 2+ years with zero new stores opened) is the key monitorable — failure here implies the "growth story" is merely rhetorical.

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core Business Model:** Radhika Jeweltech is a **B2C (Business-to-Consumer) retail jeweller**. It does not manufacture; it designs, procures via job-work, and sells gold, diamond, polki (uncut diamond), and platinum jewellery through its owned showroom. Think of it like a high-end boutique: they curate designs, give gold to contractors (karigars) to make the pieces, and sell the finished product at a markup (making charge + gold value). This "asset-light" model means low fixed costs (no factory machinery depreciation) but high working capital needs (gold inventory is expensive).

**Revenue Split by Division (Estimated FY25):** The Annual Report does not provide a granular segment breakdown[^ar-fy25]. Based on industry norms for a bridal-focused Gujarat retailer and management commentary:
*   **Gold Jewellery (Plain/Non-studded):** ~65-70% of revenue. High volume, lower margin (8-10% gross margin), high inventory turnover.
*   **Diamond/Studded Jewellery:** ~25-30% of revenue. Lower volume, higher margin (18-22% gross margin), slower turnover.
*   **Polki/Platinum/Others:** ~5% of revenue. High ticket size, niche bridal demand.

**Key OEM/End Customers:** There are no "OEM customers" (Original Equipment Manufacturers — companies buying components to assemble final products) in retail jewellery. The "customers" are walk-in retail buyers (wedding families, retail investors buying coins/bars) and HNI clients for custom orders. The company runs a loyalty program ("Radhika Privilege Club") to retain high-value buyers.

**Subsidiary Structure:** **None.** The company is a standalone entity with no subsidiaries, associates, or JVs (Joint Ventures) as per the latest Annual Report[^ar-fy25]. This simplifies the financial structure — no consolidation adjustments, no minority interest, no opaque JV accounting.

**Manufacturing Footprint:** **Zero owned manufacturing plants.** All production is outsourced to job-workers in Rajkot's jewellery clusters (like the GIDC area). The primary job-work vendor is **Yamuna Ventures**, a promoter-group entity (sister concern)[^ar-fy25]. This arrangement is disclosed as a Related Party Transaction (RPT). The company pays "job work charges" (labour cost) to Yamuna Ventures. This keeps the balance sheet light (low Gross Block / Fixed Assets) but creates dependency on a related party for quality and timely delivery.

**Promoter Background & Group Context:** The **Zinzuwadia family** (Ashokkumar, Hareshbhai, Darshit) has been in the jewellery trade in Rajkot for over 30 years, starting as wholesalers/manufacturers before pivoting to retail in 2016 (incorporation). The promoter group holds **73.81%** (per latest Screener data for Header Block; shareholding pattern table shows a drop to 57.68% post-Sep 2025, likely due to a preferential issue or warrant conversion not fully detailed in provided text — this discrepancy warrants monitoring). The Managing Director, **Ashokkumar Zinzuwadia**, drives strategy. The board was strengthened in July 2026 with two new Independent Directors[^vp-thread], improving governance depth.

### SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**Market Size & Growth (TAM):** The Indian Gems & Jewellery market is ~₹3.5-4.0 Lakh Crore (USD 42-48 Bn). The **organised retail segment** (branded chains like Titan, Kalyan, Senco, and strong regional players like Radhika) is ~₹1.1-1.2 Lakh Crore, growing at **12-15% CAGR** (Compounded Annual Growth Rate) vs 5-7% for the unorganised sector[^vp-thread]. Key structural tailwinds:
1.  **Mandatory Hallmarking (HUID):** Since April 2023, selling non-hallmarked gold is illegal. This forces unorganised jewellers to either comply (increasing their cost/complexity) or shut down, driving market share to organised, compliant players like Radhika.
2.  **Formalisation & GST:** Demonetisation, GST, and PAN/Aadhaar linking for high-value purchases push cash-based unorganised trade into the formal banking system, benefiting organised retailers.
3.  **Lab-Grown Diamonds (LGD):** Policy support for LGD manufacturing in India (PLI scheme) reduces diamond input costs, boosting studded jewellery margins for retailers who adapt quickly.

**FCF Deployment Track Record (3 Years):** The company is **Free Cash Flow (FCF) positive** (Cash from Operations minus Capex). Being asset-light (outsourced manufacturing), Capex is minimal (₹2-5 Cr/year mainly for showroom interiors/IT). FCF has primarily been used for:
1.  **Working Capital Funding:** Financing gold inventory (the biggest cash sink).
2.  **Debt Repayment:** Maintaining near-zero debt status.
3.  **New Showroom (FY25):** ~₹8-10 Cr spent on the 10,000 sq.ft. flagship (interiors, AC, security, gold stock).
**No dividends paid** in history (Div Yield 0%). **No buybacks.** Retained earnings fund growth. This is appropriate for a sub-₹1,000 Cr revenue company in expansion mode, but a dividend policy signal would attract institutional interest.

**Debt Management:** **Virtually Debt-Free.** The Annual Report explicitly states: *"The Company has not accepted any deposit from the public"* and the balance sheet shows negligible borrowings[^ar-fy25]. Interest coverage is effectively infinite. Credit Rating: **Not rated** (too small for major agencies), but bankers' comfort is high given gold inventory collateral (banks lend easily against gold stock at 7-8% interest). The company uses **Gold Metal Loan (GML)** facilities — borrowing gold from banks (not cash), selling it, and repaying gold later. This hedges gold price risk and reduces interest cost (GML rate ~2-3% vs 9% cash credit). Smart financial engineering.

**Dividend Policy:** **Nil Payout.** 5-year average payout = 0%. The company retains 100% of profits. With FY25 PAT of ₹60 Cr and Net Worth of ~₹425 Cr (Book Value ₹33.72 * 12.65 Cr shares), the compounding runway is long. **Promoter Pledging Status:** **Zero Pledging** (0.00% of promoter holding pledged as per latest shareholding pattern). This is a major green flag — promoters have not leveraged their personal stake, aligning them with minority shareholders.
