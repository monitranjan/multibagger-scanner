### SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Market leadership / moat:** Dixon Technologies is India’s largest pure-play Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) provider – think of EMS as a “factory-for-hire” where global and Indian brands outsource the entire job of assembling TVs, phones, washing machines, and even complex circuit boards instead of building their own plants [^cc-transcript]. The company’s moat is built on scale and switching costs: once a brand like Samsung or Vivo qualifies Dixon’s production lines and embeds its quality systems, moving to another contract manufacturer means re-certifying factories, retraining workers, and risking product delays – a costly hassle that keeps customers sticky [^vp-thread]. In Mobile & EMS, Dixon shipped 7.5 million smartphones in Q1 FY27 alone and is the largest feature-phone maker in India (two-thirds of national output), giving it volume leverage that smaller peers cannot match [^cc-transcript]. This leadership is reinforced by backward integration into display and camera modules, raising the cost for a customer to leave because Dixon controls more of the supply chain [^cc-transcript].

- **Structural margin story:** Historically Dixon’s operating margins (the percentage of revenue left after paying for direct production costs, like raw materials and factory wages) were thin – around 3-4% consolidated in FY22 [^ip-latest]. The structural improvement thesis rests on a shift from pure assembly (“screwdriver” work) to component manufacturing and Original Design Manufacturing (ODM, where Dixon designs the product, not just builds it) [^cc-transcript]. For example, lighting products are already 93% ODM mix and earned 6.5% segment operating margin in Q3’22 [^ip-latest]. Management guided that PLI 2 (Production Linked Incentive scheme 2.0 – a government cash reward per unit made locally) plus display and camera module ventures will add 150-500 basis points (bps = 1/100th of a percent, so 150-500 bps = 1.5%-5% extra margin) over FY27-28 [^cc-transcript]. The negative-five-day working capital cycle (meaning Dixon gets paid by customers before it must pay suppliers, so it actually earns interest on float) further lifts returns, with ROCE at 34.1% and ROE 23.4% despite temporary margin compression [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies:** Dixon is not just a TV assembler; it is aggressively entering adjacent high-growth verticals. In telecom, it is scaling microwave backhaul radios and partnered with Gemtek (Taiwan) for optical transceivers, addressing 5G/FWA (Fixed Wireless Access) demand [^cc-transcript]. In IT hardware, the Chennai campus is onboarding gaming-notebook customers, an Inventec JV (60-40 ownership) starts Q4 FY27, and SSD manufacturing begins Q3 – tapping the ₹70,000 Cr+ domestic laptop/tablet opportunity under ECMS (Enhanced Component Manufacturing Scheme) [^cc-transcript]. Home appliances launched robotic vacuums, dishwashers, and front-load washing machines, while consumer electronics initiated Mini-LED TV production [^cc-transcript]. This diversification reduces reliance on any single product and lifts mix toward premium, higher-margin goods [^vp-thread].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** The Vivo joint venture (PN3) approved July ’26 commences operations in Q3 FY27, adding branded smartphone volumes with better margins [^cc-transcript]. Display facility trials in Q3 and mass production by Q4 will mark Dixon’s entry into the most expensive phone component, a key margin lever [^cc-transcript]. The Signify lighting JV begins exports to US/Germany in Q2/Q3 FY27, opening ₹crore export revenues [^cc-transcript]. PLI 2 effective April ’26 provides incremental production incentives irrespective of export, and management expects 20-25% QoQ mobile volume growth in Q2 FY27 on a strong order book [^cc-transcript]. Nomura’s March ’26 note flagged HKC display JV approval as a 50% upside catalyst, already partially realized [^cc-transcript].

- **Biggest structural risk:** The dominant risk is severe customer concentration and low absolute operating margins that amplify any input-cost shock [^vp-thread]. Dixon’s mobile segment is ~91% of Q1 FY27 revenue, and a handful of global brands (Samsung, Vivo, etc.) drive the bulk; loss of one major account or a China supply-chain disruption (e.g., memory price hikes seen in Q1) compresses already thin profits [^cc-transcript][^vp-thread]. Additionally, the expiry of Mobile PLI 1 in March ’26 temporarily dented margins, proving subsidy dependence [^cc-transcript]. If PLI 2 retention rates with brands disappoint (management was evasive on this point), earnings quality could deteriorate [^cc-transcript].

### SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Dixon Technologies operates as an integrated contract manufacturer – essentially it is the invisible engine behind many household electronics. Its core business model is to take components from suppliers, assemble them into finished goods or sub-assemblies under the brand’s label (EMS), and increasingly design the product itself (ODM). The company earns a “cost-plus” fee: it passes most material costs to the customer and keeps a small manufacturing margin, plus any government PLI bounty [^cc-transcript]. Revenue split by division for the latest reported Q1 FY27 (total ₹15,557 Cr) shows Mobile & EMS dominating at ₹14,179 Cr (≈91% of consolidated), Consumer Electronics (TV/Refrigerators) ₹987 Cr (≈6%), Telecom ₹2,100 Cr and IT Hardware ₹1,350 Cr are sub-sets within EMS verticals, Home Appliances ₹382 Cr (≈2.5%) [^cc-transcript]. Historical FY22 mix from investor presentation shows Consumer Electronics 46%, Mobile & EMS 31%, Lighting 14%, Home Appliances 6%, Security 4% – illustrating how mobile has surged post-PLI [^ip-latest]. Key OEM/end customers include Samsung, Vivo, Xiaomi, Panasonic, Signify (Philips lighting JV), Bajaj, Wipro, Havells, Voltas Beko across divisions [^vp-thread]. Subsidiary structure includes Dixon Electro Manufacturing, Dixon Homes, Dixon Tech (Chennai) for Rexxam PCBA JV, Q Tech for camera modules, and 50% stake in Security Systems JV [^ip-latest][^cc-transcript]. Manufacturing footprint spans Noida (UP), Tirupati (AP), Chennai (TN), and a planned MP SPV – over 10 plants [^cc-transcript]. Promoter Sunil Vachani (Executive Chairman) founded the Noida-based group in 1993; the annual report confirms his re-appointment and a final dividend of ₹8/share for FY25 [^ar-fy25].
