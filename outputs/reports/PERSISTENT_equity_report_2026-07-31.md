## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Differentiated mid-tier positioning with superior capital efficiency**: Persistent sits in the sweet spot of the Indian IT pecking order — too small to be lumped with the mature Tier-1 trio (TCS, Infosys, HCLTech) but materially larger and more diversified than the mid-cap challengers. ROCE of 29.87% and ROE of 23.80% materially outpace the Tier-1 average of 18-20% and 15-18% respectively, indicating that incremental capital is being deployed at high incremental returns even at a Rs. 86,994 Cr market cap [^ip-latest].

- **AI-led deal momentum is the structural margin story**: Management has repositioned the go-to-market around AI, Cloud, and Platform Engineering, with the Software, Hi-Tech & Emerging Industries (SHTI) vertical and BFSI verticals driving deal sizes that are 2-3x the legacy ticket size. This mix shift is visible in the FY26 margin trajectory and is the primary lever for sustainable EBITDA margin expansion of 100-150 bps over FY26-28E [^cc-transcript].

- **Diversification into high-growth adjacencies reducing concentration risk**: The acquisition-led build-out in Healthcare (Hippocratic AI partnership), BFSI platformisation, and the SaaS-based Accelerite unit has reduced dependence on any single vertical to under 30% of revenue. This contrasts with the 2018-era portfolio where the top-3 verticals contributed ~65% of revenue [^ar-fy25].

- **Near-term catalysts (6-12 months)**: (i) Sustained sequential TCV growth of $400-500 Mn/quarter validating demand environment; (ii) Margin tailwind from the AI-led productivity pass-through (gen-AI levers already contributing ~70 bps in FY26); (iii) Likely re-rating once the H1FY27 results clarify whether the recent capex cycle in sales hires is yielding the expected 14%+ organic revenue growth; (iv) Potential 1-2 tuck-in acquisitions in Healthcare/IP verticals using the strong net cash balance sheet [^ip-latest].

- **Biggest structural risk**: Persistent's mid-cap scale means it is structurally more vulnerable to (i) senior talent attrition to Tier-1 peers who can pay 20-30% premia, (ii) wage-inflation shocks that fixed-bid contracts cannot absorb, and (iii) client-concentration in Hi-Tech and BFSI where capex budgets are first to be cut in a US recession scenario — a risk that the 18.6% EPS beat-driven rally in FY25 may have already begun to price in [^vp-thread].
