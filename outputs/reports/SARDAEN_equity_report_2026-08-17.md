## SECTION 2 — INVESTMENT THESIS

- **Captive-mine moat anchors a structural cost advantage in steel and ferro alloys.** SEML is among the lowest-cost integrated steel producers in India with 360,000 MT DRI capacity, 240,000 MTPA crude steel capacity, and 75,000 MT ferro-alloy capacity, all underwritten by captive iron-ore and coal linkages that insulate input costs from spot volatility — a configuration that virtually no listed mid-cap peer in the value-added steel/ferro-alloy space can replicate at this scale [^ip-latest]. The captive-mine cost arbitrage is the single most important reason SEML has historically delivered 600–800 bps of margin uplift versus non-integrated peers during commodity upcycles [^vp-thread].

- **Margin trajectory is structurally re-rating from 14–15% EBITDA band to 18–20%+.** With 113 MW Sikkim hydro now operational alongside the existing 24 MW Jashpur hydro and ~160 MW of thermal/waste-heat captive power, the captive power mix is now north of 95% of total consumption [^ar-fy25]. The CFO has publicly guided to sustained 20%+ EBITDA margins in the current steel cycle, a meaningful step-up from the 12–14% band delivered in FY22–FY24 [^vp-thread]. Each 100 bps of incremental EBITDA margin on FY26 revenue equates to roughly Rs. 30–35 Cr of incremental PAT, providing material operating leverage to the consolidated earnings base.

- **Diversification into hydro and waste-heat power is converting the asset base from commodity cyclical to quasi-utility.** The 113 MW Sikkim run-of-river hydro (commissioned FY24) is essentially a 30-year PPA-backed annuity with negligible fuel cost, and we estimate it will contribute Rs. 60–80 Cr of annual EBITDA at full-year run-rate [^ar-fy25]. Combined with the existing captive thermal/waste-heat recovery (WHR) units, captive power contribution to consolidated EBITDA is now estimated at 25–30%, providing a meaningful earnings cushion in any flat-to-down steel scenario [^cc-transcript].

- **Near-term catalysts (6–12 months):** (i) full annualisation of Sikkim hydro earnings through FY27; (ii) commissioning of expanded ferro-alloy capacity (manganese-based, leveraging captive coal and quartz); (iii) potential value-unlock from the wholly-owned subsidiary structure referenced in the 07-Aug-2026 corporate announcement [^cc-transcript]; (iv) steel cycle tailwind from continued China supply-side discipline; (v) potential re-rating as the market reclassifies SEML from "pure commodity" to "commodity + power annuity" mix.

- **Biggest structural risk is a sharp reversal in global steel realisations, particularly rebar and billet.** A simultaneous downturn in Chinese export prices, Indian infrastructure capex slowdown, and softening ferro-alloy realisations (manganese/silico-manganese) would compress blended EBITDA margins by 400–600 bps and push net debt/EBITDA back above 2.0x [^vp-thread]. The 38% trailing dividend yield — which signals a one-time capital return event rather than a sustainable payout policy — also reduces the equity cushion available to absorb cyclical drawdowns [^cc-transcript].

## SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OVERVIEW

**Core business model:** SEML operates a vertically integrated ferrous value chain anchored on captive iron-ore and coal — from DRI/sponge iron through billets, ingots, TMT bars, and value-added long products, with a parallel non-ferrous track producing manganese-based and silico-manganese ferro alloys for domestic and export markets across 60+ countries [^ip-latest]. The captive power complex (thermal, WHR, hydro) is a strategic overlay that converts process waste and free water resources into low-cost internal electricity, making SEML self-sufficient for nearly all of its power needs [^ar-fy25].

**Revenue split by division (estimated, FY26A basis):**

| Division | % of Revenue | Key Products | End Markets |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Ferro Alloys | ~20–25% | Ferro manganese, silico manganese | Steel makers (domestic + 60+ countries) |
| Power | ~10–15% | Captive thermal, WHR, 24 MW hydro (Jashpur), 113 MW hydro (Sikkim) | Captive consumption + merchant sale |

*Source: Investor Presentation latest; company filings.*

**Key customers by division:**
- **Steel/TMT:** Domestic infra projects, re-rollers, regional distributors; the 60+ country export footprint includes Europe, MENA, Southeast Asia [^ip-latest].
- **Ferro Alloys:** Major Indian steelmakers (Tata Steel, JSW, SAIL downstream) and global buyers across Asia, Europe, Americas [^vp-thread].
- **Power:** Captive consumption (~95%) with merchant sale of surplus to state DISCOMs via long-term PPAs [^ar-fy25].

**Subsidiary structure:**
- **Madhya Bharat Power Corporation Ltd.** — owns and operates the 113 MW Sikkim run-of-river hydro project, the single largest capex line of FY22–FY24 [^ar-fy25].
- **Chhattisgarh Hydro Power LLP** (24 MW Jashpur hydro, operational since 2012) [^ip-latest].
- Wholly-owned subsidiary referenced in the 07-Aug-2026 update suggests ongoing corporate restructuring within the power/holding structure [^cc-transcript].
- Several mining and trading subsidiaries supporting iron-ore, coal, and pellet operations.

**Manufacturing footprint:**
- **Chhattisgarh (Raipur):** Main integrated steel complex, DRI kilns, billet shop, TMT/wire rod mill, captive thermal & WHR power, ferro-alloy furnaces, iron-ore pellet plant [^ip-latest].
- **Madhya Pradesh:** Coal mining linkages and processing.
- **Sikkim:** 113 MW run-of-river hydro power plant (operational FY24) [^ar-fy25].
- **Jashpur, Chhattisgarh:** 24 MW small hydro power plant.
- **Iron-ore mines:** Captive leases in Chhattisgarh/MP providing ore to the Raipur complex.

**Promoter background:** The Sarda family (Raipur-based) has been in metals and mining for over three decades. **Kamal Kishore Sarda** is the lead promoter (referenced in the 14-Aug-2026 SEBI SAST disclosure) and the group is closely held at 74.85% promoter holding [^ar-fy25]. **Manish Sarda** is part of the next-gen promoter team and is publicly visible in management commentary and investor interactions [^vp-thread]. The group has built SEML from a single-plant ferro-alloy operation into a diversified metals + power platform with consolidated revenues crossing Rs. 2,500–3,000 Cr range historically and growing [^ip-latest].

## SECTION 4 — INDUSTRY & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**TAM and growth:**
- **Indian steel TAM:** ~Rs. 10–12 lakh Cr (FY26); expected to grow to Rs. 18–20 lakh Cr by FY30 driven by infrastructure capex, urban housing, and manufacturing. India per-capita steel consumption at ~90–100 kg vs global average of ~230 kg implies multi-decade runway [^vp-thread].
- **Indian ferro-alloys TAM:** ~Rs. 50,000–60,000 Cr (FY26), CAGR ~6–8% through FY30, with manganese-based alloys forming the dominant sub-segment.
- **India hydro/power TAM:** Much larger at multi-lakh-Cr scale; SEML's niche is small/run-of-river hydro and captive industrial power.

**India industry CAGR (FY26–FY30E):**
- Steel: 8–10% volume CAGR; realisation CAGR lower at 3–4% given China-driven global price discipline.
- Ferro alloys: 6–8% volume CAGR, tightly coupled to global crude steel output.
- Captive/merchant industrial power: 5–7%.

**Policy tailwinds:**
- **PLI Scheme for Steel:** Rs. 6,322 Cr outlay incentivising value-added steel, specialty alloys, and capacity additions — SEML qualifies for several sub-segments [^vp-thread].
- **National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP):** Rs. 111 lakh Cr outlay over FY20–FY30 directly benefits rebar/TMT/billet demand.
- **National Steel Policy 2017:** 300 MTPA target by 2030 supports long-cycle demand visibility.
- **Renewable/hydro push:** Sikkim and north-east hydro policy incentives benefit SEML's small-hydro portfolio.
- **Mining reforms:** Captive mine allocation policy and Mineral Laws (Amendment) Act support SEML's captive iron-ore and coal linkages.
